Introduction

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) and Fragile X Syndrome (FXS) have similar behavioral phenotypes, especially for social communication and repetitive behaviors, but they differ greatly in their etiologies. An important issue in neuroimaging is the understanding of how deviations of functional dynamics are constrained by fixed structural connectivity and topology. Linear approaches have been failing to represent the complex non-linear signaling process that defines the topology of the brain. To address this problem, we used deep learning techniques to analyze Structure-Function Coupling (SFC). We hypothesize that while FXS and idiopathic ASD share a decoupling of certain social-related brain regions, they use different strategies to combine sensory and cognitive information. This work uses an interpretable Graph Neural Network (GNN) to solve the non-linear problem of connecting the analyzed regions, trying to identify both shared and disorder-specific biomarkers that are related to the level of clinical severity.1,2

Methods

We conducted the analysis on a cohort of 244 children, among whom were 52 with FXS (all with confirmed FMR1 >200 CGG repeats), 136 with idiopathic ASD, and 56 typically developing controls. Multi-modal MRI scans, including DTI and resting-state functional MRI (fMRI), were collected from all subjects. Brain graphs were parcellated using the AAL3 atlas (166 regions).³ To analyze the non-linear SFC, we modified a Modality-Specific and Interactive Graph Convolutional Network (MS-Inter-GCN).⁴ Unlike linear correlation analysis, in the MS-Inter-GCN model, the structural and functional graphs are combined to derive a region-weighted attention value as the SFC measure for the efficiency of information translation from structural constraints to functional processes (Figure 1).

Statistical analyses comprised Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) for elucidation of group differences in regional SFC. To unravel the underlying associations between the neurobiological constraints and clinical heterogeneity, we conducted multivariate Partial Least Squares (PLS) correlation analysis. ⁵ In this analysis, the high-dimensional matrices of SFC and behavioral assessments, including scores for the ADOS-2, and Griffiths Development Scales (DQs), were reduced to a pair of latent components that best reveal the covariance between the neurobiological attributes and the clinical phenotypes.

Results

GNN-based SFC analysis disclosed group-shared and disorder-specific differences (Figure 2A). The FXS and ASD groups showed shared hypo-coupling in the right amygdala, right pallidum, and right cerebellum crus I compared to controls, as well as hyper-coupling in the right substantia nigra pars reticulata. Divergent patterns were also found to be present: the ASD group showed reduced coupling in the right lateral pulvinar of the thalamus and left precentral gyrus, while in FXS, disorder-specific decoupling was found in the right lingual gyrus.

Multivariate PLS analysis also helped to further define the patterns of SFC that were relevant to behavioral domains. In the ASD group, the first latent component (LC1) accounted for most of the variance (Figure 2B) and indicated a strong correlation between brain scores and clinical domains (r = 0.41, p < 2.47×10⁻⁶) (Figure 2C). Clinical loading analysis showed that this factor was positively correlated with CSS, SA-S, and RRB-S scores, as well as negatively correlated with DQs, suggesting that the underlying SFC pattern is reflective of a phenotype of greater severity and lower functional development (Figure 2D). The FXS population showed an even stronger correlation (r = 0.52, p < 0.003) in its significant latent factor (Figure 2E, F). However, unlike the ASD population, its loading profile was more characterized by positive values on DQs and negative values on all autism symptoms (Figure 2G).

Conclusion

Through the application of a non-linear Graph Neural Network architecture, this study has shown that although FXS and ASD share a common limbic pathology that may underlie a common set of social-emotional deficits, they have fundamentally different architectures of sensory and cognitive integration. FXS is characterized by a specific visual-lingual decoupling, whereas ASD presents with a different set of thalamic and motor changes. In addition, the multivariate analysis presented also emphasizes that these patterns of coupling are not simply structural artefacts but present a meaningful feature of strong association with behavioral severity and development.
