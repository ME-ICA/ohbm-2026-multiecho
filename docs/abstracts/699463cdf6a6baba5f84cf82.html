Introduction

Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE) is increasingly conceptualized as a network disorder.1 Despite surgery being the standard treatment for drug-resistant TLE, approximately 30% of patients fail to achieve seizure freedom,2 highlighting the critical need for robust prognostic biomarkers.

Dynamic Functional Connectivity (DFC) captures time‑varying patterns of inter‑regional interactions,3 and has revealed connectivity alterations in epilepsy that are not detectable with static FC.4 Structural Connectivity (SC), derived from diffusion MRI, constrains the repertoire of functional interactions. Recent work has begun to quantify dynamic structure‑function coupling over time.5 Despite these advances, multimodal frameworks that integrate SC and dynamic FC and evaluate their ability to predict post‑surgical outcomes in TLE are still scarce in the literature, motivating our proposed SC‑DFC approach.

In this study, we proposed a Structural Connectivity-constrained Dynamic Functional Connectivity (SC-DFC) framework by integrating DTI and rs-fMRI via multilayer network. We hypothesized that: TLE patients exhibit altered dynamic metrics (e.g., reduced flexibility) compared to healthy controls, and graphical network properties of preoperative SC-DFC can distinguish patients who achieve seizure freedom (Engel I) from those with unfavorable outcomes (Engel II/III) post-surgery.

Methods

Data acquisition: Forty drug refractory TLE patients and 33 healthy controls were recruited under local IRB approval. They underwent MRI scans at 3.0T, with T1-weighted structural scans (1 x 1 x 1 mm3), resting-state fMRI scans (2.5 x 2.5 x 2.5 mm3, 700 time points), and DTI scans (2 x 2 x 2 mm3, 36 directions b-value of 1000). Patients were followed up 2 years after surgery, 19 achieved Engel Class I. Data processing: Resting state fMRI data underwent standard preprocessing including removal of the first 10 time points, slice timing correction, realignment, regression of nuisance covariates, band-pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz), DARTEL normalization, and spatial smoothing (with 4*4*4 Gaussian kernel). Dynamic Functional Connectivity (DFC) was calculated via a sliding-window approach (window length: 50 TRs, step: 1 TR) using GRETNA, generating 641 time windows per subject. Diffusion MRI data were preprocessed using MRtrix3. Preprocessing included denoising, correction for motion and eddy current distortions, bias field correction, and conversion to MRtrix format. Constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD) was used to estimate fiber orientation distributions (FODs). Whole-brain probabilistic tractography was then performed using the iFOD2 algorithm, with anatomical constraints and dynamic seeding. Streamline filtering was applied using SIFT2 to improve biological interpretability. We constructed two multilayer networks for each subject: a standard DFC network with temporal identity coupling, and an SC-DFC network incorporating a Min-Max normalized DTI structural layer coupled to all functional layers. The GenLouvain algorithm was applied to partition dynamic communities, from which metrics including recruitment, integration, flexibility, and promiscuity were derived to evaluate the network status. Results

We categorized the participants into the Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE) group and the Healthy Control (HC) group. Independent samples t-tests were performed to compare the global mean of dynamic community metrics derived from both DFC and SC-DFC networks. The TLE group was further categorized based on post-surgical seizure status into the Good Outcome subgroup (Engel Class I) and the Poor Outcome subgroup (Engel Class II or III), and the same statistical comparisons were conducted between these two subgroups.

In the DFC network analysis, we observed significant differences between patients and controls. Global flexibility was significantly higher in the HC group compared to the TLE group (P=0.011), whereas global recruitment was significantly higher in the TLE group than in controls (P=0.019). These comparisons are illustrated in Figure 2(A). 

Regarding surgical prognosis, analysis of the SC-DFC network revealed that the Poor Outcome subgroup exhibited significantly higher global flexibility compared to the Good Outcome subgroup (P=0.022, figure2(D)). 

To investigate the specific regional contributions, we performed nodal analyses on the SC-DFC network comparing Good and Poor Outcome subgroups. Given the lateralized nature of TLE pathology, brain regions were realigned into the Ipsilateral (side of seizure onset) and Contralateral hemispheres to standardize the analysis. The brain regions exhibiting significant differences (P < 0.05) in these regional analyses are displayed as brain mapping figures in Figure 2(E).

Conclusion

Our findings confirm that TLE is characterized by aberrant dynamic network reconfiguration, evidenced by significant alterations in flexibility and recruitment within DFC networks. By employing a multilayer SC-DFC framework, we further demonstrated that preoperative network flexibility significantly differs between patients with favorable (Engel I) and unfavorable surgical outcomes. This indicates that the flexibility of the SC-DFC network may serve as a prognostic biomarker. Consequently, multilayer community detection metrics hold promise as novel, non-invasive biomarkers for both the diagnosis of TLE and the pre-surgical prediction of prognosis.
