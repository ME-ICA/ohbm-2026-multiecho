Introduction

Advanced neuroimaging and computational analysis have become central to the presurgical evaluation of drug-resistant epilepsy, where precise delineation of the seizure onset zone is critical for surgical success. Current workflows routinely combine multiple imaging modalities and extensive post-processing, yet their integration into objective, data-driven predictors of surgical outcome has only recently gained traction.

Drug-resistant epilepsy affects millions worldwide and is associated with recurrent seizures, physical injury, and profound social consequences. For a substantial subset of patients, pharmacotherapy remains ineffective or yields only transient benefits. Resective brain surgery remains the only intervention with demonstrated potential for long-term seizure freedom in this population.

Current approaches to outcome prediction usually rely on a specific selected method – morphometry, diffusion characteristics, or functional connectivity – but the integration of multimodal data into a single predictor has not yet been widely explored.

In the present project, we aim to predict surgical outcomes in subgroups of non-lesional temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) using machine learning (ML) models, potentially guiding clinical decision-making and improving long-term outcomes for drug-resistant epileptic patients.

Our study targeted two objectives:

1. Selection of imaging methods IMs and metrics most appropriate for seizure outcome prediction.

2. Testing of ML methods for surgery efficacy prediction.

Methods

Description of patients

In the present study, we focused on the surgery outcome prediction in the subgroup of non-lesional TLE patients, all patients were referred to the extended MRI protocol. In the next step, the patients underwent comprehensive clinical evaluation with the employment of scalp EEG monitoring, positron emission tomography (PET), interictal/ictal single photon emission tomography (SPECT) and their co-registration (SISCOM), neuropsychology and invasive EEG monitoring. Subsequently, the tailored resection was performed based on the results of all methods.

We divided patients into two groups: (1) seizure-free patients (ILAE 1 categorized one year after surgery), (2) patients with persisting seizures (ILAE 3-5 categorized one year after surgery).

We were able to identify 19 non-lesional TLE patients. The mean age at the MRI scanning was 37 ± 10 years, the mean duration of epilepsy was 24 ± 12 years. There were 9 females, and 10 males. The resection was done on the right side in 13 patients, on the left in the remaining 6 patients. Based on the ILAE classification, 12 patients were categorized ILAE 1, and 7 patients had persisting seizures.

The extended MRI protocol

We identified 25 IMs based on MRI, they are explained in more detail including the post-processing steps in our previous publication^1. All methods except ASLai are already the result of comparison with a healthy group and therefore represent a difference from healthy normal.

Metrics

The relation between each IM and surgical mask was characterized by three metrics, see Figure 1. 

Intensity: mean of voxel values within the surgical mask. It represents the deviation from the healthy group normal regardless of areas outside of resection.

Ratio: the ratio of the mean value of the voxels within the surgical mask to the sum of the values within the whole brain. It indicates the significance of the local deviation at the resection area in relation to the entire brain.

Extension: the ratio of the mean values of voxels within the surgical mask and outside the mask (8 mm vicinity limited to gray matter). It evaluates the difference of the resected tissue from its surroundings.

Machine learning

We obtained 75 features (25 IMs * 3 metrics = 75 features) for 19 patients. Among them, however, many methods are intercorrelated and not all of them are beneficial for outcome prediction. Therefore, we decided to select 10 methods with the best performance for detailed analysis. To select the most suitable features, a modified select k-best method based on parameters evaluation using ANOVA F-value with the limitation of highly correlated parameters was used. 

The preselected dataset was evaluated by the Leave-One-Out approach for training and validating various ML models. We tested three ML models: Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)  - Neural Network with one hidden layer with 20 neurons, Gaussian Naive Bayes (GNB), and Support Vector Machine (SVM). Each model was tested for 2 to 10 most discriminative features. The accuracy was evaluated for each feature count and each model. The overall model performance was assessed by a mean accuracy over all feature selections.

Results

Selected methods

We post-processed 25 IMs for each of our 19 non-lesional TLE patients, and every IM was characterized by three metrics (Intensity, Ratio, and Extension). The total of 75 features was reduced to 10 or fewer based on Select k-best and their F-value.

Figure 2 demonstrates the 10 most discriminative features which were subsequently used for the construction of ML models: 1. White matter concentration (ratio), 2. Sulcal depth (extension), 3. Junction (extension), 4. White matter concentration (extension), 5. Regional homogeneity (extension), 6. Cortical complexity (extension), 7. Amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation 2 (extension), 8. Mean diffusivity (intensity), 9. Normalized FLAIR (ratio), 10. Amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation 3 (extension).

Their selection highlights the importance of the Extension metric, because seven (70%) out of the 10 most discriminative features were derived by this metric.

Model accuracy

The highest average accuracy was obtained in the MLP and GNB models (80 %) while the SVM attained a lower average accuracy (70 %).

In the MLP model, we reached the highest accuracy of 89 % (MLP model for 9 most discriminative features).  The second highest accuracy was 84 %, which was achieved in both the GNB model (for 3, 6, 8 and 10 most discriminative features), and in the MLP model (for 4 and 8 most discriminative features).

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that machine learning models integrating multimodal MRI-derived features can predict surgical outcome in non-lesional temporal lobe epilepsy with clinically relevant accuracy. By combining structural, diffusion, functional, and perfusion imaging with spatially informed metrics derived from the resection mask, we achieved a stable mean accuracy of 80 % in MLP and Gaussian Naive Bayes models, with a maximum accuracy of 89 % under optimal feature selection. These results place the proposed approach within the upper range of accuracies reported in the literature, despite the specific challenge posed by non-lesional cases.
