Introduction

Despite convincing evidence from animal models, the role of the contralesional corticospinal tract (CST) in human stroke recovery remains controversial. Several studies have reported increases in fractional anisotropy (FA) of the contralesional CST following stroke, consistent with enhanced microstructural integrity, increased myelination, and improved fiber coherence.1 In contrast, other studies have failed to demonstrate a clear contribution of the contralesional CST to motor recovery, leaving its functional relevance unresolved. In parallel, somatosensory remapping has been shown to play a critical role in post-stroke recovery,2 yet the mechanisms by which structural reorganization within cortical motor and somatosensory networks supports behavioural improvement are not well understood. It remains unclear how cortical network reorganization interacts with changes in descending motor pathways. To clarify the relevance of the contralesional CST in stroke recovery, we examined whether structural remodeling of the contralesional CST was associated with functional recovery in a unique cohort of 16 patients with ischemic stroke restricted to the primary motor cortex (M1) and contralateral hand paresis.3 We further investigated the relationship between cortical somatosensory network reorganization and functional recovery, while explicitly accounting for the potential modulatory role of contralesional CST remodeling.

Methods

We prospectively recruited 16 right-handed patients with ischemic stroke (6 women, mean age: 72.9 years) from 1656 patients admitted to the Geneva Stroke Center during the study period. All patients had lesions restricted to M1 and presented with contralateral hand paresis. Imaging and behavioural data were collected at three recovery stages: acute (< 10 days), early sub-acute (3 weeks), and late sub-acute (3 months). Diffusion spectrum imaging (DSI) was used to quantify structural integrity of the CST, and the nine-hole peg test was used to assess paretic hand dexterity longitudinally. Sixteen control participants were also included at a single time point and were matched for age, gender, and cardiovascular risk factors. FA of the ipsi- and contralesional CST was used to characterize associations between tract-specific structural changes and functional recovery. In addition, structural connectivity between motor-related regions defined by the Brainnetome atlas was quantified as changes in FA across recovery. Mediation and moderation analyses were performed to determine whether contralesional CST remodeling mediated recovery directly or moderated the relationship between somatosensory network reorganization and recovery of dexterity.

Results

Hand dexterity was significantly impaired at < 10 days and improved by 3 weeks (estimated mean nine-hole peg test time: 33.0 s vs 27.1 s, p = 0.01), with performance remaining stable thereafter. Improvement in dexterity was correlated with contralesional CST remodeling (r = -0.533, p = 0.033) but not ipsilesional CST remodeling (r = -0.196, p = 0.467). Moderation analyses revealed that contralesional CST remodeling influenced the behavioural relevance of three interhemispheric structural connections. As contralesional CST remodeling increased, stronger connectivity between ipsilesional primary motor cortex and contralesional supplementary motor area (ß = 2.074, p < 0.001), as well as between ipsilesional supplementary motor area and contralesional premotor cortex (ß = 0.893, p = 0.02), was associated with greater clinical benefit. In contrast, stronger connectivity between ipsilesional and contralesional supplementary motor areas became increasingly detrimental to recovery (ß = -2.378, p < 0.001).

Conclusion

Motor recovery following primary motor cortex stroke was associated with increased remodeling of the contralesional CST. Importantly, the contralesional CST moderated, but did not mediate, the relationship between somatomotor network reorganization and behavioural recovery. These findings suggest that structural reorganization within the somatomotor network contributes directly to recovery, while the integrity of the contralesional CST amplifies the functional relevance of specific interhemispheric connections rather than serving as an independent driver of recovery.
