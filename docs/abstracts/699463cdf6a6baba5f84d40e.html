Introduction

Episodic memory deficits are a frequent cognitive comorbidity in temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE), a common form of pharmaco-resistant epilepsy. Functional imaging indicate that these deficits arise from disrupted coordination across medial temporal and transmodal networks during memory processes1. However, conventional functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) methods capture static connectivity rather than the dynamic propagation of neural signals underlying information integration. Here, we apply an optical flow framework that models local BOLD intensity changes, to estimate the instantaneous direction and speed of cortical signal propagation2. We examine how TLE-related lesions disrupt propagation and how these alterations relate to memory performance.

Methods

We studied 14 TLE patients (left/right=8/6, age=31土7 years; F/M: 10/4), and 25 healthy controls (HC; age=30土5 years, F/M: 12/13). Participants underwent fMRI at 7T while performing episodic memory encoding and retrieval tasks3. For each participant, we implemented a spherical optical flow framework to derive a three-dimensional propagation vector (v = [vₓ, vᵧ, vz]) for every face on the cortical mesh across successive pairs of timepoints. These vectors describe how neural activity propagates through space over time, analogous to motion estimation in computer vision. From these flow fields, we computed three complementary metrics: (i) average flow magnitude, indexing propagation strength independent of orientation, (ii) net flow magnitude, quantifying directional coherence of propagation and, (iii) surface divergence, computed using a Laplacian-based surface operator4 (LaPy), which characterizes regional flow convergence or expansion. Positive divergence reflects outward flow (source), whereas negative divergence reflects inward flow (sinks; Figure 1A). Group differences in average and net magnitude were quantified using surface-based general linear models controlling for age and sex, with correction for multiple comparisons. Divergence maps were examined separately for sources and sinks, followed by case–control surface-based contrasts and associations with memory performance. 

Results

In healthy controls, both average flow magnitude and net magnitude were strongest in prefrontal, parietal, and antero-temporo-limbic cortices, overlapping with default mode (DMN), frontoparietal (FPN), and limbic systems (LIM), indicating that transmodal association networks support the greatest propagation strength and directional stability (Figure 1B). Divergence patterns showed that DMN and FPN operated as major sinks, consistent with their integrative roles, whereas sensory and attentional networks acted as sources, forwarding information toward higher-order hubs (Figure 2A). In TLE patients, the overall topography was preserved, but propagation magnitudes were consistently higher across all networks, aligning with models of network hyperexcitability and altered integration5. Divergence analyses revealed DAN emerging as an additional sink, suggesting abnormal convergence of attentional control signals and impaired top-down routing. Network-wise, both source and sink strength were elevated globally, reflecting a more unstable, hyper-divergent propagation landscape in TLE.

Group differences in divergence were confined to the ipsilateral hemisphere. Regions where controls showed stronger divergence (i.e., medio-latero frontal, temporo-insular and visual areas) reflected preserved source–sink polarization and more organized propagation, whereas TLE showed reduced divergence in these same areas, indicating a shift toward more neutral, less differentiated flow. Conversely, regions with stronger divergence in TLE (i.e., fronto-temporal, posterior parietal and angular gyrus), reflected locally exaggerated outward/inward propagation, consistent with hyper-propagation around the seizure focus (Figure 2B). Reductions in divergence (HC>TLE) were correlated with memory performance at trend level, suggesting that preserved directional polarization, rather than abnormal hyper-divergence, supports successful memory retrieval.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that episodic memory impairment in TLE is associated with elevated propagation strength, disrupted source–sink organization, and a shift toward hyper-divergent, ipsilateral flow patterns. These results suggest that abnormal signal routing and reduced directional polarization may underlie memory dysfunction in TLE.
