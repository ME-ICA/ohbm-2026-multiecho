Introduction

In acute anorexia nervosa (AN), previous research has consistently documented cortical thickness deficits and subcortical volume reductions. Additionally, positive associations between leptin levels and specific amygdala and thalamus volumes have been reported in adolescents and young women with AN.1-2 In contrast, evidence for white matter alterations in AN is more heterogeneous, and their relationship to the neuroendocrine alterations – such as the markedly low leptin levels characteristic of acute AN – remains unexplored. Importantly, no longitudinal studies have examined the association between changes in leptin levels and weight gain with fractional anisotropy (FA) in a large sample of young females with AN assessed during acute undernutrition and following partial short-term weight restoration, compared to female healthy controls (HC). This study aims to characterize microstructural alterations across white matter regions and investigate potential underlying mechanisms and clinical implications by examining their associations with plasma leptin, a central marker of undernutrition.

Methods

We recruited 101 acutely underweight patients with AN and 147 HC. AN participants were assessed during acute undernutrition (AN-TP1) within 96 hours of starting nutritional rehabilitation, and 74 of all patients were reassessed after partial short-term weight restoration (AN-TP2; BMI mean increase of 27.95%). Participants underwent diffusion-weighted MRI using a spin-echo sequence (60 axial slices; 2.4 mm3 voxels; TR/TE = 1500/104 ms; 4 volumes with b = 0 s/mm2; 32 volumes with b = 1300 s/mm2). Preprocessing was conducted with QSIPrep (v0.19.0).3 Tissue-specific FA maps were obtained using the Freewater estimatoR using iNtErpolated initialization (FERNET) method4, which separated tissue diffusion from free water to estimate voxel-wise tissue fraction. Diffusion tensors were then fitted exclusively to the tissue compartment, generating free water corrected FA maps. These FA maps were analyzed using tract-based spatial statistics (TBSS).5 Images were aligned to the MNI ENIGMA-provided template space, and individual FA values were projected onto a standardized white matter skeleton (threshold: FA > 0.2). For region of interest (ROI)-based analyses, individual mean FA values were extracted from 48 white matter regions defined by the John Hopkins University atlas. Short-term weight restoration-related changes were tested using linear mixed effect models (LMEs), modeling changes in mean FA as a linear function of ΔBMI-SDS (including age as covariate). Associations with plasma leptin were then examined using additional LMEs. Significant leptin associations were followed up with regression-based mediation analyses.

Results

Our analyses revealed significant white matter alterations in the majority of white matter ROIs (FDR-q < 0.05), with AN patients showing increased FA in nearly all ROIs compared to HC. From TP1 to TP2, FA decreased in almost all examined ROIs, whereas significant longitudinal increases were observed only in the fornix and the left stria terminalis. At TP2, group differences between AN and HC were restricted to four ROIs. We also identified significant longitudinal associations between changes in leptin and FA: increases in leptin levels had an effect above and beyond increases in BMI on FA changes in the right external capsule, right inferior cerebellar peduncle, right posterior limb of internal capsule, and right superior longitudinal fasciculus (Figure 1A). Mediation analyses further demonstrated that increases in leptin levels mediated the relationship between weight gain and FA decreases following short-term weight restoration across all four ROIs, as indicated by significant indirect effects (Figure 1B).

Conclusion

This study identifies key microstructural alterations in white matter tracts in individuals with AN, characterized by predominantly increased FA at TP1. Overall, our findings confirm a mixed FA signature in acutely underweight patients with AN. These alterations might be related to axonal damage processes, followed by broad but incomplete normalization with weight gain. Mediation analyses identified increases in leptin levels as a driver of FA decreases following short-term weight restoration in four right-hemisphere tracts, pointing to a potential role of leptin in early microstructural recovery during nutritional rehabilitation. These findings advance our understanding of the neurobiological basis of AN and highlight weight restoration as a central therapeutic target for supporting white matter integrity. The findings also suggest a potential modulatory role of leptin in specific white matter tracts, highlighting a potential neurobiological target for leptin-based therapeutic approaches.
