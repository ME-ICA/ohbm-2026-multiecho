Introduction

Elevated cortical amyloid-β (Aβ) is a criterion for the diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease (AD). Aβ facilitates tau deposition and accumulation, which further leads to brain atrophy and cognitive decline. The “clearance-production” balance is likely to play a critical role in deposition of both protein aggregates. Glymphatic function can clear tau and Aβ, and is driven by cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) movements (1). There are some MRI-based imaging measures of CSF flow or relevant glymphatic clearance in humans (1). However, these methods were not designed to detect the metabolic waste changes or clearance in the brain but rather CSF movements or local structural changes, such as perivascular spaces. A recent study used a dynamic Aβ-PET signal, continuously scanned from the tracer injection to the session end, to calculate the slope of lateral ventricle signal changes, reflecting clearance efficiency. Here, this CSF signal slope will be associated with cortical tau and Aβ deposition to investigate the tau clearance by glymphatic CSF flow.   

Methods

This study included 148 cognitively normal adults (age: 83.3 ± 6.9; 86 females; 73 Aβ+) from the Berkeley Aging Cohort Study (BACS) with dynamic PiB-PET, [18]F-flortaucipir (FTP)-PET (within one year), cognitive scores, APOE4 status, and MRI-based regional volumes in the hippocampus, entorhinal, and meta-temporal ROIs. FTP-PET (75–105 min) and dynamic PiB-PET (0–90 min) were acquired at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL). PiB and FTP data were reconstructed into 35 frames and 5-min frames, respectively. All PET images underwent attenuation/scatter correction, 4-mm smoothing, and MRI-based co-registration. Regional measures were extracted using the Desikan–Killiany atlas, with partial-volume correction for FTP. PiB DVR was computed using Logan analysis with cerebellar gray as reference, and Aβ-positivity defined by a DVR >1.065. FTP SUVR was intensity-normalized to inferior cerebellar gray for entorhinal and meta-temporal ROIs.  Dynamic CSF time-activity curves were derived from eroded lateral ventricle masks (4mm elliptical erosion; OpenCV “Erosion” package) generated from individual MRI. SUV values at around 10th and 30th mins were used to compute the CSF-Slope were used to compute the CSF-Slope, which was further normalized by early-phase whole-brain AUC (2). We also extracted representative PiB frames at 4, 10, and 30 min to illustrate dynamic changes (Fig. 1, A-B).  AUC-normalized CSF-Slope (adjusted for age, sex, ventricular volume) was correlated with cortical Aβ burden, regional tau (entorhinal and meta-temporal), cognitive factor scores, and ICV-normalized regional volumes linked to AD degeneration. Pearson correlations evaluated associations and Aβ-status interactions. Mediation analysis examined whether Aβ mediated CSF-Slope–tau associations. 

Results

A larger CSF-Slope (absolute) value was used to reflect more efficient CSF clearance. Cross-sectional analyses suggested that the CSF-Slope decreased with age and lateral ventricle volume. Consistent with the previous studies (2, 3), the CSF-Slope (adjusted for age, sex, and lateral ventricular volume) was negatively correlated with cortical Aβ deposition, i.e., PiB-PET DVR (N=148; Fig.1, C-E). We correlated the adjusted CSF-Slope measures with cortical tau deposition (partial volume corrected; PVC-ed) and found that subjects with larger CSF-Slope, presumably indicating less efficient clearance, appeared to have higher tau load in the meta-temporal and entorhinal regions (N=148; both r < - 0.22, both p < 0.007; Fig.2, A-B). A mediation analysis suggested that such significant Slope-tau associations were mediated by cortical Aβ deposition (both p < 0.005; Fig.2, C-D). As the critical markers in AD pathology (4, 5), hippocampal and meta-temporal volume decrease were associated with the CSF-Slope decrease, especially in subjects with elevated cortical Aβ (significant interaction in the meta-temporal regions, p = 0.032).

Conclusion

This study used the dynamic PiB-PET slope in the lateral ventricles (CSF-slope) as a proxy of CSF-driven waste clearance in cognitively normal older adults. Lower CSF-slope values were associated with more cortical Aβ deposition and higher tau level in the meta-temporal and entorhinal regions. This relationship was mediated by cortical Aβ burden. This diminished CSF-related clearance may lead to the regional neurodegeneration, especially in the hippocampal and meta-temporal area. Our study validates the CSF-Slope method imaged by dynamic PET as an evaluation of glymphatic clearance, and suggests the role of this CSF flow-related clearance in AD tau pathology in healthy older individuals.
