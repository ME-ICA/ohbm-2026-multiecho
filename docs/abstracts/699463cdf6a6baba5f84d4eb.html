Introduction

Hemispherotomy pediatric patients present a unique case of neuroplasticity, characterized by an extremely atypical anatomical architecture confined to a single hemisphere. Prior functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) studies have demonstrated that the overall population-average architecture of resting-state functional networks is remarkably preserved in the remaining hemisphere.1 However, the presence of substantial inter-subject variability, which is often magnified by the extensive functional reorganization following surgery, severely limits the utility of these group-level templates for precise individual patient assessment, surgical planning, or specialized research applications. Accurate functional mapping of these uniquely reorganized brains necessitates a critical shift, moving beyond the spatial constraints of population templates toward capturing individual-specific network organization.

Recent methodological advancements have robustly demonstrated that using task-based fMRI data alone can generate highly accurate individualized network estimations.2 Furthermore, contemporary literature strongly advocates for merging both task-based and resting-state functional data. This synergistic combination has been shown to significantly enhance the stability and robustness of the derived individualized functional connectivity (FC) matrices and network parcellations. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the feasibility and efficacy of constructing robust individualized functional networks within the remaining hemisphere of pediatric hemispherotomy patients by capitalizing on the superior stability offered by combined task-based and resting-state fMRI data.

Methods

The study cohort included 14 pediatric hemispherotomy patients (7 right hemisphere preserved, age: 10.1±2.8 years; 7 left hemisphere preserved, age: 9.3±1.9 years) and 37 demographically matched healthy controls (age: 10.2±2.2 years). All participants underwent identical fMRI sequences, including a resting-state run and two task-based runs (word generation and landmark tasks).3 Functional Connectivity (FC) matrices were generated by calculating Fisher's z-transformed Pearson correlations between regional time series, after residualizing task effects via a General Linear Model (GLM). We performed FC matrix similarity analyses to evaluate Modality Consistency (task vs. resting-state FC) and confirm stable individual-specific signatures across functional states.

Individual-Specific Parcellations: The 15-network Multi-session Hierarchical Bayesian Model was applied to generate individual-specific parcellation maps for both patient and control cohorts.4 Quantitative evaluation of the derived parcellations included Resting-State Functional Homogeneity and Task Functional Inhomogeneity metrics. The validity and necessity of the individualized approach were directly assessed by quantitatively comparing the individualized parcellations against conventional population-average templates across all cohorts.

Results

Functional connectivity analysis confirmed high Cross-Modality Functional Connectivity Consistency across all cohorts, with mean inter-modality similarity coefficients ranging from 0.42 to 0.56. Crucially, intra-individual correlation coefficients (0.17–0.39) were substantially lower than inter-individual coefficients (0.46–0.54) across all groups, providing strong evidence for the existence of stable, individual-specific FC signatures across different functional states (task vs. rest). This established the feasibility of constructing robust, individualized functional networks in the remaining hemisphere of patients.

We demonstrated the validity and clinical necessity of the Individualized Functional Brain Network Parcellation (15-network solution). The parcellations derived from the preserved hemisphere in patients exhibited high visual fidelity and topological consistency with those derived from healthy controls, supporting structural feasibility (Fig. 1A). More importantly, quantitative comparison showed that Individualized Parcellations significantly outperformed the conventional population-average templates across all three cohorts. This superiority was evidenced by marked increases in both Resting-State Functional Homogeneity (Fig. 1B) and Task Functional Inhomogeneity metrics (Fig. 1C), confirming enhanced intra-regional consistency and inter-regional functional distinction. This robust increase in quantitative evaluation metrics unequivocally underscores the necessity of adopting individualized mapping in the highly plastic hemispherotomy population.

Conclusion

By successfully integrating task-based and resting-state fMRI data, we constructed robust and highly individualized functional networks within the single remaining hemisphere of pediatric hemispherotomy patients. The resulting individualized functional parcellations consistently demonstrated superior quantitative performance over conventional population-average templates, confirming enhanced intra-regional functional consistency and greater inter-regional distinctiveness. These findings collectively establish both the necessity and feasibility of adopting an individualized functional mapping approach. This strategy is critical for accurately capturing the unique and extensive neuroplastic reorganization that defines the functional architecture in this specialized pediatric population, offering a vital tool for personalized clinical and research applications.
