Introduction

3D GRASE tends to reduce draining vein effect compared to EPI based GE-BOLD, improving the functional specificity in mesoscale fMRI, but suffers from limited spatial coverage because it requires excessive EPI trains (geometric distortion) along the phase encoding direction and long refocusing pulse trains (blurring) along the partition encoding direction. Thus, the accelerated acquisition and reconstruction techniques are essential to capture the functional dynamics.1 To improve the functional sensitivity and specificity, temporally-variant sampling with random walk was recently introduced with dynamic compressed sensing (CS) to utilize the temporal redundancy2,3. As an alternative, deep learning has gained interest for high quality reconstruction even with limited training datasets4. 

In this work, we introduce a novel accelerated 3D zoomed GRASE by leveraging random walk acquisition strategy coupling with self-supervised deep learning for mesoscale fMRI (DeepGRASE). To this end, we employ a variable density 2D CAIPI sampling (VD-CAIPI) in the spatial domain combined with time-wise extra random encoding in the time domain, thus achieving pseudo-regular sampling with a regular blip while allowing incoherent sampling in a complementary manner across time. To exploit the random encoding across time, we incorporate the temporal averaging functional data into the deep learning framework for pseudo-supervised learning followed by enforcing consistency between model reconstructions of frame-wise undersampled data and their temporal averaging counterparts for self-supervised learning. We demonstrate that our DeepGRASE increases robustness for reconstructing images and capturing functional sensitivity at multiple experiments.

Methods

Sequence design (Fig. 1A)

Mesoscale fMRI data were acquired using a single-shot variable-flip-angle 3D GRASE sequence with accelerated undersampled acquisition. Two protocols with different zoomed FOV/spatial-resolution settings (0.8mm-iso and 0.56mm-iso) were used to evaluate time-series reconstruction performance under high-resolution, limited-coverage conditions. The detailed acquisition parameters (resolution, FOV, TE/TR, acceleration factors, etc.) are summarized in the table in Fig. 2.

Time random-walk CAIPI sampling (Fig. 1B)

To enable accelerated acquisition, temporal random-walk sampling was applied by varying the CAIPI-based ky–kz undersampling pattern across frames. Specifically, each time frame samples only a subset of ky–kz locations, while the sampling pattern is shifted/modified over time such that complementary k-space samples accumulate across frames. This temporal sampling diversity is subsequently leveraged to form a time-averaged k-space and to support frame-wise self-supervised learning.

Reconstruction and training scheme (Fig. 1. C)
 All multi-coil k-space data were converted to hybrid space via a 1D inverse FFT along kx, and reconstruction was performed as a 2D problem in the remaining phase-encoding dimensions (ky–kz). The proposed unrolled network combines SSDU4 and Noise2Recon5 by leveraging temporal random-walk CAIPI to form a time-averaged “fully sampled” k-space, from which coil sensitivity maps are estimated and used for coil combination/encoding-decoding across all inputs. Training is driven by three losses: (1) a frame-wise SSDU self-supervised loss, where each frame’s acquired samples are split into Θ (input/data consistency) and Λ (hold-out), and the reconstruction is penalized by the k-space mismatch on Λ; (2) a supervised loss on the time-averaged branch, where the time-averaged k-space is subsampled with the same Θ mask used for the frame and reconstructed, then compared against the high-SNR time-averaged target; and (3) a Noise2Recon-style consistency loss, where an additional input is created by injecting Θ-masked k-space noise into the Θ-subsampled time-averaged data, and the reconstruction from this noisy input is enforced to be consistent with the supervised-branch reconstruction obtained in (2) (i.e., the reconstruction from the clean Θ-subsampled time-averaged input), thereby explicitly regularizing the model toward noise-invariant outputs anchored by the high-SNR time-averaged target.

Results

Fig. 2. summarizes the image-domain reconstructions and activation maps obtained with CS, SSDU, and the proposed method for the visual task at 0.8 mm-isotropic and 0.56 mm-isotropic. At 0.8 mm-isotropic, all three methods produced visually comparable reconstructions with well-preserved contrast and minimal artifacts. The corresponding activation maps were also similar across methods, showing consistent spatial patterns for the visual paradigm.

At 0.56 mm-isotropic, differences across reconstruction methods became evident. CS reconstructions showed substantial residual noise distributed across the image, and the activation maps exhibited stronger background fluctuations and less distinct task-related patterns. SSDU reduced the overall noise compared to CS, but the reconstructions appeared overly smoothed, with attenuation of structural details; the activation maps were correspondingly less sharply defined. In contrast, the proposed method effectively suppressed noise while preserving anatomical structure, yielding cleaner reconstructions without visually removing fine features. Consistent with these image-domain trends, the activation maps at 0.56 mm-isotropic improved monotonically from CS → SSDU → Ours, with the proposed method showing the clearest retinotopically organized target-versus-surround activation pattern and reduced spurious/background activation.

Conclusion

This study compared CS, SSDU, and the proposed method for accelerated 3D GRASE mesoscale fMRI, focusing on how reconstruction and activation results change with resolution/zoom conditions. At 0.8 mm-isotropic, where SNR is relatively high, all methods produced comparable image-domain reconstructions and similar activation maps with consistent spatial patterns. In contrast, at 0.56 mm-isotropic, the more zoomed, limited-FOV setting led to lower SNR and revealed clear limitations of conventional approaches. CS exhibited substantial residual noise across the image, reducing functional contrast, while SSDU suppressed noise but introduced pronounced oversmoothing that attenuated structural detail and reduced the sharpness and anatomical plausibility of the resulting activation patterns.

The proposed method is specifically designed for such low-SNR, zoomed-FOV regimes. By leveraging temporal random-walk CAIPI sampling, it forms a time-averaged “fully sampled” k-space and derives coil sensitivities and a high-SNR anchor target, which stabilizes frame-wise SSDU training when reliable fully sampled ground truth is unavailable. In addition, a Noise2Recon-style consistency constraint regularizes the network to produce invariant reconstructions under k-space noise perturbations, mitigating both noise amplification and excessive smoothing in low-SNR conditions. As a result, the proposed method preserves structural fidelity while effectively suppressing noise at 0.56 mm-isotropic, yielding more coherent and reliable activation maps. Overall, this work presents a practical reconstruction framework for fMRI scenarios where fully sampled references are infeasible and where mesoscale/zoomed acquisitions are particularly vulnerable to SNR limitations, thereby improving time-series fidelity and the reliability of activation estimates.
