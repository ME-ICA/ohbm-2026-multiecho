Introduction

Ultra-low-field (ULF) MRI offers accessible and low-cost neuroimaging, providing opportunities for infant studies in low-resource settings. Several image processing tools are available through open-science research; however, they have typically been developed on higher field systems and often with adults. Early adoption of ULF as part of the Ultra-Low Field Neuroimaging in the Young (UNITY) Network has demonstrated its potential added value where high field systems are not feasible1. Paediatric ULF imaging faces unique challenges including lower SNR, reduced resolution, and motion-related artifacts, which particularly compromise automated tissue and subcortical segmentation in early infancy. To best position applied research looking at factors affecting brain development, it is vital to determine reliable workflows for image processing and assess the accuracy of derived brain measurements relative to high-field acquisitions. We compared multiple automated structural MRI pipelines to identify those that most closely reproduce high-field results across key developmental stages in the first 3 years of life.

Methods

Infants were scanned between the ages of 3 – 36 months as part of a longitudinal study (nsubjects = 187,  nsessions = 628). Following expert visual quality control, the final sample with paired high (3T Siemens Skyra) 1x1x1mm isotropic and ultra-low-field (Hyperfine Swoop®) 1.5x1.5x5mm non-isotropic MRI were (nsubjects = 110,  nsessions = 140). Before segmentation, ULF data were pre-processed with a paediatric Image Quality Transfer deep learning model, GAMBAS (Generalised-Hilbert Mamba Super-resolution), to enhance scan resolution and signal-to-noise ratio 5. The resulting images from the GAMBAS super-resolution step were 1x1x1mm isotropic. T2-weighted images were processed with four pipelines: SuperSynth, Minimorph, recon-all-clinical, and bibsnet2–4 built in Docker containers for reproducibility and to scale to other studies within the network. They have been made available on GitHub for transparency and version control (https://github.com/UNITY-Physics). Agreement between ULF-derived and 3T measurements was quantified using Lin’s concordance correlation coefficients (CCC) and relative volumetric percentage difference, with particular emphasis on performance in the youngest infants (3 months), where image quality was the lowest (Fig 1). Analyses were stratified into three age groups: <4 months (n = 28), 4–9 months (n = 39), >9 months(n = 73).

Results

SuperSynth showed the highest overall agreement with high-field data across global tissue volumes and subcortical regions, maintaining robust reliability even in infants <4 months (e.g., CSF CCC=0.89; Gray Matter CCC=0.93). Minimorph demonstrated strong performance for total brain volume (CCC=0.94 at <4 months), with age-dependent variation in subcortical areas. Recon-all displayed marked improvement with age, particularly in the putamen, where concordance rose drastically from 0.12 in the youngest group to 0.85 in 4–9 month olds. Bibsnet performed consistently but with moderate accuracy (e.g., Thalamus CCC=0.37–0.51) and notable systematic bias, specifically an underestimation of CSF volume (>10%). Accuracy improved systematically with age across all methods; the >9-month group showed the closest alignment to high-field benchmarks, while the <4-month group exhibited the largest discrepancies. Global tissues (Total Brain Volume, Gray/White Matter) showed strong cross-scanner agreement (CCC >0.87 across most pipelines), while CSF and subcortical regions had the most variation between scanners, particularly at the youngest timepoints (Fig 2).

Conclusion

Among evaluated pipelines, super-resolution based approaches provide the most robust infant morphometry under challenging low-field conditions. This supports their use in longitudinal neurodevelopmental studies starting in early infancy. Global tissue volumes can be quantified with high confidence at all ages from 3 months upwards, while subcortical measures require caution, particularly in the youngest infants. Despite the general success of super-resolution techniques, they may still struggle to segment subcortical structures such as the thalamus, conflating it with nearby white matter in this age group due to inverting contrasts during a period of intense myelination. It should be noted that the MiniMorph pipeline is a template approach developed specifically for ULF and may perform better at lower field strengths than for conventional MRI. The findings highlight that reliable ULF morphometry in very young infants is feasible but contingent on careful pipeline choice, targeted outcome measures, and age-sensitive interpretation, offering a practical roadmap for deploying ULF neuroimaging in both high- and low-resource settings.
