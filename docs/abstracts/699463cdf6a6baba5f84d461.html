Introduction

The putamen, a part of the dorsal striatum, emerges around the 8th week of gestation. It is involved in the integration of information from the cortex to facilitate multiple sensory, motor, and cognitive functions. Prior ex vivo and in vivo studies in human adults have revealed cellular and microstructural gradients, with increasing cellular density and microstructural tissue properties from the anterior to posterior end of the striatum1,2. Additionally, there are distinct connectivity gradients along the putamen’s anterior-posterior axis, as the anterior striatum has more connections to frontal areas and the posterior striatum has more connections to sensorimotor areas3,4. However, it is unknown if these microstructural gradients and connectivity profiles exist in infancy or develop across the lifespan. 

Methods

To address this gap, we obtained quantitative MRI (longitudinal relaxation rate (R1 [s-1]) and diffusion MRI in 79 full-term, healthy infants (ages: 9-479 days) and 20 adults (ages: 19-42 years). 

Results

In adults, we replicate prior findings: we find microstructural gradients of increasing R1 along the putamen’s anterior-posterior axis. However, in newborns’ putamen, R1 is low, and the gradient is less prominent at birth. Nonetheless, the anterior-posterior R1 gradient develops from infancy to adulthood, with a faster rate of development in the posterior than in the anterior putamen. Analysis of the putamen’s white matter connectivity using diffusion MRI and endpoint density analysis across the brain revealed a topographic arrangement of cortico-striatal white matter connections in adults: anterior segments of the putamen have more connections to frontal regions, including dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate, but posterior segments have more connections to sensorimotor and premotor regions. This cortico-striatal connectivity pattern is present in infants but is more diffuse, as both anterior and posterior portions of the putamen are connected to frontal areas, and there are overall less connections from the posterior putamen to sensorimotor regions compared to adults. Development of endpoint connectivity profiles can be characterized by both increasing and decreasing endpoint connectivity. Specifically, we find increases in endpoint density (i) between the anterior putamen and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate cortex, (ii) between the posterior putamen and somatosensory motor cortex, and (iii) between the entire putamen and the early visual cortex. We also find a reduction in endpoint connectivity between the anterior putamen and sensorimotor cortex, and between the posterior putamen and frontal cortex. 

Conclusion

Our findings reveal that the large-scale cortico-striatal connectivity profile of the putamen, but not its microstructural gradient, is present at birth. Additionally, microstructural gradients and fine-grained connectivity patterns of the putamen develop from infancy to adulthood. Overall, our findings have implications for in vivo diagnosis of neurodevelopmental disorders as well as for understanding how cognitive and motor functions develop during infancy.
