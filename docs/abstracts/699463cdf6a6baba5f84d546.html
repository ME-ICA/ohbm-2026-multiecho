Introduction

Personalized targeting in transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) has emerged as a leading innovation in the treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD), aiming to move beyond anatomy-based stimulation toward circuit-informed neuromodulation. Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy (SNT) represents a landmark in this direction and has received FDA approval following reports of robust clinical efficacy1. However, critical components of the SNT protocol—including target definition algorithms, preprocessing pipelines, and stimulation parameter optimization—remain ambiguous and not publicly accessible, which limit transparency, reproducibility, and independent methodological advancement. Moreover, opportunities remain to further optimize target identification, coil orientation, and stimulation dose using contemporary neuroimaging and computational modeling approaches. To address these gaps, we introduce fMRI-informed TMS for Brain Regulation using Algorithm-based Individualized Neuromodulation (FIT BRAIN) for Depression, a fully transparent, publicly available, end-to-end pipeline designed to support individualized, circuit-level TMS targeting using advanced neuroimaging and artificial intelligence–driven methods.

Methods

FIT BRAIN is an fMRI-informed neuromodulation framework that integrates advanced preprocessing, network-based target identification, and electric-field–guided stimulation optimization. Resting-state and task-based fMRI data are processed using state-of-the-art denoising and normalization pipelines optimized for individualized connectivity analyses. Regions of interest relevant to depression are defined using a combination of atlas-based constraints and data-driven functional connectivity patterns. Target selection is performed using algorithmic criteria that prioritize individualized connectivity profiles within fronto-limbic circuits, with full transparency regarding feature selection and decision rules. Coil orientation and stimulation dose are optimized using subject-specific electric-field modeling to maximize engagement of the intended cortical target while minimizing off-target effects. To assess feasibility, the complete FIT BRAIN pipeline was applied in a proof-of-concept study in 5 individuals with treatment-resistant MDD, followed by delivery of personalized TMS guided by the resulting target and stimulation parameters.

Results

The FIT BRAIN pipeline successfully generated an individualized TMS target with fully transparent preprocessing, target definition, and stimulation optimization steps. Compared with the publicly described elements of the SNT approach, FIT BRAIN incorporates enhanced preprocessing rigor, explicit and reproducible target selection criteria, and electric-field–informed adjustments of coil orientation and stimulation intensity. The feasibility study demonstrated that the pipeline could be implemented end-to-end in a clinical research setting without protocol deviations. Following personalized TMS guided by FIT BRAIN, the participant exhibited a substantial clinical improvement in depressive symptoms, supporting the practical applicability of the framework and its potential therapeutic relevance. While limited to 5 subjects, these results provide initial evidence of feasibility and clinical promise.

Conclusion

FIT BRAIN represents a transparent, scalable, and publicly available alternative to the FDA-approved personalized TMS protocol for depression. By integrating fMRI-informed circuit targeting, AI-driven decision rules, and electric-field–guided stimulation optimization, this framework advances methodological rigor and reproducibility in individualized neuromodulation. The presented feasibility study supports the implementability of the approach and motivates systematic clinical evaluation in larger cohorts. Beyond depression, FIT BRAIN offers a generalizable blueprint for precision neuromodulation across neuropsychiatric disorders.
