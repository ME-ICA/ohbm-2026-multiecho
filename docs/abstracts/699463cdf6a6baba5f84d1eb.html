Introduction

Our previous research combining fMRI with the stimulus-related negative wave (SPN) revealed that the right anterior insular cortex is involved in attentional control over the hemi-space. Our findings demonstrate that the right anterior insula is stimulated by both left- and right-hemi-space stimulation, while the left anterior insula is stimulated exclusively by right-hemi-space stimulation. These findings indicate that the right anterior insula is dominant over the left anterior insula in attentional control to unilateral stimuli, and this phenomenon known as the right-dominant unilateral effect (Ohgami et al., 2023). This right-dominant unilateral effect may explain why hemi-spatial neglect typically arises from right-hemispheric damage. In the present study, we recorded SPN to investigate whether the right-dominant unilateral effect is also applicable to somatosensory stimuli. The anterior insula has been identified as a generator of SPN, and it is well established that the right anterior insula receives somatosensory input via the posterior insula (Gray & Critchley, 2007). Somatosensory stimuli to the left side of the body may increase SPN amplitudes in the right hemisphere, while somatosensory stimuli to the right side may increase SPN amplitudes in both hemispheres. We delivered air-puff stimulations to the left and right sides of the body, varying the location (hand and foot), to examine whether somatosensory stimulation also elicits the right-dominant unilateral effect.

Methods

Participants were 40 healthy adults. They performed a time-estimation task in which they pressed a button four seconds after a cue stimulus. Three seconds after the button press, a unilateral somatosensory stimulus was presented as feedback (FB). There were five conditions: left hand (L-Hand), right hand (R-Hand), left foot (L-Foot), right foot (R-Foot), and no-feedback (NoFB). The somatosensory FB consisted of one, two, or three air puffs, indicating correct response, too early, or too late, respectively. In the NoFB condition, the feedback was omitted. SPN amplitude was defined as the mean amplitude recorded from 66 electrode sites according to the international 10–10 system during the 200-ms interval preceding the FB onset. The mean amplitudes were analyzed using repeated-measures ANOVA to evaluate hemispheric differences in each condition.

Results

Analysis of SPN amplitudes revealed a right-dominant unilateral effect in the frontolateral area when air puffs were delivered to either hand. Specifically, SPN amplitude in the right hemisphere was larger than in the left hemisphere in the L-Hand condition, whereas no hemispheric difference was found in the R-Hand condition. In the case of foot conditions, right-hemisphere dominance was observed in both the L-Foot and R-Foot conditions at the frontolateral area. However, in the centroparietal area, which is close to the somatosensory cortex for the foot, right-dominant unilateral effects were observed for both hand and foot stimulation.

Conclusion

The present findings suggest that the right-dominant unilateral effect extends to somatosensory stimuli. The effect induced by foot stimulation was localized primarily in the centroparietal region, adjacent to the somatosensory area of the foot. The results of this study suggest that somatosensory stimulation can modulate activity in both the right and left anterior insula. Damage to the insula may manifest symptoms such as alexithymia and left spatial neglect. The present findings suggest that somatosensory stimulation to the left or right hand or foot may be applicable to rehabilitation approaches aimed at activating the insula in patients with insular damage.
