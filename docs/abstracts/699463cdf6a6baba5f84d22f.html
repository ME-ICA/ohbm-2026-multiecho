Introduction

Introduction:

In 2013, we published BigBrain 1, a high-resolution (20 μm3) histological 3D-reconstructed model of the human brain.1  Recently, focus has been directed towards advances in the reconstruction and analysis of BigBrain 2.2

Established automated brain-imaging pipelines have been validated and optimized for typical in-vivo MRI data (e.g., FreeSurfer,3 ~ 0.5 mm3 - 1 mm3), but not for histological data at a substantially higher resolution (~ 100 μm3 - 200 μm3). Major obstacles include: sectioning artifacts, different tissue contrasts, staining imbalances, and sheer size of data, leading to suboptimal performances, memory bottlenecks, and prohibitively long processing times.

In this work, we obtain a whole-brain volumetric parcellation of BigBrain 2, together with the extraction of cortical surfaces, via an adaptation of FreeSurfer v8.1.

Motivation:

Successful cortical surface extraction is contingent upon accurate white / grey matter (WM / GM) tissue classification, as well as proper subcortical masking.  Layer 1 of GM (visible in high-resolution histology but not in typical MRI) presents an extra challenge since it is often incorrectly classified as WM.

We find that nnU-net4 distinguishes well between WM, GM, and layer 1.  The output of nnU-net can be submitted as a ‘T1w-like’ classified volume (WM / GM) to FreeSurfer's recon-all pipeline, while the histological intensity volume serves as input to mri_synthseg for initial volumetric parcellation and subcortical masking.

Methods

Preprocessing:

Tissue segmentation via nnU-net (77 20 μm2 coronal sections, 2 mm apart):

* Train nnU-Net with 3 training sets:

(1) (background) + (tissue mask)

(2) (background) + (layer 1) + (interior tissue)

(3) (background) + (grey matter) + (white matter)

* Default parameters with 250 iterations, 256 x 256 tiles; no modeling of subcortical structures.

* Combine layer 1 with WM / GM predictions to discriminate true WM from layer 1 in cell-body stained image.

* Run predictions on every 5th repaired section.

* Perform quality control (correct for staining artifacts).

* Resample + combine as a 3D volume at 100 μm3.

* nnU-Net is robust against staining imbalances and produces accurate tissue classification (Methods Figure, below).

mri_synthseg:

* mri_synthseg is a whole-brain convolutional neural network (CNN) volumetric parcellation tool distributed with FreeSurfer and now utilized as default in the recon-all pipeline.

* Although mri_synthseg accepts any input resolution and contrast, it is hard-coded to process only at 1 mm3. This is roughly sufficient to mask out subcortical structures from cortical surface extraction, and create ROIs for post- recon-all analyses (e.g., hippocampal subfields). 

(1) Submit 150 μm3 histological intensity volume to mri_synthseg.

(2) Upsample 1 mm3 output to 150 μm3.

(3) Smooth labels by 4 voxels (c3d -smooth-multilabel).

(4) Insert as aseg.presurf.mgz into recon-all directory.

FreeSurfer (FS) recon-all modifications:

* Insert aseg.presurf.mgz (as described in pre-processing).

* No talairach (insert our own volumetric transform .xfm); no normalization (unnecessary + prohibitively long processing time); no skullstrip (unnecessary).

* High-resolution surfaces: mris_remesh --desired-face-area 0.15 → ~ 500k vertices per hemisphere.

* mris_inflate -n 100 -F 0.008 (desired rms height)

* 2-stage pial expansion: mris_place_surface --n_averages 32 --subiters 500:

(1) white surface → layer 1

(2) layer 1 → CSF

Conclusion

Our approach provides an automated FreeSurfer whole-brain volumetric parcellation of BigBrain 2, which can serve as regions of interest for higher-resolution analyses, such as FreeSurfer's integrated post- recon-all segmentation tools (hippocampal subfields + amygdala, thalamic nuclei, brainstem substructures), as well as external analyses.

A potential benefit of FreeSurfer-extracted BigBrain surfaces is to improve multimodal surface matching (MSM) surface registration5  - which was developed and parameterized with FreeSurfer surfaces - to standard reference spaces in other software ecosystems, e.g., FreeSurfer's fsaverage, Human Connectome Project (HCP)'s fs_LR, CIVET's MNI ICBM152, Colin27, etc.  The high-resolution, histological BigBrain model is well-positioned to serve as an unprecedented bidirectional validation tool; data derived from in vivo imaging can be directly compared to its cytoarchitectural properties, which in turn can be transposed to the standard reference spaces of in vivo imaging for a wide range of functional applications.
