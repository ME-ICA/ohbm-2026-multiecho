Introduction

Previous studies using electrophysiological data have predominantly focused on pairwise interactions between neural oscillations, particularly through various forms of oscillatory coupling, yielding important insights into brain activity. However, simultaneous interactions among three or more variables—referred to as higher-order interactions (HOIs) and closely related to the emergent properties of complex systems—have largely been overlooked. Recent advances in multivariate information theory have introduced tools to characterize such interactions within the framework of partial information decomposition (PID), including the O-information rate (OIR) and transfer entropy decomposition (TE decomposition). In this study, these higher-order information-theoretic measures are applied to intracranial electroencephalography (iEEG) recordings from epilepsy patients to investigate neural interactions beyond pairwise frameworks. Classical time–frequency analyses, including spectral power and phase-locking measures, are further employed to complement the interpretation of the underlying mechanisms. By leveraging both pre-ictal and ictal recordings, this framework provides new insights into the reorganization of neural information dynamics in epilepsy and may facilitate the identification of novel biomarkers.

Methods

Intracranial EEG recordings from epilepsy patients were analyzed, including seven seizure trials with 10 seconds of pre-ictal and ictal data.1 Higher-order information analysis focused on directed interactions from the seizure onset zone (deep hippocampus) to cortical irritative regions, with an additional hippocampal region serving as an intermediate variable.

Higher-order interactions were quantified using multivariate information-theoretic measures within the framework of partial information decomposition, including the O-information rate (OIR) and phase-based transfer entropy decomposition. OIR was used to characterize frequency-resolved redundancy and synergy in multivariate neural activity.2 Transfer entropy was estimated using a multivariate, conditional formulation and decomposed to dissociate unique, redundant, and synergistic contributions to directed information flow.3 A Gaussian copula–based estimator of conditional mutual information was employed, enabling nonparametric estimation from phase time series across multiple frequency bands (delta, theta, alpha, and beta).

Classical time–frequency analyses were performed for comparison, including short-time Fourier transform–based power spectral analysis and phase-locking value (PLV) to quantify inter-channel phase synchronization across seizure states using the same sliding-window scheme.

Results

Time–frequency analysis revealed frequency-specific changes in spectral power around seizure onset which shows strong power increase in IZ (red horizontal line) across all oscillatory frequency bands (except delta band which shows increase in non-IZ), indicating a state-dependent modulation of neural activity. Pairwise phase synchronization, quantified by theta-band PLV, showed widespread increases during the ictal period, suggesting enhanced oscillatory coupling. However, higher-order analysis using the O-information rate uncovered a pronounced reorganization of multivariate interactions, with a shift toward synergy-dominated dynamics during seizures. Specifically, the low frequency band signals (from delta band to beta band) are dominated by synergistic information. Consistently, phase-based transfer entropy decomposition indicated that direct information flow from the deep hippocampus regions (SOZ) to cortical regions (IZ) was dominated by unique information, accompanied by weaker redundant and synergistic contributions, revealing a potential phase-based modulation mechanism through which deep regions influence cortical irritative zones.

Conclusion

Together, classical time–frequency analyses and higher-order information-theoretic measures consistently revealed a state-dependent reorganization of brain dynamics across seizure onset. While changes in spectral power and phase synchrony captured alterations in local and pairwise interactions, O-information rate and transfer entropy decomposition further uncovered a shift in the structure of information processing toward higher-order interactions. These findings suggest that epileptic seizures are characterized not only by changes in signal amplitude or synchrony, but by a fundamental reconfiguration of multivariate information flow. Our results highlight the importance of higher-order information frameworks for brain mapping and provide new insights into the collective dynamics underlying pathological brain states.
