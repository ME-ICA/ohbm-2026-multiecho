Introduction

The rise of big data and AI has transformed scientific research, including clinical neuroscience. Large-scale neuroimaging datasets enable AI models to detect anomalies invisible to experts and explore novel pathological mechanisms across neurological disorders such as stroke, brain tumors, and neurodegenerative diseases. These opportunities come with challenges, notably integrating heterogeneous data and standardizing feature extraction. To address this, we developed the PHI (Platform for Human Imaging), a full deployment and extension of the HIP (ebrains.eu/tools/human-intracerebral-eeg-platform; Human Intracerebral EEG Platform) originally developed at CHUV (Switzerland). The PHI extends the HIP’s philosophy to structural and functional neuroimaging, including PET and clinical/cognitive data.

Methods

The PHI builds upon the HIP platform and extends its scope to include several neuroimaging modalities: structural, functional, diffusion MRI, and positron emission tomography. The platform also supports the integration of associated clinical and cognitive data. Designed to serve a multidisciplinary user base, the PHI provides an intuitive, secure, and scalable environment for managing imaging data within a clinical setting. Access is provided through EBRAINS credentials, and the platform includes installable applications with end-to-end pipelines for processing anonymized data into preprocessed outputs and final results. The system emphasizes usability, allowing non-expert users to interact with complex datasets through a user-friendly interface.

Results

The PHI is hosted by a GDPR-certified provider with dedicated hardware and an isolated, IP-restricted network. The infrastructure includes an authentication backend with 2 vCPU, 8GB RAM, and 25GB root filesystem; a user frontend with 8 vCPU, 8GB RAM, and 25GB root filesystem; a computation backend with 16 vCPU, 16GB RAM, and 25GB root filesystem; and a storage backend scalable up to 150TB, all interconnected via a 10Gb ethernet network. To ensure security and compliance with user requirements, the platform is organized into three working spaces. The private space allows users to upload and manage their data; the collaborative space enables users who agree to work together to share data for joint projects; and the public space provides extracted features to the EBRAINS community. The PHI also features a dedicated database (PHIDB) for retrieval of imaging and clinical data. An ingestion API enables automated, standardized uploads, while a graphical interface allows querying, filtering, and access, including management of requests for other users’ data, with permissions granted based on specific agreements (Fig. 1). To date, PHIDB has ingested multimodal data from approximately 1,755 patients, stroke (n=1,192), gliomas (n=291), and Parkinson’s disease (n=272), across 4 EU centers, creating one of Europe’s largest standardized neuroimaging repositories. Finally, a key component of PHI is the Multimodal Analytical Tool for Neuroimaging (MATI v1.0), an end-to-end application for processing structural, diffusion, and functional MRI clinical data, offering a standardized framework for extracting neuroimaging features that can be used by each member of the platform.

Conclusion

The PHI represents a step toward bridging the gap between advanced neuroimaging research and clinical practice. By integrating multimodal datasets into a secure and standardized infrastructure, the PHI facilitates reproducible research, multi-center collaborations, and the application of AI algorithms to large cohorts. Importantly, its design lowers barriers for non-expert users while ensuring scalability and compliance with GDPR regulations, critical for clinical deployment. Overall, the PHI and its PHIDB provide a robust foundation for the next generation of translational neuroimaging research, with the potential to accelerate the development of precision neurological approaches.
