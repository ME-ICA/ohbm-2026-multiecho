Introduction

FMRI studies investigating laminar-specific activity have gained significant attention in recent years.
These studies hold the potential to elucidate the directionality of information flow between brain regions. A significant limitation in many studies is the reliance on user-defined regions of interest (ROIs). The definition of ROIs often involves arbitrary choices, making them difficult to justify and prone to bias, see^1 for an experimental investigation of this problem. Here we introduce Cylarim, an innovative software package for laminar fMRI analysis that addresses this problem^2. Cylarim utilizes small, overlapping cylinders that traverse the cortical ribbon, enabling a comprehensive mapping of laminar-specific activity across extensive cortical regions. The output of Cylarim is a map that highlights areas of the cortical ribbon where a specific layer activation pattern predominates. For instance, users can identify cortical regions where middle-layer activation is significantly greater than in the deep and superficial layers.

Methods

We assume the availability of high-precision segmentations of grey matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) with a spatial resolution of approximately (0.2mm)^3 . Data with lower resolution should be upsampled accordingly. The initial processing steps follow the LayNii software package3. Specifically, we extract cortical rims delineating the boundaries between CSF and white matter. Subsequently, we derive a normalized ’metric’ image representing cortical depth, scaled to the range [0,1].

Cortical cylinders are now constructed by connecting pairs of voxels: one on the GM/WM rim and the other on the GM/CSF rim. Voxel pairs are identified by finding the nearest neighbor on the opposite rim for each voxel. After excluding redundant pairs, cylinders are defined by dilating the line connecting each pair of voxels with a user-specified radius. In our experiments, we used radii of around 2mm. Note that cortical ribbon voxels are typically covered by multiple overlapping cylinders, with regions of high curvature exhibiting increased cylinder coverage. This fact can be used to implement a novel equivolume-based analysis.

Several alternative methods for the analysis of layer-specific activations are implemented in Cylarim. The most straightforward is to divide the cortical ribbon into three depth strata – “deep”, “middle”, “superficial”, and perform cylinderwise statistics within each cylinder. In addition, various other features of the cortical depth profile such as concavity or the location of peaks or valleys are implemented in the Cylarim software. Statistical significance is determined through permutation-based testing within each cylinder, and corrections for multiple comparisons can be applied by controlling the false discovery rate4.

Conclusion

We introduce Cylarim, a novel software package for laminar fMRI, distinguishing itself from other approaches in two key respects. First, Cylarim offers, for the first time, a spatially-resolved statistical inference procedure specifically designed for laminar fMRI data. Second, it allows mapping across large parts of the cortex, thus moving beyond the limitations of small, user-defined ROIs that can potentially introduce subjective bias. 

The Cylarim software is freely available at https://lipsia-fmri.github.io.
