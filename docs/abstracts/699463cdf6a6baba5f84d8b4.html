Introduction

Transcranial photobiomodulation (t-PBM) has emerged as a noninvasive neuromodulatory intervention with potential disease-specific effects on large-scale brain network dynamics. While t-PBM shows promise as a network modulator in healthy adults,1 its impact on the temporal dynamics of intrinsic brain states remains underexplored. Static functional connectivity overlooks the transient shifts in network reconfiguration that may drive the mood and cognitive states, as well as the therapeutic mechanism of t-PBM. To address this, we employed Co-Activation Pattern (CAP) analysis to dissect the spatiotemporal stability of brain networks across two distinct clinical populations: Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) and Major Depressive Disorder (MDD).

Our analytic strategy was designed to systematically isolate disease signatures from intervention effects. First, we sought to characterize the disease-specific baseline landscape (Analysis 1), establishing whether AD and MDD exhibit distinct dynamic CAP fingerprints prior to stimulation. Building on this baseline, we examined the temporal trajectory of t-PBM effects (Analysis 2), determining if t-PBM induces immediate (during-stimulation) or sustained (post-stimulation) shifts in state occurrence, incidence, and dwell time. Lastly, we assessed whether these shifts are diagnosis-specific or  nonspecific (Analysis 3), testing the hypothesis that underlying network topology constrains the response to stimulation. Finally, we leveraged the AD cohort to directly compare delivery modes (Analysis 4), evaluating whether pulsed wave (PW, high-dose) versus continuous wave (CW, medium-dose) delivery differentially engages dynamic brain states.

Methods

Data were drawn from two clinical trials: (1) a study of early AD including one visit with a single dose of transcranial photobiomodulation (t-PBM; continuous/medium vs. pulsed/high dose), and (2) a MDD study including two visits with two separate t-PBM doses (medium vs. high). Detailed cohort characteristics, participant demographics, study design, including fMRI parameters, are described elsewhere.2,3

In brief, each participant underwent three consecutive fMRI scans: pre-stimulation, during-stimulation, and post-stimulation. During the stimulation condition, participants received t-PBM at either medium- (CW, irradiance ~300 mW/cm2) or high-dose (PW, peak irradiance 900 mW/cm2, average irradiance 300 mW/cm2)  while in the MRI scanner. The analyzed sample included the following groups: AD high dose (n = 14), AD medium dose (n = 28), MDD high dose (n = 11), and MDD medium dose (n = 17). Only a subset of participants from each ongoing clinical trial was included in the present analyses; additional data will be incorporated upon completion of each study.

Standard fMRI preprocessing was performed using fMRIPrep, followed by denoising, spatial smoothing, and nuisance regression with XCP-D.4 Time series were band-pass filtered (0.01–0.1 Hz), spatially smoothed using a Gaussian kernel (FWHM = 4 mm), and parcellated using the 4S1056Parcels atlas. CAP analysis was applied to identify transient brain states via temporal clustering.5 For each CAP, we quantified occurrence rate, incidence rate, and dwell time. Functional network membership for each CAP was determined using the Yeo atlas.

Analysis 1 (Disease-Specific Baseline Differences): To examine disease-specific differences in functional network CAPs, pre-stimulation CAP metrics (occurrence, incidence, and dwell time) were compared between the AD and MDD groups using Welch’s t-test.

Analysis 2 (Within-Group Treatment Changes): To assess stimulation-related changes within each diagnostic group and dose, CAP metrics were compared across pre-, during-, and post-stimulation sessions. Separate linear mixed-effects models were fit within each diagnostic group (AD, MDD) and dose condition (medium, high), with session as a fixed effect and subject as a random intercept. All acquisition runs were treated as independent observations. Omnibus session effects were evaluated using F-tests testing the null hypothesis of no differences across sessions. For CAP metrics showing a significant omnibus effect, post hoc pairwise comparisons (pre vs. during, pre vs. post, and during vs. post) were conducted using estimated marginal means.

Analysis 3 (Between-Group Treatment Changes): Between-group differences in CAP dynamics were assessed separately for each dose condition. For each CAP and metric, linear mixed-effects models included the diagnostic group as a fixed effect and subject as a random intercept, with all acquisition runs retained as repeated observations. Omnibus group effects were evaluated using F-tests derived from the mixed-effects models, and post hoc analyses were run if appropriate.

Analysis 4 (Dose-Specific Differences within AD): Finally, medium- and high-dose conditions were directly compared within the AD group, as the delivery methods differed (continuous wave = medium; pulsed wave = high). To evaluate dose-related effects during t-PBM, CAP occurrence, dwell time, and incidence were compared between doses at the during-stimulation session using Welch’s t-test.

FDR correction was applied for each analysis across the three CAP metrics within each CAP.

Results

The six CAPs were defined by their mean expression across the Yeo 7-network parcellation. CAP1 showed positive engagement of the dorsal attention network with suppression of the somatomotor network. CAP2 and CAP3 were characterized by strong positive visual network expression and negative involvement of the salience and default mode networks, respectively. CAP4 exhibited positive expression of the salience/ventral attention network with concurrent visual network suppression. CAP5 showed strong default mode network engagement alongside somatomotor suppression, while CAP6 was characterized by positive somatomotor expression and negative involvement of the control network.

Analysis 1: The MDD group showed greater CAP5 dwell time (t(58.84) = 2.39, p = 0.030) and higher CAP5 occurrence (t(53.91) = 2.50, p = 0.030) relative to the AD group. No other CAPs demonstrated statistically significant group differences after correction. Nominal group differences were observed for CAP3 and CAP4 metrics; however, these effects did not survive correction for multiple comparisons. 

Analysis 2: Within-Group mixed-effects analyses revealed significant session-related modulation of CAP temporal dynamics exclusively in the MDD group under the medium-dose condition. CAP1 showed a significant effect of session for both dwell time (F(2, 35.5) = 7.79, p = 0.0047) and incidence (F(2, 37.0) = 4.99, p = 0.018). Post hoc contrasts indicated that CAP1 dwell time decreased during t-PBM relative to pre-stimulation (p = 0.018) and increased from during to post-stimulation (p = 0.0013), with no difference between pre- and post-stimulation. In contrast, CAP1 incidence was significantly reduced post-stimulation compared with both pre-stimulation (p = 0.013) and during stimulation (p = 0.045). Additionally, CAP3 incidence exhibited a significant effect of session (F(2, 36.0) = 5.24, p = 0.030), driven by a reduction from during to post-stimulation (p = 0.009). No significant session effects were observed for other CAPs or dose conditions in the AD group, indicating selective and state-specific modulation of CAP dynamics.

Analysis 3: Between-group differences in CAP dynamics were evaluated separately for each dose condition using mixed-effects models pooled across sessions. After FDR correction within CAPs, significant group differences were observed exclusively under the medium-dose condition, with no significant effects detected under the high-dose condition, and increased change in the MDD group. Under the medium dose, significant between-group differences emerged across multiple CAPs and metrics. CAP2 showed significant group effects for both dwell time (F(1, 44.9) = 8.07, p = 0.010) and occurrence (F(1, 44.1) = 9.38, p_FDR = 0.010). CAP3 differed significantly between groups for incidence (F(1, 44.7) = 6.39, p = 0.023) and occurrence (F(1, 44.0) = 6.82, p = 0.023). CAP4 exhibited a significant group difference in incidence (F(1, 45.0) = 7.28, p = 0.029). CAP5 showed the strongest group effects, with significant differences in both dwell time (F(1, 42.9) = 12.3, p = 0.003) and occurrence (F(1, 43.6) = 10.3, p = 0.004).

Analysis 4: Although the total irradiance remained the same, the delivery within the AD group was different (medium/continuous vs high/pulsed wave). No CAP metrics demonstrated a statistically significant dose effect after correction. A nominal effect was observed for CAP2 occurrence, with higher occurrence in the high-dose group compared to the medium-dose group (t(32.03) = 2.35, p = 0.025), although this effect did not survive (FDR-corrected p = 0.075). All other CAP metrics showed no evidence of dose-related differences (all FDR-corrected p > 0.05). Several additional metrics showed trend-level dose effects prior to correction (CAP2 incidence, CAP5 incidence, and CAP6 incidence; uncorrected p = ~0.08-0.09), but none survived correction for multiple comparisons.

Conclusion

Analysis1:  These findings indicate a selective baseline alteration in the temporal dynamics of a default mode network-dominant CAP in the MDD group relative to AD, reflected by increased dwell time and occurrence. This pattern suggests disease-specific differences in intrinsic network stability at rest, with default mode dynamics distinguishing the two clinical populations prior to stimulation.

Analysis 2: Within-CAP mixed-effects analyses revealed significant session-related modulation of CAP temporal dynamics exclusively in the MDD group under the medium-dose condition. These effects were state-specific, primarily involving dorsal attention and visual-dominant CAPs, and were absent in the AD group and under high-dose stimulation, indicating selective sensitivity to stimulation context in MDD.

Analysis 3: Diagnostic group differences in CAP dynamics were dose-dependent and most prominent under the medium-dose condition, with the MDD group showing greater modulation across multiple CAPs and metrics. These results suggest that medium-dose t-PBM preferentially amplifies disease-related differences in both the temporal expression and frequency of transient brain states.

Analysis 4: Direct comparison of medium/continuous- and high/pulsed-dose stimulation within the AD group revealed no robust dose-dependent effects on CAP dynamics after correction, despite nominal trends in visual network-related CAPs. This suggests limited sensitivity of these network states to stimulation delivery method in AD under the current experimental parameters.

Altogether, our results demonstrate diagnosis-specific organization of macro-scale resting-state brain dynamics. At baseline, MDD is marked by increased stability of the default mode network, with greater dwell time and occurrence relative to AD, indicating heightened persistence of resting intrinsic network activity. Stimulation effects were selective and dose-dependent. Medium-dose (CW) t-PBM uniquely modulated CAP dynamics in MDD, particularly within attention and visual networks, and amplified diagnostic differences across brain states (i.e., the default mode network), whereas high-dose (PW) stimulation and all types of stimulation in AD showed minimal effects. Together, these findings point to preserved, context-sensitive network flexibility in MDD and less so in AD, and identify intermediate stimulation doses as most effective for revealing and potentially targeting disease-specific macro-scale brain dynamics.
