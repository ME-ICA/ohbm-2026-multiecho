Introduction

Parenthood places high demands on emotion regulation and the ability to form a bond with a newborn infant. In postpartum depression (PPD), these processes are disrupted. PPD is the most common pregnancy complication and is associated with adverse outcomes for both parent and child. Neuroimaging research in the perinatal period has primarily focused on brain structure, demonstrating long-lasting changes in grey matter structure 1 and cortical volume 2 in healthy participants. Additionally, a negative birth experience and perinatal mood symptoms have been linked to neuroanatomical alterations in the hippocampus and amygdala, respectively 3.

In PPD, a limited number of task-based fMRI studies have reported, albeit inconsistently, blunted amygdala reactivity to negative stimuli and increased dorsomedial prefrontal cortex connectivity during negative emotion processing 4. These findings are constrained by methodological limitations, including passive emotional paradigms (e.g., viewing faces or words), heterogeneous task designs, and small sample sizes (≈15–30 PPD cases). Investigating active emotion regulation using fMRI may therefore provide important insights into the pathophysiology of PPD and inform potential treatment targets.

A meta-analysis of emotion regulation fMRI studies by 5 showed that individuals with anxiety and mood disorders display reduced activation of the frontoparietal regulatory network during cognitive reappraisal, alongside increased activation in regions associated with emotional experience, though not the amygdala. Whether these findings extend to PPD is unknown. The present study aims to investigate the neural correlates of active emotion regulation in PPD and controls. We hypothesize that PPD will be associated with altered recruitment of prefrontal regulatory networks and higher state negative emotionality compared to controls.

Methods

Participants and Procedure

 

Pregnant and postpartum mothers will be recruited from the MoM2B cohort and via social media campaigns, an ongoing prospective study of maternal health and well-being at Uppsala University Hospital, Sweden. Inclusion criteria are fluency in written and spoken Swedish, no physical illnesses or pregnancy complications (preeclampsia, gestational diabetes, twin pregnancy). Participants were screened and excluded if they had metal implants or medical devices affected by MRI, a history of severe mental illness such as schizophrenia, past alcohol or substance dependence or current severe suicidality or severe eating disorder. One hundred mothers (50 healthy subjects, 25 with prenatal onset PPD, and 25 with postpartum onset PPD) will be invited to undergo MRI scanning of brain structure and function (resting-state and task-related activity) during postpartum week 6–8.

Measures

Participants completed an emotion-regulation fMRI task in which they viewed highly aversive images and were instructed either to experience the negative emotion naturally or to suppress it using cognitive reappraisal, while continuously rating their moment-to-moment negative affect.
 PPD symptoms were assessed using the Swedish version of the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS), and psychiatric history/current episodes were evaluated using the MINI. Emotion regulation ability was assessed with the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ) and the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale (DERS).

Statistical analyses

fMRI data will be pre-processed and analyzed using SPM12. Based on established emotion regulation and threat-processing networks, analyses will employ a region-of-interest (ROI) approach to examine condition-specific BOLD reactivity and functional connectivity in the amygdala, orbitofrontal and prefrontal cortical areas as well as the insula, cerebellum, inferior and precentral occipital gyri 4,5. 

Ethical considerations

The proposed project is guided by the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and has received ethical approval from the Swedish Ethical Review Authority (Dnr: 2019-03064).

Results

Results

Preliminary results will be presented in poster form at the conference.

Conclusion

This study will provide the first characterization of neural mechanisms underlying active emotion regulation in postpartum depression. Identifying differences in regulatory engagement between healthy and depressed mothers may improve our understanding of PPD pathophysiology and inform future approaches to supporting maternal emotional functioning in the early postpartum period.
