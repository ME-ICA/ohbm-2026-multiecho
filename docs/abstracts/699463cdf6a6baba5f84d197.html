Introduction

Understanding the pathophysiological mechanisms underlying psychosis is essential for improving diagnostic accuracy and developing targeted therapeutic interventions. Altered intrinsic brain activity has been consistently documented in both early-stage and chronic phases of psychotic disorders,^1,2 yet large-scale, robust evidence remains limited. Fractional Amplitude of Low-Frequency Fluctuations (fALFF), a measure of spontaneous brain activity derived from resting-state functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI), quantifies the amplitude of low-frequency spontaneous blood oxygenation level-dependent (BOLD) signal oscillations and provides insights into intrinsic neural dynamics.^3 However, previous studies examining fALFF in psychotic spectrum disorders have produced inconsistent and heterogeneous findings, with reports of both higher and lower regional activity patterns. These discrepancies likely reflect limitations in sample sizes, methodological variability across studies, and site-specific confounds. The present study addresses these limitations by conducting a large-scale, harmonized meta-analysis through the Enhancing NeuroImaging Genetics through Meta-Analysis (ENIGMA) Consortium, aiming to characterize psychosis-associated regional alterations in fALFF.

Methods

We performed a random-effects meta-analysis using restricted maximum likelihood estimation (REML) across four harmonized resting-state fMRI datasets from ENIGMA Schizophrenia Working Group, including participants with psychosis and matched healthy controls (n>500). Preprocessing was conducted using HALFpipe version 1.2.2, and z-score fALFF maps were computed from anatomical CompCor-denoised resting-state data. Z-score fALFF was computed as the mean amplitude of spontaneous blood BOLD signal fluctuations within the 0.01-0.1 Hz frequency band, normalized by the amplitude across the full detectable frequency range, and z-scored using each subject’s whole brain fALFF mean and standard deviation. Quality control procedures excluded participants with mean framewise displacement exceeding 0.5 mm. Regional fALFF values were extracted using a combined brain parcellation that included the Schaefer 100-parcel 7-network cortical atlas (including visual, somatomotor, dorsal attention, salience/ventral attention, limbic, control, and default-mode networks) and the Xiao 22-region subcortical segmentation (including basal ganglia nuclei, thalamus, hippocampus, amygdala, and nucleus accumbens), providing detailed spatial resolution across both cortical and subcortical brain regions. For multi-site datasets, scanner-related variance was removed using ComBat harmonization while preserving biological effects of diagnosis, age, and sex. At each site, linear regression models controlling for age and sex were fit separately for each region, and partial Cohen's d effect sizes comparing psychosis patients to healthy controls were calculated. Site-specific effect sizes were then pooled using the metagen function in R.  We evaluated potential moderating effects of demographic and clinical variables, including age, sex, and antipsychotic medication status, by testing diagnosis-by-covariate interaction terms. Multiple comparison correction was implemented using false discovery rate (FDR), and heterogeneity across sites was quantified using the I² statistic.

Results

The meta-analysis revealed widespread alterations in intrinsic brain activity in psychosis, characterized predominantly by elevated fALFF across cortical and subcortical regions compared to healthy controls. At the subcortical level, individuals with psychosis showed significantly higher fALFF in the right globus pallidus interna (d=0.27, pFDR<0.05) and left globus pallidus externa (d=0.26, pFDR<0.05). Nominally significant higher fALFF was also detected in the right amygdala (d=0.17, p<0.05) and right thalamus (d=0.17, p<0.05). At the cortical level, the left visual cortex survived FDR correction (d=0.26, pFDR<0.05). Elevated fALFF (p<0.05) was observed across multiple large-scale functional networks. The visual network showed the most extensive alterations, with higher fALFF in five regions: left visual occipital cortex (ROI 2: d=0.21, p=0.008; ROI 4: d=0.19, p=0.016) and right visual occipital cortex (ROI 52: d=0.23, p=0.003; ROI 55: d=0.18, p=0.026; ROI 53: d=0.17, p=0.030; ROI 54: d=0.16, p=0.049). The default-mode network showed higher fALFF in four cortical regions: right temporal cortex (ROI 93: d=0.25, p=0.002), right ventral prefrontal cortex (ROI 95: d=0.22, p=0.006; ROI 94: d=0.16, p=0.046), and left prefrontal cortex (ROI 45: d=0.21, p=0.009). The salience/ventral attention network showed higher fALFF in two regions: left frontoinsular cortex (ROI 25: d=0.20, p=0.010) and left limbic temporal pole (ROI 33: d=0.17, p=0.033). In contrast, lower fALFF was identified in the right dorsal attention network, specifically in precentral and postcentral cortices (ROI 72: d=-0.22, p=0.005). Effect sizes ranged from small to moderate (mean Cohen's d~0.20), and between-study heterogeneity was minimal (I²~10%), indicating robust cross-site convergence and high replicability of findings. Moreover, the observed alterations were independent of age, sex, and antipsychotic medication status.

Conclusion

Overall, these findings provide the first large-scale evidence based on a joint multi-sample meta-analysis with harmonized pipelines for subcortical and cortical alterations of intrinsic brain activity in psychosis. The basal ganglia nuclei regulate information flow to and from the cerebral cortex and other related subcortical regions through striato-pallido-thalamo-cortical circuits, which are involved in a broad range of behaviors, including motivational, emotional, and cognitive processing.^4 Elevated fALFF in the pallidal-thalamic-amygdalar circuit, together with higher activity in visual and default-mode regions and lower engagement of dorsal attention areas, supports the hypothesis of a basal ganglia-driven dysregulation of cortical dynamics.^5 However, several limitations should be noted. Although the current sample includes over 500 participants, we aim to expand the sample size by recruiting additional sites to the ENIGMA Schizophrenia Working Group to further increase statistical power and generalizability. Additionally, z-score fALFF captures only one aspect of brain dynamics; we propose to replicate these findings using complementary resting-state metrics such as regular (non-z-score) fALFF and regional homogeneity (ReHo) to provide a more exhaustive characterization of neural dysfunction in psychosis. By leveraging additional harmonized data from multiple cohorts, this study provides reproducible alterations in fALFF, improving our understanding of psychosis pathophysiology.
