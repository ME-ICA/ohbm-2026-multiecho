Introduction

Identifying neural markers of treatment response is central to advancing personalized psychiatry. Connectome-based models indicate that resting-state functional connectivity can forecast clinical outcomes across several psychiatric disorders. In social anxiety disorder (SAD), most work has relied on whole-brain models suited to broad interventions such as cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), which act on multiple cognitive and affective processes. By contrast, response to treatments that target a specific neurocognitive mechanisms, such as attention bias modification (ABM), may be better captured by network-specific biomarkers indexing function within the corresponding systems. Gaze-Contingent Music Reward Therapy (GC-MRT) is an ABM intervention used as a treatment for SAD which behaviorally targets threat-related top-down attentional control, designed to reduce attentional allocation to socially threatening stimuli1. It has been tested in randomized controlled trials suggesting efficacy and symptom reduction2,3. This procedure builds on evidence that SAD is associated with biased attention to threatening social stimuli, and aims to engage neural systems for goal-directed visual attention. The dorsal attention network (DAN), spanning regions such as the superior parietal lobule and frontal eye fields (FEF), is a core system for top-down spatial attention. Given the DAN’s central role in attentional control, its intra-network connectivity is a plausible neural substrate for GC-MRT’s therapeutic effects. Building on prior work that used whole-brain predictors of treatment outcome, here we focus on DAN connectivity as a candidate circuit-level biomarker of GC-MRT response in SAD.

Methods

Forty-six treatment-seeking participants with SAD provided clinical and resting-state functional MRI data before and after either receiving GC-MRT or completing an equivalent 12-week waitlist control period. SAD symptom severity was assessed using the 17-item self-report Social Phobia Inventory (SPIN). The 12-week GC-MRT protocol comprised 11 sessions, with training designed to reduce dwell time on threatening (disgusted) faces. In each session, patients selected a 12-minute preferred music track and viewed 30 face matrices (24 seconds each) while gaze was continuously tracked. Music played when patients fixated on neutral faces and paused when they looked at threat faces (Figure 1), implementing an operant conditioning paradigm intended to promote attentional preference for neutral over threatening faces and counteract the characteristic tendency of individuals with SAD to dwell longer on threat cues.

To verify that the clinical effects observed in the full randomized controlled trial were preserved in this fMRI subsample, we conducted an analysis of variance (ANOVA) with group (GC-MRT, waitlist) as a between-subjects factor and time (baseline, post-treatment) as a within-subject factor.

For each participant, seven intra-network connectivity matrices were computed, corresponding to the seven canonical networks defined by Yeo et al.4 Within each network, Pearson correlations were calculated among nodes for the resting-state activation time series of all constituent parcels based on the 100-region parcellation by Schaefer et al.5. Separate matrices were derived for baseline and post-treatment scans, and all correlation coefficients were converted to Z values using Fisher’s r-to-z transformation. The present work focused on within-network functional connectivity of the DAN.

The association between post-treatment SAD symptom severity (SPIN scores) and intra-DAN connectivity was examined using connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM). Post-treatment SPIN scores were predicted from (1) baseline intra-DAN connectivity and (2) post-treatment intra-DAN connectivity. Following prior CPM work with relatively small datasets, we adopted a leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO-CV) scheme. In each iteration, a model was trained on N–1 participants and tested on the left-out participant. Within each training set, connectivity features were normalized and correlated with post-treatment SPIN scores for feature selection; edges showing significant Pearson correlations were retained. Significant positive and negative edges were then summed separately for each participant and used as predictors in the CPM model. Prediction accuracy was quantified as the Pearson correlation between actual and predicted SPIN scores, and significance was confirmed using a 10,000-iteration permutation test. To characterize the most informative connections, we identified “consensus” edges that were selected in all LOO-CV iterations.

To examine treatment-related group differences in intra-DAN connectivity, we conducted an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) with post-treatment within-DAN connectivity as the dependent variable, group (GC-MRT, waitlist) as a between-subjects factor, and baseline within-DAN connectivity as a covariate. Edges with at least medium effect size (Cohen’s f ≥ 0.25) were retained. To localize regions contributing most to these group differences, weighted node degree was computed for each DAN parcel as the sum of its edges showing medium or larger effect sizes. In addition, a two-sample t-test compared the GC-MRT and waitlist groups on mean post-treatment intra-network connectivity, averaged across participants.

Results

The current sample showed a significantly greater reduction in SAD symptom severity in the GC-MRT group relative to the waitlist control group. Baseline within-DAN connectivity predicted post-treatment SAD symptom severity following GC-MRT (r = 0.50, p = 0.0074), and prediction accuracy was confirmed by a 10,000-iteration permutation test (p = 0.036). Of the 105 possible within-DAN edges, one positive and one negative consensus edge were selected in all LOO-CV iterations. The positive consensus edge linked the left and right premotor cortex, including the homotopic FEF, and was positively associated with post-treatment SPIN scores (r = 0.54, p = 0.0040). The negative consensus edge linked the superior portion of the left precuneus with the right superior parietal lobule (SPL) and was negatively associated with post-treatment SPIN scores (r = −0.59, p = 0.0014).

Post-treatment intra-DAN connectivity also predicted post-treatment symptom severity (r = 0.51, p = 0.0075), with significance again confirmed by permutation testing (10,000 iterations; p = 0.041). A positive consensus edge emerged between homotopic parcels in the left and right anterior intraparietal sulcus, which correlated positively with post-treatment SPIN scores (r = 0.58, p = 0.0024). Two negative consensus edges were identified: between the left FEF and a parcel in the left SPL, and between the left FEF and the left precuneus. These edges showed negative associations with post-treatment SPIN scores (left FEF-left SPL: r = −0.55, p = 0.0040; left FEF-left precuneus: r = −0.53, p = 0.0057). Analyses using connectivity within other large-scale networks did not yield comparable predictive performance, supporting the specificity of the DAN–GC-MRT relationship. Both predictive analyses were reproduced using linear ridge regression, indicating robustness to analytic variation.

Treatment-related differences in intra-DAN connectivity were further supported by group-level comparisons. Thirty-seven of the 105 within-DAN edges showed medium or larger effect sizes (Cohen’s f ≥ 0.25), indicating altered post-treatment connectivity in the GC-MRT group relative to waitlist, after controlling for baseline connectivity. The left precuneus exhibited the highest weighted node degree across these edges, suggesting a prominent role in post-treatment DAN reorganization. A comparison of mean within-DAN connectivity post-treatment revealed overall weaker intra-network connectivity in the GC-MRT group compared to the waitlist control group. 

Conclusion

The study provides converging preliminary behavioral and neurobiological evidence that clinically meaningful improvements in SAD symptoms generated by GC-MRT relate to altered DAN functional connectivity. The finding that baseline intra-DAN connectivity is associated with treatment outcome suggests that the intrinsic organization of the attentional control circuitry may have the potential to serve as a prognostic biomarker for GC-MRT responsiveness. This is consistent with prior work demonstrating that higher baseline functional integration within attentional networks may predict greater benefit from targeted interventions in anxiety and mood disorders.

The DAN is critically involved in sustaining goal-directed attention, orienting toward task-relevant stimuli, and suppressing distractors. The current results extend this functional role to the domain of social anxiety treatment, suggesting that effective symptom reduction may depend on the capacity of the DAN to support reallocation of attentional resources away from threat cues and toward a more adaptive attentional allocation to socially relevant stimuli. The identified consensus edges, linking FEF nodes and parietal hubs such as the precuneus and superior parietal lobule, align with known frontoparietal pathways supporting spatial attention, and their association with symptom change underscores the possibility that GC-MRT may operate by recalibrating these circuits.

Furthermore, post-treatment intra-DAN connectivity predicted post-treatment symptom severity, with effects driven by edges involving the SPL, intraparietal sulcus, FEF, and precuneus. These regions are implicated in visuomotor integration, spatial updating, and internally guided shifts of attention - functions that may be particularly relevant for reorienting gaze and cognitive focus during socially demanding situations. The fact that multiple consensus edges involved homotopic bilateral regions further suggests that GC-MRT may enhance interhemispheric coordination within attentional control systems.

From a mechanistic perspective, the observed post-treatment reductions in intra-DAN connectivity, relative to the waitlist control participants, may reflect a shift toward a more efficient, less over-coupled attentional network configuration. Hyperconnectivity within control-related networks has been reported in anxiety disorders, potentially reflecting compensatory over-engagement. The GC-MRT-associated decoupling might thus index a release from maladaptive hyper-synchronization, permitting more flexible, context-appropriate allocation of attentional resources. The centrality of the left precuneus in post-treatment reorganization is noteworthy, given its role in internally directed cognition and integration of visuospatial information. Reduced overconnectivity involving this hub could facilitate a shift in social threat processing that leads to symptom reduction. The left precuneus also presents a potential target for inhibitor neuromodulation that could be applied in parallel to GC-MRT and potentially enhance its clinical effect even further.

In conclusion, this study identifies the dorsal attention network as a central neural substrate potentially underlying prognosis in GC-MRT for SAD. The results suggest that the capacity and plasticity of attention control circuits, particularly frontoparietal pathways integrating premotor, parietal, and motion-sensitive regions, may be key determinants of therapeutic success. Beyond their theoretical implications for models of attentional dysregulation in SAD, these findings highlight DAN connectivity as a promising biomarker for stratifying patients and personalizing interventions aimed at optimizing attentional allocation in socially salient contexts.
