Introduction

Evolutionary expansion of primate cortex shows marked regional heterogeneity, with association cortex expanding disproportionately relative to early sensory regions. In the frontal lobe, quantitative comparisons using cytoarchitectonic criteria suggest that human prefrontal cortex is 1.9-fold larger than in macaques.1 In contrast, temporal lobe comparisons have been limited by two fundamental, unresolved homology questions.

First, face-selective region correspondences remain unclear despite extensive study. Several competing hypotheses exist regarding macaque-human temporal lobe homologies, complicated by a major offset: macaque face patches are located dorsally in or near the superior temporal sulcus (STS), whereas most human face regions lie ventrally on the fusiform gyrus. Second, species homology of the temporal association areas is unresolved, e.g., no clear macaque homolog or precise localization of the default mode network (DMN),2 and macaque appears to lack a homolog of human multi-modal area PHT.3 Traditional approaches examining individual networks in isolation have proven insufficient to resolve these questions.

Here, we propose that functional homologies can better be inferred by analyzing conserved multi-network topological relationships rather than isolated regional correspondences. We employ HCP-harmonized fMRI to systematically compare face-processing, object-processing, and DMN organization, revealing putative homologies via the relative spatial arrangement of these interacting networks. This approach reveals both functional correspondences and enables quantification of regional expansion.

Methods

Four macaque monkeys participated in the study: three Macaca mulatta  and one Macaca fascicularis. Functional MRI data were acquired on a 3T scanner (MAGNETOM Prisma, Siemens Healthcare) using a 24-channel multi-array RF coil designed for non-human primates (Takashima Seisakusho KK). Acquisition parameters were: TR = 1 s, TE = 18 ms, flip angle = 62°, voxel size = 1.25 × 1.25 × 1.25 mm, with intravenous administration of an ultrasmall superparamagnetic iron oxide (USPIO) contrast agent (ferumoxytol, 12–18 mg/kg).

The experimental paradigm was adapted from the Human Connectome Project (HCP) task design. Visual stimuli (15° visual angle, 2 s duration, 0.5 s interstimulus interval) were presented on an LCD (BOLDscreen 24, Cambridge Research Systems) positioned in front of the animal in sphinx posture with head fixation. Each block consisted of eight images from one of four categories (FACE, BODY, TOOL, PLACE). Three blocks per category were presented within a single scan in pseudo-randomized order, with six scans acquired per session. Resting-state fMRI was performed in separate sessions with the presentation of the Inscapes movie.

The data were analyzed using the non-human primate version of the HCP (NHP-HCP) pipeline.1,4 Functional data were further denoised using spatial independent component analysis (ICA) combined with machine-learning classifiers to remove artifacts. Category-selective activation was estimated from surface-based fMRI data using a general linear model implemented in FEAT, FSL, and compared to the D99 v2.0 parcellation. Additionally, temporal ICA was applied across runs in each subject.5 A temporal node of the DMN was defined based on the gradient of the ICA component.

Results

Our macaque fMRI results revealed distinct category-selective activations along the STS. Face-selective activation appeared in multiple regions: anterior TEad and TEm at the lip of the ventral bank of the anterior STS, the middle TEO at the lip of the ventral bank of the posterior STS, anterior TEav at the temporal pole, and a broad cluster spanning the dorsal bank to the fundus of the STS, including TPO, PGa, and posterior IPa. Body-selective activation was confined to two loci in the ventral bank: anterior TEm and posterior TEm/IPa. By contrast, object- and place-selective activations were widespread across the inferior temporal cortex, extending through V1, V2, V4, TEpd, posterior TEm, posterior TEa, TEpv, and TEav, forming the ventral visual pathway (Fig. A, left panel).

The topological relationship between category-selective activations in macaques and humans (Fig. A, right panels) indicates that the macaque anterior ventral face area in anterior TEad/TEm (face patch AL) corresponds to the human fusiform face area (FFA), while the macaque posterior face area in TEO (face patch PL) corresponds to the human occipital face area (OFA). By contrast, the macaque face complex in the dorsal bank and fundus of the STS (face patches AF, MF, ML) appears more aligned with human STS face-related regions. The macaque anterior and posterior body area ASB and MSB correspond to FBA and EBA in humans, respectively.

These findings suggest that the macaque STS-centered face-processing network evolved into two spatially distinct streams in humans: ventral fusiform regions and lateral STS regions. Notably, a substantial gap exists between these two streams in humans. The gap covered a wide area of the human middle temporal gyrus (MTG): TE1a, TE1m, TE1p showed only weak visual stimuli-induced activity and little or none in TE2a. However, a potentially comparable gap was located in a smaller part of the fundus of the STS in macaques. Interestingly, independent component analysis (ICA) revealed that the gap overlaps with the temporal node of the DMN region in the temporal lobe, a part of IPa in macaque and STSvp/TE1a/TE1m/TE1p in human. This temporal node of the DMN occupied a substantially larger proportion of the cortical surface in humans (~3.3%) than in macaques (~0.3%), suggesting an evolutionary expansion of the temporal node of DMN. Since the multi-modal area, PHT was located between the DMN and EBA in humans, its macaque homologue, if it exists, might lie between the DMN and MSB in a part of IPa.

Conclusion

This study proposes a putative temporal lobe DMN homology and quantifies its expansion using an approach analogous to that used for the prefrontal cortex. Key findings include: (1) Cross-species correspondence of social perception areas is best established through conserved multi-network topological relationships rather than isolated category selectivity; (2) macaque temporal node of DMN located in a part of IPa in the STS fundus, corresponding to that of human in TE1a/TE1m/TE1p regions of MTG; (3) A candidate homologue of multi-modal area PHT in the human ventral temporal-occipital transition area is likely located in the posterior part of IPa, not in the TEO in macaque. (4) Temporal node of DMN expands disproportionately in humans (~0.3% in macaque; ~3.3% in human), but similar topological organizations with face network suggest interdependent evolution of perceptual and multi-modal systems. These results extend quantitative comparative neuroanatomy beyond the prefrontal cortex to temporal lobe association areas and provide a framework for assessing homology through topological relationships among interacting functional networks.
