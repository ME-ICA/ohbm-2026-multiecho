Introduction

Multisite neuroimaging studies have become increasingly common in neuroscience research, enabling larger sample sizes and improved statistical power to detect subtle effects on brain structure and function [1,2]. These designs are substantially challenged by scanner- and site-related variability, which can introduce non-biological variance and obscure true biological variability. Harmonization approaches have emerged, and ComBat is a widely used statistical method for removing batch effects while preserving biological variability in MRI data [1,3]. However, variability between sites may reflect genuine differences in participant characteristics, including complex biological, psychological, and demographic factors as well as heterogeneity in study population, rather than purely technical site effects. Multisite harmonization therefore risks removing meaningful within-site variability together with unwanted scanner-related variance. Assessing within-site consistency before and after harmonization is crucial for identifying approaches that better preserve biologically relevant signals without compromising the removal of site effects [4]. Site effects differ across imaging modalities and depend on overall dataset properties such as feature distributions, inter-feature correlations, and site sample sizes, underscoring the need for transparent, modality-agnostic tools that report comprehensive evaluation metrics, and multisite neuroimaging research still lacks standardized, reproducible tools for evaluating harmonization effectiveness.

Methods

We introduce a Docker-based software tool that systematically evaluates data harmonization in multisite neuroimaging datasets. Building on recent work that emphasizes the importance of preserving within-site variability in structural connectivity and functional connectivity measures, the software computes a comprehensive suite of evaluation metrics designed to (a) quantify the presence and reduction of site-related variability, (b) assess within-site consistency to evaluate preservation of original variability, (c) examine preservation or enhancement of biologically motivated associations such as age effects, (d) persistence of residual site effects in the principal components before and after harmonization. The framework is imaging-modality agnostic and can be applied to structural, diffusion, quantitative, susceptibility, and functional MRI–derived measures. To demonstrate its utility, the tool is applied to multimodal features from the CARE4Kids study [5], investigating multimodal predictors of persistent post-concussive symptoms in adolescents aged 11-17.99 years. This multi-center study includes extensive MRI-derived features, including T1/T2 structural intensity ratios, diffusion tensor imaging metrics (FA, MD, RD, AD), advanced diffusion microstructure metrics (ODI, ICVF, RDI, ISOVF), diffusion kurtosis imaging metrics (MK, RK, AK, KF), quantitative susceptibility mapping and R2* measures, and resting-state fMRI, including within- and between-network connectivity and graph metrics.

Results

The software is designed to support harmonization evaluation rather than to prescribe a single optimal configuration. For a given dataset, it automatically generates harmonization reports that summarize: (1) the magnitude of site-related differences before and after harmonization; (2) indices of within-site consistency, which indicate the extent to which original variability is preserved across sites; and (3) changes in biologically motivated associations, such as relationships between imaging measures and age, that can signal improved biological validity following harmonization [2,4]. Users can compare different ComBat configurations, including empirical Bayes versus feature-wise estimation, and directly observe how these choices impact site-effect reduction versus preservation of within-site variability, including variability that may correspond to true differences in participant characteristics and heterogeneity. The reports are formatted to be easily incorporated as supplementary material, facilitating transparent documentation of harmonization choices in multisite neuroimaging.

Conclusion

By offering a reproducible, comprehensive, and user-friendly framework, this Docker-based tool promotes standardized assessment of ComBat harmonization performance in neuroimaging research. It directly addresses concerns that harmonization can inadvertently reduce meaningful within-site variability by explicitly quantifying within-site consistency alongside site-effect reduction (Onicas et al., 2022). In addition, by emphasizing that site effects differ across imaging modalities and depend on the statistical properties of the dataset, the tool encourages researchers to base harmonization decisions on transparent, quantitative criteria rather than implicit assumptions based on independent previous multisite studies. This resource represents an important step toward transparency, standardization, and reproducibility in multisite neuroimaging harmonization practices and toward inclusion of harmonization quality-control benchmarks as part of multisite data analysis and reporting workflows.
