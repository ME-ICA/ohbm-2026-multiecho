Introduction

Cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) is recognized as a common age-related condition associated with cognitive impairment, dementia, and stroke. Among the key markers of CSVD visible on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), white matter hyperintensities (WMHs) are most widely studied: they are highly prevalent in older adults, and are strongly associated with cognitive decline, stroke risk, and mortality1. A classical approach to studying WMHs uses diffusion metrics and more specifically the fractional anisotropy (FA). However, conventional diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) metrics lack specificity, as they cannot distinguish changes related to axonal fiber integrity from alterations in water homeostasis, both of which have been reported histopathologically in association with WMHs2. To study these microstructural changes in white matter, profilometry can be used to characterize microstructural properties along the trajectory of each fiber pathway. However, this approach generates large and complex datasets that can be challenging to analyze and interpret efficiently. A data-mining framework based on the frequent pattern (FP)-Growth algorithm3, designed to uncover meaningful associations in an unbiased manner, may be adapted to fit with profilometry data. In this study, using ADNI3 data, we implement a WMH-adapted analysis pipeline that integrates tract-specific free water correction DTI (FW-DTI) measures, paired with a novel use of the FP-Growth algorithm to distill meaningful patterns from complex profilometry data.

Methods

White matter alterations in CSVD were studied using diffusion MRI from 216 cognitively normal older adults (Fig.1.a). WMHs were segmented with SHiVAi4, and diffusion preprocessing and tractography was performed with TractoFlow5 (Fig.1.b). 28 major bundles were extracted with BundleSeg and divided into 20 segments via tractometry_flow (Fig.1.c). We used freewater_flow to calculate both the extracellular free-water fraction (FWF) and FW-DTI metrics (FA-t, MD-t, RD-t, AD-t). For each segment without lesions, we computed the population mean and standard deviation across all bundle profiles and diffusion metrics to obtain a segment-specific reference baseline (Fig.1.d). To explore WMH effects on white-matter pathways, an FP-Growth algorithm was applied to high-dimensional tract profiles. Segment-wise deviations from lesion-free references were classified as below, above or equal (fig.1.e). Association rules using confidence (C) and lift (L) identified recurring patterns between WMHs  and diffusion metrics, revealing combinatorial and microstructural alterations in a hypothesis-free, data-driven manner.

Results

In profilometry data, 71.6% of bundle segments are lesion-free and 28.4% contain focal lesions. Based on lift and confidence values (Fig. 2), non-lesional segments show modest diffusion alterations, primarily decreased MD-t, RD-t, and FWF (MD-t below: L=1.19, C=0.41; RD-t below: L=1.15, C=0.42; FWF below: L=1.16, C=0.56). Overall, lift values indicated strong associations(L≈1), whether positive (>1) or negative(<1). Nevertheless, the confidence for “FWF below” (C=0.56) remains relatively high compared to the other values, suggesting a low accumulation of free water in healthy tissue. In contrast, lesional segments exhibit increased values compared to the baseline, most prominently for “MD-t above” (L=1.404, C=0.70), “RD-t above” (L=1.310, C=0.64), and “FWF above” (L=1.470, C=0.56). For the FA-t and AD-t metrics, although high confidence is observed for “FA-t below” (C=0.54) and “AD-t above” (C=0.52), the lift values remain relatively low (L=0.16; L=0.18), indicating that these metric patterns co-occur with lesion presence no more frequently than would be expected by chance. Overall, lesional segments demonstrate associations with robust -lift and confidence with increases in MD-t, RD-t, and FWF, while non-lesional segments exhibit subtle, less discriminative diffusion changes.

Conclusion

Lesion presence is linked to elevations in MD-t, RD-t, and FWF, reflecting demyelination, axonal rarefaction, and increased extracellular water2. FA-t and AD-t exhibit weak associations, conversely, changes in RD-t and MD-t show the most reliable relationships with WMH, suggesting tissue alterations are driven mainly by reduced myelin or axonal density rather than microstructural disorganization. FP-Growth complements profilometry by identifying frequent, biologically meaningful diffusion patterns, supporting the prioritization of RD-t and MD-t for microstructural profiling of WMHs, rather than relying classically on FA-t which appears less sensitive to WMH characterization. FA-t, reflecting global diffusion properties, is sensitive to various tissue alterations and remains relatively low in lesioned segments. Nevertheless, these values do not appear more specific to WMHs than to normal age-related tissue degeneration. To confirm this, lift and confidence can initially be analyzed at the segment level for a more granular assessment of structural changes, followed by statistical analyses to test the hypotheses suggested by the data mining results. Additionally, it would be valuable to study diffusion metrics using more advanced models, such as neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI), to better characterize myelin changes and confirm the nature of the tissue degradation induced by WMHs.
