Introduction

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) and cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) are first-line treatments for obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), yet treatment response varies substantially across individuals1. Neuroimaging-based biomarkers may assist in optimizing personalized treatment decisions. Current findings on the use of brain morphological and functional metrics to predict the effectiveness of the two first-line treatments for OCD are mixed but promising. Most of them have documented satisfactory predictive performance, supporting the potential utility of neuroimaging-based biomarkers in forecasting treatment outcomes2,3. However, these studies often focus on a single treatment without other treatments, making it difficult to determine whether the identified predictors reflect treatment-general or treatment-specific effects. Consequently, these findings provide limited evidence to guide the selection of personalized treatment strategies in clinical practice. This study aimed to identify treatment-general and treatment-specific brain morphological and functional predictors of response to SSRIs and CBT in patients with OCD, and to assess their performance in predicting and differentiating treatment outcomes.

Methods

A total of 80 unmedicated patients with OCD participated in this study and received baseline magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans. Subsequently, 40 patients received 12-weeks of SSRIs, while the other 40 patients underwent 12-weeks of CBT. We assessed treatment effectiveness by measuring the change rate in the total score of the Yale-Brown Obsessive-Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS) before and after treatment. For T1-weighted structural images, we conducted voxel-based analyses for grey matter probability maps and surface-based analyses for cortical thickness, gyrification index, cortical complexity and sulcal depth, using the CAT12 and SPM12 in MATLAB. For resting-state functional images, voxel-based amplitude of low frequency fluctuations (ALFF), fractional amplitude of low frequency fluctuations (fALFF) and regional homogeneity (ReHo) were derived by Phi-Pipe. Treatment-general predictive metrics were defined as those significantly associated with Y-BOCS total score reduction rate, irrespective of treatment type. Treatment-specific predictive metrics were defined as those showing significantly different associations with Y-BOCS total score reduction rate between the SSRIs and CBT groups. To evaluate the utility of brain morphometric and functional metrics in predicting and differentiating treatment outcomes in patients with OCD, support vector regression (SVR) models were established using treatment-general and treatment-specific predictors.

Results

For all psychopathological outcomes, both two groups showed significant symptom reductions following treatment (p < 0.001). Sulcal depth in the left postcentral gyrus and left superior parietal lobule, local gyrification in the left posterior insula and left frontoparietal junction (ie, precentral gyrus and postcentral gyrus), ALFF in the left frontoparietal junction (ie, postcentral gyrus, supramarginal gyrus and precentral gyrus), fALFF in the right lingual gyrus and ReHo in the right frontoparietal junction (ie, precentral gyrus medial segment and postcentral gyrus) were significantly associated with Y-BOCS score reduction rates, independent of treatment type. These metrics were considered as treatment-general predictive metrics. Gray matter volume in the right occipitotemporal regions (ie, inferior occipital gyrus, middle temporal gyrus, inferior temporal gyrus) and fALFF in the right angular gyrus and anterior insula extended toward the vicinity of the lateral cerebral ventricle showed significantly divergent correlations with treatment response between groups, exhibiting positive correlations in the SSRIs group and negative correlations in the CBT group. These metrics were considered as treatment-specific predictive metrics. We collapsed the two intervention groups and employed treatment-general predictive metrics for prediction of treatment effectiveness. The model exhibited satisfactory predictive performance, the predictive value was significantly correlated with the true value (r = 0.713, p < 0.001). Furthermore, we used treatment-specific predictive metrics to predict the effectiveness of SSRIs and CBT, separately. We used the model trained in SSRIs group to predict the effectiveness of SSRIs and CBT. Interestingly, the predictive value was significantly correlated with the true value in the SSRIs group (r = 0.500, p = 0.001), but negatively correlated with the true value in the CBT group (r = –0.313, p = 0.050). The model trained in CBT group exhibited similar performance. The predictive value was significantly correlated with the true value in the CBT group (r = 0.682, p < 0.001), but negatively correlated with the true value in the SSRIs group, although it is not significant (r = –0.263, p = 0.101).

Conclusion

In summary, our study revealed that brain morphological and functional metrics could predict and differentiate the effectiveness of SSRIs and CBT in treating OCD. We also constructed linear support vector regression models using treatment-general or treatment-specific predictors to forecast OCD symptoms reduction rate, provided exploratory insights for future investigations into better predicting and differentiating the effectiveness of first-line treatments for OCD. Our study offered a potential approach for precision treatment in clinical practice, which need to be validated on further studies.
