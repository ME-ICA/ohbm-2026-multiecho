Introduction

Spoken language serves as the cornerstone of daily human communication, yet the neurophysiological constraints that determine the upper limit of speech processing speed remain poorly understood. A key candidate is the alpha-band neural oscillation (8–12 Hz), which has been implicated in timing and gating sensory input across modalities. While alpha oscillations are known to shape perceptual sampling and attention, whether and how the intrinsic frequency of alpha rhythms—the individual alpha frequency (IAF)—sets a bottleneck for speech processing speed remains an open question. Here, we propose that IAF constitutes a fundamental temporal filter that constrains how rapidly speech can be decoded by the auditory system. Addressing this question not only advances our theoretical understanding of speech perception but also holds translational potential for developing sensory-specific interventions to enhance communication abilities.

Methods

To test this hypothesis, we integrated behavioral psychophysics with non-invasive and invasive electrophysiological techniques. First, we measured speech processing speed using an adaptive speech-identification task in which spoken sentences were systematically accelerated. Resting-state EEG was recorded to derive each participant's IAF. In a subset of participants, we employed an individualized accelerated tACS protocol, which delivered at a frequency slightly above each participant's resting IAF, to assess the causal influence of alpha entrainment on speech recognition thresholds. To examine sensory specificity, parallel experiments applied repetitive auditory stimulation (RAS) and repetitive visual stimulation (RVS), followed by re-evaluation of speech processing thresholds. Finally, stereo-EEG (sEEG) recordings from the left auditory cortex in patients enabled direct investigation of oscillatory dynamics during speech listening and repetitive stimulation.

Results

Our behavioral-EEG correlation analyses revealed a significant positive relationship between resting IAF and speech processing speed, such that individuals with higher IAF could accurately recognize speech at faster rates. Accelerated tACS, tailored to each participant's IAF, reliably improved speech recognition performance, confirming a causal role of alpha frequency in setting processing speed. Importantly, while RAS effectively elevated the speech processing threshold, RVS did not, indicating modality-specific plasticity. Moreover, sEEG recordings from the left auditory cortex demonstrated that RAS selectively accelerated the pre-stimulus IAF and strengthened neural entrainment to the speech envelope, providing a direct neural mechanism for the observed behavioral enhancements. These results collectively establish IAF as an intrinsic temporal bottleneck for speech perception.

Conclusion

This work identifies the individual alpha frequency as a core neural determinant of speech processing speed, providing a unified framework for understanding temporal constraints in auditory perception. Our findings demonstrate that alpha oscillations serve not merely as passive markers of cortical excitability but as active temporal filters that govern the rate of speech decoding.
