Introduction

Stroke recovery is shaped by the evolving spatiotemporal complexity of cerebral perfusion. Following ischemic injury, there is a dynamic interplay of pathophysiologic processes, including the no-reflow phenomenon, incomplete or delayed reperfusion, and the emergence of chronic hypoperfusion within affected and functionally connected regions.1 Together, these vascular processes create a challenging neurobiological environment that influences neuronal plasticity and functional reorganization, with important consequences for later cognitive recovery.

Arterial Spin Labeling (ASL) MRI provides a unique, non-invasive window into these processes by enabling quantitative measurement of cerebral blood flow (CBF) and arterial transit time (ATT) without contrast agents.2 ASL is highly reproducible, well validated, and particularly well suited for longitudinal studies of cerebrovascular physiology. In this study, we used advanced ASL acquisition to characterize whole-brain and hemispheric perfusion abnormalities in post-ischemic stroke patients across subacute (1-6 months) and chronic (> 6 months) stages of recovery. Our goal was to identify perfusion signatures beyond the acute phase, examining both global tissue-level changes and hemispheric asymmetries, and relating these physiological measures to cognitive performance.

Methods

Participants

Ischemic stroke patients (ages 55-90 years; mean age ~67 years; ~64% male) were recruited as part of an ongoing longitudinal study of cerebrovascular recovery and post-stroke vascular contributions to cognitive impairments and dementia (VCID). All participants underwent a one-hour MRI protocol that included high-resolution T1-weighted structural imaging and arterial spin labeling (ASL) perfusion MRI. A subset of participants returned for a second study visit approximately six months after the initial scan, allowing longitudinal assessment of perfusion changes over time. Age-matched healthy control participants (ages 55-90 years, mean age ~65 years; ~42% male) were also enrolled and scanned using the same MRI protocol to provide normative reference data.

ASL Acquisition

Quantitative cerebral blood flow (CBF) and arterial transit time (ATT) were acquired using a three-dimensional pseudo-continuous ASL (3D pCASL) sequence with background suppression on a GE 3.0 T wide-bore MRI system (GE Premier Signa). Acquisition parameters included a repetition time (TR) of approximately 6.33 s, echo time (TE) of 64.8 ms, and an isotropic through-plane resolution defined by 4 mm slice thickness with 4 mm spacing.

ASL and Structural MRI Processing

ASL data were processed using a custom analysis pipeline developed for the GE pCASL sequence. The raw 4D ASL time series underwent rigid-body motion correction, after which the baseline magnetization image (M₀), contained in the fifth volume (index 4), was extracted for quantitative scaling and spatial registration. High-resolution T1-weighted anatomical images were bias-corrected and skull-stripped using FreeSurfer SynthStrip (mri_synthstrip), followed by tissue segmentation into gray matter (GM), white matter (WM), and cerebrospinal fluid using FSL FAST.3–5

Perfusion maps were aligned to individual T1 space using boundary-based registration implemented in FSL (epi_reg), with the M₀ image serving as the EPI reference. Structural images were nonlinearly normalized to standard space using ANTs (SyNQuick), registering brain-extracted T1 images to the MNI152_T1_1mm_brain template. The resulting deformation fields were applied to the T1-registered CBF and ATT maps to generate spatially normalized images for group-level analyses.

Hemispheric Asymmetry Analysis

ROI-wise CBF and ATT metrics were extracted using the AAL3 atlas, along with global GM and WM summaries. For stroke participants, ipsilesional and contralesional hemispheres were defined anatomically using documented stroke side; bilateral strokes were excluded from ipsi-contra analyses. For healthy controls, a fixed convention (left = ipsilateral, right = contralateral) provided a reference distribution. Hemispheric mean CBF and ATT were computed across cortical ROIs, and an asymmetry index (AI) was calculated as
 AI = (Contra - Ipsi) / ((Contra + Ipsi) / 2). AI values were converted to z-scores using healthy control means and standard deviations.

Statistical Analysis

Cross-sectional comparisons (Healthy vs Patient V1) were performed using linear models adjusting for age and sex. Longitudinal change (V2 - V1) was assessed in paired patients using within-subject change scores with age and sex covariates. Multiple comparisons were controlled using Benjamini-Hochberg FDR across CBF and ATT outcomes.

Cognition

Global cognition was assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). Raw MoCA scores were used; for remotely administered shortened versions (max score = 22), scores were linearly rescaled to the standard 30-point scale. Cross-sectional and longitudinal associations between hemispheric CBF and ATT AI and cognition were evaluated using age- and sex-adjusted linear models.

Results

Forty-one healthy controls and 64 stroke patients were included in cross-sectional analyses at Visit 1 (V1; ~70 days from stroke onset, SD = 35.81); 39 patients contributed paired longitudinal data at Visit 2 (V2; ~271 days from stroke onset, SD = 46.06). All perfusion metrics were expressed as z-scores relative to healthy controls and analyzed using age- and sex-adjusted models with FDR correction.

Whole-brain perfusion

Stroke patients exhibited a significant reduction in the whole-brain gray matter to white matter CBF ratio compared with healthy controls, indicating selective vulnerability of gray matter perfusion relative to white matter (FDR-corrected p < 0.001) (Figure 1). Absolute GM and WM CBF z-scores were modestly reduced but did not survive correction. Whole-brain ATT metrics did not differ between groups, suggesting preserved global transit timing despite altered perfusion magnitude.

Time since stroke

No significant associations were observed between perfusion z-scores and time since stroke at V1. At V2, GM CBF z-scores demonstrated a trend toward decreasing values with longer time since stroke (r = −0.28, uncorrected p = 0.08), which did not survive correction for multiple comparisons. ATT metrics showed no time-dependent effects.

Hemispheric perfusion asymmetry

Whole-hemisphere ipsilateral-contralateral asymmetry indices revealed a robust group difference in CBF. At V1, stroke patients demonstrated significantly elevated hemispheric CBF asymmetry relative to healthy controls (β = 0.81, SE = 0.29, p = 0.0056; FDR-corrected p = 0.011), after adjusting for age and sex. No group difference was observed for ATT asymmetry (FDR-corrected p = 0.51).

Positive AI values indicate contralesional > ipsilesional perfusion. Stroke patients showed systematic hemispheric differences in CBF, with lower perfusion on the ipsilesional side compared with contralesional regions and healthy controls. In longitudinal analyses (V2-V1), hemispheric asymmetry remained stable over time for both CBF and ATT (both FDR-corrected p > 0.5; small effect sizes).

Cognition

Hemispheric CBF asymmetry was not associated with MoCA performance at V1 after adjusting for age and sex (p = 0.73). Baseline hemispheric CBF asymmetry also did not predict follow-up performance on the MoCA at V2 after accounting for baseline cognition (p = 0.62). In contrast, baseline MoCA score was the strongest predictor of follow-up cognition (β = 0.81, p < 10⁻⁵), indicating substantial cognitive stability across visits.

Conclusion

Using quantitative ASL MRI, we demonstrate that post-ischemic stroke is characterized by persistent abnormalities in cerebral perfusion that extend beyond global hypoperfusion. While whole-brain gray matter perfusion is disproportionately reduced relative to white matter, the most robust and reproducible physiological signature of stroke recovery in this cohort was hemispheric cerebral blood flow asymmetry. Stroke patients exhibited systematic contralesional-ipsilesional perfusion imbalance that persisted across visits and was dissociable from arterial transit timing, highlighting an alteration in perfusion magnitude rather than vascular arrival dynamics.

Despite robust hemispheric CBF asymmetry in stroke participants, asymmetry indices were not significantly associated with global cognitive performance as measured by the MoCA. This may reflect limited sensitivity of brief screening instruments to regional perfusion abnormalities or compensatory network-level mechanisms that preserve global cognition despite persistent hemodynamic imbalance. Our findings underscore the value of hemispheric and relative perfusion metrics as sensitive markers of post-stroke cerebrovascular physiology. Future analysis incorporating domain-specific cognitive measures and regionally targeted analyses could clarify the functional consequences of chronic perfusion asymmetry associated with post stroke VCID.
