Introduction

Functional Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy (fMRS) allows quantifying dynamic changes in metabolite concentration (such as Lactate and Glutamate) within activated brain areas. A focalized increase of glutamate and lactate has been reported upon stimuli eliciting a positive BOLD response at 7T1,2 . The coupling between metabolic and BOLD response has been shown to be dependent on the involved neural mechanisms1,2 . Comparatively less is known about metabolic dynamics in areas of Negative BOLD Response (NBR). Notwithstanding the origin of NBR is still debated, the most accredited hypothesis is that it originates from deactivation3 . Recent studies have reported a significant decrease of lactate4 and glutamate4,5 at 7T during an NBR induction within the occipital lobe. The aim of the current study was to show the feasibility of MRS characterization of functional metabolism during deactivation at 3T, exploiting optimized procedures. These included fine-tuning of stimulation duration in order to guarantee stable deactivation during each epoch. A retinotopic analysis was conducted to map accurately the visual cortex.

Methods

Twenty healthy volunteers (9 women, aged 27.5±3.5) were scanned on a Siemens Prisma 3T scanner. Visual stimulation consisted in a 2° radial checkerboard flickering at 7.5 Hz, alternated with a gray background in a block design. Epochs were 15s long for fMRI and 64s long for fMRS. The stimulus duration during fMRS was selected with a preliminary study as the longest period that guaranteed a stable BOLD deactivation matching the deactivated area during 15s epochs. MRI protocol included a volumetric MPRAGE (TR=2500ms, TE=2.95ms, TI=1090ms, 0.9x0.9x0.9 mm^3 voxels), followed by a functional localizer (multi-band EPI acquisition, TR=1000ms, TE=30ms, 2.4x2.4x2.4 mm^3 voxels). Two fMRS functional runs were acquired with the semiLASER sequence (TR=4000ms, TE=28ms, 128x2 transients, 8 epochs each); the spectroscopic VOI (25x20x20 mm^3) was placed in the occipital region with the highest NBR (statistical maps were obtained by online analysis on the functional localizer). Finally, retinotopic data were acquired with the same functional localizer sequence, but with an acquisition time of 112s for the eccentricity stimuli and 144s for the polarity stimuli. In particular, retinotopic visual stimuli were subdivided in four runs: 1) two clockwise and 2) counter-clockwise 4Hz-flickering wedges (phase separation of 180° and 48s period) to map the polarity of  the visual field, 3) an expanding and 4) a contracting radial 4Hz-flickering ring (28s period with a maximum eccentricity of 8.5°) to map the eccentricity of the visual field.

Functional localizer (offline) and retinotopic data were preprocessed with fmriprep involving head motion correction, slice timing correction, distortion correction, BOLD-T1w coregistration and MNI space resampling. Functional localizer data were then processed in FSL-feat (spatial smoothing 5mm) with a first level general linear model (GLM) analysis (cluster p-value 0.05) to localize the deactivated zone within the occipital lobe and the adjacent activations. Group level GLM analysis was then performed with SPM. Retinotopic data were processed with @Retinoproc, an Afni toolbox, to obtain the eccentricity and polarity map. Spectroscopic data were pre-processed with MRSpa. Pre-processing included eddy current correction, frequency and phase drifts correction and within-epochs scan averaging. An ad hoc basis-set including 17 metabolites was simulated with MARSS. Fitting and quantification using water referencing were performed with LCModel with an exclusion criterion of CRLB value above of 30%.

Results

Second level fMRI analysis results are shown in Fig.2 (two-tail t-test, p<0.05 FWE corrected). The averaged mask of the spectroscopic VOI (42.2%±3.5% GM, 48.4%±3.8% WM, 8.8%±2.3% CSF) was within the deactivated area. Average change of BOLD within the spectroscopic VOI, in which the visual cortex was so subdivided (33%±9% V1, 45.1%±6.1% V2, 22.4%±4.2% V3), was -0.4%±0.2% and significantly deactivated volume was 43%±20% of the VOI.

Lactate and glutamate concentrations decreased during stimulation (paired t-test, -0.06±0.02 µmol/g corresponding to -8.2%±2.5% and -0.08±0.02 µmol/g corresponding to -1.5%±0.4%, p<0.04 and p<0.02, respectively, Bonferroni corrected). 

Other metabolites concentrations changes were evaluated; in particular,  Glx (Glu+Gln) (-1.8%±0.5%, p<0.003, no Bonferroni corrected), Asp (-3.5%±1.2%, p<0.007, no Bonferroni corrected), and GSH (-4.1%±1.9%, p<0.03, no Bonferroni corrected) showed a statistical significant decrease during deactivation.

Other metabolites concentrations changes were evaluated in particular Glx (Glu+Gln) (-1.8%±0.5%, p<0.003), Asp (-3.5%±1.2%, p<0.007), and GSH (-4.1%±1.9%, p<0.03) showed a statistical significant decrease during deactivation.

Conclusion

This study shows that careful optimization of stimuli and experimental conditions allows the metabolic characterization of NBR at 3T. 

The significant group-level activation and deactivation pattern showed in Fig.2 is similar to previously published results4,5.

Lactate and glutamate concentration values and their decrease during the deactivation are in line with those reported at higher field strengths4,5. Coupled decreases of Glu and Lac suggest a reduction of metabolic demand and thus are supportive of a focal reduction of excitation. Our results do not show GABA changes associated to stimulation , however this study was not specifically optimized for GABA detection.
