Introduction

Ischemic stroke remains a major cause of long-term disability worldwide. Despite advances in acute management that have improved survival, many patients experience persistent impairments in gait, motor coordination, and cognition. Stroke contributes substantially to global disability-adjusted life years, highlighting the need for early prognostic markers to guide individualized treatment and rehabilitation.

Outcome prediction traditionally relies on clinical factors such as age, baseline neurological severity, and vascular comorbidities, along with imaging markers including lesion volume and location. However, recovery varies even among patients with similar lesion characteristics, suggesting that stroke is not merely a focal injury but a network-level disorder involving widespread microstructural disruption. Current prognostic models remain largely lesion-centered and inadequately capture injury in normal-appearing tissue.

Myelin integrity has emerged as a biologically meaningful determinant of post-stroke recovery [1,2]. Oligodendrocytes are highly vulnerable to ischemia, and myelin disruption slows axonal conduction and impairs network synchrony. White matter (WM) is particularly susceptible, and reduced microstructural integrity has been associated with unfavorable functional outcomes [3]. Demyelination may extend beyond the infarct core via Wallerian degeneration and diaschisis, indicating that early myelin alterations in normal-appearing regions reflect residual network capacity for recovery [4].

Recent advances in myelin-sensitive MRI enable quantification of the myelin-enhanced value (MEV) [5]. However, whether early non-lesion myelin loss adds prognostic value beyond conventional clinical and lesion-based markers remains unclear. We hypothesize that non-lesion MEV measured one week after ischemic stroke captures microstructural reserve and independently predicts 1-year functional outcome.

Methods

MRI data from patients with acute ischemic stroke acquired within one week after onset (N = 498) were analyzed. Based on the 1-year modified Rankin Scale (mRS), 399 patients were classified into the favorable outcome group and 99 into the unfavorable outcome group. All patients underwent standardized MRI scanning using a Philips system, including T1-weighted (T1w), T2-weighted Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR), and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI). The T1w sequence was acquired with repetition time (TR) = 8.288 ms, echo time (TE) = 4.604 ms, flip angle (FA) = 8°, slice thickness = 5 mm, in-plane resolution = 0.2 × 0.2 × 6 mm, and scan time = 241.6 s. The FLAIR sequence was acquired with TR = 9000 ms, TE = 135 ms, inversion time = 2500 ms, FA = 90°, slice thickness = 5 mm, in-plane resolution = 0.4 × 0.4 × 6 mm, and scan time = 162 s. The DWI sequence was acquired with TR = 3000 ms, TE = 70.52 ms, FA = 90°, in-plane resolution = 0.9 × 0.9 × 6 mm, and scan time = 63 s. All MRI data were converted from Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine format to Neuroimaging Informatics Technology Initiative format using dcm2niix.

 Clinical MRI data consisted of T1w, FLAIR, DWI, and apparent diffusion coefficient scans, and all images underwent standardized preprocessing. The overall preprocessing workflow is summarized in Figure 1. FLAIR images were registered to T1w images using FMRIB’s Linear Image Registration Tool, and T1w/T2-weighted-FLAIR ratio maps were generated to enhance myelin-related contrast. Brain extraction and tissue segmentation were performed using FMRIB’s Brain Extraction Tool and FMRIB’s Automated Segmentation Tool, yielding gray matter  and WM masks. Lesions were manually delineated on DWI/ADC images and registered to T1w space. Non-lesion masks were constructed by removing lesion voxels from each tissue compartment, and MEV was computed for normal-appearing whole brain (NAWB), normal-appearing gray matter (NAGM), and normal-appearing white matter (NAWM).

 Clinical variables included age, sex, vascular risk factors, recanalization therapies, and initial National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS). Group comparisons and generalized linear models (GLM) were used to evaluate MEV as a predictor of 1-year functional outcome. To formally assess the contribution of MEV, two GLM frameworks were constructed: Model 1 included each MEV compartment (NAWB, NAGM, NAWM) as the sole predictor to evaluate the unadjusted association with outcome, and Model 2, which incorporated MEV together with clinical covariates (age, sex, vascular risk factors, recanalization therapies, and initial NIHSS) to determine whether MEV remained an independent predictor after full adjustment. Log-likelihood ratio (LLR) testing assessed the incremental predictive value of MEV beyond conventional clinical variables. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (IRB No. B-2404-893-103).

Results

 Significant group differences in MEV were identified across all non-lesion tissue compartments. Patients with favorable 1-year outcomes showed consistently higher MEV values in NAWB, NAGM, and NAWM compared to those with unfavorable outcomes.

In the NAWB compartment, the favorable group demonstrated markedly elevated MEV (p < 0.001), indicating strong separation between the two groups. A similar pattern was observed in NAGM, where MEV remained significantly higher in the favorable group (p = 0.004). The most pronounced difference occurred in NAWM, with the favorable group exhibiting substantially greater MEV relative to the unfavorable group (p < 0.001). As illustrated in Figure 2, favorable-outcome patients showed widespread preservation of non-lesion MEV, whereas unfavorable-outcome patients exhibited diffuse reductions extending far beyond the lesion territory.

 The predictive value of non-lesion MEV for 1-year functional outcome was further assessed using generalized linear models. In Model 1, higher MEV was significantly associated with favorable outcome across all compartments: NAWB (β = 0.057, p < 0.001), NAGM (β = 0.042, p = 0.003), and NAWM (β = 0.064, p < 0.001).

In Model 2, which included clinical covariates, MEV remained a significant independent predictor: NAWB (β = 0.031, p = 0.011), NAGM (β = 0.028, p = 0.034), and NAWM (β = 0.032, p = 0.006). These findings indicate that MEV provides robust predictive information beyond conventional clinical variables.

 LLR tests were conducted to determine whether inclusion of MEV improved model performance for predicting 1-year outcome. The addition of MEV significantly enhanced model fit for NAWB LLR = 6.543, degrees of freedom (df) = 1, p = 0.010 and NAGM LLR = 4.575, df = 1, p = 0.032. The greatest improvement was observed in the NAWM model LLR = 7.476, df = 1, p = 0.006, demonstrating that NAWM MEV contributes particularly strong explanatory value. Collectively, these results confirm that MEV enhances prognostic accuracy across all tissue compartments and offers substantial incremental predictive power beyond standard clinical predictors.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that MEV, derived from T1w/T2w-based myelin mapping, provides a robust index of microstructural integrity in non-lesion tissue and is strongly associated with long-term functional outcomes after ischemic stroke. Patients with favorable outcomes exhibited consistently higher MEV across NAWB, NAGM, and NAWM, and MEV remained an independent predictor after adjustment for all major clinical variables. Importantly, inclusion of MEV significantly improved the performance of clinical prognostic models, with the greatest incremental value observed for NAWM MEV, underscoring the importance of long-range myelinated pathways in supporting recovery. Collectively, these findings suggest that MEV captures a biologically meaningful dimension of microstructural reserve beyond lesion characteristics or initial neurological severity and represents a promising biomarker for early prognostication after stroke.
