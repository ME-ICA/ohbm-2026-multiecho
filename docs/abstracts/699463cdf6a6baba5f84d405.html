Introduction

Psychopathology often emerges during youth and shows marked sex differences1.  Such sex differences in psychopathology may be related to differences in functional connectivity. Here, we examine sex differences in functional connectivity across four developmental datasets.

Methods

We used resting-state and task functional MRI data in youth ages 5-23 from the Philadelphia Neurodevelopmental Cohort (n = 1207), Nathan Kline Institute-Rockland Sample (n = 397), Human Connectome Project: Development (n = 625), and Healthy Brain Network (n = 1126). We examined sex differences in functional connectivity at the region (between- and within-network; BNC and WNC), network, and edge level using the Schaefer 200 region, 17-network atlas. We fit generalized additive models including sex, age (smooth term), and head motion. Sex effects were quantified using the t-value associated with the sex variable, where positive values indicate male > female connectivity and negative values indicate female > male connectivity. To link sex differences to cognitive processes, we evaluated the alignment of sex effects with 125 meta-analytic activation maps of cognitive terms from Neurosynth and the Cognitive Atlas, obtained using the Neuroimaging Meta-Analysis Research Environment (NiMARE)2. Spin tests3 and network enrichment significance testing4 assessed significance.  

Results

Across all datasets, we observed replicable region-level sex differences, with males exhibiting greater BNC across most regions. In contrast, females had greater WNC in the default mode network. We identified 45 cognitive terms associated with BNC sex differences and 30 with WNC. Males exhibited greater BNC in regions linked to functions such as cognitive control (r = 0.41, pspin = 0.002). Sex differences in WNC were associated with cognitive terms such as autobiographical memory (r = -0.58, pspin = 0.006). Network-level analyses showed female enrichment in connectivity within default mode subnetwork A and male enrichment within limbic subnetwork across datasets A. Edge-level analyses revealed sex differences organized along the cortical hierarchy, with males showing stronger across-hierarchy connectivity and females showing greater connectivity between hierarchically similar regions. 

Conclusion

These findings provide a robust characterization of normative sex differences in functional brain organization during youth.
