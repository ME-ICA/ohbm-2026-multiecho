Introduction

Brain plasticity plays a crucial role in functional recovery after tumor resection by supporting the reorganization of neural pathways necessary to preserve cognitive function. Moreover, plasticity can also occur pre-operatively as progressive tumor growth interferes with neuronal connections, inducing compensatory reorganization1. Quantifying pre-operative plasticity could provide valuable prognostic information for patient outcomes. Our hypothesis is that contralateral plasticity induced by slow-growing tumors allows the other hemisphere to compensate for functions disrupted in the affected hemisphere. We pose that this, in turn, should make the functional profile of the healthy hemisphere more similar to that of a healthy template of the affected hemisphere. In particular, in this study, we focus on patients with tumors located in the left insular region, which affects language function. We propose to quantify contralateral plasticity, in a learned embedding that respects the Riemannian geometry of functional connectivity matrices, as the distance between a patient's pre-operative right-hemisphere connectivity and a reference healthy left-hemisphere. We observe positive correlation between pre-operative contralateral plasticity and post-operative cognitive scores (r > 0.7), suggesting that this metric could serve as a prognostic biomarker for functional recovery.




Methods

First, we built a reference latent space from resting-state functional connectivity of a large healthy cohort (N ~ 1k) from the Human Connectome Project (HCP) dataset2, separately for the left and right hemispheres. To compute functional connectivity, we employed homotopic parcellations in order to ensure correspondence between regions across hemispheres. This allowed us to consider each hemisphere independently, obtaining separate connectivity matrices for the left and right hemispheres. Next, because connectivity matrices are symmetric positive definite (SPD) objects that reside on a Riemannian manifold, we employed the Log-Cholesky metric proposed by Lin et al.3. This Riemannian metric enables the computation of geodesic distances with simple Euclidean operations. Finally, we applied PCA to learn a low-dimensional embedding, and considered the left and right centroids as healthy template hemispheres.

The obtained embedding was then used to analyze resting-state fMRI data from N = 8 patients with slow-growing left insular tumors treated at the Lariboisière Hospital (Paris). For each patient, we quantified contralateral plasticity as the distance between the pre-operative right-hemisphere connectivity and the healthy left template within the embedding space. Cognitive function of the patients was assessed pre- and post-operatively using a standardized neuropsychological battery, with analyses focusing on the picture-naming test as a measure of language function. To mitigate domain shift between clinical data and HCP, we applied first-moment matching to the embedded representations. Finally, we measured the Pearson correlation between contralateral plasticity and post-operative cognitive scores.


Results

We observed a strong positive association (r > 0.7) between pre-operative contralateral plasticity and post-operative picture-naming performance (Fig. 1). Specifically, we employed the AICHA4 and Yan 20235 (7- and 17-network variants) functional homotopic atlases, and the symmetric Destrieux anatomical atlas. The latter was included as non-functional baseline. The strongest correlation (r = 0.93) was obtained using the AICHA parcellation (Fig. 1a). Consistent associations were also observed for the 7-network Yan 2023 homotopic parcellation (r = 0.84, Fig. 1b), while the 17-network variant yielded a slightly lower but still promising correlation (r = 0.71, Fig. 1c). The Destrieux parcellation yielded a lower correlation (r = 0.38), probably due to its coarser resolution and lack of functional specificity (Fig. 1d).

Although one subject (sub-02) may appear as an outlier, particularly with AICHA, this case is consistent with our hypothesis: this patient presented with three tumors in the left hemisphere, suggesting limited capacity for contralateral plasticity. Consistent with this interpretation, sub-02 exhibited the largest distance from the healthy left-hemisphere template and the greatest post-operative cognitive decline. Nonetheless, removing this subject from the analysis still resulted in a positive, albeit lower, correlation (r = 0.65) for the Yan 2023 7-network parcellation, supporting the robustness of the proposed metric.


Conclusion

Our results suggest that contralateral plasticity before surgery captures meaningful functional reorganization that supports cognitive recovery after tumor resection. Patients whose right hemisphere more closely resembled a healthy left hemisphere pre-operatively showed better post-operative picture-naming performance, consistent with compensatory recruitment of homologous regions. We found the AICHA parcellation to provide the best predictive power (r = 0.93), followed by the 7-network version of the Yan 2023 parcellation (r = 0.84). The robustness of this effect across parcellations, as shown in Fig. 1, suggests that the proposed metric is reliable, although the employed sample size is limited (N = 8) and further validation in larger cohorts is needed. Methodologically, embedding SPD connectivity matrices via Log-Cholesky decomposition provides a principled way to compare patients and controls in a Euclidean space. Clinically, our findings point toward the potential of pre-operative connectome-based biomarkers to guide surgical decision-making and patient counseling. Future work will aim to validate this approach in larger cohorts, explore longitudinal trajectories of plasticity, and extend predictions to additional cognitive domains.
