Introduction

The ENIGMA (Enhancing NeuroImaging Genetics through Meta Analysis)^1 Parkinson’s Disease working group (ENIGMA-PD) shares various data types from people with Parkinson's disease (PD) across more than 40 international sites. Large multi-site neuroimaging consortia like ENIGMA enable powerful brain analyses but face challenges with heterogeneous data assessment protocols. One example is questionnaire data: different sites use different questionnaires to measure the same neuropsychiatric constructs, preventing direct data pooling and limiting statistical power for brain imaging analyses. Traditional harmonization approaches for questionnaire data rely on psychometric linking methods. These, however, require overlapping response data, which is rarely available in consortia that gather data from different sites retrospectively. Thus, we propose a novel approach combining expert consensus with computational semantic similarity methods to retrospectively harmonize neuropsychiatric questionnaires for six neuropsychiatric constructs, namely depression, anxiety, psychosis, apathy, impulse control disorders, and sleep. 

Methods

We developed an eight-step protocol to create harmonized symptom dimensions for the aforementioned neuropsychiatric constructs. These constructs were measured with different validated questionnaires in patients with PD across multiple international research sites in the ENIGMA-PD working group. The eight steps are:

Step 1 - Literature review: Extensive literature review to compile evidence on how psychiatric and sleep symptom dimensions are typically defined and measured with questionnaires. 

Step 2 - Dimension selection: Definition of core symptom dimensions based on the convergence of evidence from the literature review. See figure 1 for the chosen dimensions.

Step 3 - Expert mapping: Clinicians and researchers assign items to the defined dimensions through a structured survey to establish expert-based item-dimension assignments as one source of evidence for integration. For each item i, we will calculate Fleiss' kappa to quantify inter-rater agreement on dimension assignment. To determine the required number of expert raters for our expert survey, we conducted Monte Carlo simulations. For each construct, we provide three sets of analyses (stability: convergence of kappa, precision: bootstrap CI widths, replication: between-study variability) across four true agreement levels (κ = 0.40, 0.50, 0.60, 0.70). 

Step 4 - Semantic similarity analysis and unsupervised clustering: Semantic similarity analysis using different embedding models (high-performance general models, clinical models, mental-health specific models) to generate model-based item-dimension assignments as a second source of evidence for integration. 

Step 5 - Integration of mappings: We will assess agreement between expert-based and data-driven clustering, select the best-performing model, and determine final item-to-dimension assignments using a combined evidence approach. Cohen’s kappa for overall item-level agreement and dimension cohesion for dimension-level recovery are the chosen metrics. Based on these metrics the final item-to-dimension assignment will be performed using expert evidence (E_ik) and cluster evidence (C_ik) to calculate a combined evidence score (S_ik): S_ik = (w_E × E_ik) + (w_C × C_ik), where w_E = weight for expert evidence and w_C = weight for cluster evidence. 

Step 6 - Score transformation: Calculation of dimension scores for each questionnaire and transformation into standardized C-scores, to ensure comparability across questionnaires with different response formats. 

Step 7 - Validation: Psychometric evaluation of harmonized scores using predictive validity of new scores via regression models, internal consistency of new scores using McDonald's Omega Hierarchical, and confirmatory factor analysis assessing dimensional structure fit of the new scores. 

Step 8 - Generalization: Replication on unseen questionnaire item text with a fine-tuned embedding model. Experts give a binary rating (agree/disagree) on model item assignments (binary). We will evaluate automated assignments on unseen questionnaires via expert agreement and inter-rater reliability (Fleiss' κ). 

Results

In total, 28 questionnaires were selected (selection criterion: availability of subject response data for this questionnaire at at least one participating ENIGMA-PD site) (see figure 1a). Based on our extensive literature review, we selected the dimensions for each neuropsychiatric construct which are shown in figure 1b. We compiled the items of each questionnaire in an online survey which we distributed to experts in psychiatry or/and neurology. We share a PDF of the survey in our OSF project repository (https://osf.io/caxzb). Monte Carlo simulations revealed that we need 25.8 raters to achieve adequate stability, precision, and replication metrics for the worst case scenario of κ = 0.40. As of today, we already have complete responses from 55 experts but data collection is still ongoing. Therefore, we ran a small pilot study with N=5 expert survey data. We found an overall agreement of κ = 0.733 for the depression construct, κ = 0.566 for anxiety,  κ = 0.918 for psychosis,  κ = 0.471 for apathy,  κ = 1.000 for impulse control disorders, and  κ = 0.764 for sleep. We ran the semantic similarity analysis and preliminary results for the dimension cohesion (dimension-level recovery) for the depression construct are shown in figure 2. So far, 20 ENIGMA-PD sites reported availability and data sharing agreement which leads to a sample size of N>2500 patients with Parkinson’s disease who have completed at least one questionnaire within one of the six constructs.

Conclusion

This study presents a systematic, reproducible approach for retrospective harmonization of heterogeneous questionnaire data in multi-site consortia. By integrating expert clinical knowledge with computational semantic analysis, we enable data pooling across diverse assessment instruments without requiring overlapping response data. This methodology facilitates neuroimaging analyses combined with questionnaire data on substantially larger samples within ENIGMA-PD and should provide a generalizable framework applicable to other large-scale research consortia facing similar data heterogeneity challenges. We are sharing our code on GitHub and will publicly share mappings and the fine-tuned model for reuse.
