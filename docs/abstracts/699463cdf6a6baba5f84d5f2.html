Introduction

In acute ischemic stroke, infarct evolution is highly dynamic and difficult to predict, with substantial inter-individual variability in both the tempo and spatial pattern of progression.^4 Although the classical core–penumbra model conceptualizes time-dependent infarct expansion,^1,3 accumulating evidence indicates pronounced heterogeneity in tissue fate across patients.^2 Recent studies further suggest that vulnerability is not restricted to the conventional penumbra, but may extend into metastable “hot zones,” consistent with spatially graded risk within the interpenumbral compartment.^5

Here, we investigated population-level spatial gradients of infarct core and penumbra across the middle cerebral artery (MCA) territory. By delineating distinct spatiotemporal patterns of infarct progression, we aimed to identify regional vulnerability profiles and more precisely characterize the heterogeneity that underlies infarct evolution.

Methods

We retrospectively identified consecutive patients from the Clinical Research Collaboration for Stroke in Korea (CRCS-K) registry between May 2011 and February 2014. Among patients presenting with acute ischemic stroke within 7 days of symptom onset (n=13,552), we included those who underwent baseline vascular imaging with CT or MR angiography (n=8,517). We then restricted the cohort to patients with isolated middle cerebral artery (MCA) occlusion (n=1,480) and a clearly defined onset time, operationalized as witnessed onset with last-known-normal time equal to first-abnormal time (LNT=FAT; n=975). Patients were further required to have MRI within 24 hours of onset (n=525) and proximal M1-segment occlusion only, yielding the final analytic cohort (n=162). For comparative analyses, patients were stratified into a non-revascularization group (n=79) and a revascularization group with available pre-revascularization MRI (n=83).

For each patient, the acute infarct on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) was segmented to generate a binary lesion mask (lesion=1, non-lesion=0). Lesion masks were spatially normalized and resampled to a common stereotaxic space (MNI) and restricted to the MCA territory. Total infarct volume was calculated as the number of lesioned voxels multiplied by voxel volume. Stroke onset-to-MRI time was defined as the interval between the witnessed onset time (LNT=FAT) and acquisition of the index MRI.

Within the MCA territory, we generated three voxelwise brain-mapping measures. First, a lesion frequency map was computed as the proportion of patients with a lesion at each voxel. Second, we fitted voxel-based logistic regression models with lesion presence as the dependent variable and infarct volume as the predictor, producing a voxelwise regression coefficient (beta) map for volume. Third, we fitted analogous voxel-based logistic regression models using stroke onset-to-MRI time as the predictor, producing a voxelwise beta map for time. To facilitate comparability and improve clustering stability, infarct volume and onset-to-MRI time were z-standardized before model fitting. Voxels with extremely sparse lesion events were excluded to reduce coefficient instability.

Finally, we integrated these three maps by representing each voxel with a three-dimensional feature vector consisting of lesion frequency, the volume beta, and the time beta, followed by feature-wise standardization across voxels. K-means clustering was then applied to partition voxels into spatial clusters with similar vulnerability profiles. The number of clusters was determined by jointly assessing internal validity metrics (elbow of within-cluster sum of squares and silhouette separation) and solution stability across multiple random initializations, yielding a voxelwise cluster label map and corresponding cluster centroid profiles.

Results

The lesion frequency map (left/right combined) revealed a clear spatial gradient of infarct involvement across the MCA territory. Peak lesion frequencies (warm colors; up to ~35%) clustered within a compact deep/proximal MCA region, indicating voxels that were consistently DWI-positive across patients imaged at different time points and thus most compatible with early-involved, core-like tissue. Surrounding this high-frequency hub, an intermediate-frequency band extended toward the lateral cortical MCA regions, suggesting areas that become DWI-positive only in a subset of patients and are consistent with a penumbral compartment that requires further progression before converting to core. In contrast, peripheral and border-zone portions of the MCA territory exhibited low lesion frequencies, implying relative resistance to infarction—potentially reflecting more favorable collateral supply and/or greater ischemic tolerance—such that DWI lesions appeared only in a minority of cases or with more advanced progression.

The voxelwise infarct-volume β map (left/right combined), derived from logistic regression of lesion presence on total infarct volume, demonstrated a graded dependence of regional DWI positivity on overall lesion burden. Voxels with near-zero β (low T-scores) showed minimal change in lesion probability with increasing infarct volume, consistent with regions that become DWI-positive early and are relatively invariant to infarct size (i.e., core-like or “volume-invariant” infarction). Voxels with intermediate positive β showed a modest but significant increase in lesion probability as infarct volume increased, consistent with an early-penumbral compartment that converts as infarct progression advances. Voxels with high positive β (high T-scores) exhibited the strongest volume dependence, indicating regions that are typically spared in smaller infarcts and become involved predominantly in large infarcts, consistent with late-penumbral or relatively protected (“sanctuary”) tissue that requires substantial progression before converting to established infarction.


The voxelwise onset-to-MRI time β map (left/right combined), derived from logistic regression of lesion presence on onset-to-MRI time, characterized the temporal dependence of DWI lesion detectability across the MCA territory. Voxels with positive β (positive T-scores) showed higher lesion probability with longer onset-to-MRI intervals, consistent with regions that convert to established infarction as time-dependent progression proceeds (penumbral-like behavior). Voxels with near-zero β (T-scores near 0) showed little time dependence, indicating early involvement that is largely time-invariant or rapidly reaches DWI positivity regardless of imaging delay. Voxels with negative β (negative T-scores) showed lower lesion probability at longer onset-to-MRI times; this pattern should be interpreted cautiously, as it may reflect sparse lesion events and sampling or selection effects (e.g., differences in presentation severity or treatment timing) rather than true infarct “regression."

Conclusion

In a registry-derived cohort with proximal M1 occlusion imaged within 24 hours of witnessed onset, we identified reproducible spatial gradients of infarct progression across the MCA territory by integrating population lesion frequency with voxelwise dependencies on infarct volume and onset-to-MRI time. The resulting maps delineated a compact deep/proximal core-like compartment characterized by early, largely volume- and time-invariant DWI positivity, surrounded by intermediate regions with modest volume/time dependence consistent with earlier-converting penumbra, and more peripheral/border-zone regions that required substantial progression to become involved, consistent with late-penumbral or relatively protected (“sanctuary”) tissue. Unsupervised k-means clustering of these complementary gradients provided a data-driven parcellation of MCA tissue into distinct vulnerability profiles, offering a population-level framework to quantify inter-individual heterogeneity in infarct evolution beyond the classical core–penumbra dichotomy.
