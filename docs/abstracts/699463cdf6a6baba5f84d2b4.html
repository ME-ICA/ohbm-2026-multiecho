Introduction

The Prefrontal extent of the frontal operculum represents a recently evolved structure unique to the human brain, with no homologue in non-human primates.1 Neuroimaging studies have reported activation of this region in the left hemisphere during inner speech2 and deactivation when inner speech is absent.3 However, there is no evidence for the causal involvement of the Prefrontal operculum in this complex speech process. 

Methods

To address this gap, voxel-based lesion symptom mapping4 analyses were conducted on data from 199 male Vietnam veterans with penetrating traumatic brain injuries [(pTBI); see Figure 1 for lesion overlay]. Performance on a verbal working memory task (Letter-Number Sequencing, Wechsler Memory Scale-III5) was used as an indirect measure of inner speech. Between-group comparisons were further performed using the same inner speech index and a visuo-spatial working memory control task (Spatial span from the Wechsler Memory Scale-III5) in the same patient cohort and 55 healthy control veterans with no brain injuries, to assess lateralization and task specificity.

Results

Results from voxel-based lesion symptom mapping analyses highlighted the contribution of the left prefrontal operculum, specifically its anterior subregion, to a deficit in inner speech, together with other left frontal regions (Figure 2). Confirmatory between-group analyses demonstrated that patients with left frontal opercular lesions scored significantly lower on the inner speech task compared to patients with right frontal opercular lesions, patients with control lesions in other brain regions and healthy controls. No comparable deficit was observed on the visuo-spatial control task, where no between-group differences emerged.

Conclusion

These findings provide support for the causal involvement of the left prefrontal operculum in inner speech, suggesting that this uniquely human cortical region may function as a hub for articulatory planning, in concert with other frontal areas of the left hemisphere.
