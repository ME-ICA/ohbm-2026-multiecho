Introduction

Electroencephalography (EEG) enables non-invasive, real-time measurement of brain electrical activity from the scalp. However, EEG signals have inherently low amplitudes, making them highly susceptible to various sources of noise such as electrooculography (EOG) and electromyography (EMG). In addition, the superposition of multiple neural sources limits the spatial separability of specific neural processes^1. As a result, EEG data typically exhibit low signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) and substantial inter-subject variability, which often lead to degraded generalization performance of EEG-based machine learning models when applied to data distributions different from those seen during training^2. 

To alleviate this, cross-subject and cross-session transfer learning approaches have been proposed. Nevertheless, these methods often fail to fully bridge domain gaps, incur high computational costs, and rely on additional calibration or adaptation data^3.

In this study, we propose a simple but efficient framework for predicting response time (RT) using EEG signals alone, focusing on the Contrast Change Detection (CCD) task from the Healthy Brain Network (HBN)-EEG dataset^4. Specifically, we combine a standard EEGNet-based encoder–regression head architecture with subject-wise robust normalization and a response-locked preprocessing pipeline. Zero-shot RT prediction performance is evaluated on unseen releases (and subjects) without any additional training. Furthermore, we analyze EEG signal dynamics associated with RT to jointly assess predictive performance and neurophysiological interpretability.

Methods

We used Releases 1–5 from the HBN EEG dataset provided by the Child Mind Institute, which comprises 128-channel EEG recordings collected from children and adolescents aged 5–21 years. The data are distributed in the Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS) format and include detailed annotations of stimulus onset and behavioral responses. The dataset contains passive tasks—Resting State (RS), Surround Suppression (SuS), and Movie Watching (MW)—as well as active tasks, including CCD, Sequence Learning (SL), and Symbol Search (SS). 

The main objective of this work is to focus exclusively on RT prediction in the CCD task. To improve learning efficiency and experimental consistency, only CCD sessions were used. In an exploratory analysis, simple correlations between RT patterns across CCD and non-CCD tasks were low, motivating our decision to train and evaluate the model solely on CCD data without additional pretraining. In this work, ‘zero-shot’ refers to evaluating cross-release generalization to unseen releases (and subjects) without further model adaptation.

As illustrated in Figure 1(a), the proposed framework consists of a preprocessing pipeline and an EEGNet-based regression model^5. CCD trial metadata were first loaded for each release, and only the trials containing valid behavioral responses were retained. RTs were constrained to the range of 0.2–2.4 s to exclude implausibly fast or slow responses. Raw EEG signals were then band-pass filtered (0.1–40 Hz) and notch filtered (60 Hz). For each trial, an EEG segment from -1.0 s to +0.5 s relative to the button press (t=0 s) was extracted, yielding response-locked input samples. 

To reduce inter-subject distributional differences, subject-wise, channel-wise robust normalization was applied to the EEG signals using the median and interquartile range (IQR)-based scale (IQR/1.349). RT labels were z-score normalized per subject using the subject-specific mean and standard deviation, allowing the model to focus on trial-level variability rather than absolute inter-subject differences in RT. 

The model consists of two components. First, an EEGNet backbone encoder extracts features by modeling temporal patterns (temporal block) and spatial relationships across electrodes (depthwise block), followed by further refinement in a separable block. Each block includes normalization, nonlinear activation function, average pooling, and dropout to mitigate overfitting. Second, the multi-layer perceptron (MLP) regression head maps the extracted feature vectors to a single continuous RT estimate.

Results

To rigorously assess zero-shot generalization, experiments were conducted using a leave-one-site-out strategy, in which each release was used once as the test set. For example, one configuration trained on R1, R4, and R5, used R2 for validation, and tested on R3, while another trained on R1, R2, and R3, validated on R4, and tested on R5. This setting allowed us to evaluate how well the model generalizes RT prediction to subjects from unseen releases.

In Figure 1(b), each gray dot represents the prediction error (Pred − True) for an individual trial. True RTs were divided into six bins, and bin-wise mean error curves were compared. Across both cross-release settings, the bin-wise mean errors of the proposed model (blue curve) consistently lie closer to the zero-error line (Pred − True = 0) than a baseline based on the mean RT of trials within each bin (red curves). In particular, the trial-mean baseline exhibited systematic under-/over-estimation in short RT ranges; the proposed model more closely tracked changes in true RT, indicating reduced bias. These results suggest that the regression model leverages trial-level variability to achieve more fine-grained predictions across the RT distribution than simple averaging. Quantitative metrics were also consistent across two settings. When R3 was used as the test release, the performance reached mean absolute error (MAE) = 0.228 s, root mean squared error (RMSE) = 0.296 s, normalized RMSE (nRMSE) = 0.828, and Pearson correlation coefficient (r) = 0.567. When R5 served as the test release, performance was MAE =0.235 s, RMSE = 0.299 s, nRMSE = 0.855, and r = 0.519. The stability of MAE and RMSE values, together with consistently positive correlations, indicates that the proposed preprocessing-model combination achieves a meaningful level of cross-release zero-shot generalization despite domain gaps arising from differences in hardware and recording conditions.

Figure 2 shows response-locked scalp topographies at -0.5, -0.2, 0.0, +0.2, and +0.4 s. For each release, the grand average across all subjects and trials reveals temporally evolving voltage patterns (µV, baseline-corrected; BL) centered over the occipital–parietal regions around the response onset. These CCD-related topographies exhibit systematic changes in polarity and spatial extent before and after the response, yielding a relatively consistent group-level pattern. In contrast, within the same release, the average topographies of subjects with the highest (Best subject) and lowest (Worst subject) Pearson correlations with the grand average differ markedly in polarity, spatial localization, and hemispheric lateralization. This suggests that inter-subject variability in spatiotemporal patterns likely contributes to the difficulty of zero-shot RT prediction and generalization.

Conclusion

In this study, we introduce and evaluate a zero-shot framework for predicting RT using EEG signals in the HBN-EEG CCD task. Both quantitative and qualitative analyses demonstrate its effectiveness and interpretability. Response-locked scalp map analyses revealed consistent CCD-related group-level patterns over occipital–parietal regions in both test releases, while best-worst subject comparisons highlighted substantial inter-subject variability in spatiotemporal EEG patterns, underscoring a key challenge for cross-subject generalization.

Despite this variability, the proposed model consistently produced bin-wise prediction errors closer to the zero-error line than a trial-mean baseline, indicating reduced bias and improved sensitivity to RT variability across the distribution. Moreover, comparable MAE and RMSE values across different test releases (R3 and R5) suggest that the model maintains a meaningful degree of cross-release zero-shot generalization under varying recording conditions. The observed variability in the correlation coefficient further indicates that individual differences and release-specific factors influence the strength of RT-EEG relationships.

Overall, the proposed framework demonstrates that even a relatively simple EEGNet-based regression model, combined with subject-wise robust normalization, can achieve meaningful zero-shot RT prediction while preserving interpretable neurophysiological patterns. As such, it provides a simple, reproducible baseline for future studies on cross-subject and cross-release RT decoding. Additional comparative experiments and analyses will be explored in future work.
