Introduction

Schizophrenia is a complex psychiatric disorder characterized by widespread metabolic, structural, and functional brain abnormalities, which can be identified using different in vivo neuroimaging methods. However, it is still unclear whether and how brain aberrations across modalities overlap. Recent advances in multimodal neuroimaging meta-analysis allow integration across structural and functional domains, enabling identification of convergent brain aberrations and their network-level organization. Furthermore, understanding these patterns is critical for elucidating the neurobiological mechanisms underlying schizophrenia and for guiding the development of targeted interventions. Therefore, we aimed to identify latent abnormality patterns of schizophrenia.

Methods

To identify characteristic brain aberrations in schizophrenia, we first conducted seven voxel-wise coordinate-based meta-analyses of metabolic, structural and functional MRI and PET studies. The following seven neuroimaging modalities were systematically investigated according to the Preferred Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA): volume-based morphometry (VBM), cortical thickness (CT), gyrification, regional homogeneity (ReHo), amplitude of low frequency fluctuations (ALFF), 18F-Fluorodeoxyglucose-PET (FDG-PET), and arterial spin labeling (ASL). A systematic search of publications before the 1st of November 2024 was conducted on the PubMed database. The following exclusion criteria were applied: studies were not in English language or not available for downloading as full articles, even after contacting the authors; studies used region-of-interest analyses only instead of whole-brain analyses; studies did not compare patients with schizophrenia and healthy controls; if peak coordinates of effects were not available or not in standard space; studies reported follow-up data only without baseline data in longitudinal studies. Additionally, only studies including patients with an explicit diagnosis of schizophrenia (e.g., using DSM-IV) were selected for inclusion in our meta-analysis. A total number of 301 studies comprising more than 17,000 schizophrenia patients and healthy controls were included. Unimodal meta-analyses were conducted using seed-based d mapping with permutation of subject images (SDM-PSI, version 6.22)1 with a resolution of 2 mm. A significance threshold of p < 0.001 was applied.

To examine neuroanatomical covariance between the investigated modalities, we applied non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) to decompose covariance resulting in a three-parts-based representation, which has been shown to achieve dimensionality reduction in neuroimaging data in highly interpretable ways2. 

Results

Meta-analyses revealed modality-specific patterns of brain aberrations in schizophrenia, affecting regions involved in cognitive, affective, and sensorimotor processing (Fig.1, left). 

Structural analyses identified consistent gray matter reductions in prefrontal, temporal, and insular cortices, as well as alterations in gyrification and CT in fronto-temporal regions. Functional analyses demonstrated abnormal ReHo and ALFF, predominantly in default-mode, salience, and frontoparietal control networks. Metabolic studies did not converge in any regions, whereas ASL analyses indicated cerebral blood flow alterations in prefrontal and insular cortices, as well as in the striatum.NMF revealed three components capturing covariance across modalities (Fig.1, right): (1) a primary sensory component dominated by VBM and CT, (2) a temporo-insular component dominated by ALFF and ReHo, and (3) a medial-frontal component not dominated by one specific modality. 

Conclusion

Our findings provide a comprehensive, multimodal characterization of brain aberrations in schizophrenia, demonstrating that structural and functional alterations are both regionally specific and coordinated across neural systems. The observed modality-specific patterns underscore the importance of assessing multiple neuroimaging indices to capture the complexity of schizophrenia-related brain changes. Covariance analysis via NMF revealed three principal components, which may represent fundamental axes of disease vulnerability.
