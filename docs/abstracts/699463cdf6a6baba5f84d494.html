Introduction

Background: Altered myelination has been implicated in learning difficulties observed across a broad range of neurodevelopmental conditions (Haroutunian et al., 2014, Nave & Ehrenreich, 2014). Neurofibromatosis Type 1 (NF1) is a monogenic condition that can serve as a valuable model for investigating how alterations in myelination contribute to cognitive difficulties. NF1 is highly expressed in oligodendrocytes, which are myelin-producing glial cells (Asleh et al., 2020). Loss of NF1 function has been shown to affect myelin integrity, which in turn is thought to contribute to learning impairments. Despite advances in imaging technology, there are currently no noninvasive modalities that can explicitly assess myelin. Previous diffusion MRI studies have demonstrated widespread white matter microstructural differences, suggesting aberrant myelination in NF1 (Siqueiros-Sanchez et al., 2024, Ferraz-Filho et al., 2012). Studies on quantitative myelin and lesion mapping in this population remain scarce. Our study aimed to compare two different myelin mapping techniques including magnetization transfer ratio (MTR) and T1W/T2W ratio to examine myelination in children and adolescents with NF1. This study presents the first mapping of regional myelin differences in NF1 compared to neurotypical controls.

Methods

Methods and materials: In this case-control study, 58 children with NF1 and 20 aged-matched neurotypical controls were recruited. T1-weighted (T1W) and T2-weighted (T2W) structural MRI (N=55) and Magnetization Transfer Ratio (MTR) images (N=26) were acquired from children and adolescents with NF1 (aged 11-18 years) and compared with 20 neurotypical controls. We used the Visuospatial n-back task to measure working memory performance. Eligibility criteria were (i) clinical diagnosis made using the National Institute of Health diagnostic criteria and/or molecular diagnosis of NF1.

MRI data were preprocessed using the FMRIB Software Library (FSL) analysis suite, Release 6.0. T1W and T2W structural brain images were subjected to skull-stripping and bias field correction. T2W images were then coregistered to the T1W images to match the inplane voxel resolution of the native space. The coregistered T1W and T2W images were used to produce T1W/T2W maps. The MT images were skull-stripped followed by corregistration of MTon to MToff using FSL's FLIRT. The formula MTR=(MToff – MTon)/MToff  x 100 was applied to the data to generate MTR maps. The whole brain MTR maps were coregistered to the corresponding subject’s T1W images. The T1W data was spatially normalized to the MNI152 standard space and the associated transform was applied to the MTR and T1W/T2W ratio  maps. The T1-weighted images were segmented into grey matter (GM) and white matter (WM) partial volume maps and further subjected to 50% threshold to generate binary masks. The segmented GM and WM brain masks were used to produce GM- and WM-specific MTR and T1W/T2W maps. The MTR and T1W/T2W (GM and WM maps) were smoothed to improve the signal to noise ratio within the local neighbourhood voxels using a Gaussian kernel (FWHM = 8 mm) and (FWHM = 2 mm) respectively. We repeated the MTR analyses for the 8 NF1 participants without T2WMH and compared them to controls. 

A General Linear Model was used to test for case-control myelin differences between NF1 and controls with age, gender and pituitary depth as covariates. We also tested the relationship between myelin and working memory (interaction effects) between NF1 and controls.

Results

Results: Compared to neurotypicals, children with NF1 showed significantly reduced MTR in the thalamus, basal ganglia and cerebellum (Table 2). These findings were mirrored by altered T1W/T2W ratios in similar areas (Table 3). MTR sensitivity analysis: We found reduced white matter myelin in a large cluster originating from the left thalamus (x= -6, y= -3, z= 1; k= 1254, F= 4.84, p= 0.039, FWE corrected) extending across the left pallidum and midbrain in NF1 compared to controls (Fig. 3 and 4). We also found reduced grey matter myelin in the left thalamus (x= -17, y= -28, z= 4; k= 9474, F= 6.41, p= 0.001, uncorrected) extended to left hippocampus, however this cluster did not survive the multiple comparison correction. No significant association was found between myelin and working memory performance in NF1.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Our findings present the first multimodal MRI mapping of regional myelin alterations in children with NF1. We demonstrate that these changes involve a core set of brainstem, cerebellum and diencephalic brain regions. This supports the view of central aberrant oligodendrocyte pathophysiology in NF1. The converging multimodal neuroimaging approaches (MTR and T1W/T2W Ratio) offer novel insights to myelin differences in neurodevelopmental conditions.
