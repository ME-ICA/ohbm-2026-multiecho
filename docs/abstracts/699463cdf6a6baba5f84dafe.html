Introduction

Federated Learning (FL)1 promises multi-site clinical collaboration without sharing raw patient data, yet real-world deployments remain rare. A recent systematic review of 22,693 healthcare FL publications found only 32 true clinical applications, with ~94.8% limited to proof-of-concept studies. Persistent barriers—including inconsistent data standards, heterogeneous modalities, lack of deployable platforms, and strict privacy and regulatory demands—prevent FL from fulfilling its potential in clinical research. COINSTAC (https://coinstac.org/ ) pioneered federated analysis in neuroscience and has supported many research groups, demonstrating its scientific value. However, large clinical institutions face additional operational hurdles: certificate-based authentication, strict firewalls blocking inbound connections, complex IT approval processes, and requirements for encrypted, auditable communication, custom analysis, while giving flexibility for users to tailor their analysis to specific research goals. While COINSTAC successfully pioneered federated analysis in neuroscience, NeuroFLAME (https://neuroflame.org/ ) is specifically developed to bridge this implementation gap by providing enterprise-grade security, governance, faster custom analysis development, and a user-friendly graphical user interface (GUI) for neuroimaging workflows. 

Methods

NeuroFLAME extends the paradigm by coupling a neuroscience-focused orchestration layer with NVIDIA FLARE’s2 enterprise FL substrate. A key feature for clinical deployment is the communication model, which is restricted to outbound-only mutual TLS (mTLS), eliminating the need for inbound network ports and thus aligning with strict hospital firewall policies. The platform's architecture (Fig. 1) includes an Electron-based desktop GUI for study coordination, a central orchestration API for configuration, certificate-based governance, and verifiable audit logs. NeuroFLAME significantly streamlines the creation of new custom computations by providing boilerplate scaffolding and templates, allowing researchers to focus solely on their algorithms and drastically reducing setup time. The platform further streamlines development by providing boilerplate scaffolding and templates that drastically reduce setup time for custom analysis for new authors using the platform.

Results

Federated algorithms: 

NeuroFLAME supports a wide array of federated computations or algorithms. So far, the platform has successfully developed and fully tested more than five specialized computations, including normal form regressions, iterative regression, federated constrained source-based morphometry, federated source-based morphometry, and federated ComBat data harmonization.   Fig. 2 shows one of the computation screenshots in the GUI. 

Vaults: 

Another major deliverable of NeuroFLAME is the introduction of Vaults, which are centrally hosted, curated neuroimaging datasets paired with metadata, accessible to permitted users. These Vaults offer "always-on" data resources, addressing a limitation of standard FL that relies on all institutions being simultaneously online. Researchers can analyze Vault data alone or blend it with their own local data to increase sample size and statistical power. NeuroFLAME currently hosts 7 Vaults spanning structural MRI (FreeSurfer and VBM) and functional MRI (GICA) datasets from three major cohorts: COBRE, Substance Use, and the Healthy Brain Network study. These vaults cover sample sizes ranging from 78 to 922 participants across wide age ranges. The platform supports standardized analytical methods such as single-round ridge regression for structural data and univariate regression via MANCOVA for GICA-derived functional measures. The enhanced GUI supports diverse study designs and user needs while maintaining the flexibility required for setting up different analysis workflows.

Conclusion

NeuroFLAME transforms federated learning from a research-focused proof-of-concept into a clinically aligned, deployable framework for large-scale, privacy-preserving neuroimaging studies. By integrating essential operational safeguards—such as certificate-based authentication, outbound-only communication, and robust governance features—with flexible analytical workflows and a new GUI, NeuroFLAME directly addresses the persistent implementation barriers faced by large clinical institutions. Furthermore, the introduction of centrally hosted, pre-processed Vaults significantly enhances statistical power and reproducibility by providing ready-to-use, standardized datasets for neuroimaging studies. This platform is designed to make federated learning a practical reality for clinical neuroscientific research.
