Introduction

Children with drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE) are at high risk of language impairment, yet presurgical language mapping remains challenging due to limited task compliance in fMRI and the scarcity of age-matched pediatric controls. Differential tractography, referenced to large normative diffusion MRI datasets, offers a potential avenue for identifying patient-specific white matter alterations relevant to language outcome.

Methods

Twelve children with focal, left-lateralized DRE (mean age 9.1 years) undergoing presurgical evaluation were prospectively studied. Multishell diffusion MRI data were reconstructed using generalized q-sampling imaging and compared against a large normative developmental dataset from the Human Connectome Project–Development (n = 636, ages 5–21 years). Differential tractography quantified reductions in fractional anisotropy (FA >20%) within seven bilateral language-related pathways, including the arcuate fasciculus (AF), superior longitudinal fasciculus (SLF), and extreme capsule (EC). Verbal comprehension was assessed using the Verbal Comprehension Index (VCI; WISC-V/WPPSI-IV). Linear regression analyses tested associations between tract disruption volume and VCI, and asymmetry indices were computed to assess hemispheric effects. In parallel, we acquired diffusion MRI data from 15 typically developing children scanned locally on the same scanner as patients, to further control for site- and acquisition-specific effects.

Results

Patterns of white matter disruption varied substantially across individuals, with no consistent organization by epilepsy subtype or lesion characteristics. However, greater disruption volume within left dorsal and perisylvian pathways was significantly associated with lower VCI, including the AF (β = –0.518, q = 0.0098), SLF (β = –0.535, q = 0.0098), and EC (β = –0.587, q = 0.0098). Asymmetry analyses further indicated that stronger left-lateralized FA reduction was associated with poorer verbal comprehension (e.g., AF asymmetry index: β = –0.531, q = 0.0133). No language-related tracts showed FA increases relative to the normative reference.

Conclusion

In pediatric focal epilepsy, disruption of left dorsal and perisylvian white matter pathways is highly individualized but predictive of verbal comprehension relative to normative development. These findings underscore constraints on compensatory reorganization of language networks and support differential tractography as a promising framework for individualized presurgical language risk assessment, particularly when local pediatric control data are limited.
