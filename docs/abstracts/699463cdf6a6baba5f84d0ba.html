Introduction

High-definition transcranial electrical stimulation with a 4×1 electrode configuration (4×1 HD-tES) enhances spatial focality by positioning four return electrodes concentrically around a central electrode1. This configuration exhibits a fundamental intensity-focality trade-off: larger electrode spacing yields higher intensity but reduced focality, whereas smaller spacing enhances focality at the cost of intensity.1 Neuroimaging studies have revealed substantial inter-individual variability in brain anatomy and electric field distributions, indicating that optimal configurations are inherently subject-specific and cannot be derived from group-level templates.

In practice, electrodes are typically placed according to the 10-10 EEG system for operational convenience.2 However, this discrete landmark system fundamentally limits individualized optimization: sparse landmark availability prevents precise targeting of cortical regions between landmarks, and fixed inter-electrode spacing precludes flexible adjustment of the intensity-focality trade-off. Recent continuous parameterization approaches enable more flexible montage specification but rely on stochastic optimization susceptible to local optima and require neuronavigation for implementation.3

We introduce a Scalp Geometry-based Parameter Space for 4×1 HD-tES (SGP-4×1 HD-tES) that defines electrode configurations directly on the scalp through three parameters: position, radius, and orientation. Rather than treating the parameter space as an undifferentiated search domain, we characterized the relationship between montage parameters and targeting performance, revealing structured regularities that enabled construction of a minimal search space reducing complexity by over 90% while guaranteeing globally optimal solutions. This strategy of leveraging parameter-performance structure offers a generalizable paradigm for optimizing non-invasive brain stimulation targeting.

Methods

SGP-4×1 HD-tES Parameter Space

The SGP-4×1 HD-tES parameter space represents each electrode configuration using three scalp-based parameters: position (s), radius (r), and orientation (φ) (Figure 1a). Position of the central electrode is specified using the Continuous Proportional Coordinate (CPC) system, defined by five cranial landmarks (nasion, inion, vertex, left and right preauricular points).4 For any scalp location, its coordinates (PNZ, PAL) are computed as normalized geodesic distances along the Nz-Cz-Iz reference curve and the coronal curve passing through that location (Figure 1b). Candidate positions were sampled uniformly with approximately 4 mm spacing, yielding approximately 4,000 valid positions after excluding periorbital and periauricular regions. Radius is defined as the scalp geodesic distance between the central and each return electrode, discretized into 10 values from 25 to 70 mm (Figure 1c). Orientation is defined as the clockwise angle from the posterior direction to the first return electrode, discretized into 6 values from 0° to 75° given the four-fold rotational symmetry (Figure 1d). For each position, only radius-orientation combinations keeping all return electrodes within suitable regions were retained, yielding approximately 120,000 feasible configurations per subject.

Participants and Electric Field Simulation

Thirty healthy adults (15 females; mean age 20.3 ± 1.31 years) from the Southwest University Longitudinal Imaging Multimodal (SLIM) dataset were included.5 Individual volume conductor models were constructed using the headreco pipeline in SimNIBS with standard tissue conductivity values. Electric field simulations were performed using the lead-field-free method.3 All approximately 120,000 configurations were exhaustively simulated for each subject.

Targeting Performance Metrics

Targeting intensity was defined as the volume-weighted average electric field magnitude within the ROI. Targeting focality was defined as the ratio of target-region average to whole gray-matter average field magnitude. A configuration was considered Pareto-optimal if no other configuration achieved both higher intensity and higher focality.

Parameter-Performance Characterization

Configurations were systematically analyzed to characterize how each parameter influences performance. For position, both intensity and focality decreased with increasing geodesic distance d(s, s0) from the nearest scalp point above the target (Figure 1e,h). For radius, larger values yielded higher intensity but lower focality (Figure 1f,i). For orientation, variability was quantified by fitting ellipses spanning performance ranges for each position-radius combination; orientation effects were relatively modest (Figure 1g,j).

Minimal Search Space Construction

To identify the minimal parameter domain capturing all Pareto-optimal solutions, 624 cortical ROIs were defined by uniformly sampling the cortical surface at 10 mm intervals. For each of 18,720 subject-ROI combinations, the maximum geodesic distance (dmax) among Pareto-optimal configurations was computed. Coverage of radius and orientation values was examined to determine whether full-range preservation is necessary. The resulting constrained parameter space is termed the SGP Minimal Search Space (SGPMSS).

Validation

SGPMSS optimization was validated on four targets: left primary motor cortex (L_M1), left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (L_DLPFC), left inferior parietal lobule (L_IPL), and right temporoparietal junction (R_TPJ), each defined as a 10 mm radius sphere (Figure 2d). Under the Pareto framework, SGPMSS was compared with conventional 10-10 montages (C3, F3, P3, CP6). Under the ROC-based framework, SGPMSS was compared with both 10-10 placement and lead-field-free optimization (LFOF), with performance quantified as distance to the ideal point in ROC space.3

Results

Parameter-Performance Relationships

Systematic analysis across the SGP parameter space revealed structured regularities governing targeting performance. Position emerged as the primary determinant: both targeting intensity and focality decreased monotonically with increasing geodesic distance d(s, s0) from the nearest scalp point above the target, with Pareto-optimal configurations clustering within smaller distance ranges (Figure 1e). Electric field distributions confirmed this pattern—as the central electrode moved away from s0, target engagement became progressively attenuated while off-target stimulation increased (Figure 1h). Radius governed the intensity-focality trade-off: larger radii (e.g., 70 mm) achieved higher intensity but lower focality, while smaller radii (e.g., 25 mm) produced more focal but weaker stimulation (Figure 1f). Field visualizations showed that increasing radius expanded suprathreshold stimulation extent, whereas decreasing radius confined modulation to the target region (Figure 1i). Orientation exerted relatively modest effects, with most position-radius combinations showing limited performance variability across the six orientation values (Figure 1g,j).

Minimal Search Space Identification

The distribution of dmax across all 18,720 subject-ROI combinations revealed a median of 12.0 mm, 95th percentile of 26.5 mm, and 99th percentile of 34.8 mm (Figure 2a). A threshold of 40 mm was adopted to ensure complete capture of all Pareto-optimal solutions. Coverage analysis confirmed that all radius values contributed to Pareto-optimal sets, with coverage gradually decreasing from near-complete at 25 mm to approximately 50% at 70 mm (Figure 2b). All six orientations were similarly represented (Figure 2c). The resulting SGPMSS reduces computation from approximately 16 hours to 1-2 hours per subject. When resources are limited, omitting orientation reduces time to 10-30 minutes; for single-objective optimization, computation drops to 10-15 minutes.

Validation 1: Pareto-Based Comparison with 10-10 Placement

SGPMSS-optimized configurations significantly outperformed conventional 10-10 montages across all four targets (Figure 2d,e). For L_M1, SGPMSS achieved higher intensity when matched for focality (0.36 ± 0.08 vs 0.18 ± 0.04 V/m, p < 0.0001) and higher focality when matched for intensity (19.15 ± 2.67 vs 8.48 ± 0.92, p < 0.0001). For L_DLPFC, intensity improved from 0.35 ± 0.07 to 0.41 ± 0.10 V/m and focality from 18.91 ± 2.11 to 23.35 ± 4.59 (both p < 0.0001). For L_IPL, intensity improved from 0.22 ± 0.05 to 0.33 ± 0.08 V/m and focality from 13.50 ± 1.35 to 20.14 ± 3.08 (both p < 0.0001). For R_TPJ, intensity improved from 0.21 ± 0.03 to 0.26 ± 0.05 V/m and focality from 9.05 ± 0.63 to 10.55 ± 0.96 (both p < 0.0001).

Validation 2: ROC-Based Comparison with 10-10 and LFOF

Under ROC-based evaluation, performance was quantified as Euclidean distance to the ideal point (0, 1). Figure 2f shows representative single-subject ROC distributions, where orange points represent SGPMSS configurations (darker orange indicating positions within 10 mm of s0), green triangles indicate 10-10 placement, and blue squares show LFOF optima. Optimal configurations clustered within the darkest orange region, suggesting that for single-objective optimization, a smaller distance threshold may suffice. At group level (Figure 2g), SGPMSS significantly outperformed 10-10 across all targets (L_M1: 0.21 vs 0.94; L_DLPFC: 0.07 vs 0.20; L_IPL: 0.17 vs 0.83; R_TPJ: 0.26 vs 0.96; all p < 0.01). SGPMSS achieved comparable performance to LFOF (all p > 0.05), while LFOF occasionally converged to suboptimal solutions with greater inter-subject variability.

Conclusion

This study introduces SGP-4×1 HD-tES, a scalp geometry-based parameter space that enables systematic description, optimization, and implementation of individualized 4×1 HD-tES montages. By characterizing the relationship between montage parameters and targeting performance, we revealed a hierarchical structure: position primarily determines proximity to optimal performance, radius governs the intensity-focality trade-off, and orientation serves as a fine-tuning parameter. Exploiting these regularities, SGPMSS reduces computational complexity by over 90% while guaranteeing global optima identification.

Across four cortical targets, SGPMSS-optimized configurations significantly outperformed conventional 10-10 placements, with intensity improvements of 17-99% and focality improvements of 17-126%. Under ROC-based evaluation, SGPMSS matched lead-field-free optimization performance with greater cross-subject stability.

The SGP framework offers three key advantages over existing approaches. First, intuitive scalp-based parameterization enables direct electrode specification without geometric transformation from fitted ellipsoids. Second, structure-guided exhaustive search guarantees global optima identification, avoiding convergence failures inherent to stochastic optimization. Third, scalp-based parameters enable manual electrode placement without neuronavigation, facilitating implementation in resource-limited clinical settings.

For clinical translation, this framework is particularly suited for home-based therapy applications. Electrode holders with optimized radius can be prefabricated, and electrode positions can be manually measured using cranial landmarks or marked on customized caps with pre-positioned holes. Furthermore, the characterized parameter-performance regularities may enable development of population-based targeting strategies: probabilistic optimization across large-scale datasets could yield group-level optimal montages that accommodate neuroanatomical variation without requiring individual MRI scans, substantially lowering barriers to personalized stimulation.

Several directions warrant future investigation. First, validation of whether enhanced targeting precision translates to improved therapeutic outcomes is essential. Controlled studies comparing optimized montages to standard protocols across applications—including depression treatment, motor rehabilitation, and cognitive enhancement—would establish clinical efficacy. Second, systematic evaluation of placement accuracy and therapeutic consistency in unsupervised home environments is necessary to establish guidelines for remote applications.

More broadly, this strategy of exploiting parameter-performance structure rather than relying on stochastic search within undifferentiated parameter spaces may offer a generalizable optimization paradigm applicable to other non-invasive brain stimulation modalities, including temporal interference stimulation, transcranial magnetic stimulation, and focused ultrasound.
