Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is characterized by progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra (SN). Multiple studies demonstrate that the free-water fraction (FWF), derived from diffusion MRI, is significantly elevated in the SN in PD compared with healthy controls (HC), increases longitudinally, and predicts both motor worsening and cognitive decline [1,2]. In parallel, PD is associated with increased iron accumulation in the SN/SN pars compacta (SNc), which can be quantified with quantitative MRI (qMRI) metrics such as R2* [2]. Importantly, iron-related measures and FWF are thought to reflect distinct underlying biological processes: extracellular water expansion versus magnetic susceptibility changes associated with iron deposition [2]. A recently proposed qMRI relaxation-based metric [3], the saturated water fraction (SWF), is thought to reflect water-macromolecule interactions, and may provide complementary microstructural information about neurodegeneration in the SNc. Integrating SWF with FWF and R2* therefore offers a novel opportunity to disentangle multiple, potentially interacting pathological processes in PD. We previously showed that R2* tend to differ between PD subtypes and controls [4], and here we aim to extend this analysis by incorporating SWF and FWF to more comprehensively characterize microstructural alterations in the SNc.

Methods

Quantitative MRI data were obtained for 108 PD and HC at The Hebrew University of Jerusalem [5]. Analyses were restricted to the 64 participants for whom all three maps, R2*, SWF, and FWF, were available. Participants were grouped into idiopathic PD (iPD), GBA1-PD, LRRK2-PD, and HC. Group differences were tested using ANOVA or Kruskal–Wallis tests, with appropriate post-hoc comparisons. Multinomial logistic regression assessed the combined predictive value of imaging parameters.

Results

R2* values differed across groups and emerged as the strongest imaging marker associated with PD subtype. Both iPD and GBA1-PD showed a tendency toward higher R2* relative to HC. In contrast, group comparisons revealed no significant differences in FWF and no robust differences in SWF across any groups. Multinomial logistic regression identified R2* as the only significant predictor of group membership. In both the full model, including R2*, SWF, FWF, and age, and the reduced model (R2* and age), R2* remained significant (p ≈ 0.01–0.04), whereas SWF, FWF, and age were non-significant. Model performance improved after removing SWF and FWF, indicating that these parameters did not contribute additional explanatory value. Age did not confound any of the associations.

Conclusion

In this cohort, R2* emerged as the most informative qMRI marker for distinguishing PD subtypes from HC, whereas SWF and FWF did not show group-level differences or additive predictive value. These findings suggest that iron-related alterations in the SNc remain the dominant detectable microstructural feature across PD subtypes within the current sample. While SWF and FWF may capture complementary biological processes, their sensitivity to subtype-specific pathology appears limited under the present acquisition and sample characteristics. Future studies with larger cohorts may clarify whether SWF and FWF-derived measures capture more subtle or stage-dependent aspects of nigral degeneration in PD.
