Introduction

Etiological mechanisms underlying suicidal risk trajectories in bipolar disorder (BD) were characterized by considerable heterogeneity in clinical, brain function and genetic manifestation that limits precision diagnosis and interventions. Genetic predisposition modified brain functional dysconnectivity heterogeneously across individuals even before suicidal thought and behavior (STB) are expressed in BD1 , suggesting latent trajectories of risk2. To date, trajectories of patients progressing from no STB to STB have been captured in depression and first episode psychosis3,4, characterized by clinical symptoms5. However, trajectory-specific gene-brain-behavior links in pathophysiological progression of suicide risk in BD, including genetic variations, transcriptional abnormalities, intermediate phenotypes captured by neuroimage, downstream cognitive processes, clinical symptoms and therapeutic response is currently scarce. Clinically, it would accelerate the development of pathway-informed biomarkers for early risk stratification, treatment-response prediction, and mechanism-based personalized interventions.

Methods

Totally, 802 subjects were recruited in cross-sectional and longitudinal study. In a cross-sectional resting-state fMRI study (n = 760), BD patients were grouped by the presence of suicide thoughts and behaviors (STB) across discovery (103health controls \ 265BD-STBs \ 161BD-nSTBs) and two replication cohorts (for site-effect validation: 82BD-STBs \ 46BD-nSTBs; for scanner-effect validation: 58BD-STBs \ 45BD-nSTBs). An AI-based semi-supervised clustering-generative adversarial network modeled virtual trajectories of functional dysconnectivity (Figure 1B), characterized by clinical symptoms and cognition performance. Trajectory-specific molecular fingerprints were queried against public cortical microarchitectural maps including Allen Human Brain Atlas and 5 distinct neurotransmitter systems (Figure 1C). For genetic validation, they then were orthogonally validated by estimates of gene risk scores from 61 potential suicide-related SNPs from 121 discovery patients (Figure 1D). Building on trajectory-specific susceptibility signals identified by targeted sequencing, we subsequently examined whether these loci further moderated 2-week response to pharmacotherapy and/or rTMS (Figure 1E). Moreover, reproducibility of trajectory-specific gene-brain-behavior fingerprint were re-analyzed independently in two replication cohorts (Figure 1F). Furthermore, in-person longitudinal follow-up was conducted in 42 BD patients at 6-12 months post-baseline, acquiring baseline and follow-up MRI alongside suicide-risk and cognitive assessments to test whether trajectory-linked connectivity followed clinical risk fluctuations (Figure 1G). 

Results

Whereafter, two generative suicidal risk trajectories emerged. The visual-cortex-predominant trajectory showed hyperconnectivity with poorer working memory and cognitive flexibility (adjusted p<.05); visual dysconnectivity positively mediated genetic risks of rs1631327, rs6320 (HTR5A), and rs8066602 (SLC6A4) to STB (portion of mediated = 11.4%, β = 1.94e-2, p<.05, 95% CI = [1.08e-03, 0.05]; Figure 2A). The DMN-CEN-predominant trajectory centered on default mode-executive circuitry with excessive rumination; therapeutically, rs8066602 TC/TT carriers with DMN-CEN-STB showed greater 2-week response than nSTB (β = 31.01, SE = 7.18, p<.01; Figure 2C-D). Trajectory maps reproduced across two independent cohorts (Visual: replication-1 / replication-2: r = 0.80/0.65; DMN-CEN: replication-1 / replication-2: r = 0.64/0.73, adjusted pspin<.001). Longitudinally, trajectory-specific connectivity could track suicidal risk fluctuation, deteriorating with pathological progression and normalizing with recovery (Figure 2E).

Conclusion

Overall, suicide risk in BD is organized along two reproducible trajectories with distinct gene-brain-behavior couplings. Visual cortex-predominant trajectory involved heightened visual cortex hyperconnectivity associated with HTR5A (rs1631327, rs6320) and SLC6A4 (rs8066602) risk, potentially biasing attention towards negative stimuli and impairing cognitive flexibility, thereby increasing suicide risk whereas the absence of trajectory-by-genotype moderation for these loci argued for development of visual circuit-targeted therapeutic strategies. In contrast, DMN-CEN-predominant trajectory exhibited DMN-CEN hyperconnectivity, aligned with brooding rumination and difficulty disengaging from negative thoughts and emotions, further contributing to suicide tendency; rs8066602 TC/TT carriers with suicide thoughts and behaviors showed greater 2-week response to pharmacotherapy and/or rTMS than non-STB patients, highlighting trajectory-specific sensitivity. Notably, trajectory-specific connectivity could track suicidal risk fluctuation longitudinally, deteriorating with pathological progression and normalizing with recovery. Together, these findings support circuit-based, serotonergic mechanisms of suicidality and motivate genotype- and trajectory-informed stratification and intervention in bipolar depression.
