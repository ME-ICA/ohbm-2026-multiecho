Introduction

Children born preterm (< 37 weeks gestational age) are at elevated risk for a wide range of neurodevelopmental challenges, including cognitive, motor, and behavioral difficulties, as well as increased incidence of attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism1. Although lower gestational age is associated with greater risk, gestational age alone does not account for the substantial variability in developmental outcomes observed within the preterm population2. Preterm birth is also associated with alterations in white matter structure from infancy through adulthood, and some of these alterations have been linked to cognitive and behavioral difficulties3. However, these relationships remain poorly defined, particularly in early childhood. Identifying early neuroimaging markers of neurodevelopmental risk has the potential to improve prediction of later outcomes and inform targeted intervention strategies. Establishing such markers requires linking imaging-derived measures of white matter organization to complex behavioral phenotypes.

In this study, we examined behavioral variability in children born preterm and investigated how data-driven behavioral dimensions relate to measures of white matter micro- and macrostructure.

Methods

Here, we collected diffusion MRI data at the Alberta Children’s Hospital from preterm (N=59; < 37 weeks gestational age; 33 females) children aged 4-8 years (mean age 5.34 ± 0.89 years) recruited from the Calgary area. Measures of white matter micro- and macrostructure were derived using the MRtrix3 Fixel-Based Analysis (FBA) pipeline4. Multi-shell, multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution was used to estimate fiber orientation distributions (FODs). A study-specific FOD template was generated from 20 low-motion scans and all participant FODs were registered to this template and segmented into fixels. Template fixels were used to map individual fixels, from which participant fiber density (FD) and fiber cross-section (FC) were computed. Whole-brain fixel-wise analyses were conducted, and we also applied fixel masks for the corpus callosum, corticospinal tracts, inferior longitudinal fasciculi, and inferior fronto-occipital fasciculi to extract tract-specific metrics.

We also collected behavioral data using self-report questionnaires filled by parents or guardians, including the Autism Quotient (AQ) scale to evaluate autism traits, the SNAP-IV 26 scale to evaluate ADHD-like traits, and the Child Emotion Expressivity and Awareness Measure (CAM) and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS-NA) scale to evaluate emotional dysregulation. We used feature reduction with principal component analysis to identify distinct patterns of behavioral variation across the cohort. We then assessed associations between principal component scores and fiber density and fiber cross-section using generalized linear models, controlling for sex, age at scan, gestational age, birthweight, intracranial volume, head motion in the MRI scanner, and handedness. Statistical significance was assessed using non-parametric permutation testing with connectivity-based fixel enhancement, producing FWE-corrected p-values at the fixel level.

Results

In the preterm cohort, principal component analysis identified two dominant dimensions that explained 38% and 23.2% of the variance, respectively. The first component (PC1) primarily reflected autism-related traits, with strong loadings from AQ subscales (e.g., social skills, attention switching, communication). The second component (PC2) captured ADHD-related variability, driven by SNAP inattention and hyperactivity/impulsivity scores. This indicates that, among preterm children, autism-like and ADHD-like behavioral features form separable dimensions.

In the generalized models, we observed robust associations between behavioral dimensions and white matter macrostructure. Higher PC1 scores (i.e., greater autism-like traits) were linked to significantly reduced fiber cross-section within the splenium of the corpus callosum (161 significant fixels FWE-corrected p < 0.05), indicating that children exhibiting stronger autism-related behaviors tended to show reduced fiber cross-section in these areas. For PC2 (i.e., greater ADHD-like traits) we identified widespread negative effects on fiber cross-section throughout some of the genu and much of the splenium of the corpus callosum (2790 significant fixels), indicating reduced fiber cross-section in this region among children with elevated ADHD-related behaviors. We found no effects of PC1 or PC2 scores with white matter fiber density in all analyzed tracts.

Conclusion

Overall, our findings demonstrate that distinct dimensions of behavioral variability in children born preterm – specifically, autism-like and ADHD-like traits – are differentially associated with patterns of corpus callosum macrostructure. Autism-like traits were primarily linked to reduced fiber cross-section in corpus callosum splenium, while ADHD-like traits showed strong associations with reduced fiber cross-section in both genu and splenium of the corpus callosum. Reduced fiber cross-section here may reflect altered developmental growth or reduced axonal bundle size, which could disrupt efficient interhemispheric communication that supports social and communication-related functions. These intriguing findings suggest that white matter properties of the corpus callosum could be used to predict neurobehavioral risk in preterm born children.
