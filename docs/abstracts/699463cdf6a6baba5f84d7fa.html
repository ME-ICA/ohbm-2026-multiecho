Introduction

Neurocognitive disorders (NCD), with a prevalence roughly doubling every 20 years, are a growing concern. The two main forms of NCD are Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), accounting for 50-65 % and 10-20 % of cases respectively.1 Despite their distinct pathophysiologies, differential diagnosis of DLB and AD is challenging – due to common symptoms, confounding effects of disease progression, and considerable inter-patient variability in DLB. As a consequence –and despite recent advances–, accuracy for differential diagnosis remains limited around 80%.2

Electroencephalography (EEG), being non-invasive and relatively inexpensive, is a promising tool to develop diagnostic biomarkers of pathophysiological alterations, related to neurotransmitter systems or the global excitation/inhibition balance. Periodic components of the EEG signal have long been the focus of the literature ; in DLB notably, the increased temporal variability of the dominant frequency and of delta power is thought to reflect extensive cholinergic deficits.3 Aperiodic components have recently been shown to differ between DLB and AD, but their full topographical and intra-individual temporal variability have not been analysed yet.4 As these aspects may prove valuable for differential diagnosis, we assessed them during resting-state in a small cohort of healthy controls (HC), AD and DLB patients.

Methods

Our cohort consisted of 12 HC, 12 AD and 16 DLB participants. The latest criteria were used for diagnosis of both AD and DLB, including cerebrospinal fluid biomarkers for AD (A+T+ signature); we also excluded cases of mixed pathology (A+T+ as exclusion criteria for DLB).

EEG were recorded during a 5-minute eyes-closed resting state, with 32 Ag/AgCl electrodes in saline solution, positioned according to the standard 10-20 positioning system. Data was acquired at 500 or 1000 Hz, and resampled at 500 Hz when necessary. Pre-processing with MNE-Python was fully automated, including bad channel detection with PyPrep for later interpolation and ICA and AutoReject for correction or removal of artefacts. Signal was filtered between 1 and 48 Hz, segmented into 4-s epochs, and re-referenced with REST (Reference Electrode Standardization Technique).

Power spectral density (PSD) was then calculated for each subject, channel and epoch; we relied on the multitaper method, ensuring minimal spectral leakage and stable estimates. The Fitting Oscillations and One Over Frequency (FOOOF) algorithm was used to separate periodic from aperiodic components, and estimate aperiodic parameters (exponent and offset)5; it was either applied to individual epoch PSDs or to their mean across epochs, for each subject and channel. Temporal variability per channel was measured as the difference between minimum and maximum values across epochs, after robust standardization.

Results

HC, AD and DLB groups did not differ for age or percentage of women; AD and DLB had lower cognitive scores than HC, but no difference was found between AD (MMSE: 24.4) and DLB ( MMSE: 23.2).

Topography of mean aperiodic components showed a midline predominance for all groups. We observed significant group differences for mean aperiodic components : DLB had higher offset and exponent values than controls and AD patients, but no differences were found between AD and controls ; significant clusters were larger for offset than for exponent differences. Additionally, DLB had a higher temporal variability of the aperiodic exponent than AD patients.

Conclusion

Results in our small cohort support the idea that aperiodic components during resting state differ between DLB and AD or controls, but that spectral differences between AD and controls may be more periodic than aperiodic. Given the extended (offset) or localized (exponent) topography of these differences, relaxing assumptions regarding their expected localization might help find efficient diagnostic biomarkers. 

To conclude, resting-state EEG markers of spatial and temporal variability may prove useful to distinguish between AD and DLB. Future work extending the cohort to a larger number of patients, and comparing EEG recordings during resting- versus active-state, will allow us to test and potentially increase the diagnostic accuracy and robustness of AD and DLB.
