Introduction

While Braak staging proposes that tau pathology progresses from transentorhinal to limbic and neocortical regions in Alzheimer’s disease, accumulating evidence points to substantial heterogeneity in propagation patterns and early involvement of subcortical and brainstem regions. In particular, brainstem to medial temporal lobe (MTL) pathways may constitute vulnerable routes for connectivity-mediated tau spread and consequent neural circuit dysfunction. We tested whether multimodal MRI detects microstructural and functional alterations along a brainstem–MTL axis in the Alzheimer’s disease spectrum (ADS), and whether these alterations are reflected in resting-state intrinsic activity and brainstem–hippocampal functional coupling.

Methods

Participants were drawn from the longitudinal ActiGliA cohort: amyloid-defined ADS (n=36) and cognitively healthy controls (HC, n=20). ADS participants were amyloid-positive by CSF Aβ42/Aβ40 and/or amyloid-PET; HC had CDR=0 and were amyloid-negative. All participants underwent 3T structural MRI, resting-state fMRI, and multi-shell diffusion-weighted MRI (dwMRI); a subset also completed amyloid-, tau-, and TSPO-PET. Resting-state fMRI was preprocessed with fMRIPrep and xcp-d. Regional intrinsic activity and local synchrony were quantified using ALFF/fALFF and ReHo. ROI-to-ROI functional connectivity was computed (Fisher z–transformed correlations). dwMRI was processed with QSIPrep followed by QSIRecon to derive diffusion tensor metrics (FA, MD, AD, RD) and NODDI-derived microstructural indices. Whole-brain tractography was used to generate streamline-based structural connectivity. Analyses used an extended Schaefer cortical parcellation with subcortical regions (4S256) combined with brainstem nuclei ROIs. Between-group differences were summarized using effect sizes (Cohen’s d) and p values for key ROI and connection-level comparisons.

Results

Compared with HC, ADS showed microstructural abnormalities consistent with increased diffusivity/free water in brainstem and MTL ROIs. Effects were prominent in the right locus coeruleus (MD/RD/AD: d≈0.69–0.70; ISOVF: d≈0.71) and the left amygdala (MD/RD/AD: d≈0.59–0.64; ISOVF: d≈0.67). Resting-state intrinsic activity (ALFF) was elevated in ADS within MTL and cholinergic/brainstem regions, including bilateral amygdala (d≈0.60–0.72), hippocampus (d≈0.45–0.51), nucleus basalis of Meynert (d≈0.51–0.57), and nucleus tractus solitarius (d≈0.61–0.70). Functional connectivity was selectively reduced along hippocampal–brainstem links, including right hippocampus–right locus coeruleus (p=0.004), right hippocampus–left pedunculotegmental nucleus (p=0.009), and bilateral hippocampus–right pontis oralis (p=0.022 and p=0.034). Connectivity between left hippocampus–left nucleus tractus solitarius was not significant (p=0.034). Streamline-based structural connectivity did not differ significantly between groups across the examined brainstem–MTL connections.

Conclusion

Atlas-based multimodal MRI detected abnormalities along a brainstem-MTL axis in amyloid-defined ADS, characterized by microstructural compromise (increased diffusivity/free-water–like signal) and elevated intrinsic resting-state activity in key MTL and brainstem nuclei. These changes were accompanied by selective hippocampal–brainstem functional decoupling despite largely preserved streamline-based structural connectivity, suggesting that early brainstem involvement and network dysfunction may precede tract disruptions. Further work will link brainstem–MTL dysconnectivity to tau deposition and clinical trajectories.
