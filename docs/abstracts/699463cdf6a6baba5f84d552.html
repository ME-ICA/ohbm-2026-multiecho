Introduction

The locus coeruleus (LC) is a small subcortical nucleus located in the superolateral pons, representing the largest noradrenergic cell group in the brainstem. The LC-noradrenergic system is critical for regulating attention, arousal, and its dysregulation is implicated in stress-related disorders like depression and anxiety1. Neuroimaging research of LC primarily focuses on Alzheimer's disease (AD) and Parkinson's disease (PD), where reduced LC neuron counts and neuromelanin signal intensity are well-documented in AD or PD patients. This cellular loss and abnormality in noradrenergic system may explain common behavioral and psychological symptoms2. Furthermore, activation or functional connectivity of the locus coeruleus may be associated with alterations in cognitive function3, while the comprehensive structural connectivity (SC) profile in the general population remains underexplored.

Current knowledge of LC genetics relies primarily on rodent transcriptomics, which identified genes (such as Slc18a2 and Slc6a4) governing neuronal identity and neurotransmitter synthesis4. To date, no large-scale genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have been conducted to identify common variants influencing human LC phenotypes. This study aims to bridge this gap by integrating multimodal data from the large-scale UK Biobank cohort. Specifically, we sought to: (1) map the structural connectivity profile of the LC using high-resolution diffusion MRI data; (2) investigate the associations between LC-SC and specific cognitive domains and emotional traits; and (3) elucidate the genetic basis of LC-SC through GWAS and functional enrichment analyses. By systematically characterizing the genomic architecture and cognitive-emotional associations of LC connectivity, this study provides novel insights into the biological mechanisms linking noradrenergic projections to complex human behaviors and potential neuropsychiatric risks.

Methods

Participants and imaging processing: We analyzed MRI data from 36,772 participants in the UK Biobank. We used the official imaging pipeline outputs, where diffusion MRI data underwent rigorous preprocessing using FSL's Eddy tool for eddy current, head motion, and outlier slice correction, followed by gradient distortion correction to produce the corrected 4D data.

Locus coeruleus definition: The locus coeruleus (LC) was localized using the probabilistic LC meta-mask established by Dahl et al.5, which aggregates published maps to yield a high-confidence consensus ROI. For each participant, the standard-space mask was transformed into native diffusion space using high-precision non-linear registration to ensure anatomical accuracy.

Structural connectivity analysis: We performed probabilistic tractography using FSL software and UKB-provided BEDPOSTx outputs. Streamlines were seeded from the native LC mask with high-density sampling (5,000 samples per voxel). To accommodate the complex anatomy of the brainstem, we utilized a step length of 0.5 mm and a curvature threshold of 0.2, applying distance correction to mitigate biases. To verify robustness, we employed a secondary MRtrix3 pipeline using constrained spherical deconvolution and streamline filtering to generate 200,000 biologically plausible streamlines. Connectivity matrices were subsequently constructed by mapping these streamlines to targets defined by the Desikan-Killiany and Tian Subcortex atlases.

Genetic Association and Functional Annotation: Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) were performed on quality-controlled UK Biobank data using generalized linear/mixed models (implemented in PLINK 2.0 and SAIGE-GENE+) to identify variants associated with LC structural connectivity. To elucidate the biological mechanisms of identified loci, we conducted cross-omics functional mapping and annotation using the FUMA platform. We integrated GTEx brain tissue expression quantitative trait loci and Allen Brain Atlas single-cell transcriptomics to perform tissue specificity and pathway enrichment analyses, systematically characterizing the cellular expression profiles and biological pathways of candidate genes underlying LC connectivity.

Results

Population-level structural connectivity of locus coeruleus: Probabilistic tractography was performed to map the structural connectivity profile of the LC. Figure 1a and 2a display the population-averaged streamline density map derived from 36,772 UK Biobank participants. The results demonstrate that LC projections primarily ascend along the brainstem and extend rostrally, showing dense projections towards the medial temporal lobe (including amygdala and hippocampus). These findings confirm robust structural connectivity between the LC and regions critical for emotional regulation and memory processing, providing an anatomical basis for investigating their behavioral relevance.

Associations with cognitive and Emotional Phenotypes: We examined the associations between LC structural connectivity (LC-SC) and behavioral phenotypes across 116 regions of interest using AAL2 atlas. Generalized linear models were fitted controlling for age, sex, ethnicity, Townsend deprivation index, education, and scanning site. For the left LC, we observed a significant positive association between connectivity to the left hippocampus and memory performance (t = 6.53, r = 0.039, p = 1.26 × 10-9). Beyond this core link, the Sankey diagram revealed that memory function was also supported by connectivity to supplementary motor areas and paracentral lobules. Conversely, connectivity to the right amygdala showed a significant negative association with depressive symptoms (t = -5.97, r = -0.039, p = 3.90 × 10-8). This affective network extended to other limbic and subcortical nodes, including the caudate nucleus and inferior/middle temporal gyri. However, the association between left LC connectivity to the thalamus, a key hub for sleep regulation, and insomnia symptoms was less robust and notably weaker (t = -3.40, r = -0.018, p = 0.0055).

For the right LC, connectivity to the right hippocampus was similarly positively associated with memory performance (t = 4.25, r = 0.025, p = 9.42 × 10-5). Broader network visualization linked the right caudate and frontal-occipital regions to cognitive domains. While specific links to the amygdala or thalamus did not survive FDR correction, there is a connection between the precentral gyrus and mental health indicators.

Genomic architecture of LC structural connectivity: We conducted a GWAS of LC-SC strength across target brain regions among UK Biobank participants. We identified 16 significant genes linked to LC connectivity. Notably, MRVI1 gene is reported here as a novel association with noradrenergic circuitry, which was not previously connected to LC structural networks. Functional annotation categorized these genes into distinct biological domains. Many are involved in neural plasticity and synaptic remodeling, such as RBFOX2, DNAJC1, and MLLT10. Several genes are associated with relevant behavioral phenotypes, including mental health (e.g., GZMM associated with depression; OSTF1 associated with loneliness) and cognitive function (e.g., PI4K2B and LPRC40). Furthermore, tissue-wide expression analysis using the GTEx dataset confirmed that these LC-connectivity-related genes exhibit significant differential expression specifically in brain tissues (e.g., cerebellum and cortex), validating their neurobiological relevance.

Conclusion

By leveraging diffusion MRI and genomic data from 36,772 participants, we successfully mapped the macroscopic architecture of noradrenergic projections and validated their behavioral relevance. Our findings reveal a lateralized functional organization: left LC connectivity primarily underpins the memory-emotion interface via the hippocampus and amygdala, while the right LC exhibits distinct, localized associations with executive and mental health domains.

Crucially, our genomic analyses unravel the molecular basis of these circuits. We identified 16 significant loci, including novel noradrenergic-associated gene (e.g., MRVI1), cognitive-linked variants (e.g., PI4K2B), and genes implicated in mental health (e.g., GZMM and OSTF1). Functional mapping indicates that these genes are not randomly distributed but are significantly enriched in synaptic plasticity and brain-specific expression pathways, suggesting they play a foundational role in maintaining the structural integrity of LC projections.

Collectively, these findings bridge the gap between genetic variation, neural circuit integrity, and complex behavioral phenotypes. By establishing the "Gene-Circuit-Behavior" axis of the LC, this study offers novel biological targets for understanding noradrenergic dysfunction in neuropsychiatric disorders, potentially paving the way for genetically informed biomarkers and precision therapeutics.
