Introduction

Neural computations emerge from interactions among distributed brain regions, yet linking large-scale connectivity to the formation of neural representations remains a central challenge. We address this gap using empirical neural network (ENN) models, parametrized by functional connectivity (FC) and operationalized with activity flow modeling (ActFlow). These models generate task-evoked signals as the propagation of activity over a directed computational graph1. Here we use ActFlow with fMRI in a time-resolved, V1-initiated model of the early visual cortex2, leveraging the fact that the model predicts full time series in V2–V4. This enables a flow-graph analysis at the single timepoint level, where network interactions can be quantified when sources are simultaneously active rather than relying on time-averaged summaries. 

We focus on the visual system because it is close to sensory input and offers a stimulus-anchored representational coordinate system: visual cortex is organized retinotopically, allowing representational dimensionality to be defined in relation to stimulus-space structure. We focus on collider motifs—targets receiving concurrent input from multiple sources—drawing inspiration from colliders in directed graphs used in causal reasoning3. While FC-based flow graphs do not provide strict causal identification, collider-like structures offer an interpretable measure of convergent integration in directed flow networks. We hypothesize that a parcel’s prevalence of collider-like convergence relates to the dimensionality of its representational geometry, and that this relationship changes as a function of how neural activity is routed via hierarchical versus direct pathways. Specifically (Fig. 1), we predict that collider index (CI)—positive incoming flow mass during peak target activations—positively tracks representational dimensionality across the early visual cortex, with stronger coupling under hierarchical than direct pathways.

Methods

Data. We analyzed 7T retinotopy and resting-state fMRI data from the Young Adult HCP release. From the available cohort (N=181), we randomly selected 40 participants (20 female). Data were minimally preprocessed and denoised with ICA-FIX. Retinotopy comprised six runs (TR=1 s; 1.6-mm isotropic) and resting-state comprised four runs with matched acquisition.

FC estimation. We estimated resting-state FC at the vertex level using a graph-constrained principal component regression pipeline. First, we estimated parcel-level sparse partial correlations using graphical lasso4 to reduce confounded connections and improve reliability. Parcel pairs with no edge at this level were used to zero-out the corresponding vertex-to-vertex connections. Second, for each target vertex we fit principal component regression on the surviving source-vertex time series, selecting the number of PCs via cross-validation on held-out resting-state data and then mapping weights back to vertex space.

V1-initiated activity flow. Using each subject’s FC, we implemented a multi-step, V1-initiated ENN model that predicts full time series in V2–V4 at the vertex level. Step 1 predicted V2–V4 from V1 sources. Subsequent steps predicted V2–V4 from the previous step’s predicted V2–V4 activity while clamping V1 to its empirical time series until the explained variance of the system plateaued (R2 increase between steps averaged across parcels < 1%, 6 steps in total).

In silico lesions. To simulate propagation toward hierarchical versus direct pathways, we applied lesions to FC edges: direct pathways lesioned V2→V3 and V3→V4 connections; hierarchical pathways lesioned V1→V3 and V1→V4 connections (including contralateral homologs). The V2→V4 edge was retained to keep lesion counts comparable across models. Each lesioned model was run for six steps.

Representational dimensionality. Parcel-level dimensionality was quantified as the participation ratio5 of parcel-level cross-validated representational similarity matrices (RSMs) based on retinotopic time series. We split each of the retinotopic runs into two identical halves based on the stimuli presented. To create the cross-validated temporal RSMs, for each subject and for each region, we correlated the first halves across all runs with the second halves of all the runs using Pearson’s correlation.

Collider index. For each target vertex, we defined pseudo-trials as the top fraction of timepoints (top 10% in terms of activation) in the empirical target time series after concatenating retinotopic mapping runs in time. At each pseudo-trial, incoming flow from each source vertex was computed as FC weight × source activity. Collider strength was defined as the sum of positive incoming flows and averaged across pseudo-trials, yielding one value per vertex; a scaled version divided by the number of nonzero incoming FC edges (mean positive flow per available source). Vertex-wise values were averaged within each parcel (six parcels: V2/V3/V4 × hemisphere). Within each subject, collider–dimensionality coupling was computed as the correlation across parcels, Fisher z-transformed, and tested against zero at the group level; model comparisons used identical tests with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons.

Results

Time-resolved, V1-initiated ActFlow generated full predicted time series in V2–V4 across six iterative steps, enabling flow-graph analysis at the TR level (mean R2 at the parcel level, averaged across parcels and subjects, 6th step = 41.134%). For each target vertex, we defined pseudo-trials as the top fraction of empirical target activations across all runs (concatenated in time), computed time-resolved incoming flows as FC weight × source activity, and summarized collider-like convergence as collider strength: the sum over positive incoming flows at these peak timepoints (averaged across pseudo-trials). We then aggregated vertex-wise metrics to parcel-level values for six early visual parcels (V2/V3/V4 × hemisphere) and estimated each parcel’s representational dimensionality.

At the parcel level, convergence was non-monotonic across V2–V4 (with V3 showing higher collider index than V2 and V4). This indicates that convergent drive in early visual cortex is not well captured by a simple hierarchical gradient alone, motivating analyses focused on how convergence relates to representational geometry rather than assuming a fixed ordering across areas.

Despite the lack of monotonicity across V2–V4, collider strength showed a robust relationship with representational dimensionality. We quantified coupling by computing, for each subject, the correlation between parcel-wise collider index and parcel-wise dimensionality across the six parcels. Across subjects, these within-model correlations were reliably positive (mean r = 0.14, p = 0.032), indicating that parcels with stronger convergent drive at peak activations tend to exhibit higher-dimensional representational structure.

This CI–dimensionality coupling was routing dependent. Under in-silico lesions designed to bias propagation toward hierarchical versus direct routing, coupling in the hierarchical pathways was significantly positive across subjects (mean r = 0.673, p < 0.0001), whereas coupling in the direct pathways was negative but not significant (mean r = -0.142, p = 0.141). Hierarchical pathways representations produced lower overall dimensionality than direct pathways (paired t-tests p < 0.0001 for all steps except for the first, Bonferroni corrected), yet the within-model CI–dimensionality coupling differed across regimes (hierarchical: significantly positive; direct: negative but not reliable), indicating that convergence–geometry coupling is not explained by a global shift in dimensionality alone. Together, these results show that the relationship between collider-like convergence and representational geometry is not fixed but instead depends on pathway routing constraints in a multi-step, time-resolved flow network.

Conclusion

ENN models provide a flow-graph view of early vision that links empirically estimated connectivity to time series dynamics, extending activity flow logic beyond static activation maps. Within this framework, collider convergence offers an interpretable motif for quantifying when many upstream sources concurrently contribute to a target at functionally relevant time points. 

Collider convergence did not vary monotonically across V2–V4, suggesting that convergence motifs in early visual cortex are not well-described by a simple hierarchical gradient alone. This non-monotonicity is consistent with the idea that regional convergence reflects a combination of retinotopic subfield composition, and pathway routing, rather than a single axis of “later equals more convergence.” Importantly, however, the relationship between collider strength and representational dimensionality was robust: across subjects, parcels with higher collider strength tended to exhibit higher dimensionality, indicating that stronger convergent drive coincides with richer representational degrees of freedom (i.e., higher dimensionality). This pattern suggests that convergent integration can support high-dimensional representational structure—potentially by enabling diverse combinations of upstream inputs at moments when targets are maximally engaged.

Critically, CI-dimensionality coupling was pathway dependent. Under in silico lesions that bias propagation toward hierarchical versus direct routes, coupling in the hierarchical pathways was reliably positive, whereas the direct pathways showed a negative but less reliable relationship. This dissociation indicates that the representational consequences of the same convergence motif depend on the broader routes through which activity propagates across steps. Rather than a fixed mapping from convergence to representational geometry, these findings support a more nuanced view in which pathway topology and multi-step dynamics determine whether convergent drive primarily increases shared variance (compression-like) or supports maintenance/expansion of independent degrees of freedom (expansion-like). 

Several considerations motivate our next steps. First, because collider strength is a magnitude-based convergence measure, it can be decomposed into more diagnostic subcomponents, e.g., whether high collider strength reflects many modest positive contributors versus a few dominant sources, and whether the effective number of contributors or the distribution of incoming flow better predicts dimensionality. In early visual cortex, this distribution can be expressed in stimulus-anchored terms by asking whether dominant incoming flows are retinotopically aligned versus drawn from diverse visual-field coordinates, and whether retinotopic diversity of inputs better explains dimensionality than total incoming flow strength. This would more directly test whether high dimensionality with high convergence arises from diverse mixtures of inputs versus strong but uniform drive. Second, modeling vision in terms of flows also enables a richer motif analysis that goes beyond colliders: chains versus fork motifs could be quantified on the same flow graphs and tested for distinct geometric signatures, turning motifs into a predictive mechanism.

More broadly, this work opens a route to mechanistic tests that connect flow topology to representational geometry with increasing causal precision. Time-resolved ENN can be positioned as a framework for mapping how network dynamics shape representational degrees of freedom, in a stimulus-anchored system where those degrees of freedom are especially interpretable.
