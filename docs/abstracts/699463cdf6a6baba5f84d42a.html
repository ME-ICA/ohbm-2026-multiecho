Introduction

Psychiatric diagnoses are currently based on clinical consensus of co-occurring symptoms, which do not necessarily map onto neurobiology. However, interindividual symptom variation must be reflected in variation of neural activity measures. Previously we have shown that lower-dimensional quantifications of psychosis-spectrum disorder symptom variance yield more robust brain-behavioral associations compared to composite behavioral scores along a-priori diagnostic categories (Ji et al., 2021, Ji et al. 2025), with a summary measure of functional neural connectivity (global brain connectivity, GBC). Here, we extend this framework by evaluating alternative time-series metrics derived from the resting-state BOLD signal (Fulcher et al. 2018) to determine whether diverse temporal properties of the resting-state fMRI dynamics can produce replicable and interpretable neural features in relation to behavioral variation.

Methods

Resting-state fMRI data from a Discovery (BSNIP1, N = 394) and Replication (BSNIP2, N = 298) cohort (Tamminga et al. 2014, NIMH Data Archive Collection ID: 2274 and 2165) were parcellated into 718 cortical and subcortical regions using the Cole-Anticevic Brain Atlas (Ji et al., 2019). Beyond GBC, we quantified temporal dynamics per parcel using metrics inspired by Bryant et al. (2024), who identified interpretable neural metrics relevant to neuropsychiatric disorders. Specifically, we computed embedding distance, autocorrelation timescale, forecast error, periodicity, pairwise entropy, low-frequency power, and spectral centroid, capturing complementary aspects of temporal structure, predictability, and spectral organization of the BOLD signal.

Feature computation followed a workflow based on the Theft framework (Henderson and Fulcher, 2022) for reproducible time-series analysis in R and Python, integrating standardized implementations from catch22, tsfresh, TSFEL, and feasts. Network-level averages were then visualized using raincloud plots and compared across cohorts to assess reproducibility and cross-features covariance. We compared Discovery and Replication results in order to characterize the distributions and quantify the stability and replicability of these neural metrics across a heterogeneous sample.

Results

Figure 1 presents spatial inter-metric comparisons of eight neural dynamics measures across Discovery and Replication. Consistent correlation patterns between metrics are observed across both datasets, indicating reproducibility of their relationships. While GBC shows modest associations with other time-series metrics, the remaining features exhibit high positive or negative correlations, forming a structured interdependence among temporal and spectral properties. Notably, the absolute correlation values are generally higher in Replication, with several nearing ±1, suggesting stronger coupling among metrics in that cohort. GBC shows its highest correlations with Pairs Entropy (r = 0.63). All non-GBC metrics are displayed in their raw (non-z-scored) ranges to highlight their absolute values distributions across cohorts.

Figure 2 illustrates the network-level distributions of eight neural metrics across Discovery and Replication. While GBC and pairwise entropy show unimodal distributions across individuals, several other metrics—including embedding distance, ACF timescale, and forecast error—exhibit bimodal patterns in Discovery, suggesting potential subgroups or covariate influences. Clear differences between Discovery and Replication are also apparent, particularly within Visual, Somatomotor, and Cingulo-Opercular networks. These systematic variations may reflect meaningful distinction in temporal and spectral properties beyond what GBC alone captures.

Conclusion

This comparative analysis highlights that time-series–derived neural metrics can capture reproducible and biologically meaningful features of brain function beyond conventional connectivity measures. These temporal descriptors may provide a richer basis for linking neural dynamics to behavioral phenotypes and multi-omics signatures, supporting the identification of biologically grounded dimensions of psychopathology. Such descriptors have the potential to delineate biologically grounded dimensions of psychopathology, improve data-driven patient stratification, and accelerate the identification of candidate biomarkers and endophenotypes in neuropsychiatric research.
