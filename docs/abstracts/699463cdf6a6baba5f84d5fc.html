Introduction

The first six years of life constitute a critical window for brain development, during which rapid cortical growth establishes the structural foundation for cognitive and behavioral maturation. However, normative developmental charts are derived predominantly from Western cohorts, limiting our understanding of cross-population variation during this critical period. ^1 This raises questions about the generalizability of Western-based developmental charts to non-Western populations and, when cross-population differences are non-trivial, highlights the need for population-specific reference frameworks. Here, for the first time, we examined cortical development at a fine-grained level across 0-6 years of age by comprehensively comparing data from the Chinese Baby Connectome Project (CBCP)^2 and the Baby Connectome Project (BCP).^3 By integrating global volumetric and regional cortical thickness trajectories, we delineate cohort-specific developmental patterns and establish cross-population neurodevelopmental baselines for early childhood.

Methods

We analyzed T1-weighted MRI data from age-matched infants (0-72 months) in the CBCP (N=237; 131 M / 106 F) and BCP (N=260; 163 M / 97 F) cohorts. Statistical comparisons confirmed no significant differences in age or sex distribution between the two cohorts (all p>0.05). Images from both cohorts underwent rigorous and identical preprocessing and cortical surface reconstruction using an infant-dedicated pipeline designed to minimize methodological heterogeneity.^4 Morphological metrics, including cortical thickness (CT) and other morphologic/geometric measures, were extracted using the Desikan-Killiany atlas

For global analyses, six major volumetric metrics—total intracranial volume (ICV), cerebral and cerebellar volumes, total cortical gray matter volume (GMV), cortical white matter volume (WMV), and subcortical GMV—were modeled against age using Generalized Additive Models (GAMs),^5 controlling for sex and site. Sliding-window-based comparisons using permutation tests with False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction (α=0.05) were carried out to identify age windows with significant population differences. 

For regional analysis, we characterized developmental patterns by calculating regional CT peak ages from GAM trajectories and comparing these between populations. To robustly identify peaks and mitigate the influence of fitting noise, we automatically located the time points with the maximum fitted value where the first derivative of the curve approached zero. Peak ages were then compared between cohorts using FDR correction. For surface-based analyses, all infant surfaces were resampled to a common space (the fsaverage5 template). Independent two-sample t-tests were performed at each vertex across seven target age periods spannilang from 0 to 72 months old, followed by similar FDR correction.

Results

We identified population-specific anatomical developmental trajectories at varied scales, locations, and ages during the first six years of life.

First, in global volumetric measures, the BCP cohort generally exhibited larger absolute values across major metrics (e.g., ICV and WMV) compared to the CBCP cohort (Fig. 1a). Sliding-window permutation tests localized significant divergences of these global metrics to metric-dependent age windows, ranging from early (e.g., 0-30 months) to later stages (e.g., 50-70 months).

Second, regional CT peak ages revealed divergent cortical maturation timing patterns between populations (Fig. 1b). Although both cohorts showed the typical sensorimotor-to-association cortex maturation gradient, CBCP infants demonstrated extensively earlier peaking age compared with BCP infants. Statistical analysis confirmed widespread group differences spanning extensive areas of the frontal, parietal, and temporal lobes, with particularly pronounced divergence in high-order association cortices such as the precuneus, fusiform gyrus, and pars opercularis.

Finally, vertex-wise comparisons revealed significant region-specific differences (Fig. 1c) that contrasted with global volumetric results. CBCP infants exhibited significantly greater CT than BCP infants, primarily in frontal, parietal, and temporal association cortices around 15 months old. Although these differences attenuated over time, they persisted in several high-order function-related brain regions at later ages. Together, these findings suggest that population-level differences are metric-dependent, reflected as greater global volumetric expansion in Western infants and regionally distinct cortical thickening patterns in Chinese infants.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates population-specific cortical developmental trajectories across the first six years of life. Leveraging multi-scale analyses—ranging from global volumetric measures to regional CT and vertex-level morphometry—we comprehensively characterized infant structural brain development and identified distinct population-specific patterns. The developmental divergence observed during infancy, followed by progressive convergence, provides a foundation for future studies to investigate the potential shaping influences of dynamic gene-environment-culture interactions in early brain maturation. These findings underscore the need for population-specific developmental references and caution against the uncritical application of Western-derived normative standards to diverse populations in early childhood.
