Introduction

The advent of multilayer networks analysis has recently complemented our methodological armamentarium for the representation of brain connectivity networks. These geometrical tools allow to capture multiple types of interactions and interdependent brain subsystems across modalities/frequencies/etc. Here we propose a novel hyperbolic embedding framework promoting multilayer network analysis and its application to structural networks in epilepsy. Embedding techniques permit to simultaneously map nodes into a commun lower-dimensional geometric space.

Methods

We compute heterogeneous node sets and inter-layer connections, producing layer-specific hyperbolic embeddings yielding high granularity for intra-layer analysis and cross-layer comparisons, without compromising the global multilayer structure within hyperbolic space. In this sense, our method exceeds existing approaches, which commonly embed layers independently.

Results

Through experiments on synthetic multilayer stochastic block models, we show that our framework effectively maintains community structure even when layers contain different sets of nodes. We then applied our methodology to pathological wiring in the case of temporal lobe epilepsy. We were able to successfully cluster disease-related nodes across in left temporal lobe epilepsy patients as compared to control participants, using each patient as an independent layer within the multilayer approach. These cluster clearly distinguished the left temporal lobe from the control group, where layer-independent methods failed to do so, thus corroborating its suitability for comparative analysis.

Conclusion

Our work leverages a novel hyperbolic embedding framework for multilayer networks analysis, which has to potential to improve our understanding of structure and behavior of brain networks.
