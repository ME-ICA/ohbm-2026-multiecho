Introduction

Research shows a key role of the innate immune system and chronic inflammation in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer’s Disease (AD),1 including an impact of variants of TREM2 (triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2).2 In adults with AD, TREM2 variant R47H has been associated with microglia abnormalities and higher neuritic plaque burden,3 increased tau and pTau-181 levels, more impaired cognition, faster cognitive decline, and a shorter time between AD diagnosis and death.4 The few studies that have assessed the impact of the R47H variant on brain MRI measures have found both increased4 and decreased5 subcortical grey matter volumes in R47H carriers. 

TREM2 is a potential therapeutic target for AD, and a thorough understanding of the impact of specific TREM2 variants on AD biomarkers and symptoms is critical. This analysis compares MRI measures of volume between elderly persons with and without the R47H TREM2 variant, with the goal of assessing TREM2-related differences across the AD continuum. 

Methods

Fifteen participants with the TREM2 variant R47H (VAR) and fifteen age, sex, and diagnosis-matched controls (NVAR) were identified from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) database (adni.loni.usc.edu). Participants were cognitively normal (CN) or diagnosed with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) or AD (Figure 1).

Each participant had a T1-weighted anatomical MPRAGE and T2-weighted FLAIR taken at 3T. The MPRAGE was segmented using the Desikan-Killiany atlas in Freesurfer 7.4.1 (https://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/) to obtain measures of regional white and grey matter volumes (corrected for intracranial volume). The MPRAGE and FLAIR images were used to obtain T2 white matter lesion volumes (T2LV). 

Measures of cognitive function included scores on the Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE), Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test immediate recall (RAVLT-R) and learning (RAVLT-L), and Digit Symbol Substitution (DSS). 

Linear regression was used to compare MRI measures by group (VAR, NVAR) and included terms for age and sex. The Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied to the p-values for the full model across all MRI measures. For those models surviving FDR correction, a region was considered significant if the effect of group (TREM2 variant) reached the p < 0.05 level. For MRI measures that showed group differences, age- and sex-corrected residuals were used to perform posthoc analysis of differences by variant group. Diagnosis was not included in the regression model due to the small sample size, but ANOVAs were used to compare residualized MRI measures by diagnosis for each group. 

Results

Figure 1 shows that the VAR and NVAR groups were well-matched on demographic variables. Eight of the 15 VAR participants had cognitive measures, and their performance did not differ from their NVAR matches.  Group differences: The groups did not differ on T2LV (p < 0.841). For grey matter volumes, the VAR group showed larger volumes of the left hippocampus (p < 0.0079), left amygdala (p < 0.0010), left pars triangularis (p < 0.015), left postcentral gyrus (p <0.034), and right subcentral gyrus/sulcus (p < 0.036) compared to the NVAR group. The VAR group also showed larger volumes of the white matter underlying the left pars triangularis (p < 0.036), left rostral middle frontal gyrus (p < 0.031), and left inferior parietal lobule (p < 0.001). Figure 2 shows volume measures that were significant for the effect for group, represented by age- and sex-corrected residuals and plotted by group and diagnosis. None of the measures reached significance for diagnosis in either group.

Conclusion

Our results are in line with those of Dijkstra et al, finding increased volumes of the amygdala and hippocampus in R47H carriers with AD. Moreover, these differences were also seen in cognitively intact R47H carriers, suggesting that variant-related differences include regionally larger brain volumes prior to disease onset. It is possible that the larger volumes seen in R47H carriers with AD result from a combination of larger initial volumes and the influence of factors other than neurodegeneration, such as inflammation, leading to rapid functional decline. Due to the rarity of TREM2 mutations, analyses of specific TREM2 variants typically include small sample sizes. The independent replication of the findings from Dijkstra et al. is therefore a significant step forward in the field, although it requires additional clarification using longitudinal data.
