Introduction

Postpartum depression (PPD) is a highly prevalent postpartum complication with longterm consequences for mother-infant bonding as well as infants‘ motor and emotional development (Slomian et al., 2019). In major depressive disorder (MDD), altered neuroplasticity - particularly hippocampal volume loss - has been consistently associated with poorer treatment outcomes (Schmaal et al., 2020). In PPD, findings on brain plasticity show contrasting results and are largely derived from small subclinical samples (Ballesteros et al., 2025), highlighting the need for replication in larger studies to identify plasticity mechanisms that are potentially distinct from those observed in MDD. 

Methods

In this observational study, we analyzed T1-weighted MRI scans and psychometric data from mothers diagnosed with PPD, admitted for in-patient treatment to a psychiatric parent-baby unit. Separate logistic and linear regression models were fitted for each right and left hippocampal volume as predictor of treatment outcome defined as treatment response (≥50 % reduction in MADRS) and overall symptom reduction (percentage change in MADRS from admission to discharge). In a second step, we additionally examined how depressive symptoms are modulated by bonding quality and breastfeeding status. 

Results

The final sample included 32 participants (M=31.83years, SD±4.87) with moderate to severe postpartum depression (MADRS: M=29.87, SD±8.88). Hippocampal volume was significantly associated with treatment response and overall symptom reduction. Breastfeeding status predicted depressive symptom severity. Depressive symptom severity was negatively associated with mother-infant bonding quality. 

Conclusion

This study builds on our previous findings, which linked reduced hippocampal volume to greater symptom reduction, by replicating these results in a larger sample of clinically affected mothers and demonstrating that these associations also extend to treatment response at discharge. These observed patterns differ from those typically reported in MDD, further supporting the notion of distinct neuroplastic processes in PPD.
