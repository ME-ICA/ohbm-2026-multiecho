Introduction

Mood disorders are among the most prevalent psychiatric conditions, yet the heterogeneity in patients' phenotype often leads to late diagnosis and treatment-resistance. Some patients experience severe forms characterized by increased risk in suicidal behaviors, early onset in childhood and adolescence, marked early cognitive deficits, and multiple comorbidities, reflecting deviations from typical developmental trajectories1. Notably, research has identified that such individuals often display atypical brain sulcation patterns2, features established prenatally and stable throughout life3, which point to fetal neurodevelopmental anomalies. This finding supports the hypothesis that the most severe mood disorders are determined by perinatal factors and deviation in perinatal brain maturation. We hypothesize that specific perinatal factor profiles predict early deviations in normative development, manifesting as the earliest emergence of psychopathology (e.g., early-onset depressive symptoms), predictive of the most severe mood disorder subtype. Exploring the multimodal nature of these perinatal determinants, including genetic risks, early environmental pressure such as gestational and obstetric complications, and their respective impact on perinatal brain maturation, requires large-scale datasets and thoroughly phenotyped children. Hence, we leveraged longitudinal data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study (11 897 children), aiming at identifying and modeling the complex interplay of perinatal determinants that predict distinct psychopathological trajectories during adolescence. 

Methods

Participants

This study used the data from the ABCD Study annual release 5.1 held in the NIMH Data Archive (NDA). This release includes a clinical assessment every year during four years, starting from baseline where children are aged 9 to 11. Sulcal measures were extracted from 10,069 participants using sMRI at baseline. We selectively retained data from the oldest child in each family and restricted the sample to singleton pregnancy and children who completed psychopathology assessment at each timepoint, leading to a refined dataset of 7,031 children. 

Psychopathology

Psychopathology was assessed every year using the CBCL. We focused on internalizing symptoms sub-score (anxiety, depression, withdrawal, ruminations) predictive of depressive episodes. A T-score above 65 meets the threshold for clinical psychopathology. A T-score between 60 and 64 is considered borderline clinical range and predictive of later psychopathology. Finally, a T-score under 60 is considered to be within normal limits.

We identified four psychopathological trajectories during adolescence based on this clinical threshold :

-Control group: Individuals with CBCL scores under 60 across all timepoints

-Early Onset group: Individuals exhibiting CBCL scores above 65 since the first timepoint

-Late Onset group: Individuals below 60 at the first timepoint but above 65 at the latest timepoint

-Early Remitters group: Individuals above 65 at the first timepoint but below 60 at the latest timepoint

Genetic risks 

To comprehensively assess genetic risk, multiple PRS were computed for each disorder when possible, accounting for the variation in risk captured across various Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS). We considered 15 PRS for Major Depressive Disorders (MDD), 2 PRS for Autism Spectrum Disorder and ADHD, 1 PRS for Bipolar Disorder and Anxiety Disorder and 5 PRS for Schizophrenia. As PRS are derived from GWAS predominantly conducted on populations of white ancestry, only children of white ancestry were included in analyses involving genetics (N = 3,686).

Perinatal complications 

Perinatal complications were extracted from the Developmental History Questionnaire and included all gestational and birth complications, alcohol, tobacco and marijuana exposure during the first trimester of pregnancy, cesarean section, preterm birth, parental ages at birth and unplanned pregnancy. 

Sulcal morphometry 

Raw anatomical images (T1-weighted) were corrected for intensity inhomogeneities and segmented using FastSurfer 1.0. We extracted sulcal measures using BrainVISA version 5.1.1. Sulci were labeled according to a predeﬁned anatomical nomenclature of 123 sulcal labels. We then extracted length, mean depth, surface area, and width (or fold opening)4. The most variable and shallow sulci may be not detected in many participants. Hence, only sulci with less than 5% missed detection in each participant were considered, which resulted in 84 sulci of interest. An additional filtering was implemented to deal with outliers : the z-score of the sulcal measure being assessed was computed and the set of subjects showing an absolute z-score > 2.5 was discarded from current analysis. It corresponds to between 1% and 2% of subjects for each sulcus. 

Statistical analysis

Using linear mixed modeling, we compared perinatal brain development, as measured by sulcal morphometry, across the distinct trajectory groups, taking the control group as a reference.

Subsequently, using both linear mixed modeling and Bayesian latent profile analysis, we compared groups and identified perinatal factors known to impact brain maturation (including polygenic risk scores for different psychiatric disorders, gestational complications and obstetric complications). 

Finally, we measured the interactive effects between these factors explaining the differences between groups using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). 

P values are corrected for multiple comparisons using the FDR method. 

Results

4,710 children were identified as part of the control group, 277 children display early onset psychopathology, 275 children display late onset psychopathology, 310 are early remitters and 1459 could not be categorized in either of these groups (Figure 1). 

Using linear mixed modeling with research site as a random factor, we identified differences in genetic risks between different trajectory groups. Children with early psychopathology display the highest risk, with significantly higher scores in eight different PRS for MDD as compared to controls (pFDR < 0.01). Nonetheless, children in this group are not significantly different from the late onset group in terms of PRS. Children with late onset psychopathology display higher scores in MDD in four different PRS as compared to controls (pFDR < 0.01), and higher risks in MDD in two PRS as compared to early remitters ( pFDR < 0.05). Intriguingly, early remitters group display higher scores for schizophrenia in one PRS as compared to controls (pFDR < 0.05), early onset (p < 0.05) and late onset groups (pFDR < 0.05), but display lower scores for two PRS of MDD as compared to the early onset group. 

Regarding sulcal morphometry, both the early onset and early remitters groups displayed a decreased global sulcal surface as compared to controls, even when accounting for age, sex at birth, ICV, ethnicity and MRI scanner serial number (as a random factor). We found no local differences between groups regarding sulci measures (sulcal surface, length, mean depth, opening). 

Regarding perinatal complications, mothers of the early onset group, had significantly increased risks of severe bleeding, severe nausea, pre-eclampsia-eclampsia, severe anemia, urinary tract infection, high blood pressure, previa abruptio, other medical care, tobacco, alcohol, marijuana use during the first trimester of pregnancy (pFDR < 0.01) as compared to mothers of controls. The mothers of the early remitters group showed higher risks of severe nausea, severe anemia, urinary tract, high blood pressure, tobacco and marijuana use during the first trimester of pregnancy (pFDR < 0.05) than the controls’ mothers. Mothers of the late onset group did not display higher risks of complications than controls’ mothers. Differences remained significant after accounting for sex at birth, ethnicity and research site (as a random factor). 

Bayesian latent profile analysis to better characterize trajectory groups and SEM to assess the interaction between perinatal determinants, are still ongoing. 

Conclusion

We showed that children with early onset internalizing symptoms at the age 9 to 11, exhibited increased genetic risks of MDD, decreased overall sulcal surface, and higher risks of gestational complications, notably, placental dysfunction and drug taking during pregnancy, when compared to controls. Children with later onset of internalizing symptoms display higher polygenic risk scores for MDD than controls but no difference of sulcal surface nor risk of perinatal complications. It seems that these children did not experience adverse perinatal environments and have no alterations in perinatal brain maturation, perhaps accounting for the later onset during adolescence. Interestingly, children in the early remitters group showed an increased PRS for schizophrenia as compared to all other groups while a decreased risk for MDD as compared to the early onset group. They also displayed decreased sulcal surface and higher risk of gestational complications as compared to controls. Hence, while these children seem to be remitters from a clinical perspective, they display, to some extent, similar perinatal risk factors as the early onset children. It will be interesting in post-hoc analysis to identify the factors that can account for this trajectory. Overall, early onset internalizing symptoms seems to be accounted for by a combination of genetic risks, adverse perinatal events, and a deviation in perinatal brain maturation, identifiable very early on. It remains to characterize the interaction between all these factors, and quantify their explicative power on psychopathological trajectory.
