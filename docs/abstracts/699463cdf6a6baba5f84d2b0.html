Introduction

Patients with psychotic disorders may exhibit negative symptoms in addition to delusions and hallucinations. Negative symptoms (blunted affect, alogia, anhedonia, avolition, social withdrawal [1]) are the most important predictors of long-term impairments in social functioning and quality of life [2]. Furthermore, negative symptoms respond poorly to currently available pharmacological and psychotherapeutic interventions [3-4]. This study investigated the efficacy of a psychotherapy specifically designed to reduce negative symptoms in patients with psychotic disorders. The innovative MOSAIC psychotherapy combines individual cognitive behavioral therapy and group training for social skills and cognition over eight months to improve negative symptoms and social abilities [5]. In addition to clinical assessments, changes in fMRI activation during perception of nonverbal social cues (emotional facial and vocal expressions) were evaluated to identify neural correlates of treatment-related improvements.

Methods

MOSAIC (30 individual CBT & 30 group training sessions over 8 months) was compared with SUPPORT (supportive talks and pleasant group activities with equal duration and frequency) in a randomized controlled trial [6]. 60 patients with psychotic disorders with marked negative symptoms participated (PANSS-negative > 10 [6]) and 54 patients completed the intervention (27 per group). Assessments for the primary (PANSS-negative, [6]) and secondary endpoints (SSPA [7], CDSS [8]) were carried out at baseline (T0) and after the 8-month treatment (T1). Specific treatment effects of MOSAIC as compared to SUPPORT were evaluated using t-tests, in which the differences in post- and pre-intervention data (T1-T0) were tested for mean differences (Fig. 1). Furthermore, fMRI data (3T, Siemens Prisma) were obtained during perception of nonverbal emotional cues (facial and vocal expressions, [9]) and evaluated for treatment-related changes using SPM25 (www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/spm, see Fig. 2).

Results

The statistical analysis revealed no significant differences between MOSAIC and SUPPORT regarding the improvement of negative symptoms (p = 0.36, Cohen’s d = 0.1). However, pre-post comparisons showed reduction in negative symptoms with medium to large effect sizes within each group. Mean PANSS negative scores decreased by 4.7 ± 5.8 points for MOSAIC (p < 0.001, d = 0.82) and by 4.1 ± 7.7 points for SUPPORT (p = 0.005, d = 0.53). In addition, a significant dose-response relationship with therapy adherence was observed in both groups [5]. At neural level, the fMRI activity during social cue perception showed no significant main effect of time, nor group-by-time interaction. However, individual improvement in negative symptoms correlated with decreasing hemodynamic activity (T0 > T1) within the bilateral temporal voice area (TVA [10]) during social cue perception (p<0.05, corrected at cluster level, Fig. 2).

Conclusion

MOSAIC showed no significant superiority over supportive treatment. Nevertheless, the results support the empirical evidence that psychotherapeutic treatments combining individual and group sessions can contribute to the alleviation of negative symptoms. The observed decrease in hemodynamic activation within the bilateral TVA associated to reduction of negative symptoms corroborates the link between clinical improvement and neuroplastic changes in cerebral regions contributing to social cognition and emotional processing [10].
