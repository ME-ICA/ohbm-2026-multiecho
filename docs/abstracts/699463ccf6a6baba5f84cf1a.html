Introduction

Subjective cognitive decline (SCD) is a subjective experience of cognitive decline, without objective cognitive impairment1, and now has been recognized as a risk stage of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) or other dementias2. Previous studies have found that SCD has a higher GMV atrophy and lower glucose metabolism than normal participants in cingulate gyrus, insula, and temporal lobe3, which are typical early degenerative regions of AD and have been verified associated with multiple cognitive dimensions. However, the relationship between subjective complaint (worry) and neurodegeneration is still unclear which may even include some specific changes of brain functional patterns. Some studies suggest a nonlinear functional connectivity (FC) compensation trajectory occur on early-stage of SCD4. In this study, we aim to explore the relationship between subjective complaint and cognitive decline, and its potential brain structural and functional neurobiological mechanisms, to further understanding of the relevant mechanisms in the course of SCD.

Methods

We collected 544 participants’ magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and clinical neuropsychological test data, including normal control (NC, n = 223, age: 66.52 ± 6.60), normal but worry (NC_w, n = 175, age: 64.78 ± 7.87) and subjective cognitive decline (SCD, n = 146, age: 68.51 ± 5.38). SCD is diagnosed based on SCD-I1, and the definition criteria of NC and NC_w is whether they subjective report worry. MRI data includes T1-weighted image (T1w) and resting state function MRI (rs-fMRI, TR = 800 ms). Clinical neuropsychological test includes Montreal Cognitive Assessment Basic (MoCA_B), Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination-III (ACE-III), Shape Trails Test Parts A and B (STT-A/B), Auditory Verbal Learning Test (AVLT) N4-N7 and Animal Verbal Fluency Test (AFT). We also collected gender, age, and education years and add them as covariates for all analyses (Fig.1A).

Voxel-based morphometry analysis (VBM) processing and analysis used SPM12, and total intracranial volume (TIV) has been added as covariate. Rs-fMRI processing was performed using fMRIPrep and AFNI, we do head motion control, regression, bandpass filtering and smoothing. Then we calculated whole brain seed-FC maps with each VBM cluster. The model set of voxel-wise analysis of seed-FC is same as VBM, but change TIV to mean FD. All voxel-wise analysis just between NC and NC_w group, and uniformly restricted FDR.p < 0.05 and cluster size > 200 voxels (Fig.1A, Fig.2A). Region of interesting (ROI) analysis between three groups using ANCOVA. we propose FC compensation score (FC-CS), which is the cosine of residual after regressing gray matter volume (GMV) and other covariates (Fig.2 B).

Results

NC_w group has a significant GMV decrease than NC in left middle and anterior cingulate cortex, bilateral hippocampus and left fusiform in VBM result (Fig.1A). ROI analysis shows that both NC_w and SCD group has a significant lower GMV than NC, but no difference between them (Fig.1B), which means these regions are worry-specific. Cognition analysis shows a reverse pattern (Fig.1A), both NC and NC_w group have significant higher cognitive performance than SCD in all 5 cognitive dimensions, but no difference between them. SCD group’s cognitive decline is still within normal range instead of objective cognition impairment.

To further explore whether the stage delay between neurodegeneration and cognitive decline causes by brain functional compensation (Fig.1A red box), we conducted seed-FC analysis. Among all seed-FC clusters, there is no significant linear relationship between them and GMV decrease, but a quadratic relationship between left fusiform GMV and its FC with right middle temporal (Fig.2A&C). And if we just fit linear model with NC group, it’s significant. These may show that FC compensation starts with worry for GMV decline. ANCOVA between groups also seems to confirm this, NC_w group has a higher FC intensity than both NC and SCD (Fig.2C). 

To verify that FC compensation can ‌protect from cognitive decline, we propose FC-CS (Fig.2B) and use mediation analysis to verify whether this is the reason of the objective cognitive decline lag (Fig.2A&B). The suppression effect results of FC compensation show that it temporarily offsetting the negative impact of GMV decrease on cognition, including MoCA_B, ACE-III and AVLT (Fig.2D).

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that FC compensation can act as a buffer between neurodegeneration and cognitive decline in the early course of SCD, which is triggered by worry related brain atrophy. So, in another perspective, worry is an externalized self-alarm for individuals on the brink of cognitive decline, and individuals with higher FC may actually in a higher risk state. At the same time, we propose FC-CS, a new risk assessment indicator with potential value for further clinical screening and early intervention.
