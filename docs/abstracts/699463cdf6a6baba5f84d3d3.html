Introduction

Nyaope, a combination heroin and cannabis drug-use profile endemic to socioeconomically disadvantaged South African communities, poses a growing public health concern due to its addictive properties. We previously identified highly significant cortical atrophy in right hemispheric frontotemporal regions1 yet the question remains, is the cortical thinning predisposed or acquired from drug use? Intracranial volume (ICV) is used as a marker of pre-morbid brain size2 and very little research has been done on cortical scaling properties relative to ICV in substance use disorders (SUD), especially in Africa. Additionally, corresponding white matter abnormalities remain unexamined in this cohort. We, therefore, investigated cortical scaling and tract-specific alterations in white matter microstructure in nyaope users and healthy controls.

Methods

Male nyaope users (n = 28) were screened and recruited from government-funded substance rehabilitation centres. Nyaope users were included based on their primary substance of choice being nyaope and urine analysis being positive for opioids and cannabis. Healthy, age-and sociodemographically- matched male control participants (n = 32) were recruited from the community. Nyaope users and healthy controls were excluded if they were HIV positive or had not undergone an HIV test with a negative result in the six months prior to recruitment. High resolution 3D T1 weighted images and diffusion weighted images (DWI) were acquired using a 3 T General Electric Discovery1. DWI data were acquired using a single-shot echo-planar imaging sequence with 41 diffusion gradient directions, including one non-diffusion-weighted (b = 0 s/mm²) image. ICV, total cortical thickness and volume were extracted from T1-weighted images using Freesurfer v7.4.1 and the Desikan-Killiany atlas. Measurements were logarithmically transformed, and multiple regression analyses were performed using R v4.5.1. Diffusion tensors were reconstructed from DWI using QSIRecon and fractional anisotropy (FA)  maps were derived using FSL’s DTIFIT tool and the JHU White-Matter Tractography Atlas. Group comparisons of FA between nyaope users and healthy controls were performed using FSL’s TBSS. Inter- and intragroup overlap analyses were conducted based on cluster coordinates and tract-level convergence. Intra-group regressions assessed associations with duration of nyaope use. Multiple comparisons were corrected for using threshold-free cluster enhancement and false discovery rate detection.

Results

Cortical volume exhibited a significant positive scaling relationship with ICV in both the nyaope users (b=0.67, R² = 0.51, p = 0.00002) and control group (b=0.73, R² = 0.52, p = 0.000007), corrected for multiple comparisons with no significant difference in scaling between groups. In contrast, cortical thickness showed no significant association with ICV in either group. There was a significant effect of age on cortical volume in nyaope users (p<0.05) and not in controls. Both cortical volume (p<0.05) and thickness (p=0.02) had a significant negative relationship with age in the nyaope users which was not present in controls and cortical volume scaled negatively with duration of nyaope use (p=0.02). No significant differences in white matter volume were found between groups. Cerebellar white matter was significantly reduced bilaterally in nyaope users relative to controls but this effect did not reach significance following correction for multiple comparisons. Compared with controls, nyaope users demonstrated reduced FA (p<0.05, uncorrected for multiple comparisons) primarily in the left anterior thalamic radiation, inferior and superior longitudinal fasciculi, inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, and cingulum. Longer duration of use was associated with FA reductions in the fornix, cingulum, uncinate fasciculus and anterior thalamic radiation (p<0.05, uncorrected for multiple comparisons). Overlap analyses of the intergroup comparison and duration of nyaope use identified convergent vulnerability, in the left anterior thalamic radiation (p<0.05). 

Conclusion

Our findings validate previous findings that cortical volume but not thickness scales to ICV. Nyaope users display greater age-associated reductions in cortical volume when compared to controls, which suggests that the cortical atrophy in this cohort is not preexisting but due to drug use. It also confirms the importance of considering ICV, age and duration of use in cortical comparisons in SUD. Nyaope use is associated with bilateral white matter degradation, particularly within thalamo-cortical and fronto-limbic pathways, regions linked to executive control, reward processing, and emotional regulation. We again found highly significant cortical changes in this cohort and although white matter reductions were found in corresponding white matter tracts, these effects did not reach significance following correction for multiple comparisons which is unexpected for chronic opioid use. This suggests a specific cortical vulnerability in this cohort. Further longitudinal investigation is needed to fully understand the mechanism of cortical and axonal degradation following nyaope use as well as the neurocognitive sequelae.
