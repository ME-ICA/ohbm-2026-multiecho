Introduction

Very premature birth (<32 weeks gestational age - GA) can interrupt critical third trimester brain developmental mechanisms, increasing risk for long-term cognitive, sensory, and motor difficulties and neurodevelopmental disorders [1]. Investigating developmental trajectory of preterm neonates is therefore essential to identify atypical brain maturation. EEG studies have revealed developmental changes in the power spectrum of brain activity, from the preterm period throughout the first postnatal year, with alterations observed in preterm infants compared to full-terms [2]. However, traditional spectral measures mix oscillatory and non-oscillatory activity, limiting the interpretation of developmental changes. Recent spectral parameterization methods address this limitation by decomposing the power spectra into an aperiodic component, reflecting the non-oscillatory background neural activity, and periodic components, reflecting rhythmic oscillations [3], opening new avenues for understanding early brain activity development.

We aimed to investigate the early maturation of aperiodic and periodic EEG activity in infants born very premature compared to full-terms. We acquired EEG data at term-equivalent age (TEA) and 2 months later and used spectral parameterization to: (i) examine spectral profile evolution from TEA to 2 months, (ii) compare preterm and full-term profiles, and (iii) explore how variability in the brain activity of preterms at TEA could relate to clinical factors or brain microstructure as assessed with diffusion MRI.

Methods

The study included 41 very preterm infants born before 32 weeks gestational age (GA at birth: 26.9±1.7 weeks, range: 24.1-30.8; 22 males, 19 females), who showed no severe brain anomalies in their clinical MRI at TEA, and 13 full-term neonates (GA at birth: 40.1±1.0 weeks; 7 males, 6 females) without neurodevelopmental risk factors. 

EEG acquisition and processing: High-density EEG (128-channel; Magstim EGI) was recorded at a sampling rate of 1000 Hz during natural sleep. Adequate data were available from  40 infants at TEA (postmenstrual age [PMA]: 40.9±0.8 weeks, range: 38.4-42.1), and from 16 infants at 2 months corrected age (2mCA; n=16; PMA: 50.2±0.5 weeks, range: 49.1-51.0), with 15 infants contributing longitudinal data. Full-terms were assessed shortly after birth (0month; n=11; PMA: 41.8±1.0 weeks) and at 2 months (n=10; PMA: 50.3±0.9 weeks), with 8 providing longitudinal data. Vigilance states were classified by a neonatal neurophysiology expert and analyses exclusively focused on active/Rapid Eye Movement (REM) sleep. Preprocessing was performed using APICE pipeline [4]. Only infants with at least 1 minute of artifact-free active/REM sleep were included. Power spectral density (PSD) was computed using Welch's method and decomposed into aperiodic and periodic components using the FOOOF algorithm [3], with PSDs parametrised within [0.5-30 Hz] range (fixed mode; maximum 7 gaussian peaks fitted; bandwidth 0.5-18 Hz). We extracted aperiodic offset (baseline power), aperiodic exponent (power decay slope), and periodic theta power (dominant 4-7 Hz peak in active/REM sleep) globally (all channels averaged) and for three bilateral clusters (anterior, central, posterior channels). For infants with inaccuracies in theta fits due to high-frequency noise (n=13), the PSD was re-parametrised over [0.5-10 Hz] range with identical settings.

MRI Acquisition and processing: Preterm infants underwent clinical 3T MRI at TEA. T2-weighted sequences were acquired in three planes (TE = 150 ms, TR = 5500 ms) and reconstructed to obtain 0.8mm isotropic resolution. Brain tissues were segmented to generate masks of the cortex, white matter and central grey nuclei. Diffusion-weighted imaging with 42 directions at b=1000 s/mm² were also acquired (TE=75ms, TR=5000ms, 2mm isotropic) and corrected for motion and susceptibility-induced distortions [5]. Fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) maps were computed from diffusion tensor model, and averaged FA and MD values were obtained over masks of cortex and white matter. 

Statistical Analysis: Linear mixed-effects models examined Group (preterm/full-term) and Session (TEA/2mCA) effects on global and cluster-based aperiodic offset, aperiodic exponent, and theta power, analyzing both the full dataset  and longitudinal subset. For preterm infants at TEA, we examined the impact of established risk factors (infant sex, GA at birth, small weight for GA at birth, and history of invasive mechanical ventilation) on spectral metrics using an ANOVA. Linear regression examined associations between EEG metrics and MRI-derived FA/MD measures, while controlling for age at test. All significant effects were followed by post-hoc comparisons using estimated marginal means.

Results

Robust maturational changes emerged in spectral metrics from TEA to 2mCA, indicating that global aperiodic offset and periodic theta power significantly evolved over this developmental period(t(73)=3.60, p<.001; t(33.26)=7.09, p<.001, respectively) (Figure 2). Cluster-based analyses revealed regional differences in aperiodic offset and exponent maturation, showing more pronounced difference between TEA and 2mCA in the posterior areas (t(174) = 11.59, p<.001; t(178) = 4.65, p<.001,  respectively). Regional differences were also observed for periodic theta power, with higher power over the anterior and central areas compared to posterior areas (t(163)=2.46, p=.039; t(163)=2.99, p=.008, respectively). No significant differences were observed between preterm and full-term groups. Analysis restricted to the longitudinal subset corroborated these findings.

Spectral metrics further showed sensitivity to clinical risk factors: Male sex was associated with lower theta power (F(1,33)=6.61, p=.014), low GA at birth (24-26 weeks) was associated with lower exponent (F(2,34)=5.39, p=.009), and having small weight for GA at birth was associated with higher theta power (F(1,33)=4.83, p=.035). Invasive ventilation related to both lower offset (F(1,34)=6.95, p=.013) and exponent (F(1,34)=6.38, p=.016). 

Linear models linking PSD metrics and MRI-derived tissue microstructural properties in preterms at TEA revealed a significant inverse association between periodic theta power and cortical FA: higher theta power was associated with lower FA (t(34)=-2.28, p=.029), which reflects a more advanced maturation of the cortex. No other significant associations emerged.

Conclusion

Using parameterized spectral decomposition, we studied developmental changes in EEG activity from TEA to 2mCA in preterm and full-term infants. This approach allowed us to disentangle the evolution of the aperiodic background neural activity and periodic theta activity across this developmental window and underscored regional differences in trajectories, suggesting that brain activity maturation unfolds heterogeneously across different regions. While preterm and fullterm infants showed broadly similar developmental trajectories, they also showed substantial interindividual variability. Within the preterm cohort, variability in spectral metrics at TEA was associated to distinct clinical risk factors, and to a lesser extent, to MRI-derived indices of cortical maturation, the latter suggesting a link between functional and structural development. These findings emphasize the sensitivity of refined spectral features for characterising early functional brain development and for understanding how perinatal adversity shapes the emerging brain activity. Future analyses will include a larger number of full-term infants to strengthen comparative assessments. Ongoing longitudinal follow-up at 42 months with standardized neurodevelopmental assessments, will determine whether early EEG evaluations may help predicting motor and cognitive outcomes.
