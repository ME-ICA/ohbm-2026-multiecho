Introduction

The diagnostic landscape of neurodegenerative diseases, such as Alzheimer's disease (AD) and related dementias, relies heavily on non-invasive imaging methods like Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) to identify pathological changes in vivo. However, the neuropathological examination of post-mortem tissue remains the definitive gold standard for diagnosis. Bridging the gap between the subtle, temporally complex changes captured by living brain imaging and the static, terminal pathology observed upon autopsy represents the greatest challenge in translational neuroimaging today.

Diffusion MRI (dMRI), specifically Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI), offers a unique opportunity to probe the microstructural integrity of cerebral white matter (WM) non-invasively. However, changes in these conventional DTI indices are notoriously non-specific, potentially reflecting multiple underlying tissue pathologies such as axonal loss, demyelination, gliosis, or edema. Moreover, recent advance of whole brain tractography and structural connectome would bring various benefit for clinical applications, especially for the efficacy of the drugs and interventions. 

The aim of this paper is to introduce a Japanese brain bank cohort to perform this essential validation, integrating advanced multi-shell dMRI techniques with comprehensive immunohistochemical profiling to establish the comprehensive value of validated structural biomarkers for clinical prognosis and the elucidation of network-based pathology (connectomics).

Methods

The research draws upon the invaluable resource of the Fukushimura Brain Bank, a specialized dementia hospital maintaining a pathological autopsy program since 1991. 

A. MRI acquisition utilized a commonly deployed 1.5T Siemens Aera scanner. The dMRI protocol incorporated multi-shell data, acquiring diffusion-weighted images at b-values of 1000 s/mm 2(30 directions) and 2000 s/mm 2 (30 directions), often supplemented by additional 60-direction scans optimized for Neurite Orientation Dispersion and Density Imaging (NODDI). While 1.5T scanners present challenges regarding signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and magnetic susceptibility artifacts compared to 3T systems, leveraging this widely available clinical platform allows for a direct assessment of the clinical feasibility and translational value of dMRI biomarkers.

B. Image Processing and Tractography

Imaging data undergo rigorous preprocessing and analysis using specialized software (FSL, FreeSurfer, MRtrix3). The multi-shell acquisition permits the application of advanced multi-fiber models, such as Constrained Spherical Deconvolution (CSD) to estimate Fiber Orientation Distribution (FOD). Metrics derived include conventional DTI measures (FA, MD, AxD, RD) and measures from Fixel-Based Analysis (FBA).

C. Neuropathological analyses

All autopsied cases underwent standardized neuropathological examination using standard diagnostic protocols. Additionary, comprehensive large glasses of coronal sections are prepared for extensive immunohistochemistry. This approach allows for the quantitative assessment of key molecular pathologies, particularly the spatial distribution and burden of Tau proteins or gliosis (Holzer staining) , providing the anatomical image-pathology correlation.

Results

Since the commencement of diffusion tensor imaging acquisition in 2020, we have successfully accumulated 89 invaluable registered cases that underwent ante-mortem multi-shell DTI scanning followed by definitive post-mortem autopsy validation (as of December 2025, out of 168 total autopsies). This represents the successful construction of an exceptionally large cohort linking in vivo imaging to neuropathological ground truth. The cohort is heterogeneous, including Alzheimer's disease (N=14), Lewy body disease (N=22), age-related tauopathies (N=8), and psychiatric disorders (N=5, including 4 cases of developmental disorder).

The reliability of dMRI findings is challenged by inherent technical limits, particularly issues related to tractography algorithms and the scanner hardware used. The use of a 1.5T scanner inherently faces challenges related to signal quality and resolution limitations. However, employing multi-shell acquisition allows the application of robust multi-fiber models, which aim to compensate for the inability to achieve high spatial resolution by providing higher angular resolution. This approach presents a dataset that must be verified to pave the way for broad clinical implementation. The validation focuses on the utility of images from standard clinical conditions, confirming that robust, quantifiable microstructural data aiding specific diagnoses can be derived.

Conclusion

Despite inherent limitations of the prevalent 1.5T MRI system, this project enables the assessment of the clinical utility of commonly acquired and advanced dMRI data. Integrating the imaging data with detailed neuropathology provides critical insights into the biological significance of MRI markers, accelerating the development of reliable biomarkers for early diagnosis and prognostic applications through pathology-driven correlation.

The continuous accumulation of longitudinal, validated data from this cohort will serve as a permanent resource, overcoming the technical and methodological ambiguities inherent in modern connectome research and translating raw imaging signals into genuine, clinically actionable biological insights.
