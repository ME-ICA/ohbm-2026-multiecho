Introduction

Short-range association fibres, commonly known as U-shaped fibres, connect adjacent cortical gyri and are fundamental for local cortico-cortical integration. Despite their functional importance, U-fibres remain poorly characterised compared to major white matter tracts due to several imaging challenges: their small size, high curvature, and proximity to the cortical grey matter boundary make them difficult to reconstruct with diffusion MRI tractography. Also, U-fibres from neighbouring gyri frequently intersect, creating crossing configurations that standard clustering approaches fail to disentangle. Finally, U-fibres exhibit high anatomical variability across individuals, as their trajectories closely follow gyral and sulcal folding patterns, which vary substantially between subjects.

 A key limitation of current approaches is the lack of methods to systematically cluster and match U-fibre bundles across subjects. While major white matter pathways can be segmented and compared using standardised atlases or semi-automatic approaches enabling robust group-level statistical analyses, no equivalent framework exists for short association fibres. This gap prevents researchers from investigating U-fibre alterations in clinical populations or relating their microstructural properties to behaviour.

 Here, we present an end-to-end pipeline that addresses these challenges. Our approach combines geometrically constrained filtering to isolate true U-shaped trajectories, a novel crossing-aware clustering strategy to separate adjacent fibre systems, and a MegaTrack-based framework^1 to match corresponding bundles across subjects in standard space. We apply this pipeline to 100 Human Connectome Project (HCP) participants, demonstrating that U-fibres can be clustered, matched across subjects, and quantified using the same diffusion metrics and volumetric measures currently employed for major white matter tracts.

Methods

Data and Tractography: Diffusion MRI data from 100 healthy participants were selected from the Human Connectome Project^(2,3) and processed using deterministic spherical deconvolution tractography with StarTrack^4.

U-Shaped Fibre Filtering:  U-shaped fibre trajectories vary morphologically across the brain. To isolate true u-shaped pathways, we applied geometrical filtering requiring fibres to: (i) start and end in cortical grey matter within the same hemisphere, (ii) satisfy length constraints 20 mm < L < 100 mm, and (iii) satisfy an inter-endpoint distance constraint D ≤ L/π, where D is the Euclidean distance between endpoints and L is the streamline length^5.Streamlines connecting to deep grey matter were removed, as well as deep loops. Interhemispheric streamlines crossing the mid-sagittal plane were also excluded.

Clustering and Crossing Fibre Separation: For anatomically coherent clustering, we computed the centre of mass (CoM) of each streamline and applied hierarchical clustering based on pairwise CoM distances. The optimal number of clusters was then determined using the elbow method which identifies the inflection point where additional clusters yield minimal improvement in within-cluster similarity. To resolve crossings within clusters, we developed a 2D projection approach: each U-fibre cluster was projected onto a 2D plane tangential to its CoM and streamline alignment with representative bundle directions was quantified. Streamlines were grouped based on predefined angular thresholds, effectively disentangling orthogonal crossings while preserving coherent fanning patterns. For each subject-level cluster, a single representative streamline was then extracted.

 MegaTrack Framework and Quantitative Analysis: To enable cross-subject comparability, we constructed a MegaTrack^1 by normalising representative streamlines from each subject to MNI space and concatenating them into a single MegaTrack tractogram. Group-level clusters were obtained by re-clustering these representative streamlines. Since each representative streamline is linked to its original subject-level cluster, group clusters can be traced back to identify corresponding bundles across subjects.

For quantitative analysis, each group-level cluster was mapped back to native space, where all streamlines from the corresponding subject-level clusters were used. These native clusters were then used to generate density-weighted masks and diffusion-derived metrics as well as tract volume estimates. These measures were summarised across subjects to obtain cluster-wise quantitative profile indices.

Results

We applied the proposed pipeline to U-shaped fibres extracted from 100 HCP participants. After subject-level clustering and crossing fibre separation, representative streamlines were aggregated into the MegaTrack and re-clustered to obtain group-level U-fibre bundles. Figure 2, show a representative selection of average density for 4 U-shaped fibres. These average clusters show anatomically plausible U-shaped trajectories following the banks of neighbouring gyri, with compact spatial distributions across individuals.

For each group cluster, we mapped back to native space and extracted quantitative measures from all contributing subject-level streamlines. Diffusion metrics were consistent across clusters: mean FA ranged between 0.35–0.38 (±0.03–0.04), mean MD between 0.61–0.64 (±0.02–0.03 ×10⁻³ mm²/s), and mean RD between 0.50–0.51 (±0.02–0.03 ×10⁻³ mm²/s). The small inter-subject standard deviations indicate that these U-shaped bundles exhibit homogeneous microstructural properties across healthy young adults. Clusters occupying homologous regions in opposite hemispheres showed nearly identical diffusion profiles, supporting the bilateral organisation of these short association systems. These results demonstrate that the proposed pipeline enables full statistical analysis of U-shaped fibres across subjects. Extended analyses characterising U-fibre variability and reproducibility across the HCP dataset will be presented at the meeting.

Conclusion

In this study, we introduce an end-to-end pipeline for clustering, matching, and quantifying U-shaped fibres across subjects. The approach combines three key components: geometrically constrained filtering to isolate true U-fibres, crossing-aware clustering using 2D angular separation to disentangle adjacent fibre systems, and MegaTrack-based aggregation to match corresponding bundles across subjects. This provides explicit mapping between group-level and subject-level clusters.

 Application to 100 HCP participants demonstrates that U-fibres can be systematically identified and quantified with consistent diffusion metrics across individuals. This framework enables the same rigorous statistical analyses currently applied to major white matter tracts to be extended to short association fibre systems, opening new opportunities for investigating U-fibre alterations in clinical populations and their relationship to behaviour.
