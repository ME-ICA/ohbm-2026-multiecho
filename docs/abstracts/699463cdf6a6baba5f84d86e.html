Introduction

Emotional experiences engage brain processes operating across multiple physiological scales, yet most neuroimaging studies in affective neuroscience rely on a single modality. Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) measures hemodynamic responses associated with changes in blood flow and oxygenation which provide an indirect index of neural activity and may not fully capture the energetic demands of emotional processing. In contrast, functional [18F]fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG-fPET) provides a dynamic measure of cerebral glucose metabolism that is closely linked to synaptic and neuronal activity and is independent of neurovascular coupling.

Recent advances in functional PET methodology enable task-related changes in glucose metabolism to be measured within a single scanning session, making it possible to study transient cognitive and affective states 1. Simultaneous acquisition of fPET and fMRI allows direct comparison of metabolic and hemodynamic responses under identical experimental conditions, offering a powerful framework for examining how different emotional states engage brain resources across physiological dimensions 2.

Here, we used simultaneous FDG-fPET and fMRI to measure metabolic and BOLD responses while participants viewed neutral, frightening, and sexual movie clips. By contrasting emotional and neutral viewing across modalities, we aimed to characterize relationships between hemodynamic and metabolic responses during naturalistic emotional experiences.

Methods

Eighteen healthy adults participated in a simultaneous PET–MRI experiment while viewing blocks of neutral, frightening, and sexual movie clips (fMRI N = 18; fPET N = 17). Movie clips were validated in a separate behavioral sample and presented in a counterbalanced order during a single scanning session. 

To enable dynamic metabolic imaging, [18F]fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) was administered via continuous intravenous infusion throughout the experiment. Task-related effects were estimated using voxelwise general linear models, applied to hemodynamic responses in fMRI and to dynamic FDG uptake to quantify condition-specific changes in cerebral glucose metabolism.

For fPET, task-related glucose metabolism was modeled using a voxel-wise general linear model including regressors for baseline metabolism, emotional and neutral movie viewing, and an intercept. The baseline regressor was defined individually from gray matter voxels without significant fMRI activation during emotional viewing. Condition regressors were modeled as linear ramp functions during stimulation periods.

Primary contrasts compared frightening and sexual movie viewing against neutral viewing. Group-level statistical inference was performed using family-wise error correction.

Results

Whole-brain voxelwise analyses revealed robust hemodynamic responses to emotional movie viewing (Figure 1). For frightening versus neutral movies, fMRI showed significant BOLD increases in a network including the insula, posterior medial cortex, and occipital visual regions (FWE-corrected, N = 18). Sexual versus neutral movies elicited more widespread BOLD responses, encompassing occipito-temporal visual areas, extrastriate body area, supramarginal gyrus, and posterior medial cortex .

In contrast, fPET results differed across emotional conditions. For frightening versus neutral movies, no significant task-related changes in cerebral glucose metabolism were observed at the group level (FWE-corrected, N = 17). For sexual versus neutral movies, fPET revealed significant increases in glucose metabolism in regions partially overlapping the fMRI effects, including extrastriate body area, supramarginal gyrus, and posterior medial cortex.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the feasibility and utility of simultaneous FDG-fPET and fMRI for characterizing brain responses to emotional stimuli. By measuring hemodynamic and metabolic signals concurrently, we provide a direct comparison of two complementary indices of brain activity during emotional movie viewing.

Across conditions, emotional movies elicited robust fMRI BOLD responses in distributed cortical networks. In contrast, task-related changes in cerebral glucose metabolism measured with fPET were more selective, with significant effects observed for sexual but not frightening movies at the applied statistical threshold. This pattern highlights that hemodynamic and metabolic responses may not scale uniformly across emotional domains and underscores the added value of metabolic imaging for probing the energetic demands of affective processing.

More broadly, the present findings illustrate how multimodal PET–MRI can refine interpretations of emotion-related brain activity by disentangling vascular and metabolic components of task engagement. While data collection is ongoing, this approach already provides a framework for examining how different emotional states recruit brain resources across physiological dimensions not captured by a single imaging modality.
