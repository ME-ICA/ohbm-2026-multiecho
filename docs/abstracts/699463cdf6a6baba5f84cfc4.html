Introduction

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is an effective treatment for craniocervical dystonia, yet therapeutic responses exhibit substantial heterogeneity. The sources of this variance remain elusive, challenging patient selection and expectation management. Prior studies indicate that clinical predictors lack reliability and explain only a small proportion of outcome variance.1 Critically, outcome variability is not adequately explained by surgical accuracy; divergent outcomes persist even when electrodes are optimally positioned.2 These limitations highlight the pivotal role of pre-operative functional brain integrity in determining therapeutic outcomes, which can be objectively assessed via resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI). We hypothesized that the baseline amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF) captured by pre-operative rs-fMRI signatures can measure functional integrity of the cortico-basal ganglia-thalamo-cortical circuit and predict therapeutic efficacy.

Methods

This multicenter study analyzed a Discovery cohort (n=26, Beijing Tiantan Hospital) and an independent Validation cohort (n=10, Beijing Fengtai Hospital). Notably, the Discovery cohort comprised 52 resting-state scans (dual AP/PA runs per patient) acquired on a 3.0T GE scanner to enhance reliability, whereas Validation subjects underwent standard single-run scanning on a 3.0T Siemens scanner. High-resolution anatomical images were acquired in both cohorts. Preprocessing was performed using fMRIPrep 24.1.1 and AFNI, including motion/slice-timing correction and nuisance regression.3 ALFF maps were calculated on bandpass-filtered (0.01–0.1 Hz) data, z-standardized (zALFF), and finally averaged across the dual runs to yield a single robust feature map per patient.4 Features were extracted from 11 a priori regions of interest (ROIs) defined via FSL-FIRST (the thalamus, putamen, and caudate) and Brainnetome/DISTAL atlases (primary sensorimotor [M1, S1], supplementary motor and dorsal/ventral premotor cortices; subthalamic nucleus, globus pallidus internus and globus pallidus externus). The primary outcome was 12-month improvement (%) of Burke-Fahn-Marsden Dystonia Rating Scale-Motor part scores (BFMDRS-M). We developed a sequential predictive framework using relaxed Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression: Step 1 identified super-responders (>75% improvement; excellent outcome), while Step 2 stratified the remaining patients into moderate responders and non-responders (<25% improvement; minimal benefit).5 Model performance was evaluated via leave-one-out-cross-validation and independent external testing.

Results

The sequential framework demonstrated robust predictive performance. In the Discovery cohort, Step 1 successfully identified super-responders with an AUC of 80.6%, and Step 2 effectively stratified the remaining patients (Responder vs. Non-responder) with an AUC of 81.0% (Figure 2A-B). Crucially, the model exhibited exceptional generalizability in the independent Validation cohort: Step 1 achieved 100% classification accuracy for super-responders, and Step 2 maintained 100% sensitivity for identifying moderate responders. Feature importance analysis via refitted regression coefficients revealed distinct predictive signatures (see Figure 2C-D): Lower zALFF in the M1 consistently predicted therapeutic benefit across both steps of the framework. The caudate was also retained in both steps but demonstrated divergent predictive roles: a positive association with super-responder status and a negative association for moderate responders. Lower zALFF in the Putamen predicted the therapeutic benefit fir super-responder. Moreover, the identification of super-responders was specifically driven by positive functional patterns within the S1 and thalamus.

Conclusion

We identify a specific pre-operative functional signature within the cortico-basal ganglia-thalamo-cortical circuit—involving the M1, S1, caudate, putamen, and thalamus—that predicts DBS efficacy. The scanner-independent nature of this sequential framework supports its potential as a promising clinical tool for patient stratification. Integrating these fMRI markers into pre-surgical evaluation could improve candidate selection and facilitate personalized outcome prediction for craniocervical dystonia.
