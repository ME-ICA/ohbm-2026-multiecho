Introduction

Creativity is a complex human capacity vital for generating novel and meaningful ideas. Dance provides a compelling model where creativity unfolds as an embodied process, with expressive movement ideas generated through lived sensorimotor experience. We define embodied creativity as the integration of imaginative idea generation with sensorimotor representations, a capacity highly cultivated in professional dancers through extensive training. Neuroscientific research confirms that long-term dance training shapes brain systems involved in both movement and imaginative processes. Neural correlates implicated in dance expertise (NCD) involve sensorimotor regions, basal ganglia, and cerebellar circuits. Furthermore, the neural correlates of creativity (NCC) in the context of dance cover both general creative processes (like divergent thinking and insight) and those specific to the domain of dance (such as motor and visual processing). From an embodied perspective, creative performance may rely on the intrinsic interaction between NCC and NCD. However, it remains unclear whether long-term dance experience is associated with strengthened intrinsic connectivity between these networks. This study used resting-state fMRI to examine the functional connectivity FC between NCC and NCD in professional dancers, hypothesizing that dancers exhibit enhanced integration between these networks, providing a neural signature for embodied creativity.

Methods

A total of 29 dancers (DANCE; mean age of 23.14±2.95 years) and 28 non-dancer controls (CON; mean age of 22.79±2.69 years) were recruited and matched for age and educational level. The DANCE group averaged 14.52 years of dance training with 28.76 hours of practice per week, while the CON group had less than three years of institutional training in dance or music, with none currently practicing. Resting-state fMRI data (3T TrioTM; TR = 2.5 s, TE = 30 ms, 3.4 mm isotropic) was preprocessed (DPARSF/SPM12) including exclusion of subjects with > 2 mm/2° motion, MNI normalization, 6 mm FWHM smoothing, 0.01–0.1 Hz filtering, and omission of GSR. The study investigated resting-state FC between NCC and NCD using seed-based FC analysis. The seed region of interest (ROI) was a grand composite mask representing NCC, amalgamating key networks (default mode and executive control networks) and component masks for divergent thinking, insight, motor creativity, and visual creativity. Between-group comparisons used two-sample t-tests with significance set at peak-level p<0.005 and cluster-level pFWE<0.05. General creativity performance was assessed using the Abbreviated Torrance Test for Adults (ATTA), focusing on originality and flexibility. Correlation analyses were performed to examine the association between the strength of the resultant NCC-NCD FCs and ATTA metrics, with Bonferroni correction applied.

Results

The DANCE group demonstrated significantly higher originality scores (p=0.002) compared to the CON group, but no differences were found in flexibility or other indicators. Critically, the DANCE group showed enhanced FC between NCC and NCD (p<0.05, FWE-corrected), involving core motor regions: bilateral putamen, globus pallidus, and posterior cerebellum. The correlation analysis revealed distinct associations in the DANCE group, with no significant correlations observed in the CON group. The strength of FCs linking NCC with NCD showed a significant negative correlation with originality scores (e.g., left putamen: r=-0.484, p=0.008; left GP: r=-0.513, p=0.004). Conversely, the DANCE group displayed a significant positive correlation between flexibility scores and the strength of FCs linking NCC and NCD (e.g., left cerebellar crus I: r=0.479, p=0.009). Collectively, the NCC-NCD FC demonstrated a negative correlation with originality and a positive correlation with flexibility. This pattern suggests that embodied processes may differentially support adaptive versus generative components of creativity.

Conclusion

Dancers demonstrate their innate creative abilities, which are enhanced by the robust connection between the NCC and NCD. This connection, shaped by motor experience, underpins the neural basis of a dancer’s embodied creativity. Our results show a functional bridge between abstract creative thinking (NCC, including DMN and ECN) and subcortical/motor NCD regions (basal ganglia, thalamus, and cerebellum), supporting the concept that prolonged physical training induces neuroplasticity that functionally links imaginative thought with embodied action systems. Intriguingly, the relationship between the NCC-NCD connectivity and creative performance suggests a unique interplay: reduced FC fosters innovative expression, while increased FC enhances creative flexibility. This adaptable neural linkage enables professional dancers to dynamically modulate the coupling between creative thought and motor action, reflecting the crucial pursuit of equilibrium between originality and flexibility in creative expertise.
