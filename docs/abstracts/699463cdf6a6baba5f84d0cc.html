Introduction

Anhedonia, characterized by deficits in reward processing, affects approximately 92% of adolescents with major depressive disorder (MDD) and is strongly associated with intense suicidal ideation. However, anhedonia demonstrates limited response to psychological interventions, paralleling pharmacotherapy.1 These limitations highlight the critical need for safe, effective, and rapid-onset interventions to address anhedonia in adolescent populations with MDD.

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) offers a promising non-invasive alternative with few side effects. However, conventional TMS demonstrates limited efficacy for anhedonia, largely due to imprecise targeting and unclear neural mechanisms.2 Although the Stanford Accelerated Intelligent Neuromodulation Therapy (SAINT) offers a highly effective approach targeting the sgACC, a region governing negative affect, to ameliorate depression, strategies for restoring positive affect (reward processing) remain underexplored.3 This gap persists because candidate TMS targets for modulating anhedonia remain unclear, and the efficacy of interventions aimed at rescuing positive affect requires further validation.

To bridge this gap, we developed a symptom-specific, circuit-based TMS strategy for adolescents. First, leveraging large-scale data from the REST-meta-MDD Multicenter Project,4,5 we identified the NAc as a core hub of anhedonia-related dysfunction. Second, we conducted a randomized double-blind trial (n = 88) to validate this target, demonstrating that individualized NAc-targeted TMS significantly ameliorated anhedonia and depressive symptoms without serious adverse events. Finally, multimodal MRI analysis revealed that clinical improvement was driven by the restoration of NAc activity. This mechanism was further validated in an independent cohort (n = 91), indicating that anhedonia relief is contingent upon NAc modulation regardless of the stimulation site.

Methods

Identification of Anhedonia-Specific Circuits via Multicenter Data

To overcome target heterogeneity and identify robust neural substrates of anhedonia, we analyzed large-scale resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data from the REST-meta-MDD Consortium. Data underwent a standardized preprocessing pipeline, including slice-timing correction, motion correction, and spatial normalization. To address inter-site variability inherent in multicenter data, we applied ComBat harmonization to the mean amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (mALFF), a validated index of spontaneous neuronal activity.

We performed voxel-wise correlation analyses between harmonized mALFF and specific anhedonia/negative emotion subscores derived from the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD). Regions showing the highest voxel significance density associated with anhedonia were prioritized as candidate targets. This data-driven approach identified the nucleus accumbens (NAc) as the core hub of dysfunction, guiding the subsequent interventional strategy.

Randomized Controlled Trial Design and Participants

To validate the efficacy of NAc-targeted modulation, we conducted a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled trial (NCT05544071). We recruited 88 adolescent patients with MDD exhibiting prominent anhedonia (HAMD-17 ≥ 17; Snaith-Hamilton Pleasure Scale [SHAPS] ≥ 20). 

Participants were randomized (1:1) to active or sham TMS groups using block randomization. Blinding was strictly maintained for patients and raters using a 90° coil angulation method for the sham condition. All participants underwent baseline and post-treatment multimodal MRI scanning on a 3T scanner to facilitate target localization and mechanistic analysis.

Precision Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation Protocol

We developed a circuit-based, individualized targeting strategy. For each patient, we computed the voxel-wise functional connectivity between the identified core hub (left NAc) and the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC). The specific DLPFC subregion exhibiting the strongest positive functional connectivity to the NAc was selected as the individualized stimulation target.

Neuroimaging Analysis of Neural Mechanisms

To elucidate the mechanism underlying clinical improvement, we analyzed longitudinal MRI data. Pre- and post-treatment mALFF and mean Regional Homogeneity (mReHo) maps were generated to quantify changes in local activity and synchronization. We employed paired t-tests to identify regions with significant neural alterations and correlated these changes with reductions in SHAPS scores. Furthermore, we examined seed-based functional connectivity changes from the NAc to the whole brain, applying False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction to validate whether the restoration of NAc circuitry functional integrity mediated anhedonia relief.

Independent Cohort Validation

To verify that anhedonia relief is contingent upon NAc modulation regardless of the stimulation site, we analyzed an independent validation cohort (n = 91) from independent randomized trials (NCT04653337, NCT05577481). These patients received individualized TMS targeting emotion-regulation circuits (sgACC or pgACC) rather than the NAc-circuit. We applied identical preprocessing and analysis pipelines to determine if clinical improvements in anhedonia in this independent sample were similarly associated with the restoration of NAc activity, thereby confirming the specificity and generalizability of the proposed mechanism.

Results

Multicenter data analysis revealed the NAc as a central hub for anhedonia in MDD

Leveraging the large-scale REST-meta-MDD multicenter dataset, we identified the neural substrates of anhedonia to guide precision neuromodulation. Voxel-wise analysis revealed that the left NAc exhibited the highest density of voxels significantly correlated with anhedonia severity (Figure A, left panel). Specifically, reduced mean amplitude of mALFF in the left NAc was significantly associated with higher scores on anhedonia-related items of the HAMD-17 (r = -0.207, p = 0.002; Figure A, middle panel). Then, by analyzing the connectivity strength, spatial concentration, and subregion size between the DLPFC and the NAc (Figure A, middle-right), we generated individualized stimulation targets within the left DLPFC across the adolescent cohort (Figure A, right panel).

Individualized TMS ameliorates anhedonia via restoration of NAc activity

We validated the efficacy of these targets in a randomized controlled trial involving adolescents with MDD. Longitudinal MRI analysis revealed that clinical improvement was driven by the restoration of local NAc activity. 

Regarding spontaneous neuronal activity, the active TMS group exhibited a significant increase in left NAc mALFF compared to the sham group (Figure B, panel a). Importantly, the magnitude of this mALFF increase was positively correlated with the alleviation of anhedonia, as measured by the reduction in SHAPS scores (r = 0.368, p = 0.030; Figure B, panel b). 

Regarding local neural synchronization, similar restorative effects were observed. The active group showed significant increases in mean Regional Homogeneity (mReHo) within the left NAc compared to sham (Figure B, panel c). This restoration of neural synchronization was strongly and positively correlated with clinical anhedonia improvement (r = 0.636, p < 0.001; Figure B, panel d), suggesting that re-establishing coherent NAc activity is critical for symptom relief.

Association between functional connectivity changes and symptom relief

Beyond local activity changes, we examined the relationship between anhedonia improvement and NAc-centered functional connectivity. We identified specific circuits where increased functional coupling between the left NAc and key cortical-subcortical regions was significantly associated with treatment response (Figure C, panel a).

Significant positive correlations were found between reductions in SHAPS scores and increased FC of the left NAc with the left anterior orbitofrontal cortex (r = 0.574, p < 0.001; Figure C, panel b), right hippocampus (r = 0.552, p < 0.001; Figure C, panel c), left parahippocampal gyrus (r = 0.529, p < 0.001; Figure C, panel d), left caudate (r = 0.528, p = 0.027; Figure C, panel e), and right inferior occipital cortex (r = 0.519, p < 0.001; Figure C, panel f). These findings indicate that the alleviation of anhedonia is accompanied by strengthened functional connectivity between the NAc and broader reward and default mode networks.

Validation of NAc specificity in the independent cohort

To verify that anhedonia relief is contingent upon NAc modulation regardless of the stimulation site, we analyzed an independent adult cohort receiving TMS targeting sgACC/pgACC rather than the NAc.

While there were no significant group-level differences in NAc mALFF between active and sham groups in this cohort (Figure D, panel a), individual variations in NAc mALFF change remained significantly correlated with reductions in anhedonia-related HAMD scores (r = 0.348, p = 0.026; Figure D, panel b). Furthermore, mReHo analysis revealed significant alterations in the active group (Figure D, panel c), and these changes in NAc synchronization were associated with anhedonia improvement (r = 0.656, p < 0.001; Figure D, panel d). This validation confirms that the restoration of NAc function is a distinct and necessary pathway for alleviating anhedonia, irrespective of the primary cortical target.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study establishes a precision neuromodulation framework for addressing anhedonia in adolescent MDD. Leveraging large-scale multicenter neuroimaging, we identified the NAc as a core hub of anhedonia-specific dysfunction. Our randomized controlled trial demonstrated that individualized TMS targeting the NAc-circuit yields robust and sustained symptom relief, underscoring the efficacy of symptom-specific interventions. Notably, clinical improvement was associated with the restoration of NAc spontaneous activity and local synchronization, as well as strengthened functional connectivity within reward networks. Crucially, this NAc-dependent mechanism was corroborated in an independent cohort, confirming that anhedonia relief is contingent upon NAc modulation regardless of the stimulation target. These findings overcome the limitations of conventional TMS targeting and pave the way for personalized, circuit-based treatments for transdiagnostic reward deficits.
