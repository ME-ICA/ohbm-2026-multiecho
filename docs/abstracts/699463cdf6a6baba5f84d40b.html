Introduction

In visuomotor control, the right hemisphere has been associated with visuospatial, and the left hemisphere with visuotemporal processing.1-3 In right-handed people, asymmetric bimanual tasks result in a preferred use of the left hand for spatial processing and of the right hand for temporal processing. It is unclear, how the two specialized cerebral hemispheres interact with each other when task to hand arrangements respect or do not respect hemispheric processing preferences.

Methods

We thus investigated interhemispheric interactions in the cortical visuomotor network in 24 right-handed participants during asymmetric bimanual isometric movements, using magnetoencephalography. The task involved spatially and temporally challenging visuomotor tracking with one hand and a precisely timed ballistic grip without spatial control demands with the other creating a dual task scenario with either an optimal or a non-optimal task to hand assignment. We investigated time-resolved partial directed connectivity between left and right EMG-coherent sources during task preparation and execution. We contrasted connectivity between each homotopic and non-homotopic pair of left and right sources in the optimal and non-optimal condition. Significant time-frequency clusters were  identified using the standard Monte-Carlo cluster-based permutation dependent t-test with a cluster-forming threshold of 0.01 and an alpha of 0.05.4

Results

When the right hand performed the ballistic grip while the left hand performed visuomotor tracking (optimal condition), preparatory interhemispheric broadband partial directed coherence from left premotor areas to right visuomotor regions were stronger compared to the non-optimal condition. In contrast, the non-optimal condition showed stronger preparatory interhemispheric connectivity from right inferior parietal cortex to the left hemispheric visuomotor network.
 

Conclusion

Our results5 indicate that the dual task problem is solved by cooperative interactions between specialized cerebral hemispheres with, both, a left-right and a rostro-caudal gradient for temporo-spatial processing.
