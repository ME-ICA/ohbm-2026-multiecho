Introduction

Background

The Locus Coeruleus (LC) is prominently affected by neuronal loss in the earliest stages of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Assessing LC integrity can serve as an important early biomarker for assessing AD progression. Neuromelanin (NM) accumulates in LC neurons and NM imaging has therefore been proposed as a means of imaging the LC. As signal intensity is taken as a proxy for cell density, a quantitative imaging approach of the LC, which is less variable across sites and time is desirable. The present study used a multi-parameter mapping (MPM) protocol optimized for LC imaging to compare weighted and quantitative maps in healthy younger, healthy older adults and individuals with AD.

Methods

Structural MRI data was acquired in a group of 26 healthy younger adults, 26 healthy older adults and 26 individuals with Alzheimer’s disease on a 3T Siemens Prisma system with the standard 64-channel RF coil. Three sets of partial volume T1-weighted, MT-weighted, and PD-weighted images yielded quantitative maps (R1, MTsat, PD, and R2*) in each individual within one scan session. Capitalising on the geometrical form of the LC, the imaging slab was oriented parallel to the dorsal pons. In-plane resolution of 600mm x 600mm was used to aid visualisation of the LC, while elongated through-plane voxels of 3mm were used to boost the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). To further boost SNR, scans were averaged across 3 repetitions (Sasaki et al., 2006). The combined TR and flip angle (α) dictated the contrast of the acquisition. These were 24.5 ms/21˚ for the T1w scan; 24.5 ms/6˚ for the PDw scan; and 24.5 ms/12˚ for the MTw scan. Gradient echoes were acquired with alternating readout polarity at six equidistant TE between 3.35 and 17.35 ms. Other acquisition parameters were: FOV = 212 mm (AP) × 186 mm (RL) x 64 mm (HF), slab-selective RF excitation, readout bandwidth BW = 430Hz/pixel; GRAPPA (factor 2) in the AP direction using 40 integrated reference lines. Qualitative and quantitative methods were used to assess precision and reproducibility of weighted and quantitative maps for LC imaging.

Results

Qualitatively, LC visibility was higher in weighted images. The LC was also apparent in R1 maps, but less clearly visible in MTsat and R2* maps (cf. Figure 1A/1B). LC contrast ratio (with pons as reference), was reduced in Alzheimer’s disease compared to younger adults as detected by MTw scans (p=.001) and to older adults as detected by T1w (p<.001), MTw (p<.001), and PDw scans (p=.007). No group differences were detected in quantitative maps, suggesting less sensitivity to pick up typical LC integrity reductions across groups (cf. Figure 1C).

Conclusion

Although the LC was most visible in R1 images among the quantitative maps, our findings indicate that R1 maps capture LC signal intensity less well as compared to non-quantitative LC imaging. This is suggested by a qualitative assessment of LC visibility between weighted images and quantitative maps, as well as an inability to detect known group differences in statistical analyses on quantitative maps (cf. Figure 1C). Going forward, reliability differences across manual and automatic segmentation approaches on this dataset will be compared. Future research should improve the sensitivity of quantitative maps for LC assessment by combining sequences capturing different aspects of LC tissue properties.
