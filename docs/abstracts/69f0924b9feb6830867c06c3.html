Introduction

Cerebral blood flow (CBF) plays a fundamental role in delivering oxygen and nutrients to the brain. Mapping regional CBF is therefore essential for understanding normative variation in the brain’s metabolic activity across the lifespan. Previous work using gold-standard Positron Emission Tomography (PET) studies with radiowater ([15O]H₂O) have relied on relatively small samples. Fully quantitative measurements of CBF using radiowater-PET have traditionally remained small-scale due to the invasiveness of arterial blood sampling required for the input function. Recent advances in long-axial field-of-view PET systems now enable large-scale investigations of normative CBF by eliminating the need for arterial sampling1.

In this context, our study presents the largest coherent dataset to date of radiowater-based CBF measurements, acquired with the Biograph Vision Quadra (Siemens) at the Turku PET Centre. The dataset comprises regional CBF measurements from n = 303 healthy adults aged 21–86 years. Our objective was to characterize normative CBF levels and their variation across the adult lifespan throughout the whole brain. We hypothesized that advancing age would be associated with lower CBF, and that women would exhibit higher CBF than men2. Furthermore, we explored the association between CBF and body adiposity using body mass index (BMI).

Methods

Radiowater PET scans were acquired from n = 303 healthy individuals (59% female; age range 21–86 years; mean ± SD = 54.2 ± 15.2 years). Participants with neurological abnormalities, including history of stroke, were excluded. A subset of participants (n = 44; age 22–59) underwent repeated scanning (total n = 192 scans) to evaluate measurement repeatability.

Voxel-wise CBF (K₁; mL/100 g/min) was quantified using an automated one-compartment model implemented in the in-house TURBO pipeline, employing an image-derived input function extracted from the descending aorta. Log-linear mixed-effects (LME) models with subject-specific random intercepts were used to examine age-, sex-, and BMI-related differences in K₁. These models were also used to estimate between- and within-person variation. Normal variation in K₁ was summarized using the coefficient of variation (CoV%), and reliability was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), defined as the ratio of between-subject to within-subject variance.

Results

An average grey matter CBF of 42.2 mL/100 g/min was observed at age 50 (Figure left panel; regional CBF range: 28.7 – 58.1 mL/100 g/min), accompanied by large inter-individual differences (Fig. left panel; regional between-subject CoV: 15.8±1.4 % mean±SD), while ICC of regional CBF indicated low within-subject variation relative to between-subject variation (Fig. left panel;  ICC = 0.7 – 0.9). LME analyses revealed negative associations between age and CBF in major portions of grey matter (Fig. right panel; punc.<0.0001, cluster-forming threshold 100 voxels). Associations between CBF and age were best described by a log-linear model, suggesting constant age-related differences across the adult life span. Average rate of age-related differences in gray matter CBF was -7.1 % per decade, coupled with substantial regional variation (range -4.0 – -17.2 %). More pronounced age-related differences were observed in cingulo-opercular regions (-7.9 % per decade) relative to unimodal somatomotor regions (-5.6 % per decade). Most pronounced sex-related CBF differences were observed in the medial prefrontal cortex and caudate (Fig. right panel; mean -7.8 % for males). BMI-related CBF differences were particularly strong in the brainstem (Fig. right panel; mean -5.1 % per 10 kg/m2).

Conclusion

Total-body PET permits large-scale, quantitative investigation of CBF in healthy humans by using image-derived input function from the aorta. The present data represents the largest unified database of quantitative PET CBF measurements to date, setting a new standard of normative CBF growth charts across the adult lifespan. Large inter-individual differences in regional CBF were observed, in accordance with prior large-scale studies using semi-quantitative methods2. Future studies should further investigate the causes and implications of between-person variability in CBF.
