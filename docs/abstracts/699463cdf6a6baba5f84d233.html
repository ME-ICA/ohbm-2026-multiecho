Introduction

Patients with psychiatric disorders have an increased risk of suicide (80%), and exhibit deficits in recognizing and describing emotions and complex mental states in themselves and others1,2. When individuals have difficulty identifying and regulating the emotional experiences and mental states, negative emotions tend to be amplified, adaptive regulatory strategies are reduced, impulsivity increases, and sensitivity to interpersonal stressors and frustration is heightened3. These maladaptive cognitive patterns may weaken the psychological buffer that typically prevents suicidal ideation from escalating, thereby facilitating the transition from suicidal ideation to concrete suicidal behaviors4. The psychopathological mechanisms linking these abnormalities to suicidal tendencies and brain structure in this population remain unclear.

Methods

We recruited psychiatric patients with recent suicide attempts (SP = 29), psychiatric patients without a history of suicide attempts (PC = 24), and non-psychiatric controls (NC = 24), and conducted structural and functional MRI during the Reading the Mind in the Eyes Task (RMET). Furthermore, several measures of affective disturbance (alexithymia, emotional awareness, hopelessness, suicidality) were administered. We compared gray matter structure and task-related activation during the RMET among the three groups. Subsequently, regression analyses were used to examine associations with affective measures. All analyses were thresholded at p <.05 family-wise error (FWE) corrected and were verified by small volume correction (SVC). 

Results

Gray matter volume of the left middle temporal gyrus was significantly reduced in the PC group compared with the NC group (t = 5.09, PFWE < .05), but no volumetric abnormalities were observed in the SP group. Regression analyses indicated that the gray matter volume of several regions, including the medial orbitofrontal cortex, postcentral gyrus, and middle temporal gyrus, was associated with alexithymia scores (r = -0.29), emotional awareness (r = -0.27), and suicidal thoughts (r = -0.59). During the RMET, no significant group differences were observed in either behavioral performance or brain activation. However, task-related activation in the left medial frontal gyrus was related to the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (r = -0.42), hopelessness (r = -0.48) and suicidal thoughts (r = -0.31), particularly in the SP group. Further mediation analysis showed that hopelessness significantly mediated the association between frontal activation and suicidality (suicidal thoughts: Indirect Effect = -1.91, p < .001, 95% CI [-2.85, -0.87]; suicidal ideation: Indirect Effect = -10.21, p < .001, 95% CI [-15.16, -5.31]).

Conclusion

No structural and functional brain abnormalities were associated with previous suicidal attempts in people with psychiatric disorders. However, we underscore the critical role of the frontal lobe, particularly the medial orbitofrontal and medial prefrontal regions in the recognition and regulation of complex emotions and mental states, including hopelessness, in individual transitions to higher suicidal risk 5. These complex relations may inform interventions aimed at improving emotional cognition and regulation to lower hopelessness and thereby lowering suicidal risk in psychiatric populations.
