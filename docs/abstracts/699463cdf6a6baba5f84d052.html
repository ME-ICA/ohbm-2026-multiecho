Introduction

Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) is a clinical condition at the very beginning of dementia continuum whose heterogeneity prevents a precise prediction of clinical evolution1. While current evidence mostly addresses biomarkers of pathology (Aβ and τ) and their correlation with brain structure (e.g. volume loss), little is known about the large-scale organization of network dynamics. A critical issue that waits for a solution is that the group analysis strategy, frequently used to investigate brain pathologies, gets short in capturing the multifactorial nature and the heterogeneity of subject profiles typical of the dementia syndrome. Thus, a multiparametric analysis projected in a subject-specific space is needed to provide better insight into the link between network structural and functional alterations and large-scale brain dynamics.

The structural and functional reorganization of brain network architecture with dementia progression can be assessed with graph theory (GT) measures2 applied to resting state connectivity data, but this type of analysis alone cannot address the reorganization of circuit dynamics. On the other hand, a recent technology, The Virtual Brain (TVB)3, can be used to simulate the neurophysiological underpinnings of network dynamics in a non-invasive and personalized way. 

In this work, we combined TVB with GT to extract information from MRI data and project the parameters into a high-dimensional space. Networks supporting specific integrative and sensorimotor functions [including the default mode network (DMN), limbic network (LN), attention network (AN), and somatomotor network (SMN)] were considered in a cohort including healthy controls (HC), MCI (both AD biomarker positive and negative, i.e., MCI+ and MCI-) and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) patients. The main aim was to investigate the potential of multiparametric analysis of MRI data to yield single subject profiles correlating network mechanisms with Aβ and τ, and neuropsychological scores. 

Methods

Subjects: 18 HC (9f, 69±5y), 22 MCI (14f, 75±6y, checked for cerebrospinal fluid -CSF- biomarkers) and 20 AD (17f, 71±7y) underwent MRI using a 3T Siemens Skyra scanner,  including: resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) (GE-EPI, TR/TE=2400/30ms; voxel=3x3x3mm3, 200 volumes), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) (SE-EPI, TR/TE=8400/93ms, voxel=2.5x2.5x2.5mm3, b=1000/2000s/mm2 with 30 directions each, 7 b0-images), and 3DT1-weighted volume (MPRAGE, TR/TE/TI=2.3/2.96/900ms, voxel=1x1x1mm3). Patients underwent a neuropsychological examination to assess global cognitive status (MMSE) and performance in multiple domains sensitive to cognitive impairment. 

Preprocessing of DWI and rs-fMRI data: DWI data was denoised and corrected for motion and eddy currents distortions. 30 million streamlines whole-brain anatomically-constrained tractography was performed, estimating fibers orientation distribution with multi-shell multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution and using probabilistic streamline tractography. fMRI preprocessing included Marchenko-Pastur principal component analysis denoising followed by slice-timing correction, realignment, co-registration to the 3DT1 volume, polynomial detrending, nuisance regression of 24 motion parameters and CSF temporal signal, and temporal band-pass filtering (0.008-0.09 Hz).  

Structural connectivity (SC) and experimental functional connectivity (expFC) matrices: an ad-hoc atlas was created combining 93 cortical/subcortical cerebral (AAL/FIRST) and 31 cerebellar (SUIT) labels. The time-course of BOLD signals was extracted from rs-fMRI data for each node; six networks known to support specific functions (Fig.1A) were selected according to Buckner and Yeo atlases. For each functional network, the SC matrix was obtained extracting a subset of nodes and connections from the whole-brain tractogram. Both static (expFC) and dynamic (expFCD) network FC were reconstructed from experimental data. Network topological organization was assessed by applying graph theory analysis to SC and expFC matrices (Fig.1A).

TVB simulation: for each subject and each network, TVB simulations were performed using the SC matrix (Fig.1B) as input and endowing the nodes with the Wong-Wang neural mass4. This neural mass model represents each brain area in TVB “avatar” as excitatory and inhibitory neural populations connected through NMDA and GABA synapses and allows to obtain subject-specific information about excitation/inhibition (glutamate/GABA). All model parameters were initially set as in Deco and colleagues4 except for those tuned during model optimization: G, representing long-range coupling strength, and local parameters defining the strength of inhibitory (GABA) synapses (Ji), the strength of excitatory (NMDA) synapses (JNMDA) and the strength of local recurrent excitation (w+), providing an individual representation of the excitation/inhibition. Simulation generated an 8-minute rs-fMRI time-series per node that were used to calculate the simulated FC and FCD matrices. Parameters were adjusted iteratively using expFC and expFCD extracted for each network as a gold standard to maximize the fitting of the model. 

Statistics and machine learning (SPSSv21, Orange3): a multivariate general linear model (Bonferroni corrected) was applied to detect network GT and TVB differences between clinical groups. Machine learning algorithms (random forest-RF for features selection followed by k-mean clustering) were combined to find the key network measures for reconstructing subject-specific topological and excitation/inhibition profiles, at different disease stages. A multiple regression analysis was performed to investigate the relationship between neuropsychological scores and the combination of GT measures and TVB parameters in the networks of interest.

Results

GT measures revealed the topological organization of resting-state networks. From a structural point of view, MCI patients showed higher clustering coefficient, global efficiency, and nodal strength in the DMN compared to AD (p<0.05). From a functional point of view, MCI patients showed higher values of clustering coefficient, global efficiency, and intra functional connectivity (intraFC) in the AN, VN and SMN compared to AD (p<0.05). AD patients also showed lower global efficiency, clustering coefficient and intraFC in AN, VN and SMN, accompanied by higher betweenness centrality in AN compared to HC.

TVB simulations yielded subject-specific parameters describing the dynamics of excitation and inhibition in resting-state networks. Model parameters related to excitation levels (JNMDA and w+) of the DMN showed significant differences between groups, with AD patients showing higher JNMDA and lower w+ compared to HC and MCI (p<0.05, Bonferroni corrected).

In aggregate, at group level, none of the GT or TVB parameters showed significant differences between MCI and HC, while several differences emerged between HC or MCI and AD.

Machine learning algorithms reconstructed multiparametric subject-specific profiles and a posteriori analysis of subject-specific distributions between clusters was performed to evaluate the correspondence between GT and TVB parameters, Aβ and τ biomarkers (MCI+, MCI-), and cognitive decline (MMSE score) in MCI patients. 

Topological parameters of LN, AN and SMN were identified by RF as the most informative ones distinguishing pathological from healthy subjects and K-means analysis identified three main topology-based clusters (Ct1,Ct2,Ct3; Fig.2A). Ct1 was characterized by lower values of AN global efficiency (p<0.001) and higher values of LN functional small-worldness (p<0.05) compared to other clusters. This cluster mainly contained AD patients. Ct2 showed higher values of LN node strength (p<0.001) and SMN structural global efficiency (p<0.005) compared to the other clusters, while AN global efficiency was increased compared to Ct1 but decreased compared to Ct3 (p<0.001). This cluster mainly contained MCI patients. Ct3 showed higher values of AN global efficiency (p<0.001) and lower values of structural LN betweenness centrality (p<0.05) compared to the other clusters. This cluster mainly contained HC subjects and MCI patients. 

TVB derived recurrent excitation (w+) of LN, AN, DMN, FPN, and global coupling (G) of SMN were identified by RF as the most informative parameters distinguishing pathological from healthy subjects and K-means analysis identified three clusters (Cd1,Cd2,Cd3; Fig.2B). Cd1 was characterized by higher values of w+ in LN (p<0.001) and G in SMN (p<0.05) compared to other clusters  and mainly contained HC and MCI patients. Cd2 showed lower values of w+ in DMN (p<0.001) compared to other clusters  and contained most of AD patients. Cd3 showed higher values of w+ in the AN network (p<0.001) compared to other clusters   and mainly contained MCI patients. 

A posteriori analysis of topology-based cluster composition showed that the majority of MCI- was in Ct3 together with the majority of HC, while MCI+ mostly fell in Ct2, without a significant difference in MMSE score. The a posteriori analysis of dynamics-based cluster composition showed heterogeneity of MCI- and MCI+ between clusters. However, MCI patients belonging to Cd2 showed significantly lower MMSE compared to MCI patients in other clusters.

To assess the correlation between GT and TVB parameters with subject-specific clinical severity, patient-specific neuropsychological scores were used in a backward regression model against network-specific parameters (Fig.2C). Interestingly, combinations of GT and TVB parameters were more effective than individual measures in predicting neuropsychological scores across multiple cognitive domains, with an explained variance (adjusted R2) reaching in some cases ~70%. 

Conclusion

This work provides the first evidence that a subject-specific profiling of MCI patients can be performed through the combination of GT, capturing network topology, and TVB, simulating network dynamics. This approach identifies a rich set of network alterations that allows to identify clusters of MCI patients positive to AD molecular biomarkers (Aβ and τ) or sensitive to global cognitive changes (MMSE), and to correlate network parameters with neuropsychological scores. Conversely, no differences between MCI and HC did emerge at the group level (not unexpectedly, differences emerged between HC or MCI and AD, though). Thus, the GT/TVB multiparametric analysis extending over multiple network properties and cognitive domains in single patients is superior to classical low-dimensional analysis at group level for addressing the well-known heterogeneity of the clinically defined MCI condition. 

The present findings at group level indicate that synaptic excitation along with information trafficking, segregation and integration are still globally unimpaired in the DMN of MCI, while they deteriorate in AD. Notably, the higher TVB values of JNMDA and w+ in the DMN of AD than MCI patients provide a novel non-invasive measure of hyperexcitation. Multiparametric GT/TVB analysis led to the novel identification of fine-grained patient-specific alterations in MCI. The topological analysis (GT) revealed a reorganization of LN, AN, and SMN. A crucial finding is that subjects grouped into three clusters, one hosting most MCI+ and another most MCI- patients, together with the highest number of HC, revealing a close relationship between network topology and pathological markers. The dynamical analysis (TVB) revealed marked changes in the recurrent excitation of LN, AN, and DMN, and in the global coupling of SMN, allowing to group subjects into three clusters distinct from those obtained with topological analysis. Here, while the segregation of MCI+ and MCI- is less clear-cut, there is a significant reduction of the MMSE score in MCI subjects belonging to the cluster with the lowest values of network recurrent excitation. Consistently, an extended recurrent excitation in cortical circuits boosts local processing supporting decision making and working memory tasks5, which are reduced in MCI and even more in AD. The combination of topological and dynamical parameters (GT and TVB) predicted neuropsychological scores (up to 70%) in multiple cognitive domains better than the individual measures alone, implying that the measures are (at least partly) independent and provide an incremental amount of information. 

In aggregate, while classical group analysis captures changes in the AD brain compared to MCI and HC, single subject multiparametric analysis reclassifies subjects, unveiling changes in MCI that correlate with pathology biomarkers (GT) and the global cognitive state (TVB). The low integration/segregation balance and recurrent excitation in MCI+ and AD patients places DMN, AN, and LN networks at the core of the pathogenetic process, reflecting a combination of pathogenetic alterations and compensatory mechanisms. Biomarkers and MMSE should be reconsidered within specific subsets of subjects laying in the multidimensional parametric space generated by the GT/TVB analysis. Indeed, correlations of network topological and dynamical parameters with cognitive measures of memory and executive function gain clinical relevance as they predict the neuropathological state. These observations go beyond group level analysis and indicate that multiparametric analysis of data and simulations extended over multiple domains in single patients can advance our understanding on the physiopathological underpinnings of the MCI brain, opening new perspectives for personalized treatments in prodromal dementia stages.
