Introduction

Mapping the brain and its disorders goes beyond the study of isolated brain regions towards formal investigation of the interactions between them. The epileptogenic network concept demonstrates how reoccurring seizures arise from the collective dynamics of an aberrant system.1 In focal epilepsy patients, seizures tend to spread from an epileptogenic zone (EZ) to a connected propagation zone (PZ). The structural and functional properties of these epilepsy networks are well characterized by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and clinical stereotactic electroencephalography (SEEG). 

EZ and PZ networks are typically reconstructed using functional connectivity – the statistical interaction between pairs of regions – with largely discordant results across different methods and modalities.2 Conceiving neural activity as the flow of information, multivariate O-information can effectively disentangle the information-sharing dependencies of the brain by distinguishing between redundancy and synergy-dominated higher-order subsystems.3 Such higher-order interactions describe the relationship between three or more variables and may offer valuable insights into epilepsy networks that are not inherently accessible to traditional pairwise metrics. 

Considering this, we explored O-information extracted from the 7 Tesla (7T) resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data of drug-resistant focal epilepsy (DRFE) patients and healthy controls – focusing on triplets of regions contained within SEEG-defined EZ and PZ networks. 

Methods

We retrospectively included 13 DRFE patients from the Improving Epilepsy Surgery Management and Prognosis Using Virtual Brain Technology (EPINOV) clinical trial and demographically matched 13 healthy controls. Inclusion criteria included quality 7T anatomical and functional MRI data with no signs of major brain abnormalities. The EPINOV trial received approval from the governing ethics authority and all participants provided written informed consent.

MRI data acquisition included B1+ and 0.6 mm3 T1 mapping followed by 1.6 mm3 rs-fMRI. Scans were conducted with either a whole-body 7T MAGNETOM or MAGNETOM TERRA system (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) using a 1H 1Tx/32Rx head coil (Nova Medical, Wilmington, USA). All SEEG recordings were performed as part of clinical patient workup and followed national French guidelines.

T1 images were B1+ corrected, skull-stripped and segmented using FreeSurfer. Cortical and subcortical regions were parcellated using the Virtual Epileptic Patient atlas and served as regions-of-interest (ROIs) for subsequent connectivity analyses. rs-fMRI data was preprocessed using fMRIPrep (v25.1.2) followed by an established nuisance regression protocol. 

Regional epileptogenicity index (EI) values were computed from patient SEEG recordings.1 ROIs that displayed an EI ≥ 0.4 or 0.1 ≤ EI < 0.4 were assigned EZ and PZ network membership respectively. ROIs with an EI < 0.1 as well as those not covered by the SEEG evaluation were considered to minimally contribute to the spread of epileptiform discharges and omitted from the current analysis. All patients displayed at least three EZ regions. 

Across patients and controls, the O-information for all triplet combinations of EZ and PZ regions were computed from preprocessed rs-fMRI timeseries data using the BraiNets HOI (Higher Order Interactions) package for Python. This delivered four unique connection types both within (EZ-EZ-EZ, PZ-PZ-PZ) and between (EZ-EZ-PZ, EZ-PZ-PZ) epilepsy networks (Figure 1A). Control group subjects were assigned to corresponding triplets (ctrl) based on patient epilepsy network ROIs.

O-information is defined by the difference between the total correlation and the total dual correlation of a triplet. With each of these quantities linked to redundancy and synergy respectively, a triplet containing positive O-information is dominated by redundant information whereas a triplet containing negative O-information is dominated by synergistic information (Figure 1B).4

Results

O-information significantly differs between groups and triplet types (p < 0.001). All triplet types displayed positive mean O-information (redundancy dominance) apart from PZ-PZ-PZ triplets (Figure 2A). Triplet type differences vary significantly between patients and controls (p < 0.001) but follow similar patterns – with increasing involvement of PZ and PZctrl regions leading to more synergistic (or less redundant) dependencies. Variance decomposition revealed within-subject variability was significantly lower in patients for EZ-EZ-EZ (p = 0.025) and EZ-EZ-PZ (p < 0.001), indicating more stereotyped O-information across seizure-generating triplets. This effect weakened with the progressive recruitment of PZ and PZctrl regions (Figure 2B). 

Conclusion

Preliminary results indicate differences in O-information and a shift towards redundant interactions among EZ and EZctrl network triplets. Whilst the normal balance between redundancy and synergy3 appears to be maintained in focal epilepsy (Figure 2C), epileptiform discharges arise from networks that are interictally dominated by redundant higher-order information. A predisposition for redundancy at epileptogenic regions could establish the necessary conditions for the highly synchronized brain signals that give rise to seizures.1 Intriguing group differences emerge at the subject-level, where DRFE patients demonstrate a clear loss of heterogenous O-information in epileptogenic triplets. However, subsequent analysis revealed these results to be driven by zero-variance subjects (i.e. those that displayed an EZ composed of exactly three regions) and have since been discarded.

Next steps for the current study will include a substantial increase in sample size (50 patients), clarification of interindividual differences in epilepsy networks and comparison of O-information to structural and functional connectivity.
