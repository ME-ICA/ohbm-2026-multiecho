Introduction

Cognitive impairment in Lewy body diseases (LBD) arises from dysfunction across multiple neurotransmitter systems. Cholinergic dysfunction in posterior cortical regions is particularly linked to decline in memory, language, and visuospatial domain, while prefrontal dysfunction relates more to attention and executive functions.¹ The locus coeruleus (LC), interconnected with both limbic system and frontoparietal networks, is a key neuromodulatory hub for episodic and emotional memory as well as attention and executive functions.² Although executive dysfunction is well described in LBD, memory and language impairment have been less studied. We therefore examined the individual contributions of the cholinergic Nucleus basalis of Meynert (NBM) and the LC to short-term memory and verbal fluency tests in our cohort of prodromal LBD subjects.

Methods

We included prodromal LBD subjects with longitudinal data (three timepoints): 18 patients with mild cognitive impairment with Lewy bodies (MCI-LB) and 34 patients with 1–2 core clinical features of DLB without MCI. In total, 69 subjects had baseline data, 52 had the first, and 27 had also the second follow-up. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was performed on a 3T Siemens Prisma scanner. Free-water imaging was used to assess microstructural properties (free-water parameter, FW) of the NBM. Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI was used to evaluate LC integrity, focusing on the right caudal LC based on prior evidence of its differences between healthy controls and MCI-LB patients.³ We predicted decline in the following cognitive subtests: Philadelphia Verbal Learning Test and Semantic and Lexical Fluency. Linear mixed models with random intercepts for individual subjects were used. We evaluated MRI markers within interaction with time as predictors, controlling for age, sex, and varying time between time intervals. 

Results

Lower baseline FW in the NBM predicted steeper decline in short recall on the Philadelphia Verbal Learning Test, particularly from baseline to the second follow-up (β = -4.96, p = .0491), whereas right caudal LC was not a significant predictor. Lower integrity in both the NBM (β = -4.30, p = .020) and right caudal LC (β = 6.23, p = .034) predicted decline in lexical fluency across both follow-ups. All effects were independent of age, sex, and varying time between baseline and follow-ups.

Conclusion

Prodromal LBD subjects with lower NBM and LC integrity show greater vulnerability to decline in verbal short-term memory and lexical fluency, highlighting the contribution of cholinergic and noradrenergic nuclei to early cognitive changes in LBD.
