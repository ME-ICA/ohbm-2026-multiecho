Introduction

Head motion is one of the main sources of bias in resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) analysis 1-4, yet most public fMRI analysis tools 5-8 fail to address intra-volume (slice-wise) motion. Although fast TR acquisitions - such as the Human Connectome Project (HCP) rs-fMRI protocol – can reduce intra-volume motion, its impact on functional connectivity (FC) has not been systematically examined. In this study, we integrate the SLice-Oriented MOtion COrrection (SLOMOCO) method 5, 6 into the HCP fMRI analysis pipeline and assess its effects using 100 HCP rs-fMRI studies, comparing SLOMOCO pre-processed results with standard HCP pre-processed datasets.

Methods

Data: We analyzed 100 WU-Minn HCP subjects, including only datasets with four full rs-fMRI scans and available cardiac and respiratory recordings.

SLOMOCO Pipeline: Slicewise physiological regressors were generated using RETROICOR7 (second harmornic cardiac and respiratory Fourier series of regressors). SLOMOCO was integrated into the HCP minimal preprocessing pipeline 8 (https://github.com/Washington-University/HCPpipelines). EPI image was pre-resampled with inter-/intra-volume motion and gradient distortion correction in EPI space, then 23 regressors (13 motion, 8 physiological, plus linear detrending) were applied to remove nuisance signals. Images were post-resampled via the anatomical alignment to MNI space. Notably, SLOMOCO performs two resampling steps, whereas standard HCP preprocessing resamples as one step to MNI space. HCP FIX-denoised (HCP-FIX) 9, 10 data were used for comparison, which typically includes >100 regressors (24 motion + > 75 FIX components) in addition to white matter (WM) and cerebral spinal fluid (CSF) signals.

rs-fMRI Analysis: Pre-processed EPI images were bandpass-filtered (0.01–0.08 Hz). Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated across 360 ROIs in both hemispheres 11, and converted to z-score as functional connectivity (FC). ROIs were grouped to large scaled networks including Visual (VIN), Somatomotor (SMN), Dorsal Attention (DAN), Salience (SAN), Limbic (LIN), Frontoparietal (FPN) and Default Mode (DMN). Averaged FC value was calculated within each network. 

Statistical Analysis: For each subject, scans with the smallest and largest mean relative root mean square (relRMS) values were selected as low- and high-motion datasets. Paired Student’s t-tests were conducted to compare FC across 7 networks between low- and high-motion datasets with both SLOMOCO and HCP-FIX pre-processing. Sex differences in FC were assessed using 4 concatenated fMRI scans. A multiple linear regression model of FC in each network was tested with age and intracranial volume (iVol) included as covariates. 

Results

Figure 1.A shows averaged FC measures across 100 subjects in both hemispheres. The Pearson correlation coefficient (CC) matrices were computed using SLOMOCO (lower-left triangle) and HCP-FIX (upper-right triangle) pipelines. SLOMOCO yielded lower mean FC with the smaller variability than HCP-FIX (0.258 ± 0.133 vs. 0.267± 0.185). Significant FC differences between low- and high-motion datasets were observed in SMN, SAN, and LIN when using HCP-FIX preprocessed datasets and in SAN using the SLOMOCO pipeline (all p < 0.05), shown in Fig1. B.

No sex difference in FC was detected in any network with either SLOMOCO or HCP-FIX preprocessing method. In contrast, intracranial volume (iVol) was a significant predictor for most FC networks. The Pearson CC between sex and iVol was 0.65 (p < 10⁻¹²). Full statistical results are presented in Table 1.

Conclusion

The SLOMOCO pipeline produced FC matrices with lower mean values and reduced variation compared with HCP-FIX. The lower FC mean in SLOMOCO likely reflects its more effective motion-correction steps, including slice-wise correction and slice-/voxel-wise motion–nuisance regression than in HCP-FIX. While HCP-FIX pre-processing pipeline showed a different FC between paired low- and high-motion datasets in the SMN, SAN and LIN, the SLOMOCO pre-processed datasets detected FC difference only in SMN. Those findings in HCP-FIX could be false discoveries driven by the residual head motion bias, suggesting that SLOMOCO provides more effective intra-volume motion correction and reduces the motion related specificity in FC analysis. Further validation using established quality-control metrics is warranted 12, 13

The reduced variation in SLOMOCO-derived FC network may be related to the partial volume effects introduced by the additional resampling step in SLOMOCO, although overall variance of FC networks appears preserved. Neither pipeline revealed sex differences in FC across networks. However, SLOMOCO-deriven FC was strongly predicted by intracranial volume (iVol). Notably, iVol correlated with FC across all networks in SLOMOCO, but only in DSN and DMN in HCP-FIX. Previous studies reported higher FC within the VIN and SMN in females and lower FC within the DMN compared with males 14. However, sex differences in intracranial volume and gray matter volume are well established 14, 15, and the strong correlation between sex and iVol in our dataset likely masks direct sex effects on FC. Expanding the sample size with additional HCP data may help disentangle sex-related differences from anatomical covariates such as iVol (note the marginal Student t-score of Sex in SLOMOCO preprocessing datasets, shown in Table1)
