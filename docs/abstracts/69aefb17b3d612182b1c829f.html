Introduction

Focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) is the most common cause of surgically-treatable, drug-resistant epilepsy in children.^1 Depression and anxiety are common comorbidities in epilepsy.^2 Mounting evidence from functional imaging studies implicates default mode network (DMN) or limbic connectivity abnormalities in depression and anxiety.^3,4 The Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) is a parent-reported test that assesses emotional, behavioral, and social problems in children and adolescents. 

This study examines whether FCD network overlap with large-scale Yeo functional networks is associated with depression and anxiety in pediatric epilepsy. We evaluated whether greater FCD percentage overlap with DMN (FCDDMN) or limbic network (FCDlimbic) are associated with DSM-Depressive Problems and DSM-Anxiety Problems scores for patients ages 6-18* years-old (y) (Note: 18y* includes patients older than 18 years old who finished CBCL6-18.) We hypothesized that FCDDMN or FCDlimbic are associated with 1) depressive symptoms as measured by elevated DSM-Depressive Problems scores; or 2) anxiety symptoms as measured by elevated DSM-Anxiety Problems scores. We also aimed to determine whether associations varied by developmental stage or gender, given known age-related and sex-related differences in internalizing psychopathology (e.g. increased incidence of depression in teenagers, with higher incidence in females). 

Methods

MRIs were processed using our pipeline from previous studies to determine FCD percentage overlap to cortical functional networks. Lesion masks were drawn manually on preoperative 3 Tesla or 1.5 Tesla T1-weighted, or T2-weighted images using FSLeyes, co-registered to the native spoiled gradient recalled acquisition MRI (SPGR) image and warped to Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space. The adult-based Yeo 7-network parcellation is applied to the image for identification of pediatric functional networks as previously published.^5

Sixty-nine patients with FCD-related epilepsy completed CBCL testing; Fifty-six patients finished the CBCL 6-18y version (CBCL6-18). General linear models were used to test FCDDMN or FCDlimbic overlap association with CBCL DSM-Anxiety Problems or DSM-Depressive Problems Scores, controlling for age group (6-12y vs. 13-18*y), gender, and age × gender interactions. Secondary analyses were conducted in adolescents (13-18*y), with exploratory models in children who completed the CBCL 1.5-5y version (CBCL1.5-5).

Results

Fifty-six patients (55% male, n=31) had preoperative CBCL6-18 sampled at median of 12.8y (IQR: 8.8-15.1y). FCDDMN overlap (B = 0.080, 95% CI [0.002, 0.159], t = 2.06, p = .045, partial η² = 0.086) and gender (B = 4.41, 95% CI [0.39, 8.43], t = 2.21, p = .032, partial η² = 0.098) were associated with higher DSM-Depressive Problems T-scores. 25 patients (52% male, n=13) were adolescents (≥13y) with FCDDMN overlap (B = 0.19, 95% CI [0.068, 0.318], t = 3.25, p = .004, partial η² = .369) and gender (B = 8.0, 95% CI [2.52, 13.47], t = 3.07, p = .007, partial η² = .344) were associated with higher DSM-Depressive Problems T-scores. Exploratory analyses in the 1.5-5y group showed no network-symptom associations. Network overlap was not associated with DSM-Anxiety Problems in any age group.

Conclusion

FCDDMN overlap is associated with increased depressive symptoms, particularly during adolescence, whereas FCDlimbic overlap is not. Depressive symptoms are stronger in adolescent females. Anxiety symptoms are unrelated to network in this cohort. These findings underscore the importance of lesion-network interaction contributing to depression vulnerability in developmental epilepsy and highlight DMN involvement as a potential neurobiological marker of depressive risk.
