Introduction

Music is among the earliest and most fundamental socio-cognitive realms of the human species. Listening to music regularly could help in keeping synapses and neurons more active. In this study, the focus was on testing and analyzing mental concentration levels while participants engaged in a task. Music was introduced as a variable during the task to observe its impact on concentration. To conduct this study, a psychological test known as the Sustained Attention to Response Task (SART) was selected. A computer-based test that is intended to assess a person's capacity to hold off on responding to unpredictable and infrequent stimuli while responding quickly and rhythmically to frequent stimuli. The test was designed in such a way that digits are shown to participants in random sequence for a little while. Participants are instructed to click the mouse as quickly as possible in reaction to each digit, with the exception of a specific number. Participants are asked to react as rapidly as they can while making the fewest mistakes as this checks their multi-tasking ability as to how much they are in sync with their brain and muscle response which could reflect one’s concentration levels. Functional near-infrared spectroscopy, or fNIRS, is a wearable, non-invasive technique for identifying cerebral hemodynamic responses (blood flow variations) using near-infrared light. fNIRS is safer and less prone to noise than other BCI method signals. fNIRS has a better spatial resolution but a worse temporal resolution in comparison. Although there exists related work on effect of music on ones emotions, functional connectivity and even episodic memory but determining and understanding user’s concentration levels using fNIRS, the need remains for holistic approach that enables an effective analysis of the effect of music. To this end this study relies on the use of SART test to effectively determine and analyze the effect of music on concentration levels of the brain.

Methods

The experiment consists of two parts: In the first part, SART test without music and in the second part, with music was performed. As there are diverse genre of music and each person has their own preference. In order to reduce this problem one musical tune of specific genre in this case instrumental music that was a melody which consisted of a series of instruments being played (no lyrics) was selected which was played for each participant. 

Twenty-one volunteers 10 males and 11 females between the ages of 20 and 42 took part in this study. There were no volunteers who had a history of neurological or psychiatric conditions that would have affected the study's findings. All subjects received clear instructions and thorough explanations of the experiment. The experiment was approved by the Air University, Islamabad ethical committee and each participant signed a consent form that included information about their medical history and full approval of participation before performing the experiment. To collect the fNIRS data, an experimental setup that used a dual-wavelength continuous time multi-channel (NIRx fNIRS system from Europe) with eight light sources (Tx) and eight detectors was used as the basis (Rx). On the PFC, optodes were placed. 

The two phases of the experiment consisted of a 30s initial and final rest with tasks period of 2mins for both which made (104 trials of SART test each) without and with music with a rest period of 30s between both tasks which comes after the a message of STOP appears on the computer screen after ending of first task phase. Each participant was asked to perform the SART test which basically a standard computer based cognitive task consisting of numbers being displayed randomly from 1 to 9 on the screen followed by a mask (making it one trial of about 900ms) person has to keep pressing the SPACE bar on each number except for 3. The results of the SART test are being recorded simultaneously with the brain signal for later to be evaluated later as the test results are to be taken as a standard to be compared with the concentration results obtained from the brain signals. The total trial time is 5mins approx. and is done only one time for each participant. 

The signal handling techniques used to get the results of analysis were completely done on MATLAB 2017b software. The data processed here only pertains to variations in oxygenated hemoglobin concentration (HbO). The fluctuations in concentration related to HbO were band-pass filtered using a zero-order filter executed using a third-order Butterworth filter with a passband of 0.01-0.1 Hz to remove physiological disturbing impacts (Heartbeat, Mayer waves, Breathing) and offsets all this filtering was done in NIRx software and later analyzed in MATLAB.

The SART test analysis was done on an open-source website PsyToolkit that basically designs the test and alteration can be done in it as per the experiments requirements[35], [36]. The results of the test are saved in the PC and then uploaded in the analysis tab of the same website and the results of the reaction time (RT) and accuracy are for performing comparison across subjects for ‘music’ and  ‘no music’ cases.

The analysis of the mental concentration levels from the brain signals is done by firstly applying machine learning algorithms to see the data accuracy and to know if the two datasets are different from each other or not. After that to check the brain concentration, the idea that if brain is concentrating more so the average amplitude of the plotted wave of all the channels for HbO change will be higher for high concentration and lower for lower concentration levels. Through this a comparison is done with the channeled data for ‘no music’ and with ‘music’ to verify our results and is compared with the SART test as well.

Results

In order to evaluate the SART results, the two parameters namely reaction time (RT) and task performance accuracy are considered. In terms of the test decreased reaction time and high accuracy represents higher mental concentration but in some cases slight increased reaction time (at a certain threshold) is considered positive.  The results obtained at the end of the task for both classes were put into the analysis tool provided by the website used for the test. The SART test results came out to be quite promising as thirteen subjects out of twenty-one showed an increase in concentration levels while listening to music as compared to without music, as majority of the results displayed decreased RT and high accuracy while some showed high accuracies but a bit of increased RT. Those results were also considered positive, as RT increase can also happen due to some side-effects of the test. The remaining eight showed negative results showing increased RT and accuracy as well without music as compare to music. 

After the SART test analysis concentration analysis through brain signals was done which showed positive results with thirteen participants out of twenty-one showing more brain activation in average channel data for music (shown by amplitude difference between two conditions) as compared to without music for increased activation in  (𝑡) while the remaining eight showed more activation for without music. Those who performed better without music also showed this pattern in their results. The results are compared with SART test that acts as a standard showing little to no difference between the both outputs for each subject. The brain activation maps were done for each participant averaged data across all channels for both states which showed positive results or increased concentration with music as compared to without from which best, average and worse were selected to represent the result. 

Figure shows the activation map for best, average and worst respectively, for each case for a specified interval where activation was considered to be the highest. To check the statistical significance of the brain signal results, the t-test was carried out between music and non-music data for each participant which is also shown in Table 2. The significance level is set at the standard p-value of 0.05. This means that the null hypothesis is true if the p value is greater than 0.05; else, it is false.

The aim of this study was to check the effect of music on concentration levels of brain that does it increase or decrease while doing a mental task while listening to music but in this study we restricted the music genre to just instrumental music of one type a melody which consisted of a series of instruments being played no lyrics and this particular song was played for each participant. 

The participants were to perform Sustained Attention to Response Task (SART) as the name suggests by analyzing the reaction time and accuracy after performing the test tells a person’s concentration or attention levels. This is to act as a standard for later analyzing mental concentration levels with and without music. 

The design of experimental paradigm is done in such a way that both brain data acquisition and SART test are done simultaneously so we can get both results at the same time to avoid offsets and other factors like tiredness and annoyance caused by the log duration of the task. These factors were considered as in some cases although the participant showed good mental concentration with music, in the SART test RT came out to be slightly higher in some cases with increased accuracy. 

Overall the concentration with music came out to be more promising as compared to without music while performing the test for both SART and brain signal data thirteen participants showed increased concentration while the remaining eight showed decreased. The p-value for the data also came below 0.05 thus proving the statistical significance of the dataset. 

Conclusion

The purpose of this study was to evaluate and analyze the effect of music on brain concentration levels while performing a task. The selected task was the SART test. From the research it has been found that listening to music has better effect on concentration levels as compared to performing the task without music, which is clearly evident from the SART test results as well the average channel data for change in HbO concentration (for both states). This serves as the basis of our finding that increased HbO change shows high level of brain concentration levels on the task and vice versa. The t-test result also proves the statistical significance of our data for all subjects with p-value<0.05. The comparison of the two states with and without music has shown that brain concentration levels tend to increase with music rather than without music.
