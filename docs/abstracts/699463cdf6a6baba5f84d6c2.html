Introduction

Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) is a highly heterogeneous disorder, with patients presenting diverse clinical manifestations and variable responses to pharmacotherapy1. MRI studies have explored heterogeneity of brain structure and function in OCD by stratified OCD patients based on grey matter measures and functional connectivity2, but not white matter (WM) measures yet. Although studies have detected the association between various clinical profiles and distinct WM measures3, the heterogeneity of WM changes in OCD remains poorly understood, especially regarding its potential impact on pharmacotherapy outcomes. Therefore, we utilized a semi-supervised machine-learning clustering algorithm, HYDRA, to distinguish patients from HCs by combining multiple linear classifiers, whereby each hyperplane separates a subgroup of patients from HCs resulting in a ‘1-to-k’ mapping4. Additionally, we evaluated the WM alterations after pharmacotherapy using the longitudinal data, to clarify the relationship between WM structural differences and symptom improvement.

Methods

A total of 79 medication-free OCD without comorbidity and 86 HCs were enrolled in the present study. 30 patients were followed after 3 months of selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRI) treatment, while 29 patients were followed after 6 months of SSRI treatment. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) images were performed on a 3.0T MR scanner equipped with a 32-channel head coil. Clinical symptoms were evaluated at each timepoint using Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale (YBOCS), Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD, 24-item version) and Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA). Paired t-test was used to analyze the improvement of clinical symptoms after treatment. 72 tracts were reconstructed automatically using TractSeg toolbox (Figure 1), and fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), radial diffusivity (RD) and axial diffusivity (AD) of each tract were extracted for analyses. HYDRA was utilized to perform clustering analyses on 4 diffusion measures of 72 tracts to identify potential subtypes of OCD (10-fold cross-validation). Statistics: 1. We compared diffusion measures between OCD and HCs using an analysis of covariance, controlling for age and gender; 2. We conducted paired t-test to compare the diffusion measures and clinical characteristics between baseline and followup in different OCD subtypes; 3. Correlation analyses was performed to examine the association between clinical symptom improvement and FA changes after treatment, controlling for age, gender, and education. False discovery rate (FDR) method was used for multiple comparison corrections.

Results

Three OCD subtypes were identified. Compared to HCs, OCD subtypes showed divergent WM alterations: Subtype 1 (S1, 31 OCD) exhibited widespread decreased FA, increased MD, RD, and AD; Subtype 2 (S2, 21 OCD) exhibited no significant alterations; Subtype 3 (S3, 27 OCD) exhibited decreased FA, increased MD, RD, and AD (Figure 2). There is no difference of clinical symptom severity across 3 subtypes at baseline. After 3 and 6 months of SSRI treatment, all subtypes showed significant symptom improvement, but no significant difference in symptom severity across these subtypes were detected. Each subtype showed similar WM changes at different followup timepoints, but treatment-induced alterations between these subtypes were distinct: S1 exhibited increased FA, decreased MD, RD, and AD in multiple tracts with the most significant changes were observed in SCP_L, MLF_R, SLF_I_R, SLF_II_R, ATR_R, STR_R, ST_FO_R, ST_PREF_R, T_PREF_R; S2 exhibited no significant alterations after treatment; S3 exhibited decreased FA, increased MD, RD, and AD in multiple tracts with the most significant changes were detected in CC_7, UF_L, MLF_L, SLF_I_L, SLF_II_L, ST_OCC_L, T_PAR_L, T_OCC_L. However, no significant correlation between diffusion measure alterations and symptom improvement was found in any OCD subtypes.

Conclusion

Our study identified three OCD subtypes on the basis of WM structural measures. These subtypes exhibited similar symptom severity but distinct patterns of WM alterations compared to HCs. Medication treatment brought out similar symptom improvement across subtypes, and treatment-induced microstrutural changes were detected in S1 and S3 within similar brain networks, but correspond to different hemispheres. The present findings indicate that OCD is composed of neuroanatomical subtypes that have distinct microstructural alterations response to pharmacotherapy, which strengthen our understanding of pathophysiology underlying treatment in OCD patients.
