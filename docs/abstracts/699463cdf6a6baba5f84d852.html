Introduction

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition associated with widespread alterations in large-scale functional connectivity (FC).  However, a critical tension exists between clinical diagnostics and neurobiological research: while frameworks like DSM-5 ^2 rely on discrete categorical diagnoses, the Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) ^3 initiative suggests that psychopathology emerges from continuous variations along fundamental neural dimensions. Bridging these categorical and dimensional perspectives requires a principled normative reference space that can map discrete diagnostic labels onto the continuous manifold of brain network dynamics, in which the position of ASD-related network patterns can be meaningfully defined. However, existing classification-based and cohort-specific approaches lack such a reference, leaving the organizational nature of ASD heterogeneity and its functional significance difficult to adjudicate.

Methods

Datasets

We analyzed resting-state fMRI data from the ABIDE I and II datasets, including 461 individuals with ASD and 446 Typically Developing Controls (TDC), spanning an age range of 5 to 25 years.^1 To mitigate class-frequency bias, we applied a stratified balancing procedure combining Propensity Score Matching and adaptive resampling.

Methodological framework

Our methodological goal was to construct a stable normative reference geometry for large-scale brain network organization, in which ASD-related functional connectivity patterns can be positioned and compared without introducing additional modeling assumptions. To this end, we developed a Normative Continual Learning Variational Autoencoder (NCL-VAE) that preserves the geometry of typical developmental organization while accommodating ASD-related variation.^4

ROI-based time series were binarized via thresholding to extract robust co-activation patterns that capture backbone network dynamics. The NCL-VAE consisted of a variational autoencoder coupled with an auxiliary age regressor. The model was trained sequentially, first on TDC data and subsequently on ASD data, using an improved Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) strategy to prevent catastrophic forgetting and maintain a shared normative manifold. This continual learning formulation enables explicit tests of whether ASD-related FC patterns form discrete clusters or align along shared axes of network reorganization. Importantly, it provides a unified generative framework in which heterogeneity across individuals, putative subtypes, or behavioral phenotypes can be examined within a common geometric coordinate system, without introducing assumptions about categorical structure or disease-specific mechanisms.

Results

Normative latent-space organization of ASD-related FC patterns

The NCL-VAE yielded a coherent latent representation across datasets, enabling ASD-related functional connectivity patterns to be evaluated within a shared normative geometry. Latent space representations revealed that ASD-related FC patterns do not occupy an independent or orthogonal network regime. Instead, they substantially overlap with the continuum defined by typical development, consistent with the RDoC perspective of continuous variation rather than categorical distinctness.

Alignment with a constrained axis of network reorganization

Projection of individual FC patterns onto the normative latent geometry showed that ASD-related variation is structured predominantly along a low-dimensional axis associated with large-scale network de-differentiation. This organization indicates that ASD-related connectivity alterations are embedded within constrained directions of network reorganization, rather than reflecting unconstrained remodeling of network architecture.

Generative validation via normative axis traversal

Counterfactual traversal along the normative reorganization axis, inferred exclusively from TDC data and extrapolated toward reduced network segregation, extrapolated toward an extreme state, generated FC patterns characterized by progressive global de-differentiation. As visually evident in the differential connectivity maps (Fig. 2B/D), the extrapolated state exhibits widespread reductions in functional coupling relative to the baseline , which is indicated by predominant negative difference values. Quantitative assessment confirmed this trend, revealing a monotonic reduction in average connectivity strength from 0.809 (Baseline State) to 0.741 (Extrapolated State). Spatially, the network-annotated maps (Fig. 2C/E) revealed that this reduction represents a structured erosion of functional boundaries across major cortical networks (e.g., Default Mode, Attention), rather than random noise. These generated patterns recapitulate key under-connectivity features observed in empirical ASD data.

Conclusion

Together, these findings clarify a central ambiguity in the interpretation of ASD-related functional connectivity alterations. Rather than requiring a disease-specific network architecture, ASD-associated dysconnectivity can be consistently positioned within a shared, low-dimensional space of brain network organization defined by constrained axes of reorganization that have been classically associated with aging-related changes in the literature.
More broadly, this work points to a general organizational principle in which large-scale brain network reconfiguration does not occur freely in arbitrary high-dimensional directions, but is constrained by intrinsic network structure to unfold along a limited set of allowed reorganization axes. By combining normative modeling with continual learning, our framework establishes a stable geometric coordinate system for examining heterogeneity, individual differences, and phenotype-related variation without introducing new modeling assumptions.
