Introduction

Multimodal strategies that combine emerging and established biomarkers are crucial for robust Alzheimer’s disease (AD) staging; however, identifying optimal combinations remains a significant challenge. We leveraged recent advances in plasma biomarker research and the established utility of structural MRI (sMRI) in characterizing neurodegeneration across AD stages.1 Therefore, we aimed to explore whether combining blood-based and structural MRI biomarkers could enhance diagnostic discrimination across the AD spectrum in a Brazilian cohort composed of 113 subjects: 45 Cognitive Unimpaired (CUI), 23 amnestic Mild Cognitive Impairment (aMCI), 31 AD, 14 non-AD dementias, while accounting for age, sex, and education, known risk factors for dementia in Brasil and most relevant in countries in development.2 We further aimed to contribute by assessing interactions among landmarks of AD progression by correlating plasma biomarkers with image parameters.

Methods

The cohort included 356 T1w structural MRI from 261 subjects who were enrolled from 2011 to 2020 in a longitudinal study from a memory clinic environment in Rio de Janeiro, Brasil.3,4 From the cohort, we included a convenience sample by selecting the last MRI session and clinical assessment of 113 participants who met the inclusion criteria: T1w structural MRI images acquired with the same protocol at the same equipment, a 3T Philips Achieva; have an MRI acquisition and concurrent blood sample analyzed for the following plasma biomarkers: pTau217, pTau181, pTau217/Aβ42, pTau181/Aβ42, NfL, and GFAP. The number of subjects included in this study 113 subjects: 45 were Cognitive Unimpaired (CUI, 71.9 ± 4.6 years old), 23 amnestic Mild Cognitive Impairment (aMCI, 72.5 ± 5.9 years old), 31 AD (77.8 ± 7.1 years old), and 14 non-AD dementias (74.4 ± 7.2 years old). Non-AD dementias included patients with Lewy Body Dementia (LBD), Vascular Dementia (VD), and Frontotemporal dementia (FTD).

Plasma concentration of pTau217, pTau181, pTau217/Aβ42, pTau181/Aβ42, NfL, and GFAP assessed on a SIMOA HD-X instrument (Quanterix, Billerica, MA) as described in Santos et al., 2025.5

T1w MRI (TR/TE 7.2/3.4 ms; matrix 240x240 mm; FOV 240 mm; slice thickness 1 mm; 170 slices, Philips Achieva 3T) were processed with FreeSurfer v7.2.0 to extract hippocampus, ventricles, cortical, and white matter volumes, along with average cortical thickness. The image processing pipeline included visual quality control and minimal corrections in the brain mask and white surface when necessary.

To assess what combination of plasma and sMRI biomarkers would best discriminate between the groups, we included all variables in a Multinomial Logistic Model, with a Backward Stepwise strategy to remove the terms that would not improve the result based on AIC statistics. Age, Education, Sex, and Estimated Total Intracranial volume (eTIV) were included as forced entry terms to covariate and handle the possible bias and correct for head size. Diagnostic-stratified partial correlations of these variables were assessed to extend the understanding of biological events, also including Age, Education, and eTIV as covariates.

Results

The optimal model, composed of pTau217/Aβ42 and Lateral Ventricle, Inferior Lateral Ventricle, Hippocampus, Cortex, and Cerebral White Matter volumes, achieved 74% diagnostic accuracy, outperforming models based solely on plasma (56%) or imaging (72%) variables. Moreover, the model captured the temporal and spatial progression of AD (Figure 1), replicating the amyloid cascade burden hypothesis staging and the AT(X)N framework. aMCI differed significantly from CUI primarily by pTau217/Aβ42 concentration, whereas AD was distinguished by pTau217/Aβ42 and all imaging neurodegeneration terms. Education acted as a neuroprotector with a lower Odds Ratio in advanced dementia stages.

Partial correlation of biomarkers indicated that some of the variables not included in the model might have been replaced with those that aggregate more information and are more specific to AD progression.

Conclusion

We present an evidence-based model that reflects the temporal and spatial gradients of multiple AD biomarkers, replicating the amyloid cascade burden hypothesis staging. We verified this hypothesis by studying a Brazilian cohort cross-sectional subsample to increase data diversity and provide local validation of international findings. The results presented here reinforce the importance of both specific neurodegeneration index (Hippocampus volume), a global neurodegeneration term (Cortical volume), and a plasma biomarker specific for the AD physiopathology (pTau217/Aβ42, pTau181/Aβ42, or pTau217) when possible, to better classify a subject. This study also presents a measured effect of the neuroprotector effect of education, despite the high education levels of the sample, confirming the vast literature on the importance of formal education for dementia prevention.

We believe future studies could contribute to extending these findings by including longitudinal data and improving the discrimination model used to increase general accuracy.

These results also contribute to the recent advances in associations of plasma and structural MRI biomarkers by showcasing these relations in four scenarios. Although the resultant models did not include GFAP and NfL, the partial correlation results indicate their relevance in assessing the pathological evolution and their potential in anticipating larger neurostructural damage.

These findings reinforce the necessity of combining specific fluid and structural biomarkers, while considering their interactions, to improve early detection and monitoring in diverse clinical and research settings.
