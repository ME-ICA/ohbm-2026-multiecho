Introduction

Patients with major depressive disorders (MDD) hold profound negative biases towards self, others and the world (1), which tends to persist after recovery and drives the maintainence of depressive symptoms (2). Such negative bias originate from an inability to adjust expectations based on new information (3), a crucial process known as belief updating. Reversing the rigidity in belief updating has been the crucial target of intervention for multiple medical and psychotherapy treatments, including ketamine and cognitive behavioral therapy, both of which are hypothesized to restore the balance between priors and perceptual evidence (4). This underscores a systematic investigation on the hierarchical neural disrruption of belief updating of MDD.

The predictive processing theory posits that the rigidity of belief updating among MDD patients arises from a systematic alteration of information flow across the cortical hierarchy (4). This alteration stems from their unique energy-conservation strategy in adaptation to energy shortage (5). To minimize energy-consuming prediction errors (PEs) (i.e. signals that indicate a mismatch between prior beliefs and new sensory input), MDD patients would overweight prior beliefs (negative expectations) while attenuating the precision of ascending PE signals (5). An important outcome associated with this precision adjustment to be expected is enhancement in top-down and attenuation in bottom-up information flow (5). Previous studies have evidenced the change of precision in PE signals. However, a direct empirical test on the hypothesized dynamic hierarchical information flow, specifically, the simultaneous demonstration of the enhanced descending (top-down) information flow and reduced ascending (bottom-up) flow between critical hubs of the predictive processing circuit, remains lacking in MDD. This study aims to elucidate the neural dynamics underlying rigidity in belief updating across cortical hierarchy.

Methods

Sixty-nine unmedicated MDD patients were recruited from the Outpatient Department of Beijing Anding Hospital, affiliated with Capital Medical University. Sixty-two healthy controls (HC) were recruited from bulletin board postings.

Participants played a single, responder-role anonymous Ultimatum Game (UG) while conducting two equal-length (7 minutes) sessions of fMRI scanning in a E-prime 2.0 setup. Each participant played 24 trials with human proposers and 24 trials with computer proposers. We incorporated a mixed design of 2 (group: MDD/HC) * 2 (proposer type: human/computer) * 24 (ratio variation).

The analyses comprise behavioral and fMRI analyses (Fig. 1).

Computational modeling formulated the process of choice valuation, fairness norm learning, and choice selection. In the choice valuation process, structured in accordance with the Fehr-Schmidt inequality aversion formula, the relative weight of fairness concern to monetary concern was depicted by a free parameter named fairness sensitivity. The subjective fairness norm was updated in each round following the Rescorla-Wagner reinforcement learning rule. The belief-updating capability was indexed by the learning rate, a free parameter to be estimated in the model. Trial-wise PE and iteration of fairness norm could be extracted from the model. Model estimation for the MDD and the HC group were conducted separately given sample heterogeneity. Group-level and individual-level parameter estimates were performed simultaneously with the hierarchical Bayesian estimator in Rstan. 

Magnetic resonance images were acquired on a 3-Tesla Siemens Prisma MRI scanner at Beijing Anding Hospital, affiliated with Capital Medical University. Standard preprocessing steps were done. 

The trial-wise fluctuating PE was specified as a parametric regressor, resulting in the PE contrast map for each individual. We conducted an independent sample t-test to compare the group differences between MDD and HC on the neural encoding of PE (peak-level p <.001, cluster-level FWE p <.05). 

Dynamic causal modeling (DCM) was performed using DCM 12.5 in SPM 25.01.02. The following events were modeled as regressors in the 1st level GLM of the DCM analyses: (1) the proposer display stage, (2) the proposal display stage when individuals view the proposals with the 12 most negative PEs among all 48 proposals in the game when individuals experience the highest violation of expectations, (3) the proposal display stage when individuals view other proposals, (4) the choice stage; (5) the feedback display stage. The division of the proposal display stage follows the common use of the lowest quantile as the cut-off to dichotomize the split ratio (a continuous variables) in previous studies. More importantly, the other proposal condition provides a baseline where Mean (PE) approximates 0. Three matrices are specified in DCM, namely A, B, and C. As we did not center inputs, the A matrix represents the baseline intrinsic effective connectivity independent of task modulations. B specifies the modulation of the two focal proposal display events (when norm adaptation took place) on the effective connectivity. C specifies the driving inputs for each stage. A fully connected model was specified for matrix A. Only the diagonals were specified for matrix B to estimate the task modulation effects on the self-connections. The bilateral MOG from the visual cortex and the bilateral PI were the driving input regions in all five stages. 

Brain regions modulated by PE among HC generated from the second-level GLM (peak-level p <.001, cluster-level FWE p <.05) were selected as volumes of interest (VOIs). They were the left MOG, right MOG, left PI, right PI, left AI, right AI and ACC/MCC. Time series of VOI were extracted and group differences were estimated with Parametric Empirical Bayes. Results were reported at a threshold of posterior probability (free energy with versus without parameters) larger than .99 (indicating very strong evidence).

Results

The two groups exhibited no between-group differences in age, gender and educational level. The MDD group had significantly elevated self-reported depressive and anxiety scores compared to the HC group (p < .001). Excluding participants with excessive head motion in the fMRI analysis, the two groups did not differ in demographic information either.

The MDD group had a credibly higher level of initial fairness norm, a significantly lower level of learning rate, a credibly higher level of fairness sensitivity towards human proposers, but no credible difference towards computer proposers compared to the HC group. 

In the second-level GLM, we identified regions which encoded the learning signals among the MDD and the HC group. Specifically, among the HC group, activations in the postcentral gyrus, bilateral PI, right MCC, and right SPL/precuneus were positively modulated by PE, whereas activations in the bilateral MOG/Fusiform/Cuneus, ACC/MCC, and bilateral AI were negatively modulated by PE (cluster-level FWE p < .05 with a peak-level p < .001) . No regions encoding learning signals were detected among the MDD group (cluster-level FWE p < .05 with a peak-level p < .001). Group comparisons showed that the right PI was less positively modulated by PE in the MDD group than the HC group (cluster-level p < .05, peak-level p < .001). Extracting the average parametric estimate from the right PI, we found a significant correlation between brain activation and the learning rate (Pearson r = .33, p < .001). 

For baseline intrinsic connectivity specified by matrix A (Fig. 2a), several abnormal patterns of change among MDD patients could be delineated. First, MDD patients had increased excitatory connectivity originating from ACC/MCC to lower-level brain regions (i.e. lAI and rMOG), suggesting increased top-down prediction of presentation (belief) in MDD. Second, MDD patients generally had reduced excitatory connectivity originating from bilateral AI to other lower-level regions (i.e. from lAI to rAI and rMOG, from rAI to lAI and rMOG) and reduced absolute value of connectivity from the visual cortex (i.e. lMOG and rMOG) to rPI in opposite reversed excitatory/inhibitory nature compared to the HC group. Third, MDD patients had reduced self-connection among multiple brain regions (i.e. bilateral AI and ACC/MCC), indicating that these regions were more easily excited by afferent activity in MDD. 

For task modulation of the proposal display event when individuals viewed the 12 proposals with the most negative PEs and individuals experience the highest violation of expectations, a strong modulation effect on the self-connections were found in lMOG, bilateral PI, lAI and ACC/MCC in the commonalities matrix . For task modulation of the proposal display event when individuals viewed the other proposals excluding those with the lowest PEs, a strong modulation effect on the self-connections were found in rMOG and bilateral PI in the commonalities matrix. These findings indicated that processing of proposals with highest violation of expectations elicited excitation in a broader range of regions (i.e. lAI and ACC/MCC), which are related to emotional arousal and executive control, than other proposals. MDD group had reduced self-connection (i.e. reduced self-inhibition, or enhanced disinhibition) in the rPI in the task modulation of the proposal display event when individuals viewed the other proposals (Fig. 2b). For task modulation of the proposal display event when individuals viewed the 12 proposals with the lowest PEs, no group difference was found. 

Conclusion

Our study reveals impaired belief-updating in MDD within a social context, characterized by a lower learning rate and a stricter initial fairness norm toward human proposers.  In the norm adaptation process, rPI was less positively modulated by the learning signals (i.e. PE) among MDD. With DCM we found that MDD patients generally showed reduced connectivity originating from lower-level brain regions,but increased excitatory connectivity originating from ACC/MCC to lower-level brain regions, suggesting increased top-down prediction of presentation (belief) in MDD. MDD patients also had a reduced self-inhibition among bilateral AI and ACC/MCC. For task modulation of the proposal display events, MDD patients had a decreased self-inhibition in the rPI in the task context when they viewed the other proposals excluding the 12 proposals with the most negative PEs. 

Notably, computational modeling revealed that MDD patients exhibited heightened fairness sensitivity specifically toward human proposers, reinforcing prior findings of social hypersensitivity in depression, and selectively towards human proposers. Computational modeling confirmed impaired belief updating, supported by behavioral data showing a smaller main effect of time on decision outcomes among MDD patients, and echoing impaired belief-updating capability among MDD group from previous studies. 

Consistent with our hypothesis, the current study featured two patterns of alterations in the insular-cingulate communication among MDD patients. On the one hand, we found a unified reduced bottom-up effective connectivity among lower-level regions. Specifically, the reduced magnitude of effective connectivity from MOG to PI may signal a reduced amount of new environmental sensory information being integrated into higher-level regions, whereas the reduced excitatory effective connectivity from bilateral AI to other lower-level regions prohibits calibration of sensory inputs based on PEs, which reduces the precision of sensory inputs and PEs. Our overall decreased precision of sensory inputs and PEs among MDD patients aligns with an impaired PE signaling in the previous study. On the other hand, we found an enhanced excitatory effective connectivity from ACC/MCC to lower-level regions (i.e. AI and visual cortices), implying an enhanced top-down influence of established fairness norms on fairness appraisal. We also found that among MDD patients, the ACC/MCC and bilateral AI had reduced self-inhibition (i.e. enhanced sensitivity towards inputs from other regions), which reflects excessive precision over predictions in the top-down direction among these regions. As the prefrontal regions are densely and reciprocally connected with both the ACC/MCC and the AI in cognitive control processes, the reduced self-inhibition of ACC/MCC and bilateral AI among MDD patients may imply that they may be more sensitive towards inputs of higher-level representations about established prior beliefs about fairness norm from the right middle frontal gyrus (MFG), which was shown to be related to the encoding of fairness norm among the HC group  in predictive processing. These propositions align with previous studies showing an increased intrinsic functional connectivity between prefrontal cortices and ACC/AI among MDD patients. Overall, our findings align with the predictive processing theory of depression in terms of an overly precise negative priors (i.e. an overly strict fairness norm), an impaired upward PE signaling to update the priors, and excessive top-down control of priors to guide lower-level perception. We thus provide the first supportive evidence for the system of change in predictive processing among MDD patients. Future studies could develop intervention methods that are effective in reversing the unbalanced insular-cingulate neural dynamics in hierarchical inference.
