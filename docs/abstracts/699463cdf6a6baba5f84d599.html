Introduction

Intraoperative intracranial electrophysiology has long been used in neurosurgery to map human brain function, traditionally relying on macroelectrodes or low-density microelectrode recordings1. The relatively recent adaptation of Neuropixels probes for the human operating room has enabled neuroscientists to study human brain function using high-density, laminar, single-neuron recordings during neurosurgical procedures, with unprecedented resolution and yield2,3. However, evidence regarding safety, feasibility, and experimental conditions remains scattered across institutions. Here, we systematically review the literature on intraoperative human Neuropixels recordings to synthesize current evidence on study characteristics and reported safety outcomes. 

Methods

This systematic review was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines and was prospectively registered in PROSPERO. A comprehensive literature search was performed in MEDLINE (PubMed) and Embase, supplemented by backward citation searching, conference proceedings and clinical trial registries. Eligible studies included journal articles, preprints, conference abstracts, and clinical trial reports describing intraoperative Neuropixels recordings in human neurosurgical patients, Non-human studies and intracranial recordings using electrodes other than Neuropixels were excluded.

Titles and abstracts were screened independently by two reviewers, with full-text review performed when necessary to confirm eligibility. Data were extracted using a predefined data collection form capturing study characteristics, centre, patient population, anesthesia state, cortical region, task context, probe variant, and reported safety outcomes. We specifically assessed whether studies reported sequential case series or selected cases.

Results

The initial literature search identified 191 records. After title and abstract screening and full-text review, we identified a total of 13 reports describing intraoperative human Neuropixels, including 6 journal articles, 1 journal methods paper, 1 preprint, 2 conference abstracts, and 3 clinical trials. Of these, 7 articles reported sufficient patient-level data to permit data extraction and quantitative synthesis, comprising 75 patients across epilepsy, tumor, and functional neurosurgical procedures.

Recordings were predominantly obtained from temporal (46%) and frontal (45%) cortices, with more limited sampling of medial temporal (6%) and parietal (3%) regions. Recordings were conducted primarily in awake or monitored anesthesia care conditions (83%), with the remainder under general anesthesia (17%). Awake recordings predominantly involved speech production and perception. Only a subset of studies explicitly reported sequential case series, while in other reports it was unclear whether all attempted implants were included. To avoid bias from selective reporting, safety outcomes were evaluated exclusively in studies with individual participant data (65 patients). Within this subset, probe facture was relatively rare, reported in 5/65 implants (7.7%), occurring predominantly during early cases and less frequently in later reports, coinciding with increased team experience and the adoption of thick-shank Neuropixels (97 μm or 132 μm). In all cases, fractured probe segments were successfully retrieved intraoperatively, no neurological sequelae were reported.

Conclusion

Use of Neuropixels in human participants is being actively adopted by a small but growing community, with accumulating evidence supporting its feasibility and safety in specialized centres. We found that most reports are primarily designed to address basic neuroscience questions, for which detailed safety reporting naturally lies outside the scope of these publications. Nevertheless, as interest from additional institutions in establishing Neuropixels recording programs increases, clear and consistently reported safety data will help support smoother adoption. One practical approach would be a living systematic review that allows centres to contribute safety and feasibility data within a common minimum standard of reporting as new cases are performed.
