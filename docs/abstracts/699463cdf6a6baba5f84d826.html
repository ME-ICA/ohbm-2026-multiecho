Introduction

Recent developments in antipsychotics targeting muscarinic-cholinergic receptors strongly implicate this system in the pathophysiology of psychotic symptoms. While previous neuroimaging studies in people with psychotic disorders have found grey matter aberrations in cholinergic nuclei such as basal forebrain, the spatial colocalization of these alterations with acetylcholine receptor and transporter expression remains largely unexplored.

Methods

Here, we investigate whether grey matter alterations associated with symptom severity, measured using positive and negative syndrome scale (PANSS), are associated with normative cortical and subcortical M1-muscarinic (mAChR), α4β2-nicotinic (nAChR) receptor, and presynaptic vesicular acetylcholine transporter (VAChT) topographies. We used structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) data from the Human Connectome Project S1200 release (n=1113) and from 45 individuals with available retest sessions 1 to test intercorrelations between cholinergic seed and target regions markers. Analyses of schizophrenia symptoms were performed with PANSS and MRI data from 196 participants of the Transdiagnostic Connectome Project. T1-weighted magnetic resonance imaging data were preprocessed using the Computational Anatomy Toolbox (CAT12.9), deriving normalized grey matter probability maps with a resolution 1.5 mm isotropic. Basal forebrain voxels were delineated based on post-mortem cytoarchitectonic maps 2, thresholded at 50% probability, and average grey matter volume was extracted for each participant. A general linear model was fit to estimate grey matter associations of negative and positive PANSS subscales, while controlling for age, sex, intracranial volume and site effects as additional regressors. Single participant indices for M1-mAChR3, α4β2-nAChR4 and VAChT5 were determined based on the colocalization of their density topography with an individual’s residual grey matter map. Negative and positive symptom effects and their general linear model contrast maps were tested for spatial colocalization with normative M1-mAChR, α4β2-nAChR and VAChT density patterns using Spearman correlation coefficients, and statistical inference accounted for spatial autocorrelation using 1000 surrogate null maps.

Results

Cholinergic seed and target region markers showed good test-retest reliability for the 45 HCP participants undergoing two independent MRI sessions. M1-mAChR indices showed the most pronounced intercorrelation with basal forebrain grey matter in subcortical regions. Grey matter associations of positive symptoms were significantly spatially colocalized with VAChT and M1-mAChR density patterns. Topographical correspondence was particularly strong in subcortical regions, with the highest associations in nucleus accumbens and the lowest in thalamic regions.

Conclusion

The results show that cholinergic seed and target region markers have both, converging and complementary associations within an individual’s grey matter expression. Spatial colocalization of positive symptom effects and M1-mAChR density underscores implications of this receptor for psychosis symptoms. Further research should combine neuroimaging of cholinergic and dopaminergic seed and target region markers, as well as different disease stages of psychotic episodes and medication status to understand how cholinergic and dopaminergic system interplay underlies schizophrenia symptoms.
