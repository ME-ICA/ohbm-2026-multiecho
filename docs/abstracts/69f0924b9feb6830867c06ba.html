Introduction

Neuroimaging research relies on increasingly complex computational workflows and large-scale datasets. The Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS) ^1 has successfully standardized data organization, while Boutiques ^2—a descriptive command-line framework—has facilitated the reproducible execution of processing pipelines. However, standardizing the entire flow of multi-site data processings and tracking the status of individual distributed datasets for collaborative projects remain significant challenges. Moreover, software availability alone does not guarantee accessibility; researchers frequently struggle with complex installations, container management, and intricate system configurations. These challenges hinder reproducibility, impede the adoption of FAIR principles ^3, and complicate the harmonization required for advanced applications such as federated learning.

To address these hurdles, Nipoppy ^4 provides a lightweight, standardized framework for the curation, processing, and tracking of clinical-neuroimaging data, from raw clinical and imaging inputs to extracted imaging derived phenotypes (IDPs). Crucially, to mitigate deployment barriers, we introduce the Nipoppy Pipeline Store—a registry of ready-to-use, containerized pipelines actively developed by the community
 (https://zenodo.org/search?q=metadata.subjects.subject%3A%22Nipoppy%22).

However, to address the challenges of reproducible neuroimaging research, we must expand our scope beyond tool development. The core aim of this work is to evolve Nipoppy into a comprehensive ecosystem and community integrated with the BIDS community. This strategic shift is centered on three pillars:

  1. FAIR pipelines: Creating a centralized Nipoppy Pipeline Store on Zenodo.

  2. Standardization: Better integration with existing standards (e.g., BIDS Study specification and Boutiques).

  3. Education: Launching a comprehensive video tutorial series on using Nipoppy.

Methods

Pillar 1: FAIR pipelines

Nipoppy provides a standardized framework to organize, process, and track clinical-neuroimaging studies. The newly developed Nipoppy Pipeline Store serves as a centralized registry of (pre-)processing pipelines, made FAIR-compliant via persistent records on Zenodo. By combining containerized pipelines with Boutiques’ command-line descriptors and invocation files, we ensure the reproducible execution of studies. Nipoppy simplifies pipeline reuse and accelerates the onboarding of new researchers by making pipelines discoverable, portable, and shareable across institutions.

To facilitate pipeline integration and dissemination, we transitioned from packaging pipelines with Nipoppy to leveraging the Zenodo platform. We created a standardized pipeline bundle with required configuration files and developed new CLI commands to facilitate pipeline creation, upload, search, and installation from the Nipoppy Pipeline Store. In addition to the public “Nipoppy” tag for identifying compatible records, we established a Zenodo community to curate pipelines supported by the software. As a proof of concept, the ENIGMA-PD working group created a dedicated Zenodo community to aggregate the various Nipoppy pipelines used in their processing workflows.

Pillar 2: Standardization

In collaboration with Boutiques, we implemented features enabling Nipoppy to use native Boutiques descriptors rather than relying on a custom schema. This allows Nipoppy pipeline descriptors to be utilized by the broader neuroimaging community without being limited to the Nipoppy ecosystem. Conversely, this facilitates the reuse of existing Boutiques descriptors when creating new Nipoppy pipelines.

Furthermore, we are working closely with the BIDS community to develop the BIDS study specification (https://github.com/bids-standard/bids-specification/pull/2191) to allow a rawdata folder in the study layout, and integrate the specification as a Nipoppy layout (https://github.com/nipoppy/nipoppy/pull/687). This collaboration ensures that Nipoppy aligns with standards adopted by the wider community, thereby facilitating interoperability between neuroimaging studies, regardless of whether they are processed with Nipoppy or other software tools.

Pillar 3: Education

To complement our documentation, we developed an ongoing video tutorial series teaching the fundamentals of Nipoppy, with specific learning objectives from data curation to IDP extraction. To further support adoption, we launched a Discord server to host our user support and Q&A forum. Additionally, Nipoppy developers have actively participated in several events—including Brainhack SOBP, the Taiwan Open Brain Science Workshop, and OHBM Brainhack—hosting tutorials and providing hands-on guidance to new users.

Results

Dissemination & Community Growth

Since its launch in May 2025, the Nipoppy Pipeline Store has grown to host 28 pipelines from 11 unique contributors, amassing over 1,000 total downloads (as of Dec. 9, 2025). We successfully curated 15 pipelines within the core Nipoppy Zenodo community, while the ENIGMA-PD working group established an independent community hosting four specialized pipelines.

Technical Standardization

We successfully removed the dependency on custom schemas by integrating native Boutiques descriptors. Currently, 11 legacy pipelines have been converted to utilize native Boutiques descriptors, with the remaining custom descriptors scheduled for deprecation. Regarding the BIDS Study Specification, we developed a prototype Nipoppy layout aligned with the evolving standard. We also collaborated with BIDS maintainers to advance the proposal (https://github.com/bids-standard/bids-specification/pull/2191), specifically regarding the inclusion of a rawdata directory within the study specification.

Education

Our video tutorial series (https://nipoppy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/videos/how-to) currently includes five modules covering core commands (init, bidsify, process, etc.). Production is underway for five additional tutorials to cover advanced features, conceptual overviews, and output file interpretation. The Nipoppy Discord community has over 50 members and more than 30 posts in its dedicated user support forum (https://discord.gg/6qBZz6AVFh).

Conclusion

This work establishes Nipoppy as a comprehensive ecosystem for reproducible neuroimaging. By integrating a centralized pipeline registry, community standards, and educational resources, we provide a robust framework that lowers deployment barriers and address the challenging needs of neuroimaging research. Moving forward, we aim to expand the Nipoppy Pipeline Store and strengthen integrations with community tools like Neurodesk and NiWrap.
