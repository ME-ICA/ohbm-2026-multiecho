Introduction

Resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) is a widely used tool for investigating the brain’s functional organization. Despite its widespread use, rs-fMRI has yet to deliver reproducible, clinically meaningful biomarkers. A key limitation is the methodological heterogeneity in the field. Transforming raw data into reliable rs-fMRI features involves numerous preprocessing and analytical steps, each requiring decisions which can affect results. This limitation is particularly problematic in large-scale, multi-site initiatives such as the ENIGMA Consortium, where methodological heterogeneity can hinder cross-site integration and bias results.

A central challenge lies in the lack of consensus on denoising strategies. While multiverse analyses have been proposed to explore this variability1, it remains unclear how to select a single pipeline to report as the primary result. Previous studies have assessed confound denoising using selected quality control metrics2, but no consensus exists on the most effective approach.

This project provides a guideline for denoising based on motion-quality metrics and quantitative analytic insights into functional connectivity, a cornerstone of contemporary rs-fMRI applications. This guideline aims to inform the selection and reporting of the optimal denoising strategy per dataset and clarifies the trade-off between denoising choices and analytical results.

Methods

Datasets

We analyzed two open-access rs-fMRI datasets: (1) the UCLA Consortium for Neuropsychiatric Phenomics3 (ds000030; n = 272) and the Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange 1 4(ABIDE1; n = 1112).

Data preprocessing

Preprocessing and connectivity matrices were generated using a combined atlas with HALFpipe5, applying 18 distinct denoising strategies (Figure 1A). All strategies included high-pass filtering. 

Both automatic and visual QC procedures were performed. Automatic QC excluded participants with a mean framewise displacement > 0.5 mm or a maximum displacement > 3.0 mm. After QC, the final samples included n = 641 for ABIDE1 and n = 193 for ds000030.

Correlation matrices across pipelines

To assess the convergence and divergence of denoising strategies on averaged connectomes, feature-by-feature correlation matrices were computed for each subject and then averaged across subjects to produce a single group-level 18×18 correlation matrix (Figure 1A).

Evaluation metrics

To assess the effects of denoising on connectomes, we used Wonkyconn (https://github.com/HALFpipe/wonkyconn), a BIDS-App compatible with HALFpipe outputs (Figure 1B), which automatically generates five metrics across two categories: 

Motion-based metrics:

  • Distance-dependent motion effects (DM-FC): correlation between Euclidean distances between brain regions and their corresponding Quality control / functional connectivity values (controlling for age and sex).

  • Global correlation (GCor): average correlation across all voxel pairs in the brain, representing the overall connectivity strength.

Analytical insight metrics:

  • Network similarity: Pearson correlation between each subject’s seed-based map and Yeo’s default mode network (DMN) binary map.

  • Network differences: Difference between the mean connectivity value of the DMN vs vision network and DMN vs fronto-parietal network.

  • Gradient similarity: Spearman correlations between each subject’s five connectivity gradients and group-level gradients reported in the literature.

Temporal degrees of freedom (tDOF) were also estimated, accounting for the total number of nuisance regressors and censored volumes. Each pipeline was ranked from 1 to 18 for each metric: lower values indicated better performance for motion-based metrics, whereas higher values indicated better performance for analytical insight metrics. 

Results

Across both datasets, all denoising strategies produced positively correlated connectomes, indicating that the connectivity structure is largely preserved despite differences in preprocessing. 

In ds000030, pipelines including GSR formed a cluster with high inter-correlation, while showing lower correlations with pipelines that did not include GSR (Figure 2 a). In ABIDE1, four main clusters emerged: GSR pipelines (highly correlated with each other, except when combined with ICA-AROMA), ICA-AROMA pipelines, motion parameter-only pipelines, and pipelines including WM/CSF regression (Figure 2 b). 

Denoising pipeline differed across quality metrics and the ranking varied across datasets (Figure 2 c-d). For motion-based metrics, pipelines including all available confounds ranked highest for both datasets, but with the largest loss of tDOF (27%). The baseline pipeline ranked lowest for motion control but preserved all tDOF (0%). 

For analytical insight-based metrics, in ds000030, pipelines including motion or tissue signal ranked highest with 17% tDOF loss, whereas the baseline pipeline ranked lowest. In ABIDE1, aCompCor+GSR pipelines performed best (19% tDOF loss), while ICA-AROMA pipelines ranked lowest (12% tDOF loss).

Conclusion

Overall, our findings demonstrate that denoising choices introduce substantial and systematic variability in rs-fMRI analyses, despite preserving a shared connectome structure across pipelines. The emergence of distinct pipeline clusters, and their differential performance across datasets, underscores that no single denoising strategy is universally optimal. Instead, pipeline performance is strongly dataset-dependent and reflects trade-offs between motion control, preservation of network structure, and loss of tDOF

By jointly evaluating motion-based and analytical insight metrics, our work provides a principled framework for selecting and justifying a primary denoising pipeline on a per-dataset basis, rather than relying on arbitrary or convention-driven choices. Importantly, these metrics enable transparent comparison across pipelines while maintaining compatibility with multiverse analyses. This approach is particularly critical for large-scale, multi-site consortia such as ENIGMA, where methodological heterogeneity can hide biological effects and limit cross-site comparability.

Our results argue against selective reporting of a single pipeline and instead support a reproducible workflow in which analytic variability is explicitly quantified. Adopting such standardized, metric-driven denoising guidelines will improve cross-study comparability, strengthen the robustness of rs-fMRI findings, and facilitate the development of clinically meaningful functional connectivity biomarkers in psychiatric research.
