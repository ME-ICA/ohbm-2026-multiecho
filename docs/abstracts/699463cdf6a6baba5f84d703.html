Introduction

Background: Somatic awareness is a fundamental practice for novice mindfulness practitioners. It has been proposed that mindfulness meditation training enhances brain function through somatic awareness in both non-clinical and clinical populations [1]. However, the neural signatures underlying this mechanism remain poorly understood.

Methods

In our study, we conducted mindfulness interventions in both healthy and major depressive disorder (MDD) populations. Participants in the healthy population were randomly assigned to a 4-week brief mindfulness (MD) workshop, i.e., the Koru Mindfulness curriculum [2] or a non-treatment (NC) group, while participants in the depressed population were randomly assigned to an 8-week mindfulness-based cognitive therapy group or a waitlist (WL) group. All healthy participants underwent two sessions of 10-minute resting state fMRI scans pre-intervention and at two-month follow-up, while all MDD participants underwent a session of 6-minute resting state fMRI scans pre- and post-intervention. At last, 30 healthy participants (aged 23.83±3.56 years) remained in the MD group, 21 (aged 23.86±2.39 years) in the NC group; while 16 MDD participants (aged 30.44±6.17 years) remained in the MD group, 19 (aged 29.10±6.56 years) in the WL group. We examined whether somatomotor network (SMN)-related edge-centric functional connectivity (eFC) is modulated by mindfulness, uncovering the population common and population-specific functioning pathways. Specifically, we revealed the eFCs showing significant group-by-time interaction in each population with different thresholds (p < 0.01 or p < 0.001), and projected these eFCs to Yeo’s 7-network [3] plus a subcortical network and performed a permutation test to examine whether there was an above-chance number of eFCs in a category of Yeo’s network. In addition, we tested whether such SMN-related eFCs can predict the insomnia status in two populations separately via a support vector regression analysis.

Results

Our findings revealed population-common eFC changes after mindfulness intervention involving the SMN and attentional networks (Figure 1. C, F, I & L). Additionally, we identified population-specific eFC profiles, regulated by mindfulness training, engaging SMN-subcortical networks in the healthy population (Figure 1. C, F) and SMN-default mode network interactions in the depressed population (Figure 1. I, L), demonstrating the neural signatures underlying different focuses of mindfulness via somatic awareness in differential populations. Furthermore, support vector regression analysis demonstrated that these altered eFCs, regulated by mindfulness, significantly predicted improvements in insomnia after meditation training in both populations (Figure 2). 

Conclusion

In summary, our results highlight both shared and distinct neural functioning signatures underlying mindfulness-related mental health improvements via somatic awareness in both the healthy and depressed populations.
