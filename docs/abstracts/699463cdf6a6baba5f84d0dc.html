Introduction

Cognitive dysfunction is a common complication following cardiac surgery.1 However, the impact of cardiopulmonary bypass (“on-pump”) versus off-pump surgical techniques on postoperative brain states remains controversial.2 In this study, we employed connectivity gradient analysis to characterize differences in cortical hierarchical reorganization between on-pump and off-pump cardiac surgeries, aiming to elucidate patterns of postoperative brain network changes and to provide a potential for informing prognosis after cardiac surgery.

Methods

A total of 93 patients were included in the study (on-pump, n = 33; off-pump, n = 60), along with 30 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. All participants underwent resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI), blood biochemical testing, and behavioral assessments before and within one week after surgery. Individual functional connectivity matrices were used to compute brain connectivity gradients, reflecting the hierarchical organization of the cortex.3 The first two principal gradients were selected for further analyses. The global gradient dispersion was calculated to assess overall network differentiation. The mean gradient values and centroid radial distances were extracted across seven canonical functional networks to characterize global and network-specific hierarchical changes.4 Two-sample t-test was applied to detect the postoperative effects of surgical techniques on cortical hierarchical organization. The approach of machine learning was used to inform the prognosis of cardiac surgery in patients.

Results

Connectivity gradient analysis revealed significant alterations in cortical hierarchical organization after cardiac surgery. Compared with healthy controls, both on- and off-pump surgical groups exhibited reduced global gradient dispersion, indicating decreased cortical differentiation. However, only the off-pump group showed a significant postoperative decline relative to preoperative levels, suggesting a greater impact of this type of surgical intervention. At the network level, the off-pump group demonstrated a significant postoperative decline of centroid radial distances in frontal-parietal, default mode, and sensorimotor networks, while the on-pump group had no significant change. Cardiac comorbidities further amplified these effects. The patients with both coronary artery disease and hypertension exhibited the most pronounced gradient reductions. Finally, we designed a linear discriminant analysis model incorporating preoperative gradient features, surgical types, and comorbidity variables, which can be used to predict postoperative status, with an accuracy of prediction reaching 75%.

Conclusion

In summary, the fMRI results revealed the changes in brain networks after both on- and off-pump cardiac surgeries. Our findings provided evidence that the gradient metrics of brain networks may serve as preoperative biomarkers to characterize and predict the status of patients after cardiac surgery.
