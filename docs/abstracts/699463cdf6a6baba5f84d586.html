Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is fundamentally a tauopathy, characterized by the abnormal accumulation and propagation of misfolded tau proteins throughout vulnerable brain circuits1. To that end, striking efforts have been made to model the propagation of tau aggregates under the constraint of brain connectomes2. However, current computational models exclusively focus on biological mechanisms, despite growing evidence that exposome factors critically influence how tau aggregates disseminate across neural circuits. To address this limitation, we develop a multi-layer graph diffusion model where we allow tau aggregates to spread through both structural connectivity (SC) and functional connectivity (FC). Central to this integration is an explicit characterization of how SC and FC interact at each brain region to jointly contribute to pathological spread. After evaluating the model performance in predicting future tau aggregation, we specifically investigate the effect of modifiable risk factors, such as cardiovascular risks and mobility decline, on the propagation of tau aggregates. Our findings on ADNI dataset3 indicate that (1) tau pathology follows a distinct spatial pattern of selective vulnerability, in which either SC or FC emerges as the dominant conduit of spreading, and (2) modifiable risk factors play a significant role in reshaping these selective patterns of preference pathways.

Methods

Data description. To characterize how tau pathology spreads along functional and structural networks, we analyze multimodal longitudinal ADNI (Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative) data (T1-weighted MRI, resting-state fMRI, diffusion MRI, and tau PET) comprising 839 scans from 539 participants (55–94 years). Most participants contributed one scan; others had 2–5 scans separated by ~1 year. Each brain is parcellated into 148 cortical and 12 subcortical regions (Destrieux atlas; FreeSurfer). Structural connectivity (160×160) was derived via probabilistic tractography, and functional connectivity (160×160) from correlations of regional BOLD time courses. Tau SUVR (Standardized Uptake Value Ratio) values are calculated using the cerebellum and brainstem as reference tissue.


Multi-layer graph diffusion model for tau propagation. Given baseline tau  x0∈R160, our model predicts future tau x1∈R160  by leveraging both SC and FC with adjacency matrices  S=[sij]  and  F=[sij], respectively. We embed tau SUVR into an energy-based latent space u=ϕ(x), where ϕ  is a learnable mapping function. To account for this, we introduce two coupled latent states,  us and uf, representing SC-driven and FC-driven propagation, respectively. Let  ∆s and  ∆f denote the Laplacians of the SC and FC graphs, we allow each state to evolve on its own connectivity network while interacting through learnable cross-layer terms, forming a two-layer network model: ∂us/∂t=-∆sus+λsMsuf ,  ∂uf/∂t=-∆fuf+λfMfus, with the constraint ϕ-1(us+uf) = x1. Trainable coupling matrices Ms , Mf  and weights λs ,  λf are introduced to enable modeling cross-talk between SC and FC layers. Following the notion of optimal control, we cast the energy functional into a linear quadratic regulator  L=1/2 usT Qus+1/2 ufT Ruf, where  Q and  R are diagonal weight matrices. Using Pontryagin’s Minimum Principle, we have the closed-form solution4 for Q̂  and  R̂. Furthermore, we integrate the principle of multi-layer graph diffusion into a physics-informed neural network, as shown in Fig. 1 top. Specifically, the multi-layer perception (MLP) layers (with parameter θ) map tau SUVR to potential energy  us and  uf,   Ms and Mf  compute SC-FC interaction as a feedback control to the multi-layer tau propagation. The coupled PDE is numerically integrated to obtain us  and  uf, and a supervised neural network  ϕθ-1 maps their terminal condition to predicted tau x̂¹  at the follow-up time point. The objective minimizes  ‖x¹ − x̂¹‖₂2 ensuring faithful prediction while maintaining biological interpretability of SC-FC contributions.  

Uncover the selective vulnerability of connectome conduits in AD. This feed-back control mechanism learns subject- and region-specific weights, indicating how SC and FC synergistically steer tau propagation. Designed toward such mechanistic interpretability, the latent variables us and uf explicitly capture SC- and FC-dominant propagation components, allowing the detection of regional shifts in dominant pathways as the disease progresses. We hypothesize that such shifts can be quantitatively examined against known modifiable risk factors, including mobility impairment, hypertension, smoking, and alcohol use.

Results

Model performance evaluation. In a 5-fold cross-validation, joint modeling of SC and FC shows a clear gain in prediction performance. As the scatter plots shown in Fig. 1 bottom, we perform an ablation study under three regimes: SC only (left), FC only (middle), and SC+FC interaction (right)—with each point colored by its anatomical lobe. Fitting a simple linear regression between observed and predicted tau SUVR yields: SC only (slope=0.93, r2=0.99), FC only (slope=0.89, r2=0.97) and SC+FC (slope=0.98,  r2=0.99). MAE (mean absolute error) between observed and predicted tau SUVR at each brain region is mapped to the cortical surface, with color maps displayed at the far-right side. Apparently, our multi-layer model (SC+FC) recovers an unbiased (near unity) relationship to ground truth.



Selective vulnerability of network conduit in tau propagation. In Fig. 2, we show the bar plot of the disentangled volumes of tau aggregation associated with SC ( us in red) and FC ( uf in blue), across brain lobes, using all ADNI subjects that have longitudinal tau PET scans. For each brain region, we examine whether SC leads the predominant role in local tau propagation (i.e., us > uf  at a significant level of p<0.05) or vice versa, using a one-sided paired t-test. As shown in the left panel of Fig. 2, in the cognitively normal population, tau spread within association cortices and subcortical hubs is largely FC-driven. Specifically, the Frontoparietal, Default Mode, Ventral Attention, Dorsal Attention, and subcortical regions exhibit median FC fractions of approximately 80–90%, suggesting that short-term tau accumulation (over 1–2 years) is more aligned with synchronous neural activity than with underlying structural tracts. In contrast, as illustrated in the right panel of Fig. 2, the population with modifiable risk factors demonstrates a clear shift in the dominant pattern of tau propagation, as detailed below.

Effect of mobility impairment and cardiovascular risk on the selective mechanisms of tau propagation. 

To further examine how modifiable health risks shape network-level vulnerability, we assessed the selective mechanisms of SC- versus FC-driven tau propagation across subgroups like gait impairment, alcohol misuse, hypertension, and smoking. First, mobility impairment exerted the most widespread influence, shifting dominant pathways across multiple large-scale systems. Individuals with abnormal gait showed reversals in the sensory cortices, somatomotor regions, cingulo-opercular and dorsal–ventral attention networks, and auditory association areas. This broad rebalancing toward FC-driven propagation suggests that mobility decline may amplify short-term, activity-dependent spread of tau, particularly within systems supporting visual perception, auditory processing, and attentional control. Second, cardiovascular risks also produced selective and coherent changes. Hypertension was associated with dominance shifts concentrated in early sensory and motor cortices, consistent with vascular-related disruptions that preferentially weaken SC propagation along primary pathways. Alcohol misuse, in contrast, primarily affected the ventral attention and auditory systems, indicating a redistribution of propagation routes toward FC-dominant mechanisms in circuits involved in salience detection and sensory integration. Smoking exhibited a distinct pattern, with effects restricted mainly to the default mode network, where SC-to-FC reversals suggest increased reliance on functional co-activation within transmodal association cortices.

Together, these findings highlight that mobility and cardiovascular risks do not uniformly accelerate tau spread but instead reconfigure the balance between structural and functional connectivity. Such reorganization underscores the importance of health disparities in shaping the mechanistic pathways through which tau pathology disseminates across the connectome.

Conclusion

In this work, we systematically quantified structure–function propagation balance across multiple risk factors, including gait impairment, alcohol misuse, hypertension, and smoking. Each factor induces distinct patterns of SC–FC dominance shifts. By showing network-specific reversals rather than global changes, our analysis highlights a mechanistic pathway through which behavioral and vascular risks reorganize large-scale brain communication. These findings provide a simple and interpretable connectome-level marker for characterizing early network alterations associated with modifiable risk states.
