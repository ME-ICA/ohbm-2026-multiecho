Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is characterized by persistent impairments in social communication and interaction1. Most electroencephalography (EEG) studies in ASD have relied on resting-state paradigms, which capture spontaneous activity but place minimal demands on social information processing and may therefore underestimate neural differences that emerge in real-life social contexts2,3. In contrast, task states such as viewing socially rich videos more closely approximate everyday interaction and impose direct demands on developing social networks, providing a window into how network coordination may diverge between ASD and typically developing (TD) youth.

EEG microstates, often described as atoms of thought, index transient large-scale network configurations and have shown sensitivity to several psychiatric conditions4. However, it remains unclear whether microstate properties and temporal dynamics differ between ASD and TD children/adolescents across rest and social tasks, and whether such differences primarily arise from changes in signal amplitude or from reorganization of phase-based functional connectivity. Moreover, the developmental and behavioral relevance of these microstate-related connectivity patterns, particularly their links to age and to individual variability in social behavior, remains insufficiently understood.

The present study focused on children and adolescents (<18 years) with ASD and TD controls, comparing EEG microstate dynamics during eyes-open rest and a naturalistic social video task. We combined microstate metrics, global alpha-band amplitude, and microstate-resolved phase-locking value (PLV) to dissociate amplitude from phase coordination effects, used data-driven clustering to identify connectivity modes, and tested whether individual differences in global phase synchrony moderated the association between autistic traits and gaze toward social cues, with preliminary consideration of developmental stage.

Methods

We analyzed EEG data from 89 children and adolescents with ASD and 62 TD participants under 18 years of age. All participants completed eyes-open resting-state EEG followed by a social video viewing task while eye movements were recorded simultaneously. Social gaze behavior was quantified as the proportion of viewing time directed to socially relevant regions of interest, and autistic traits were assessed using the Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS).

EEG analyses focused on the alpha band5. For both rest and task, continuous EEG was submitted to microstate analysis with a fixed number of microstates (K = 5). For each participant and condition, we derived mean duration, occurrence rate, coverage, and a multiscale sample entropy measure computed on the microstate sequence to index temporal complexity and instability. Group-level comparisons first examined ASD–TD differences in microstate metrics separately for rest and task using ANOVA, followed by multiple-comparison correction. To assess the diagnostic informativeness of these metrics, we trained linear support vector machine (SVM) classifiers to distinguish ASD from TD based on microstate features within each state, and quantified performance using accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and area under the ROC curve (AUC), as well as feature weights.

To probe underlying mechanisms, we decomposed the continuous EEG into segments assigned to each canonical microstate. Within each microstate, we computed (i) global alpha amplitude and (ii) a whole-brain functional connectivity matrix based on PLV across all electrode pairs. For each microstate and individual, we derived a microstate-resolved global PLV index. At the microstate level, we compared ASD and TD groups on global alpha amplitude and PLV using ANOVA with false discovery rate correction across microstates.

Next, we applied data-driven clustering to identify PLV-based connectivity modes. For each participant, we constructed a 5-dimensional feature vector comprising robustly standardized PLV indices for the five microstates, after age- and sex-adjustment using TD data as reference. In rest and task separately, ASD and TD participants were pooled. We then performed robust column-wise standardization, followed by principal component analysis (PCA) and retained enough components to explain approximately 85% of the variance. K-means clustering was run on the PCA scores, and the optimal number of clusters was determined using multiple criteria (silhouette, Calinski–Harabasz, and gap statistics), with stability evaluated via repeated solutions and adjusted Rand index. This procedure consistently yielded two stable clusters corresponding to relatively high-PLV and low-PLV connectivity modes.

Finally, we examined group and mode differences in global PLV using factorial ANOVAs with Group and PLV cluster as between-subject factors, conducted separately for rest and task. For task-related microstate C, we additionally tested ASD–TD differences within the high-PLV mode and explored associations with age.

To assess whether phase synchrony modulates the link between autistic traits and social behavior, we fitted linear moderation models with social gaze proportion as the dependent variable, SRS score as the predictor, global microstate-specific PLV as the moderator, and age as a covariate. Moderation analyses were conducted separately for children under 12 years and for adolescents 12 years and older, with simple slopes examined at low (−1 SD), mean, and high (+1 SD) PLV levels.

Results

At rest, ASD and TD groups showed relatively small differences in classical microstate metrics: durations, coverage, and occurrence rates of most microstates exhibited only modest shifts. In contrast, during the social video task, between-group differences were significantly amplified. Multiscale sample entropy of the microstate sequence did not differ between groups at rest, whereas during the task ASD and TD diverged significantly across multiple time scales (Fig. 1A). Across-state comparisons revealed no significant rest–task differences in ASD, whereas TD participants exhibited robust microstate reconfiguration from rest to task, suggesting reduced task-related flexibility in ASD.

SVM classification based on resting-state microstate features yielded only modest accuracy and AUC, indicating limited discriminative power. In contrast, classifiers trained on task microstate metrics showed substantially improved sensitivity, specificity, and overall AUC (Fig. 1B). The duration of microstate C and the coverage of microstate A emerged as the features with the largest absolute weights (Fig. 1B).

Across all microstates, global alpha amplitude did not differ significantly between ASD and TD, indicating no robust group differences in alpha power at the microstate level. By contrast, microstate-resolved global PLV showed significant ASD–TD differences for every microstate examined (Fig. 1C), and PLV values within each microstate were correlated with GFP, suggesting that ASD is characterized by widespread alterations in task-related functional connectivity patterns that cannot be explained by amplitude differences alone.

Clustering of PLV profiles identified two stable connectivity modes, high PLV and low PLV, in both rest and task conditions. Factorial ANOVAs demonstrated that, within both ASD and TD groups, high- and low-PLV modes differed significantly in global phase synchrony at both states (Fig. 1D). In the task condition, participants belonging to the high-PLV mode for microstate C still showed significant ASD–TD differences in PLV, and these differences were further associated with age.

Moderation analyses further demonstrated that among ASD participants under 12 years, microstate-specific PLV significantly moderated the relationship between SRS scores and social gaze. Simple slope analyses showed that, in microstate A and C, autistic traits were strongly and negatively associated with social gaze in the low-PLV mode, whereas this association disappeared in the high-PLV mode (Fig. 1E). No significant moderation effects were observed in adolescents aged 12 years and older. 

Conclusion

This study provides evidence that task-related EEG microstate dynamics and phase-based functional connectivity are more sensitive than resting-state measures for characterizing alterations in children and adolescents with ASD. Whereas classic microstate metrics at rest showed only subtle ASD–TD differences, the social video task revealed pronounced group divergences in both microstate temporal properties and sequence complexity, particularly involving microstates A and C. TD participants displayed reconfiguration from rest to task, while ASD microstate patterns remained comparatively rigid, suggesting that atypical social functioning in ASD may be linked to reduced flexibility in large-scale network dynamics under socio-cognitive demands.

By separating amplitude and phase components, we demonstrate that ASD–TD differences during the social task arise primarily from aberrant phase coordination rather than from changes in alpha-band amplitude. These findings indicate that ASD is characterized by altered organization of functional connectivity embedded in transient network states. Data-driven clustering further revealed high-PLV and low-PLV connectivity modes in both rest and task conditions, across ASD and TD participants. Despite this shared mode structure, ASD individuals in the task-related high-PLV mode, especially for microstate C, show age-related lower PLV relative to TD. This pattern suggests that atypical increases in task-related phase synchrony may reflect a core, developmentally sensitive alteration in ASD, superimposed on broader interindividual variability in connectivity.

Crucially, microstate-specific PLV moderated the association between autistic traits and social gaze behavior in ASD children under 12 years, but not in older adolescents. These results imply that the development of microstate-resolved connectivity may buffer or amplify how autistic traits manifest in observable social attention during childhood.

Overall, our findings refine the view of ASD as a disorder of dynamic network coordination that is accentuated in social contexts relative to rest, and highlight microstate-specific phase synchrony as a promising neural marker for developmental monitoring.
