Introduction

         Diffusion MRI (dMRI) provides a rich set of microstructural measures that are sensitive to aging, disease, and behavior, yet most studies rely on a single modeling framework and a limited subset of parameters. Advances in diffusion modeling such as free-water elimination diffusion tensor imaging (FWE-DTI)1, neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI2), and multi-shell multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution (MSMT-CSD3) enable parallel estimation of complementary tissue properties from the same acquisition. However, the rapid growth in available dMRI measures has outpaced the development of scalable workflows for evaluating them efficiently and consistently, particularly in large cohort studies.

         Voxel-based analysis (VBA) provides an exploratory framework for identifying spatially localized effects across the brain, but its application to multi-parameter dMRI remains effortful to configure, difficult to reproduce and extend, and challenging to interpret. These barriers are especially limiting for trainees, non-specialist neuroimagers, and investigators seeking to screen candidate diffusion measures for downstream computational modeling or prediction. As a result, potentially informative microstructural parameters are often excluded a priori rather than evaluated empirically. 

         We introduce the Scalable Workflow for Analysis and Reporting in Multi-parameter MRI Microstructural Mapping (SWARM4), a software framework designed to expedite voxelwise analysis of multi-parameter diffusion MRI, and to enhance both execution at scale and interpretation of results. Built around common neuroimaging tools for high-dimensional dMRI data analysis, SWARM4 supports cohort-level studies by automating parallel analyses across diverse diffusion models and by generating standardized, anatomically grounded outputs that facilitate discovery, interpretation, and feature selection.

Methods

          SWARM4 is a modular, Python-based framework designed for high-throughput voxelwise analysis and reporting of dMRI data in high-performance computing (HPC) environments. The software comprises two complementary components: the Scalable Automated Voxelwise Analysis Tool (SAVAnT), which automates configuration and execution of parallel mass-univariate statistical analyses, and the Significant Voxelwise Effect Reporter and Loader (SiVERAL), which integrates, summarizes, and reports spatial effects in anatomically interpretable formats (Figure 1A). SWARM4 leverages MRtrix33 for high-dimensional voxelwise analysis and its outputs are easily adapted to other neuroimaging analysis toolkits.

          The framework emphasizes configurability and flexibility across datasets and computing environments. Users can specify imaging parameter sets derived from diverse diffusion models, define region- or tissue-specific analysis masks, model main effects and statistical interactions, and deploy analyses across local or cluster-based systems. SWARM4 automatically generates all required file lists, design and contrast matrices, and execution scripts, thus enabling reproducible submission of large-scale analyses to HPC clusters with minimal manual intervention.

          As an illustrative example, we applied SWARM4 to dMRI data from 114 participants (cognitively unimpaired and consensus-diagnosed mild cognitive impairment) in the Center for Neurodegeneration and Translational Neuroscience, with available plasma measurements of the inflammatory chemokine RANTES. Diffusion MRI data for the demonstration application were acquired on a Siemens Skyra 3T scanner using a multi-shell, SE-EPI protocol: slice thickness = 1.5 mm, TR = 5218 ms, TE = 100 ms, flip angle = 78°, phase encoding P→A; b-values = 0, 500, 1000, and 2500 s/mm² and 79 diffusion directions per shell. Preprocessing included de-noising, and corrections for Gibbs ringing, susceptibility distortions, and eddy-currents and motion artifacts using FSL and MRtrix3. 

         Image processing followed the documented workflow for MSMT-CSD and fixel-based analysis (FBA), including estimation of individual and average response functions, computing fiber orientation distributions (FODs), and nonlinear registration of FODs to generate a sample-specific template. All subjects' FODs were transformed to the sample FOD template for estimation of a fixel skeleton and fixel-wise fiber density images. From these, we estimated primary (N1) and sum (sumN4) fiber density as scalar images via the fixel2voxel max and sum function. We also estimated 1) conventional DTI measures (FA, MD) as scalar measures using the dwi2tensor function in MRtrix3; 2) free water-corrected DTI parameters (FAfwc, MDfwc) using MATLAB code; and 3) CUDA diffusion modelling toolbox (cuDIMOT5) for NODDI to derive parameters neurite density index (NDI), orientation dispersion index (ODI), and the isotropic volume fraction (FISO)). 

           All scalar image data were spatially normalized to the sample FOD template and spatially smoothed with a 1mm Gaussian kernel prior to voxelwise analysis. A general linear model was evaluated within all voxels of a white matter mask using permutation testing with threshold-free cluster enhancement as implemented in mrclusterstats. RANTES level, served as the regressor of interest, while controlling for centered covariates age, head size (eTIV), handedness, and sex; continuous measures were centered at their respective sample means.

Results

           SWARM4 produced a comprehensive set of statistical outputs and data visualizations, including voxelwise effect maps (Figure 1B-D), tract-level summaries, and an interactive HTML report consolidating analysis specifications, sample characteristics, and results. The report summarizes cohort descriptors (N = 114; age, sex, handedness, intracranial volume, and RANTES distributions), parameter-specific voxel counts, and model-wise significance summaries within the white matter mask (Figure 2).

           Permutation-based voxelwise analyses, corrected for family-wise error, identified significant associations with RANTES for free-water–corrected mean diffusivity (MDfwc), NODDI isotropic volume fraction (FISO), and MSMT-FBA primary (N1) fiber density. MDfwc and FISO exhibited positive associations, whereas N1 fiber density showed negative associations. No significant voxelwise effects were observed for conventional FA, uncorrected MD, or NODDI orientation dispersion. The spatial extent of significant effects varied across parameters, with the largest number of suprathreshold voxels observed for N1 fiber density, followed by FISO and MDfwc.           

           SiVERAL reporting modules quantified spatial commonality across parameters, identifying regions of partial overlap between MDfwc and FISO effects and complementary, directionally opposing patterns with N1 fiber density. Similarity metrics indicated the strongest correspondence between MDfwc and FISO, consistent with shared sensitivity to extracellular water content. Significant voxelwise effects were automatically mapped to major white matter tracts using TractSeg4 applied to the study-specific template, revealing prominent involvement of commissural, projection, and long-range association pathways.

           SiVERAL’s automated post hoc analyses sampled mean diffusion values from significant voxelwise effect masks and evaluated their associations with RANTES using linear regression models that included the same covariates used in voxelwise inference (age, sex, handedness, and intracranial volume). These analyses confirmed robust parameter-specific relationships (|r| ≈ 0.35 to 0.45) and were visualized using automatically generated scatter plots with fitted regression lines.

Conclusion

           SWARM4 provides an automated, scalable framework for voxelwise analysis and reporting of multi-parameter diffusion MRI data that lowers technical barriers to high-dimensional neuroimaging analysis. By streamlining configuration, execution, and interpretation of mass-univariate analyses, SWARM4 supports multiple audiences, including cohort imaging studies seeking to maximize data utility, investigators performing data reduction and effect localization for training computational models, and students or trainees using voxelwise analysis as a learning resource for image analysis and interpretation.

           The framework’s standardized reporting of spatial overlap, tract-level involvement, and post hoc effect validation enables efficient screening of diffusion measures and biological predictors prior to confirmatory modeling. Future development will extend SWARM4 into cloud-based services, integrate with REDCap for large-scale cohort imaging studies, and incorporate cross-validation modules using external datasets such as ADNI. Planned extensions also include compatibility with additional voxelwise inference engines (e.g., FSL randomise and PALM) and automated sensitivity analyses to evaluate covariate effects and effect modification, further enhancing reproducibility and translational relevance.
