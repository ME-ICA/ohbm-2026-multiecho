Introduction

Accurately identifying vessels in brain tissue MRI is important as small vessels are fundamental to severe brain disorders such as cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD), Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), as well as vessel physiology and brain waste clearance. Microbleeds and microinfarcts can be identified in cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA) in living patients with FLASH (T2* contrast) and TSE (T2 contrast) respectively1. Ex vivo MRI can provide very high resolution to visualize basic vessel anatomy, but the contrast differs from in vivo MRI and functional and flow-related contrasts are unavailable. In ex vivo MRI, vessel contrast varies along vessels, perhaps depending on whether blood products remain. In this study, we explored several MR sequence parameters with the goal of optimizing the imaging protocol for the best segmentation using a vessel segmentation algorithm currently under development2. The medial temporal lobe (MTL) is essential to memory function, and medial temporal lobe structures are affected early on in the course of AD. Understanding the microscopic anatomy of vasculature in the MTL may contribute to our understanding of vascular contributions to AD and other dementias. 

Methods

We packed a small sample of the medial temporal lobe in a 50 mL Falcon tube filled with fomblin (an MR-invisible perfluorocarbon material) and imaged the sample in a small solenoid (ID 25 mm) in a 7 T Siemens Terra.X system. We explored the following sequence types (at 200 um isotropic resolution): turbo spin echo (3D TSE; base protocol TR 1000, TE 102 ms, TF 13, FA 180 deg., BW 110 Hz/px, Tacq 55:45 min:s) and spoiled gradient echo with matching geometry (3D FLASH; base protocol TR 50 ms, TE 20 ms, FA 20 deg., BW 40 Hz/px, Tacq 32:02 min:s). TSE scans provide T2 contrast and FLASH scans provide proton density (PD), T1, and T2* contrast. We mapped these contrast and relaxation parameters and generated histograms for vessels (automatically labeled) vs. background tissue (manually labeled gray and white matter) (Figure 1). We used the total vessel volumes generated by automatic segmentation to quantify and compare the performance of the various protocols. We masked the background of the scans as well as the segmentations to diminish the number of falsely labeled vessels, counted using FreeSurfer’s fslstats. 

Results

We used FreeSurfer to produce parameter maps (PD, T1, T2 and T2*), and we manually labeled a subset of gray matter (GM) and white matter (WM) voxels. The histograms suggest that accurate segmentation of vessels cannot be achieved based only on image contrast i.e. the geometric properties of vessels are also needed. Starting with the base protocols for TSE and FLASH listed above, we varied one parameter at a time and compared the volume of the segmented vessels between protocol variants. Conclusion

We found that the volume of vessels detected when the algorithm analyzed TSE (T2-weighted) images peaked at TE 41 ms, corresponding approximately with the mean T2 of the tissue classes. In 200um FLASH scans with a FA of 20 degrees and TE of 20 ms we found that increasing the SNR had minimal effect on the volume of vessels found. We concluded that the SNR of a single volume is sufficient and averaging does not further improve the segmentation. The volume of vessels found peaked at TE 20 ms across FLASH scans with varying TE, which is consistent with the peak vessel T2* at around 20 ms in the T2* histogram (Figure 1). Varying the flip angle in FLASH scans initially caused an increase in the volume of vessels found until it peaked at 30 degrees and decreased at higher flip angles.While SNR peaks at the Ernst angle (25 degrees for TR 50 ms and T1 500 ms), T1 contrast increases beyond the Ernst angle and CNR may therefore also peak at a slightly higher flip angle. An empirical optimum (30 degrees in this experiment) based on segmentation performance is justified given the broadly distributed T1-range of vessels. Due to FLASH resulting in sharper images than TSE, FLASH may be preferred for vessel imaging. FLASH scans are also more time-efficient than TSE scans. In conclusion, a FLASH scan with TE 20 ms and FA 30 degrees yielded the best result with this vessel segmentation algorithm based on vessel volume. Vessel segmentation is challenging given the broadly overlapping distributions of MR parameters in vessels and in other tissues, especially in MTL where a wide range of structures exist with varying MR parameters. The segmentation algorithm therefore also relies on geometry to identify vessels, and this is also challenging in the MTL where the geometry of other structures somewhat mimics vessels.
