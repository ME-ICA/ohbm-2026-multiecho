Introduction

Disturbances in large-scale visual processing, particularly an altered balance between dorsal (DVS) and ventral visual stream (VVS) engagement, are increasingly implicated in perceptual distortions related to appearance concerns1. Prior work shows that these networks can be modified through attentional manipulation2 or noninvasive neuromodulation3; however, no study has directly tested whether perturbing dorsal-stream circuitry causally alters dynamic effective connectivity during self-face viewing. In individuals with body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) symptoms, we combined theta burst stimulation (TBS) with a visual attention modulation paradigm to examine whether excitatory and inhibitory stimulation shift DVS and VVS network dynamics in predictable directions, and whether these changes relate to global visual processing biases. We hypothesized that following visual modulation, intermittent TBS (iTBS) would increase DVS connectivity and decrease VVS connectivity during naturalistic own-face viewing, whereas continuous TBS (cTBS) would produce the opposite pattern. We further predicted that global visual processing biases, indexed by the face inversion effect, would be enhanced after iTBS with visual modulation and reduced after cTBS with visual modulation4.

Methods

Thirty-eight adults (28 with DSM-5 BDD, 10 with subclinical BDD; 23 female, 15 male; ages 18–40) completed two counterbalanced sessions of iTBS and cTBS across separate study days. Stimulation targeted the left and right lateral parietal cortex. Each stimulation session was followed immediately by an fMRI visual attention modulation paradigm in which participants viewed photographs of their own faces while fixing their gaze in the center of the image. During naturalistic face-viewing runs occurring before and after visual modulation, dynamic effective connectivity was estimated using time-varying multivariate autoregressive modelling, which quantifies directed (Granger-causal) influences between regions over time. Twelve intra-hemispheric connections were grouped into four a priori categories: DVSLower (calcarine to superior occipital gyrus), DVSHigher (SOG to inferior parietal lobule; SOG to superior parietal lobule), VVSLower (calcarine to inferior occipital gyrus), and VVSHigher (IOG to fusiform gyrus; IOG to inferior temporal gyrus). Global visual processing was indexed with a face inversion effect task administered before and after each TBS with visual modulation session, consisting of two-alternative forced-choice face-matching trials with upright and inverted faces presented at short (500 ms) and long (5000 ms) durations. Linear mixed models tested visual-stream-level × viewing-phase interactions (naturalistic viewing before vs after visual modulation) on dynamic effective connectivity to assess whether each stimulation session differentially modulated dorsal and ventral pathway connectivity. Paired-samples comparisons assessed stimulation-related changes in face inversion effect response times separately for short- and long-duration trials.

Results

Visual stream level × viewing-phase interactions indicated that changes from pre-to-post visual modulation differed by pathway. Under iTBS, connectivity increased between runs in DVSLower (β=.10, p<.001) and DVSHigher (β=.04, p=.007), decreased in VVSHigher (β=−.09, p<.001), and showed no significant change in VVSLower (β=.01, p=.73). Under cTBS, DVSHigher increased (β=.05, p=.002) while DVSLower decreased (β=−.09, p<.001); neither ventral pathway changed significantly (VVSHigher: β=.02, p=.27; VVSLower: β=−.02, p=.32).

Paired-samples comparisons evaluated changes in face inversion effect response times before versus after TBS with visual modulation. iTBS produced a significant increase in the face inversion effect for short-duration trials, t(38)=2.44, p=.019, mean Δ=.058, with no significant change for long-duration trials, t(38)=1.37, p=.180, mean Δ=.039. Conversely, cTBS did not alter the face inversion effect for short-duration trials, t(37)=1.48, p=.146, mean Δ=.040, but significantly increased the face inversion effect for long-duration trials, t(37)=2.14, p=.039, mean Δ=.053.

Conclusion

Applying excitatory or inhibitory theta burst stimulation with visual attention modulation yielded dissociable effects on visual-stream dynamics and perceptual processing in individuals with BDD symptoms. Across both stimulation conditions, higher-level dorsal visual stream connectivity increased between naturalistic viewing runs following visual modulation, consistent with prior findings in the absence of neuromodulation2. Critically, iTBS and cTBS exerted opposing effects on lower-level dorsal visual stream connectivity. iTBS selectively enhanced lower-level dorsal visual stream connectivity while concomitantly suppressing higher-level ventral stream connectivity, consistent with a shift toward rapid global visual processing. This pattern was accompanied by an increased face inversion effect at short stimulus durations, supporting facilitated engagement of early perceptual global processing mechanisms. In contrast, cTBS was characterized by reduced lower-level dorsal connectivity alongside increased higher-level dorsal connectivity, l and no additional modulation of ventral pathways. Behaviorally, cTBS was associated with enhanced face inversion effects only at longer stimulus durations. Together, these findings indicate that excitatory and inhibitory stimulation of lateral parietal cortex do not simply exert opposing effects on visual networks but instead differentially reconfigure dorsal stream dynamics across hierarchical levels. This study provides mechanistic evidence that neuromodulation can modify connectivity and perceptual processing in individuals with BDD symptoms. Findings establish proof-of-concept for combined TBS and perceptual retraining interventions, with implications for novel treatment development in disorders of body image. The next steps are to examine whether stimulation-related connectivity changes are associated with concurrent changes in symptom-relevant clinical measures.
