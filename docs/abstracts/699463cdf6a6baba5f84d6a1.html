Introduction

Schizophrenia is associated with lower synaptic terminal density1 and reduced mitochondrial complex I (MC-I) levels2, as measured by Positron Emission Tomography (PET). Synapses are rich in mitochondria, and it is still unknown if changes in MCI-I levels are secondary to lower synaptic density. In this study, we tested if MC-I levels, as measured by [18F]BCPP-EF PET imaging, are lower in subjects with schizophrenia compared to healthy controls when normalised to synaptic terminal density, as measured by synaptic vesicle protein 2A [11C]UCB-J PET imaging. 

Methods

39 participants with a DSM-5 confirmed diagnosis of schizophrenia (n=19 taking antipsychotic medication; 33M; Mean age±SD 31.7±11.5) and 46 healthy control subjects (33M; mean age±SD 45.4±18.7) completed [18F]BCPP-EF PET, [11C]UCB-J PET and structural MRI scans. Parametric volume of distribution (VT) images were generated using a one-tissue compartment model for [11C]UCB-J and the multilinear model 1 for [18F]BCPP-EF. The parametric images were spatially normalised to  MNI152 space and ratios of [18F]BCPP-EF VT to [11C]UCB-J VT (MC-I:SV2A ratios) were calculated for each subject (Figure 1). Images were grey-masked, and a voxel-wise ordinary least squares analysis was performed, thresholded at Z>2.3, (cluster-corrected at p<0.05) with age included as a nuisance regressor. If the ratio is homogeneous across the brain, it would imply that MC-I signal is collinear with SV2A and if the ratios are different in schizophrenia, it would suggest that reductions in MC-I occur separately to lower synaptic terminal levels.

Results

Across grey-matter regions, the range of the MC-I:SV2A ratios in both groups was 0.8–2, with the lowest values in frontal and temporal regions (MC-I:SV2A ratios of 0.8-1.2) and highest values in the cerebellum and occipital lobe (MC-1:SV2A ratios of 1.7-2.0), figure 1. Compared to healthy controls, subjects with schizophrenia had significantly lower MC-1:SV2A ratios in the bilateral amygdalae, bilateral hippocampi and orbitofrontal cortex (OLS, Z>2.3, cluster p<0.05), figure 2.

Conclusion

Our results suggest that lower mitochondrial complex I levels are not a function of lower synaptic terminal density in schizophrenia and may occur independently. This is the first study to test the relationship between these two markers in the same subjects, finding that MC-I density per synapse varies across the brain, which could underlie regional differences in energy availability. We reveal for the first time MC-I density per synapse distributions across the brain, which constitutes the first in vivo neuroimaging technique able to capture this biological feature.
