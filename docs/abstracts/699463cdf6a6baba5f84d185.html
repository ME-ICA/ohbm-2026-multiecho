Introduction

We introduce a Common Coordinate Framework (Primate CCF) as a resource for facilitating the systematic integration of anatomical, functional, and molecular data across primate species. Heretofore, non-human primate (NHP) neuroimaging has lacked standardized resources and processing pipelines similar to those provided by the Human Connectome Project (HCP)^1, which have greatly accelerated progress in human neuroimaging. This gap has hindered cross-species comparisons and translational research potential. We present here an extensive suite of high-resolution volumetric and surface-based templates for multiple NHP species, including rhesus macaque (Mac25Rhesus), cynomolgus macaque (Mac25Cyno), and common marmoset (MarmosetRIKEN25) collected by HCP style approach^2, along with refinements to human reference data (HCP1071). These templates incorporate both volumetric and CIFTI-format surface representations, enabling direct compatibility with established human neuroimaging analysis frameworks, as well as existing resources such as histology-based atlases. Our approach integrates multiple technical innovations: (1) species-specific processing pipelines adapted from HCP methods, (2) registration drift correction to prevent template feature displacement, (3) high-quality surface reconstructions including symmetric flat maps, (4) registration of legacy parcellations in ex-vivo brain (NMT, D99, M150, SAM, Nencki/Monash, BMA) to the spatial and surface coordinates, (5) hierarchical visualization tools for multi-scale anatomical exploration, (6) implementation of the Atlas Ontology Model (AtOM) framework^3, which provides standardized machine-readable metadata and hierarchical organization of brain parcellations. This integrated platform addresses critical needs in comparative neuroscience and provides essential infrastructure for translational brain research.

Methods

Template Construction: High-resolution T1-weighted, T2-weighted, functional and diffusion MRI images were acquired at 3T using multi-channel phased-array coils (Autio et al., 2020) in anesthetized rhesus macaques (n=25, male/female=17/6, age=10.1±1.8 years), cynomolgus macaques (n=25, 11/14, 8.7±4.4 years), and common marmosets (n=25, 12/12, 4.9±4.4 years) using scanning parameters reported previously (Table 1, in ^2). Task fMRI for visual category stimuli was also collected in alert macaques utilizing a ferumoxytol contrast (N=3) ^4. Animals varied in age, sex, and origin (documented for each cohort). 

HCP-NHP Processing Pipelines: We adapted HCP minimal preprocessing pipelines for NHP data (Hayashi et al., 2021), incorporating species-specific volume reorientation (e.g., sphinx to supine), mammalian brain extraction tool (bet4animal), static magnetic field (B0) distortion correction with optimized TOPUP configurations, brain-scale adjusted motion correction, and T2w-to-T1w registration using optimized FreeSurfer boundary-based registration. The 3D template space utilized optimized voxel dimensions ensuring spatial symmetry at as many one-decimal resolutions as possible through the prime factorization strategies of the origin, as in MNI template. Cortical segmentation and surface reconstruction was done using FreeSurferPipelineNHP pipeline and FreeSurfer v6.0 enabling users make manual editing in order to achieve accurate pial and white surfaces throughout cerebral neocortex of each individual. Volume- and surface-based registration used FSL FNIRT and multimodal surface matching (MSM), respectively, with optimized configurations. Template creation used group-wise averaging and dedrifting. Volume-to-surface mapping of T1w/T2w contrast used ribbon-constrained sampling optimized for NHP cortical thickness to create myelin maps. Transmission bias correction was applied for reference myelin templates (Glasser et al., 2022). For legacy atlas mapping, structural images (T2w or magnetization transfer ratio) were processed through ex-vivo mode HCP-NHP pipeline, followed by volume-to-surface mapping of histologically-defined cortical parcellations in macaques (D99, CHARM, M150) (Saleem et al., 2021; Jung et al., 2021, Wang et al., 2022) and marmosets (SAM, Nencki-Monash, BMA) (Saleem et al., 2024; Majka et al., 2021; Woodward et al., 2018). Only gray matter subcortical areas were included to construct cortical and subcortical CIFTI label files. The functional MRI data was denoised and cleaned by implementing ICA+FIX, followed by manual editing and training of classifications. We implemented the AtOM framework providing standardized, machine-readable metadata describing four fundamental atlas elements: reference data (template images), coordinate systems (3D Cartesian, cortical surface-and-volume 'grayordinates'), anatomical annotations (parcellations) and terminologies (hierarchical nomenclature). Hierarchical viewing capabilities were implemented in Workbench (wb_view) for multi-scale exploration of anatomical parcellations. Interspecies registration utilized Multimodal Optical flow Registration Framework (MMORF) with novel configurations and pipelines with dedrifting, utilizing T1w, T2w, and diffusion tensor volumes

Registration Drift Correction (DeDrifting): Registration drift causes template features to systematically drift from the typical subject, reflected in regional expansion or contraction in volume and cortical surface area size, shape, and position, introducing cross-study spatial comparison inaccuracies. We implemented surface drift correction following established methods (Glasser et al., 2016) but we applied the same principle for both volume and surfaces. Cross-individual averaged sulcal anatomy was registered between hemispheres and surfaces/sulcus were resampled to the midpoint of the left-right hemispheres.

Results

High-Quality Templates, Pipelines and atlas: We generated symmetric, high-resolution templates for three NHP species and refined human reference data. Mac25Rhesus and Mac25Cyno templates at 0.5mm isotropic spatial resolution included T1w, T2w, diffusion tensors, functional connectome, diffusion-based neurite maps, and tractography. The MarmosetRIKEN25 template at 0.25mm isotropic resolution captured fine anatomical details appropriate for this smaller-brained species. The T1w/T2w cortical myelin map clearly revealed homologous organization of the early sensorimotor areas across species (Figure 1). HCP1071 refinements improved symmetrical flatmaps and resampling of temporal ICA denoised functional connectome onto the symmetrical surfaces. All templates demonstrated good anatomical definition with minimal residual asymmetries (mean myelin laterality index <1% in human, 0.5% in cynomolgus, mean thickness laterality index <1% in human and cynomolgus, mean functional asymmetry <3 % in rhesus). Symmetric flat maps preserved areal relationships while minimizing metric distortions (mean areal distortion <0.2% in human, cynomolgus, and marmoset), thus improving 2D visualization of cortical organization. All surface metrics were successfully integrated into CIFTI grayordinate spaces compatible with existing HCP visualization tools. Adapted HCP-NHP pipelines demonstrated robust performance across species. Automated processing success rates for structural analysis exceeded 99% for complete pipeline execution, followed by quality check and manual editing of the surfaces in all the subjects. Surface registration achieved high anatomical correspondence (sulcal depth correlations r>0.97 across subjects within species of cynomolgus) of the manually edited individual surfaces. Legacy atlases were successfully mapped to new NIFTI and CIFTI template spaces (Figure) with AtOM-compliant metadata. Drift corrected template volumes closely matched median subject volumes (differences <1%) while non-drift volume did not (~5% per iteration). Cortical surface area measurements showed improved agreement between template space and native space after correcting surface drift (mean areal distortion: 0.5% in cynomolgus and 0.1% in marmoset). Cross surface registration resulted in symmetrical meshes including anatomical (pial, white, midthickness), inflated, and flatmap surfaces. The inter-species volume registration worked between major species pairs including human-rhesus, with additional pairs planned.

Conclusion

This work establishes a standardized framework for multi-species primate neuroimaging that addresses long-standing limitations in NHP research infrastructure. High-quality surface and volume templates and atlases in the current primate CCFs may provide opportunities to accelerate comparative and translational neuroscience. Our contributions include multiple technical innovations that advance the state-of-the-art in NHP neuroimaging. The symmetric template construction with prime-factorization-based voxel arrangements ensures optimal geometric properties for subsequent analyses. Species-specific surface reconstruction and symmetric flat mapping provide useful tools for cortical analysis previously available only for human studies. Our implementation of registration drift correction addresses a systematic bias that can compromise spatial accuracy in template-based analyses. The parcellation and cross-study comparisons. By adapting proven HCP methodologies to NHP imaging, we establish standardized processing workflows that ensure reproducibility and comparability across laboratories. CIFTI-format outputs enable direct integration with existing human connectomics analysis tools, reducing technical barriers to cross-species research. Open-source pipeline implementations (HCP-NHP) facilitate adoption and community-driven improvements. Our implementation of the Atlas Ontology Model (AtOM) provides machine-readable, FAIR-compliant metadata that formalizes atlas versioning, coordinate system specifications, and hierarchical anatomical terminologies. The primate CCFs presented here are beginning to be explored in our companion presentations^4,^5.
