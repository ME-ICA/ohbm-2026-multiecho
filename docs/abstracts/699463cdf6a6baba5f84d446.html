Introduction

Greater education is associated with a lower risk of dementia, and this association is modulated by the APOE genotype1, pointing to Aβ pathology as a potential mechanism linking the two. Consistent with this notion, increasing evidence suggests a negative association between education level and Aβ burden at preclinical stages and younger ages2. Nevertheless, the biophysiological mechanisms underlying the education-Aβ association remain largely unknown. Recent studies have identified a highly structured, infra-slow (<0.1 Hz) global brain activity, measured as global mean BOLD (gBOLD) signal in resting-state fMRI, and linked it to Aβ and tau pathology in Alzheimer’s disease (AD), presumably due to its role in perivascular clearance3. Interestingly, this gBOLD activity varies by education attainment4, suggesting its potential mediating role for the education-Aβ association. To test this hypothesis, we examined the three-way relationships among education, gBOLD activity, and cortical Aβ burden in middle-aged adults, and assessed how these association modified by sex and APOE genotype. 

Methods

We identified 81 middle-aged (46-60 yrs, 57 females) cognitively normal subjects with both resting-state fMRI and PIB-PET data (93±169 days) in the OASIS project5. The gBOLD presence, i.e., regional BOLD correlations with the gBOLD signal, was computed to quantify the strength of gBOLD activity in different brain regions. The Aβ SUVR in DKT parcels was summarized and provided by OASIS, and we normalized it with a composite reference region consisting of the whole cerebellum, brainstem, and white matter. Pair-wise correlations between gBOLD, Aβ SUVR, and years of education were calculated and compared across brain regions, with controlling for age, sex, and APOE genotype (Fig. 1). In the frontal regions with significant correlations among the three quantities, the mediation analysis was conducted to test whether the gBOLD presence mediates the association between education and Aβ SUVR. The above analyses were then stratified by sex and APOE genotype.

Results

Greater education was significantly associated with stronger gBOLD presence in the higher-order brain regions, such as the cingulate, middle frontal, and inferiorparietal cortices, whereas the negative education-gBOLD correlations were seen specifically in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) (Fig. 1A). Significant negative correlations were observed in similar cortical regions between years of education and Aβ SUVR (Fig. 1B), as well as between gBOLD presence and Aβ SUVR (Fig. 1C). In the overlapped frontal regions of the three correlation maps, the education-Aβ association (r = –0.39, p<0.001) became non-significant (r = –0.17, p>0.05) after controlling for the gBOLD presence (Fig. 1D). The mediation analysis confirmed that gBOLD presence mediates the education–Aβ correlations (p=0.019, PM=40%) (Fig. 1E). 

Distinct patterns emerged between males and females in the stratified analysis by sex. While the gBOLD-Aβ association remained strong, education’s correlations with both gBOLD presence and Aβ SUVR were largely diminished in males (Fig. 2A). Lastly, both the education-Aβ and gBOLD-Aβ correlations are significantly dependent on the APOE genotype, with much stronger associations for APOE+ individuals (i.e., e4 carriers) (Fig. 2C). By contrast, the education-gBOLD association did not differ between APOE− and APOE+ groups (Fig. 2C). Thus, both sex and APOE genotype significantly moderate the association between education attainment and Aβ burden, but through distinct mechanisms: sex alters the education-gBOLD relationship (Fig. 2B), whereas APOE genotype disrupts the gBOLD-Aβ link (Fig. 2D). 

Conclusion

Our results show that greater education is associated with stronger gBOLD activity and lower Aβ burden at higher-order brain regions in healthy middle-aged adults. Importantly, these associations are moderated by sex and APOE genotype, albeit in distinct ways. The decreased education-gBOLD association accounts for the diminished education-Aβ associations in males, with a preserved gBOLD-Aβ association. In contrast, the moderating effect of APOE genotype on the education-Aβ association appears to arise from changes in the gBOLD-Aβ relationship. These findings suggest that gBOLD activity mediates the association between education and Aβ, and its modulation by sex and APOE genotype, in healthy middle-aged subjects, potentially contributing to the protective effect of education on dementia risk.
