Introduction

Diffuse gliomas disrupt brain networks far beyond the radiographic lesions, but structural imaging, molecular subtype, and clinical status still play a significant role in determining the prognosis. These markers do not show the physiological network dysfunction that affects progression and survival. Magnetoencephalography (MEG) provides a direct assessment of extensive neural dynamics, facilitating the quantification of this hidden disease burden.

Methods

We investigated preoperative MEG recordings from 130 adults with frontal or fronto-insular glioma (69 glioblastoma, 29 astrocytoma, 32 oligodendroglioma) who had surgery between 1994 and 2023. We additionally assessed 94 recordings from healthy individuals matched by age and gender.  Beamforming was utilized to retrieve source-level cortical temporal data. We measured band-limited (1 to 70 Hz) spectral power, whole-brain imaginary coherence, and the aperiodic (1/f) spectral slope.  We employed voxelwise general linear models with cluster-based permutation correction to analyze group effects. We used Cox regression, Harrell's and Uno's C indices, and time-dependent inverse probability–weighted AUCs with bootstrap correction to assess the predictive accuracy of these models for progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). To assess the robustness of MEG predictors, we performed bootstrap resampling of Cox models across the entire glioma cohort and within molecular subtypes.

Results

Patients had elevated delta–theta power in the frontotemporal, insular, cingulate, and medial temporal cortices, alongside diminished alpha–gamma power in the posterior and sensorimotor regions. Long-range studies indicated that slow-frequency coupling in association networks increased and posterior alpha connectivity decreased, but only in glioblastoma. The aperiodic slope was flatter across both sides of the association cortex and the medial hubs. It was flatter in glioblastoma and steeper in oligodendroglioma. A consistent pattern of network-level dysfunction linked to an altered excitatory–inhibitory balance is suggested by an increase in slow-wave local synchronization, a decrease in posterior alpha-gamma long-range activity, and a flattened aperiodic slope. While MEG features in multivariate survival models provided independent prognostic information, clinical features predicted the outcome. The aperiodic slope had the highest hazard ratio, greater than 2.4 (CI = 1.5–4.3), predicting patients with flatter aperiodic slopes tend to progress earlier. Across all gliomas, MEG measurements improved the progression-free survival (PFS) C index from 0.73 to 0.84. The majority of the prognostic improvement was due to aperiodic slope and local synchrony during the beta band. Bootstrap analyses reveal that MEG prognostic signals are subtype-specific, with greater stability within molecularly homogeneous groups than in the pooled glioma cohort. Time-dependent discrimination is enhanced throughout clinically significant intervals, particularly between 12 and 96 months of the follow-up period.  The greatest significant advancements were observed in early PFS prediction (1-2 years) for glioblastoma and long-term prognosis (3-6 years) for IDH-mutant gliomas. 

Conclusion

Together, these results establish that tumor-related network dysfunction, captured noninvasively with MEG, represents a missing physiologic dimension in glioma prognostication that is not reflected by conventional clinical or molecular markers, which significantly improves the ability to predict survival in diffuse glioma. MEG-based network measures establish a direct link between tumor biology and clinical risk, facilitating preoperative risk stratification and longitudinal management.
