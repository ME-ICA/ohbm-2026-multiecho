Introduction

Surgical resection remains the cornerstone treatment for brain tumors, but when lesions are located near function-critical regions, surgery carries a substantial risk of neurological morbidity. Neuromodulation-induced cortical prehabilitation (NICP) has emerged as a promising strategy to promote neuroplastic reorganization of critical functions away from the peritumoral region before surgery. However, its safety, feasibility, mechanistic effects and clinical efficacy require systematic evaluation.

Methods

In this Phase I feasibility clinical trial (NCT05844605), 23 patients with brain tumors located in critical motor or language areas were enrolled, of whom 21 completed the intervention. The NICP program integrated inhibitory non-invasive brain stimulation (rTMS and/or multichannel tDCS) with intensive task-specific training over 10–20 sessions prior to surgery. Safety, feasibility, clinical outcomes, and functional neuroimaging markers were assessed before and after the NICP intervention, and after surgery. Functional MRI analyses quantified changes in activity within the stimulation target and peritumoral functional regions.

Results

The intervention demonstrated high feasibility: 100% of participants initiating NICP completed the required sessions, and patient satisfaction was excellent (mean PATSAT = 92.5/100). No major adverse events occurred, and tumor volume remained stable pre- to post-intervention. Clinically, neurological, motor, and cognitive functions were largely preserved, with selective improvements in memory and visual-attention sequencing post-NICP. At the neural level, 82.3% of patients showed reduced activation within the stimulation target  (t(20) = 2.59, p = 0.017), and 64.7% exhibited decreased voxel activity in peritumoral regions. Reductions in stimulation-target activity correlated with decreases near the tumor (β = 0.31, t(19) = 3.53, p < 0.01). Importantly, greater peritumoral activity reduction significantly predicted higher tumor resection percentages (β = –0.21, p = 0.040, n = 12; Figure 1), and exploratory survival analyses using simulated curves based on historical controls suggested longer-than-expected survival in the NICP cohort. 

Conclusion

Non-invasive NICP is safe, feasible, and well-tolerated in patients with brain tumors undergoing surgery in function-critical cortical regions. The intervention induces measurable functional reorganization of vulnerable networks while preserving motor and cognitive function, and it shows promising associations with surgical outcomes and simulated survival, providing preliminary signals of clinical efficacy. These findings support the integration of NICP as a preoperative neuroplasticity-enhancing strategy and justify larger randomized controlled trials to confirm its impact on function and survival.
