Introduction

Normative modelling has transformed neuropsychiatric research by shifting inference from group-level case-control differences to individual-level deviations from typical brain variation1. However, most existing approaches are univariate, modelling each brain region independently, thereby ignoring inter-regional covariance and incurring substantial multiple-comparison burdens. Multivariate dimensionality reduction method using autoencoder specifically offers a potential solution2, but standard approaches require pre-specifying the latent dimensionality and often produce correlated representations that conflate distinct sources of variation.

We propose a Decorrelated Multi-modal Variational Autoencoder that solves these challenges through three specific innovations: (1) Learnable Gates (L0 regularization) that automatically optimize latent dimensionality3; (2) a Covariance Penalty that enforces disentanglement; and (3) Product-of-Experts (PoE) fusion to integrate disparate neuroimaging modalities into a unified individual-level representation4. By applying Bayesian linear regression-based normative modelling to the learned latent dimensions, we derive individual deviation profiles suitable for subtyping and clinical prediction.

Methods

Data: We aggregated T1-weighted structural MRI data processed with FreeSurfer 7.3 from 116 datasets across 215 acquisition sites. The Training set comprised 20,655 healthy controls (50% male, Age 26.5±16.7y). Model performance was evaluated on a held-out Test set (N=8,341, 65% male, Age 22.9±19.2y) and a large, out-of-distribution External Validation set (N=15,419, 50% male, Age 54.5±22.3y). Input features included subcortical volumes (33 regions), cortical thickness (68 parcels), and surface area (68 parcels).

Model Architecture: The autoencoder was initialized with a capacity of 32 latent dimensions. We used L0-regularized "Hard Concrete" gates to prune redundant dimensions during training, allowing the model to self-tune to the optimal number of active latent dimensions 3. A decorrelation loss was applied to minimize the covariance between latent dimensions, ensuring each factor captured unique variance. Figure 1A shows the method overview.

Normative Modelling and Interpretation: We applied Bayesian Linear Regression 5 to the learned latents (covarying for age and sex) to derive individual deviation Z-scores. To interpret the biological meaning of these latents, we developed a perturbation-based decoding method: for a reference normative subject, we systematically perturbed single latent dimensions to their extremes (2.5th centile) to visualize the resulting anatomical changes (atrophy/hypertrophy) in signal space.

Clinical Validation: We validated the model’s clinical utility in a transdiagnostic psychosis cohort (N=92) with diverse behavioural phenotypes aggregated from multiple clinical series. We correlated individual latent deviations with symptom scores (PANSS), cognition (IQ), and medication load.

Results

Model Performance: The model converged to 10 active latent dimensions, being initiated at 32 dimensions, effectively filtering noise. The learned representation demonstrated high disentanglement (mean inter-latent correlation r=0.11-0.16) and robust generalization, predicting age with high accuracy in the External dataset (R^2=0.75). Figure 1B shows the model performance. Latent trajectories were consistent across training, test, and external datasets, demonstrating robust generalization across cohorts and sites (Figure 2A). The normative modelling fitting performance is shown in Figure 2B.

Figure 2C shows the perturbation-based decoding, which demonstrated that individual latent dimensions correspond to brain features.

Clinical Disentanglement: Despite the heterogeneity of the N=92 clinical cohort, specific latent dimensions tracked distinct symptom domains, demonstrating a "double dissociation" of pathology. Deviations along Latent 10 were significantly associated with PANSS negative symptom severity, including blunted affect and social withdrawal. In contrast, Latent 9 deviations selectively tracked positive symptomatology, showing significant associations with hallucinatory behaviour (p = 0.003), distress (r = −0.56), and hostility. Deviations in Latent 1 were associated with general cognitive ability, including Full Scale IQ and attentional measures(r = −0.25). Notably, Latent 5 was the only dimension significantly associated with antipsychotic medication dosage (chlorpromazine equivalents; p = 0.002), suggesting successful separation of medication-related effects from disease-related brain variation.

Stability: In the longitudinal cohort, the "dominant latent profile" (the dimension with the highest Z-score for an individual) proved to be a stable trait rather than a transient state. We observed above 87% replication rate of individual subtypes across timepoints, supporting the use of these dimensions as stable biomarkers (Figure 1C).

Conclusion

We present a self-tuning, multi-modal framework that disentangles brain heterogeneity into interpretable, clinically distinct factors. Assigning individuals to their dominant latent deviation provides a parsimonious and robust framework for studying heterogeneity and longitudinal change in complex brain disorders. By successfully isolating dimensions specific to negative symptoms, hallucinations, and medication effects in a psychosis cohort, we validate the utility of multivariate normative modelling for transdiagnostic subtyping across different views of data. The framework is modular and extendable.
