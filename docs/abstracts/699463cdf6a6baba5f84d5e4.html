Introduction

Accurate, non-invasive mapping of brain temperature could transform monitoring of cerebral metabolism, inflammation, and neuropathological processes such as brain cancer.^1 MRI-based thermometry is promising but remains challenging due to sensitivity to motion and other artefacts. Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) thermometry leverages the temperature dependence of CSF diffusivity but is typically restricted to ventricular regions and often yields only a single global estimate.^2,3 We developed a multi-shell DWI thermometry workflow with voxel-stability filtering to improve spatial precision and to support reliable temperature estimation in ventricular and extra-ventricular CSF compartments.

Methods

We analysed multi-shell DWI scans from 12 healthy adults (b = 200–700 s/mm²). Voxelwise CSF temperature estimates were derived per b-shell using established DWI-thermometry relationships.^2,3 We then applied a voxel-stability filter that retains voxels showing consistent temperature estimates across shells (i.e., low inter-shell dispersion), reducing susceptibility to flow and partial-volume contamination. We evaluated the spatial distribution of stable voxels and temperature estimates in the third ventricle and in additional CSF regions, including peripheral CSF surrounding the brain and a deep inferior CSF compartment below the third ventricle, which are areas usually excluded previously due to artefact concerns. We contextualised this approach relative to prior phantom/human validation and motion-compensated gradient refinements.^2-4

Results

Prior to stability filtering, shell-wise temperature estimates showed broad variability across b-shells, consistent with artefact sensitivity. After stability filtering, remaining voxels exhibited narrow, overlapping temperature distributions across shells and formed compact clusters in ventricular CSF (Fig. 1). In addition, stable-voxel clusters were observed in extra-ventricular CSF compartments (peripheral CSF surrounding the brain and an inferior deep CSF region below the third ventricle), supporting feasible temperature estimation in CSF regions commonly excluded in earlier workflows.

Conclusion

Multi-shell DWI combined with voxel-stability filtering provides a data-driven strategy to improve the plausibility and spatial specificity of CSF-based thermometry. By identifying voxels with consistent temperature estimates across shells, this approach reduces artefact sensitivity and supports temperature mapping beyond the ventricles. Reliable non-invasive thermometry is a key step toward establishing brain temperature as a quantitative biomarker of cerebral metabolism and neuroinflammation.
