Introduction

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the globus pallidus internus (GPi) is an established therapy for pediatric dystonia. However,  individual anatomical variability, heterogeneous white-matter pathways, and variations in electrode placement make the identification of a consistent therapeutic optimal stimulation region challenging, yet the precise stimulation region that yields the greatest clinical benefit remains unclear. This study proposes a multimodal imaging–based method to identify the effective stimulation region within the GPi for pediatric dystonia by integrating structural MRI, diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), postoperative CT, and structural eigenvector centrality (sEC) derived from patient-specific DTI connectomes.

Methods

Preoperative T1-weighted MRI and DTI were used to reconstruct individual GPi anatomy and delineate major motor-related white-matter tracts. Postoperative CT was co-registered to MRI to localize DBS leads and identify active contacts. Whole-brain tractography was performed to generate individual structural connectivity matrices. Structural eigenvector centrality was computed to quantify the network influence of GPi and to evaluate how strongly each region was embedded within the motor network. Voxel-wise mapping and spatial normalization into a MNI template enabled the aggregation of stimulation volumes across subjects. Based on this, we can identify if clinical outcomes are correlated with sEC values.

Results

Conclusion

By combining anatomical MRI, DTI-based tractography, postoperative CT localization, and structural eigenvector centrality analysis, this study provides a robust and data-driven method to identify the optimal GPi stimulation region for pediatric dystonia. This multimodal structural-connectivity framework enhances targeting precision, informs DBS programming, and supports personalized therapy in the developing brain.
