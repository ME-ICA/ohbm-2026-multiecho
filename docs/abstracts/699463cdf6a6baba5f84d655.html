Introduction

Synaptic dysfunction and network failure are central features of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and are thought to precede overt neurodegeneration. Elevated cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) growth-associated protein 43 (GAP-43), a marker of synaptic integrity, is closely associated with AD pathology and reflects early synaptic damage.1 The medial temporal lobe (MTL) is the epicenter of early AD pathology. Recent high-resolution structural studies have delineated MTL subregions and demonstrated that areas with high susceptibility to neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs), referred to as High-NFT regions, exhibit the earliest structural alterations.2 Despite these advances, the mechanistic link between synaptic integrity and functional network anchored to specific MTL subregions remains poorly understood. 

        Although GAP-43 reflects synaptic shedding and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) captures functional connectivity (FC), few studies have examined their relationship within a pathology-guided MTL framework. In particular, it is unclear how MTL FC defined by regional NFT vulnerability relates to synaptic integrity, and whether synaptic dysfunction or network failure temporally precedes the other during disease progression.

        Therefore, the present study aimed to characterize the interplay between synaptic integrity and pathology-guided MTL functional networks across two cohorts from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) and in-house dataset.

Methods

From the ADNI database, the discovery cohort included 386 participants (243 cognitively normal [CN], 119 mild cognitive impairment [MCI], and 24 AD) with available T1-weighted MRI, rs-fMRI, PET measures of amyloid-β (Aβ) and tau pathology, and clinical assessments. A subset of participants (n = 157) also had CSF GAP-43 measurements. Longitudinal analyses were conducted in 144 ADNI participants with imaging data from two or more time points. To assess the robustness of cross-sectional findings, an independent external validation cohort was recruited from Rehabilitation Medicine Department of Jiangsu Shengze Hospital affiliated with Nanjing Medical University, comprising 100 participants (37 CN, 35 subjective cognitive decline [SCD], and 28 MCI). 

        Resting-state fMRI data were preprocessed using the DPABI toolbox following a standard pipeline, including realignment, co-registration, normalization, temporal filtering, and spatial smoothing. T1-weighted images were segmented into MTL subfields using the ASHS-PMC-T1 pipeline. Subfield labels were grouped into High-NFT and Low-NFT regions of interest (ROIs) based on established pathological vulnerability,3 spatially normalized to standard space using ANTs, and resampled in DPABI. These ROIs served as seeds for voxel-wise FC analyses in whole-brain gray matter, followed by Fisher z-transformation. Group differences in FC were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Gaussian random field correction (voxel-level p < 0.01, cluster-level p < 0.05, two-tailed). After harmonizing site effects using ComBat, age, sex, education, and head motion were included as covariates in FC analyses, and the time interval between MRI acquisition and CSF sampling was controlled for in GAP-43–related analyses. Cross-sectional analyses proceeded in three stages: (1) post hoc group comparisons of mean functional connectivity values, which were replicated in the external cohort; (2) multiple linear regression analyses examining associations among FC, GAP-43, PET pathology, and clinical measures; and (3) serial mediation analyses modeling pathways from Aβ and tau pathology to cognitive outcomes (MMSE and MoCA) via GAP-43 and FC, as well as the reverse ordering. Mediation effects were considered significant when bias-corrected bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (CI) did not include zero. 

        For longitudinal analyses, cross-lagged panel models were constructed to assess directional relationships among Aβ pathology, GAP-43, FC, and cognition. Statistical significance in longitudinal models was determined using False Discovery Rate (FDR)-corrected bootstrap p-values.

Results

In the discovery cohort, ANOVA analyses revealed significant FC reductions in the High-NFT hippocampal region with prominent clusters located in the inferior temporal gyrus (ITG) and precuneus. Post hoc tests demonstrated a progressive decline across the disease continuum, from CN to MCI and then to AD groups (p < 0.05; Figure 1a). These findings were independently replicated in an external validation cohort, which exhibited comparable FC reductions in the precuneus and middle frontal gyrus. Notably, reduced FC between High-NFT hippocampus and precuneus already existed in individuals with subjective cognitive decline (SCD) relative to healthy controls (p < 0.05; Figure 1b).

        Regression analyses further revealed a systematic reversal in the direction of associations between FC or GAP-43 and clinical measures across disease stages. For cognitive scales in which higher scores indicate better performance (MMSE and MoCA), regression coefficients progressively decreased, shifting from positive associations in the overall cohort to negative associations in the AD subgroup. In contrast, for measures in which higher scores reflect greater impairment, regression coefficients increased, transitioning from negative values to significant positive associations at the AD stage (Figure 2a). Biologically, lower FC in the ITG was significantly associated with higher Aβ burden in Braak stage 3–6 regions (p < 0.05). Within the AD group, reduced precuneus FC was significantly related to both widespread Aβ deposition (Braak stages 1–6, p < 0.05) and advanced tau pathology (Braak stages 5–6, p < 0.05). In contrast, CSF GAP-43 levels were positively associated with Aβ burden in Braak stages 3–6 regions, displaying an inverse pattern relative to the FC–pathology relationship (Figure 2b).

        To investigate potential mechanistic pathways, we performed serial mediation analyses. Tau pathology in Braak stages 3–4 exerted significant indirect effects on cognitive performance (MMSE) through two parallel mediators: right precuneus FC and CSF GAP-43. Although tau pathology significantly predicted both mediators and each mediator independently predicted cognition, the serial pathway from FC to GAP-43 was not significant in the cross-sectional model (Figure 2c).

        Finally, longitudinal cross-lagged panel modeling revealed a significant unidirectional association. Baseline FC in the right pars triangularis of inferior frontal gyrus (IFGtri), significantly predicted follow-up GAP-43 levels (n = 50, β = −0.329, p = 9.0 × 10⁻⁵; FDR-corrected p = 0.021), whereas the reverse pathway was not significant. Higher baseline FC was associated with lower subsequent levels of the synaptic damage marker, indicating that functional network disruption temporally precedes changes in CSF GAP-43 (Figure 2d).

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that functional disconnection in Alzheimer’s disease is anatomically anchored within pathologically vulnerable MTL subregions. The consistent involvement of the precuneus underscores its role as a central hub of the default mode network (DMN), a system characterized by high metabolic demand and early susceptibility to Aβ deposition. The additional involvement of the inferior temporal gyrus (ITG) likely reflects the neocortical spread of tau pathology originating from the MTL. Together, these nodes indicate that High-NFT–guided seed-based functional connectivity captures a critical intersection at which network vulnerability and pathological propagation converge in early AD.

        Regression analyses further revealed a stage-dependent shift in the clinical relevance of functional connectivity. In earlier disease stages, higher connectivity was associated with better cognitive performance. In contrast, at the AD stage, this relationship reversed, with higher connectivity paradoxically predicting worse clinical outcomes. This polarity reversal suggests a transition from early functional disruption to pathological hyperconnectivity, in which increased network activity reflects inefficient or aberrant signaling rather than preserved neural function.

        At the biological level, both functional connectivity and GAP-43 were closely linked to core AD pathologies. The negative associations between functional connectivity and Aβ and tau burden support its role as a sensitive proxy of pathological impact, likely rooted in the microstructural degeneration of underlying white matter tracts, a process tightly linked to tau pathology. 4 In contrast, the positive association between CSF GAP-43 and Aβ burden highlights its distinct role as a marker of synaptic shedding. The opposing relationships of these two biomarkers with pathology provide a strong rationale for examining their temporal and causal interactions.

        Mediation analyses demonstrated that tau pathology influences cognition through two parallel pathways: network disruption and synaptic injury, which operated independently without a significant serial link from functional connectivity to GAP-43. Extending these findings, longitudinal modeling established a temporal framework that clarifies their interplay. Specifically, baseline functional connectivity in the right inferior frontal gyrus, pars triangularis—a region recently identified as contributing to neural activity patterns that buffer against AD pathology5 —predicted future elevations in CSF GAP-43. This unidirectional effect suggests that failure of compensatory functional networks represents a critical tipping point that drives downstream synaptic shedding, thereby explaining the absence of a direct serial relationship in cross-sectional analyses.

        Collectively, these findings position pathology-guided MTL functional connectivity as a mechanistically informative marker for tracking the transition from network dysfunction to widespread synaptic failure. Its high sensitivity, including the ability to distinguish individuals at the subjective cognitive decline stage in the validation cohort, highlights its potential utility for prognostic stratification and therapeutic monitoring.

        Several limitations should be acknowledged. The longitudinal findings require replication in larger cohorts, and future studies should incorporate a broader panel of synaptic biomarkers to more comprehensively characterize the molecular cascade following functional network failure.
