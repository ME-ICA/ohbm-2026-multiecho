Introduction

Substance use comorbidity is highly prevalent among individuals with schizophrenia spectrum disorders (SSDs) and represents a major clinical and public health concern. Co-occurring substance use not only worsens the course and prognosis of psychotic illnesses but also significantly increases the risk of adverse social outcomes, including violence and criminal behavior. Resting-state electroencephalogram (rs-EEG) is a non-invasive tool to measure brain signals and is widely used in research and clinical use. It has also been applied in studies of substance use disorders and may provide objective neurophysiological markers relevant to behavioral manifestations. In this study, we used rs-EEG to identify neural alterations associated with substance use comorbidity (biomarker) that may contribute to behavioral changes and elevated risk for violence.

Methods

All individuals with SSDs were collected in Taoyuan Psychiatry Center, Taiwan, which were divided into four groups: (1) violent crime and substance use history (SUH) (n=17, age: 40 10.83, 94.12% in male), (2) nonviolent crime and SUH(n=10, age: 45.1 8.3, 90% in male), (3) violent crime and no SUH (n=22, age: 38.045 10.22, 90.9% in male), (4) nonviolent crime and no SUH (n=16, age: 46.88 9.76, 43.75% in male). We conducted frequency-domain (relative power, coherence) and time-domain analyses (sample entropy, multiscale entropy) in a group comparison to examine brain features of Substance use. In addition, we used a linear regression model and multiple corrections with the Holm-Bonferroni method to find the potential brain features for people who have substance use. An aligned rank transform and partial Spearman correlation were conducted for brain features, clinical performance, and other factors, such as criminal history.

Results

Alpha coherence between Fp1 and Fp2 tended to be higher in violent patients with substance use comorbidity compared with those without (p = 0.075), and also differed between patients with and without a criminal history (F = 4.84, p = 0.0342). Substance-use patients demonstrated a significant alteration in the right temporal region (T6, scale 1, p = 0.0332). Marginal differences were also observed in the prefrontal (Fp1, Fp2), frontal (F7, F8), as well as right central (C4), parietal (P3 P4), and occipital (O1, O2) regions compared to the non-substance use group. Additionally, a marginally significant interaction between substance use status and violent versus non-violent status was observed for entropy at O1 (scale 1) across the full sample (p = 0.1).

Conclusion

Our findings indicate that substance use comorbidity in SSDs is associated with alterations in both functional connectivity and neural complexity during rest. Moreover, prefrontal interhemispheric alpha coherence differed between patients with and without a criminal history, suggesting that such rs-EEG features may reflect behavioral patterns associated with past criminal activity. The observed changes in prefrontal coherence and temporal entropy, as well as their interaction with violent status in occipital entropy, highlight the potential of rs-EEG as a neurophysiological marker underlying behavioral disturbances and increased violence or criminal risk in this population.
