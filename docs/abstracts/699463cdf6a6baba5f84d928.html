Introduction

Establishing reliable psychopathological frameworks and unraveling their neurobiological underpinnings is critical for elucidating mental disorder etiology and advancing precision diagnostics and therapeutics. Current categorical diagnostic systems fail to align with disorder-specific neurobiological substrates, as aberrant genetics, brain structure, and function patterns are frequently shared across diagnostic labels—highlighting the imperative to dissect unique neurobiological correlates of transdiagnostic symptom dimensions1. 

Notably, about35% of mental disorders manifest before age 14, a period of profound plastic brain structural development, rendering the investigation of brain-structural correlates of psychopathological dimensions in children and adolescents particularly pressing.Psychopathology is increasingly conceptualized as a hierarchical construct, encompassing a higher-order general psychopathology factor and lower-order dimensions: Internalizing , Externalizing, and Neurodevelopmental . While prior research in typically developing populations has documented associations between these dimensions and morphological features such as gray matter volume, cortical surface area, thickness, such relationships remain poorly characterized in clinical cohorts2 3 4.Critically, advances in surface-based morphometry now enable extraction of a comprehensive panel of morphological features beyond conventional metrics5, including gray matter volume, cortical thickness, surface area, sulcal depth, fractal dimension, gyrification indices, and curvature measures.. Each feature has unique cellular, genetic, and developmental underpinnings, yet their specific associations with transdiagnostic dimensions remain unelucidated. Thus, the present study aims to investigate the specific association patterns between transdiagnostic psychopathological dimensions and diverse brain morphological features in a clinical sample of children and adolescents.

Methods

We analyzed data from 1,113 participants (6–22 years) in the publicly available Healthy Brain Network (HBN) cohort. Structural MRI data were processed to extract a comprehensive panel of brain morphological features: conventional metrics (gray matter volume, cortical thickness, surface area) and gyrification (Fold Index, Mean Curvature , Curvature Index , Gaussian Curvature) using Freesurfer. Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression—an advanced multivariate technique ideal for disentangling latent associations between high-dimensional neuroimaging and behavioral data—was applied to identify unique association patterns between these morphological features and items from the Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL). Rigorous quality control included exclusion of scans with motion artifacts, poor segmentation, or incomplete behavioral data; age, gender, and socioeconomic status were included as covariates in all analyses. 

Results

PLS analysis revealed dimension-specific associations between brain morphological features and five well-validated pediatric transdiagnostic dimensions: General Psychopathology, Externalizing Component, Internalizing Component, Thought Problems, and Neurodevelopmental Component. 

A novel finding was the Fold Index, and Mean Curvature exhibited selective associations with General Psychopathology and Internalizing Component, whereas , Curvature Index Gaussian Curvature, gray matter Volume, cortical Thickness, and SurfArea showed unique correlations with Externalizing Component, Thought Problems, and Neurodevelopmental Component but not General Psychopathology.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that a set of brain  morphological  measures capture distinct neuroanatomical correlates of transdiagnostic psychopathology in children and adolescents. This highlights the innovative insight that integrating understudied morphological indicators enhances our ability to disentangle the neurobiological heterogeneity of pediatric mental disorders. Importantly, these results underscore the potential of a multi-metric morphological approach to develop more specific and sensitive neuroimaging biomarkers—critical for improving early detection and personalized intervention strategies for pediatric mental health conditions.
