Introduction

Maintaining a balanced excitation-inhibition (E/I) ratio is critical for normal brain function. Disruptions to the E/I ratio during aging are thought to contribute to neurodegenerative diseases, including Alzheimer’s disease (AD), but the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we applied our previously established large-scale biophysical parametric feedback inhibition control (pFIC) model to estimate cortical E/I ratio1. First, we applied the pFIC model to the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset, including cognitively normal (CN) participants and participants with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), to estimate individual-level E/I ratio. We then examined associations with CSF amyloid beta 42 (Aβ42). After controlling for age, sex, education, and head motion, we observed a negative correlation between E/I ratio and CSF Aβ42, indicating that lower CSF Aβ42 level was associated with higher E/I ratio. Regionally, higher-order association cortices exhibited greater E/I increases than lower-order sensory-motor regions as CSF Aβ42 decreased. Mediation analysis further suggested that the relationship between CSF Aβ42 and cognition was partially mediated by E/I ratio, potentially reflecting excitotoxicity. 

We next applied the same modeling framework to the Memory and Ageing Cognition Centre (MACC) dataset2, including CN participants and cognitively impaired, non-demented (CIND) participants, and examined associations with plasma p-tau217. Results mirrored the ADNI findings: E/I ratio positively correlated with plasma p-tau217, higher-order regions were more affected, and the relationship beteen plasma p-tau217 and cognition was mediated through E/I ratio. Overall, our findings reveal convergent relationships between cortical E/I ratio and both CSF and plasma AD biomarkers, providing mechanistic insight into AD pathology and supporting a role for neuronal excitotoxicity in cognitive decline.

Methods

We used our previously established parametric feedback inhibition control (pFIC) model to obtain an individual-level cortex-wide E/I ratio estimate using resting-state fMRI data1. Breifly, the pFIC model consists of differential equations at each cortical region, capturing the dynamics of excitatory and inhibitory neuronal populations (Figure 1A right). The local neuronal dynamics are governed by excitatory and inhibitory connections within and between these populations. Excitatory populations of different regions are connected through structural connectivity (Figure 1A left). By fitting the model parameters to the individual-level fMRI data, simulated time courses of both excitatory and inhibitory synaptic gating variable (SE, SI) can be generated with the optimized model parameters. Motivated by animal studie, E/I ratio is defined as the ratio between temporal averages of SE and SI.

We applied the pFIC model to the ADNI dataset comprising 302 participants: 194 cognitively normal (CN; age=72.5±6.45; M/F=73/121) and 108 individuals with mild cognitive impairment (MCI; age=73.0±8.23; M/F=60/48). All participants had available CSF Aβ42 measurements. CSF collection dates, fMRI acquisition dates, and diagnostic assessments occurred within one year to ensure reliability. CSF Aβ42 values were log-transformed to reduce distributional skew. We fit a generalized linear model (GLM) including individual-level mean cortical E/I ratio, log-transformed CSF Aβ42, age, sex, head motion (i.e., framewise displacement, FD), and years of education (Figure 1B). To visualize the association, we computed an E/I ratio residual by regressing out the covariates (age, sex, head motion, education) from the mean cortical E/I ratio and plotted it against CSF Aβ42, where each dot represents one participant (Figure 1C). At the regional level, linear regression models were fit between regional E/I ratio residuals and CSF Aβ42 across participants. The resulting slopes reflect the change in regional E/I ratio per unit change in CSF Aβ42 (Figure 1D). Finally, to gain mechanistic insights into the relationship between CSF biomarker and cognition, we conducted a mediation analysis with CSF Aβ42 as the independent variable, the Phenotype Harmonization Consortium (PHC) memory score as the dependent variable, and the E/I ratio as the mediator. In the mediation framework, a denotes the coefficient linking CSF Aβ42 to E/I ratio, b the coefficient linking E/I ratio to the PHC memory score, and c′ the direct effect of CSF Aβ42 on PHC memory. The indirect effect (a × b) quantifies mediation through E/I ratio (Figure 1E).

We then applied the same framework to the Memory and Ageing Cognition Centre (MACC) dataset of 240 participants: 95 CN (age=68.6±7.64; M/F=41/54) and 145 cognitively impaired but non-demented (CIND) individuals (age=74.0±6.72; M/F=66/79). Individual-level E/I ratios were estimated from fMRI data, and plasma p-tau217 values were obtained within one year of imaging and diagnostic visits. A GLM was fit using mean cortical E/I ratio, plasma p-tau217, age, sex, head motion, and education (Figure 2A). The E/I ratio residual (covariates including age, sex, head motion and education were removed) was plotted against plasma p-tau217, where each dot represents one participant (Figure 2B). Regional associations were quantified as linear slopes between regional E/I ratio residuals and plasma p-tau217 and visualized as a cortical surface map (Figure 2C). We also performed mediation analysis with plasma p-tau217 as the independent variable, a composite memory score as the dependent variable, and the E/I ratio as the mediator. Because the PHC memory score in ADNI primarily reflects verbal memory and Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) subscores, we constructed an analogous composite memory score in the MACC dataset using Word List Recall, Story Recall, and corresponding MMSE subscores. Mediation notations (Figure 2D) follow those used in the ADNI analysis.

Results

Previous studies have shown that aging is accompanied by hyperexcitability and an elevated E/I ratio⁴. Combined with the well-established finding that lower CSF Aβ42 reflects more advanced AD pathology, we predicted a negative association between CSF Aβ42 and the E/I ratio. As expected, the GLM revealed that mean cortical E/I ratio was negatively associated with CSF Aβ42 after controlling for age, sex, head motion, and education (Figure 1B). This negative association can be visualized on a scatter plot, showing that lower CSF Aβ42 corresponded to higher E/I ratio (r = -0.176, p = 0.0021; Figure 1C). Because the pFIC model provides E/I ratio estaimtes at the regional level, we next examined its regional variability. Linear slopes relating regional E/I ratio residuals to CSF Aβ42 demonstrated more negative associations in higher-order association regions than in lower-order sensory-motor regions (Figure 1D). This hierarchical pattern suggests that association cortices exhibit greater increases in E/I ratio in response to reduced CSF Aβ42 and may therefore be more vulnerable to AD-related pathology and cognitive dysfunction. These findings motivated a mediation analysis to test whether E/I ratio helps explain the relationship between CSF Aβ42 and cognition, quantified by the PHC memory score. Although CSF Aβ42 showed a significant direct association with cognition, the indirect path through the E/I ratio accounted for ~17% (axb/c’ = 0.0874/0.514 = ~17%) of the total effect. This indicates that part of the cognitive decline associated with reduced CSF Aβ42 may be attributable to elevated E/I ratio levels.

Given increasing interest in plasma-based biomarkers for clinical application, we next tested whether these patterns generalize to a different cohort and biomarker modality. Applying the same analytic framework to the MACC dataset using plasma p-tau217, we again observed a significant association between mean cortical E/I ratio and biomarker levels. As plasma p-tau217 increases with AD progression, the directionality was reversed: higher plasma p-tau217 corresponded to higher E/I ratio after adjusting for age, sex, head motion, and education (Figure 2A-B). Regionally, higher-order association cortices again showed steeper increases in E/I ratio per unit increase in plasma p-tau217 relative to sensory-motor regions (Figure 2C), mirroring the hierarchical pattern observed with CSF Aβ42. Mediation analysis further supported this convergence. The indirect pathway from plasma p-tau217 to the composite memory score, mediated through the E/I ratio, was statistically significant (Figure 2D). This result parallels the ADNI findings, even though the cohorts differed in demographics and biomarker modality.

Together, these results indicate that both decreasing CSF Aβ42 and increasing plasma p-tau217 converge on a shared downstream feature, which is an elevated cortical E/I ratio. This suggests that hyperexcitability may represent a shared mechanistic pathway linking neuronal excitotoxcity induced by early AD pathology to subsequent cognitive impairment.

Conclusion

Across two independent cohorts and two biomarker modalities, we found convergent evidence that AD-related pathology was associated with increases in a marker of cortical E/I ratio. Lower CSF Aβ42 and higher plasma p-tau217 each associated with elevated E/I ratio, with the strongest effects in higher-order association cortices. Mediation analyses further showed that disrupted E/I ratio partially explained the relationship between AD biomarkers and cognitive ability. Together, these findings suggest that hyperexcitability may serve as a mechanistic link between molecular pathology and cognitive decline, highlighting the E/I ratio as a promising in-vivo marker of early neurodegenerative processes.
