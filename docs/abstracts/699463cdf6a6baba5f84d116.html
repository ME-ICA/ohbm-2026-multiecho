Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition, with identifiable genetic mutations in ~30% of cases. Pathogenic variants in SETD5 gene affect chromatin modification and transcriptional regulation, influencing neurocognitive development. Individuals with SETD5 variants often present with intellectual disability, ASD traits, hearing and visual impairments, and language deficits (OMIM #613792). Despite these observations, cerebral structural and perfusion abnormalities in this population remain poorly characterized.
 This study aimed to investigate structural and perfusion brain alterations in children with SETD5 variants and examine associations with sensory profiles, focusing on olfactory and auditory modalities.

Methods

Eleven children with pathogenic SETD5 variants (mean age 11.07 ± 3.81 years, range 6–17) were prospectively evaluated at Necker Hospital. All participants underwent 3T MRI including 3D T1-weighted, Cube T2, FLAIR, and arterial spin labeling (ASL) sequences. Sensory assessments were performed, including standardized olfactory testing (U-Sniff kit, 12 odorants). In addition, 7 out of the 11 patients also underwent audiometry evaluation.

Perfusion images measuring cerebral blood flow (CBF) at rest were acquired for all participants using a 3D pulsed continuous ASL sequence. All participants also underwent a 3D T1–weighted FSPGR sequence. All scans were acquired with a 3 Tesla (Signa General Electric) scanner at Necker Hospital, Paris, France. 

All images were visually inspected and radiological abnormalities were assessed by expert neuroradiologists. ASL images from one patient were excluded due to dental retainer artefact.  Structural T1-weighted and ASL images were analyzed using SPM12 implemented in Matlab. Structural T1 images were segmented into grey matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid and spatially normalized in MNI space using the CAT12 toolbox. ASL images were co-registered to native grey matter images and normalized to MNI space using deformation matrices from the T1 normalization step, with final isotropic resolution of 1.5 mm³. ASL images were smoothed using an isotropic 10-mm Gaussian filter.

A whole-brain voxel-by-voxel group analysis was performed comparing smoothed, normalized ASL images from ten SETD5 patients (mean age 10.41 ± 3.34 years) to twenty-four age matched controls (mean age 8.40 ± 2.57 years) using the general linear model framework within SPM12.

Results

Radiological analysis showed that all patients exhibited brain abnormalities, including corpus callosum dysmorphia (7/11), mild cerebellar atrophy (8/11), ventricular enlargement (2/11), posterior fossa cyst (1/11), and olfactory bulb malformations (9/11), including one case of agenesis. Among patients presenting bulb malformation, six had impaired U-Sniff olfactory performance.

Audiometry revealed bilateral severe hypoacusis in two patients, complete deafness in one patient, and normal results in four patients.

Whole brain ASL analyses showed significantly reduced rest CBF in the right temporo-parietal junction (t = 7.05, z = 5.34, p < 0.05, FWE-corrected), right medial frontal gyrus(t = 6.72, z = 5.18, p < 0.05, FWE-corrected), and right medial orbitofrontal cortex (t = 5.86, z = 4.72, p < 0.05, FWE-corrected) in SETD5 patients relative to controls. No regions showed increased perfusion.

Conclusion

This study presents the first multimodal MRI characterization of children with SETD5 variants, integrating structural, perfusion, and sensory data. Olfactory bulb malformations appear to be characteristic of SETD5, with hypoperfusion in medial orbitofrontal regions implicated in olfactory processing. ASD traits, typical of this population, may partially mask sensory deficits, emphasizing the need for targeted olfactory and auditory assessments.
 These findings highlight the link between sensory profiles and neuroimaging abnormalities, providing insight into genotype–phenotype correlations in SETD5-related ASD. Future work will integrate auditory profiles, cognitive assessments, and functional MRI to define sensory-cognitive networks in this population.
