Introduction

Hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) remains a leading cause of neonatal brain injury despite therapeutic hypothermia (TH), which, although neuroprotective, does not fully prevent brain tissue loss or support subsequent optimal postnatal brain growth1. Quantitative volumetric MRI provides a sensitive non-invasive approach to characterize early structural brain development by capturing trajectories in global and regional brain volumes. Recent advances in infant-specific segmentation pipelines, including infant FreeSurfer2, enable reliable extraction of anatomically defined regions and facilitate the assessment of brain growth trajectories across time and clinical groups. Sildenafil, a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor, has demonstrated neuroprotective and neurorestorative effects in preclinical models of HIE3, however, its impact on early human brain development has not been established. This study investigates whether adjunctive sildenafil, administered alongside TH, is associated with altered neonatal brain growth trajectories as measured by volumetric MRI.

Methods

Participants and Clinical Procedures: Neonates with HIE admitted to the neonatal intensive care unit were prospectively enrolled if they met established criteria for therapeutic hypothermia (TH). TH was initiated within 6 hours of birth and maintained at 33.5°C for 72 hours. A subset of neonates with MRI-confirmed brain injury on day 2 despite TH received adjunct enteral sildenafil every 12 hours for 7 days as part of the SANE trials (SANE-01/02), according to predefined eligibility criteria. Healthy term neonates without perinatal complications were enrolled as controls. The study was approved by the institutional review board and written informed parental consent was obtained for all participants.

MRI Acquisition: MRI examinations were acquired on a 3T scanner (Philips Achieva X) using a 32-channel head coil, including a 3D T1-weighted gradient-echo sequence (TR 24 ms, TE 4.6 ms, voxel size 1.0 mm isotropic). Scans were acquired on days 2, 10, and 30 of life, including during active cooling for neonates treated with TH. Pediatric neuroradiologists, blinded to clinical group and treatment, scored injury severity using previously established criteria.

Segmentation and Volumetric Processing: Volumetric analyses were performed using Infant FreeSurfer (dev-4a14499) and neonatal brain segmentation templates within the first month of life. Scans with significant motion artifact were excluded following visual quality control. Automated segmentations generated by the default infant FreeSurfer pipeline underwent systematic visual inspection and manual correction using ITK-SNAP (v4.0.2) to account for rapid brain maturation and evolving tissue contrast. Corrected segmentations were used to extract regional volumes, including total brain (including cerebellum and brainstem, and excluding ventricles), basal ganglia (including thalamus), thalamus only, cerebellum, and brainstem. Regions of interest (ROIs) were selected based on known vulnerability to HIE and relevance to early postnatal growth. 

Statistical Analysis: Brain growth trajectories were analyzed using generalized linear mixed models comparing neonates without brain injury (NoBI), those with injury treated with TH alone (BI+TH), and those treated with TH plus sildenafil (BI+TH/S) across the three imaging time points. Models included random effects and were adjusted for injury severity, intracranial volume, and gestational age at scan. Statistical analyses were performed in SPSS (v29.0.2) and figures were generated using R (v4.5.1).

Results

A total of 112 neonates were included (13 healthy controls, 48 NoBI+TH, 30 BI+TH, and 21 BI+TH/S), contributing 210 MRI examinations obtained across days 2, 10, and 30 of life. 

By day 2, total brain volume did not differ significantly between groups. Basal ganglia + thalamus volume was significantly higher in BI+TH/S relative to NoBI, whereas Cerebellar volume was significantly reduced in BI+TH/S compared with NoBI and BI+TH; thalamic volumes analyzed separately were not significantly different. Brainstem volumes were comparable across groups. 

By day 10, thalamic and cerebellar volumes were significantly lower in BI+TH/S, while total brain, basal ganglia + thalamus, and brainstem, were not significantly different. 

By day 30, total brain and basal ganglia + thalamus volumes were significantly lower in BI+TH compared to NoBI and BI+TH/S, whereas volumes in the BI+TH/S were no longer significantly different from NoBI. Cerebellar and thalamic volumes were significantly reduced in BI+TH but not in BI+TH/S relative to NoBI. Brainstem volumes did not differ significantly between groups (figure 1).

Conclusion

Volumetric MRI identified distinct brain growth trajectories in neonates with HIE. Adjunctive sildenafil in addition to TH was associated with transient early reductions in cerebellar and thalamic volumes that normalized by day 30, whereas brain injury treated only with TH resulted in sustained reductions in total brain, basal ganglia + thalamus, and cerebellar volumes. These findings support the feasibility of ROI-based infant FreeSurfer volumetry for tracking early neonatal brain growth and suggest its utility as an imaging biomarker for evaluation adjunct neuroprotective and neurorestorative therapies in HIE.
