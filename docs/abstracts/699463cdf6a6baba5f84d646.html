Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is increasingly recognised as a systemic disorder with potential effects on the brain. However, the neural circuits and biological pathways linking hepatic and metabolic alterations to brain structure and cognition remain poorly understood^1.

Methods

Retrospective cohort of 2,689 adults (mean age, 60.9 years) who underwent T1weighted brain MRI and abdominal dual-energy CT. HS was indexed by the liver-to-spleen attenuation ratio (CT-HSI=−L/S). Brain morphometry was extracted, generalized linear models tested associations, and piecewise regression modeled effects across the clinical cutoff (L/S=1.0). Tissue quality was assessed using the CT Hounsfield units (HU) of the visceral fat and muscle. Cognition was examined in a subset (K-MoCA; n=198).

Results

After full adjustment, HS showed selective cortical thickening, most prominently in the left parahippocampal gyrus (β=0.027; p=0.03). Piecewise models revealed onset effects above the cutoff in limbic-striatal circuits, including the bilateral caudate (β≈0.11; p=0.005). In contrast, diffuse cortical thinning and subcortical atrophy were chiefly linked to hyperglycaemia, blood pressure, and visceral adiposity. Lower visceral-fat HU was associated with a thinner cortex and smaller limbic nuclei, whereas higher muscle HU was linked to a thicker cortex and larger hippocampal/amygdala volumes. CT-HSI was not associated with MoCA; hs-CRP (β=−0.042; p=0.005) and FIB-4 (β=−0.154; p<0.0001) predicted lower scores.

Conclusion

MASLD influences the brain through distinct pathways. HS preferentially targets the memory–reward circuitry, whereas dominant metabolic phenotypes and tissue quality account for diffuse vulnerability. Integrating metabolic profiling and HU-based body-composition metrics into opportunistic imaging may enable phenotype-informed, region-specific brain-risk assessment.
