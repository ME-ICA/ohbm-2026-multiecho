Introduction

White matter bundles provide the foundation for efficient communication across the human brain. These bundles are laid out during gestation, however, detailed knowledge about the microstructural development that is taking place within these bundles in utero is limited. Prior cross-sectional research revealed complex and bundle-specific microstructural changes during fetal development, however the trajectory of these changes remains debated: while some studies have described linear relationships between diffusion metrics and gestational age4, others have identified nonlinear trajectories1,2, or found no meaningful changes in these metrics across gestational development5. To address this debate, here we characterized developmental trajectories longitudinally within individual fetuses. After developing a dedicated pipeline for the analysis of fetal dMRI data over a wide range of gestational ages and in the individuals’ native brain space, we applied it to a small (N=13) longitudinal fetal dataset, collected as part of the developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP). This enables us to characterize the microstructural maturation of white matter bundles longitudinally in utero and hence to clarify previously conflicting findings of the microstructural developmental trajectories of the fetal brain.

Methods

We analyzed dMRI and sMRI data from 12 fetuses shared by the dHCP – 11 fetuses were scanned twice and 1 fetus was scanned 3 times during in utero development (mean gestational age (GA)±SE: first scan: 25.09±0.182 weeks, second scan: 33.86±0.25 weeks, third scan: 33.86 weeks; range of GAs at the time of measurement: 21.14 - 37.14 weeks). The dMRI data were collected at 2 mm isotropic resolution, with TR/TE = 5810/76 ms and b-values: b= 0 s/mm2, b= 400 s/mm2, and b= 1000 s/mm². The sMRI data (T2w) were collected at 1.1 mm isotropic resolution, with TR/TE = 2265/250 ms and six directions. Both, dMRI data and sMRI data was preprocessed by the dHCP and segmentations of gray and white matter were provided. From this starting point, we developed a bespoke pipeline for fetal dMRI data analysis using a combination of tools from MRtrix3, DIPY, FSL, DSI-Studio, and pyAFQ. First, the preprocessed dMRI images were registered to the whole-brain T2-weighted images. Next, tissue response functions were computed separately for white matter (Tournier algorithm), gray matter (Dhollander algorithm), and CSF (Dhollander algorithm) in MRtrix3. As tissue boundaries are not easily distinguishable in fetuses based on dMRI data alone, these boundaries were defined from the sMRI segmentations instead. Next, voxel-wise fiber orientation distributions (FODs) were calculated using multi-shell multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution (msmtCSD) in MRtrix3 for the white matter and the CSF. Tractography was optimized using the sMRI tissue segmentations (anatomically constrained tractography, ACT), which is particularly useful when gray and white matter cannot be separated in the FODs. We employed probabilistic fiber tracking and seeded a fixed number of streamlines (N=6) in each voxel at the gray matter / white matter interface to ensure that connectomes were comparable across various brain sizes, as the fetal brain grows rapidly. MRtrix3 software was also used to generate maps of fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) for each individual using the tensor model. Next, for each individual and timepoint 20 white matter bundles were manually delineated in DSI-studio and then imported into pyAFQ to evaluate developmental changes in FA and MD in each bundle. To model the mean developmental trajectory of FA and MD across GA, we averaged FA and MD in each bundle along its length, and fitted mixed linear and polynomial models to bundle-averaged statistics with subject as a random effect and gestational age as predictor. We then compared model fits by Likelihood Ratio Test and compared both the model intercepts and instantaneous slopes between bundles. Finally, we fit separate linear models relating FA and MD to GA across the first and second measurement in each fetus. We  evaluated both the effects of GA at first measurement, as well as the time interval between measurements on each individual's developmental slopes in both diffusion metrics.

Results

We successfully identified 20 white matter bundles (Fig. 1) in each fetus at each timepoint and then evaluated their microstructural development. Overall, we found opposing and non-linear trajectories for bundle-averaged FA and bundle-averaged MD changes during in utero development. For FA, we observed an initially steep decrease that leveled out with increasing GA in most bundles - a trajectory which was best captured by the second degree polynomial models (2nd degree polynomial model significant in 15 of the 20 evaluated bundles, polynomial model outperformed linear model in all bundles except for the forceps minor (FMi), left inferior longitudinal fasciculus (ILF), right anterior thalamic radiation (ATR), and right superior longitudinal fasciculus (SLF)). Both the intercept and the derivative (instantaneous slope) of FA development varied across bundles, suggesting non-uniform development of FA across the white matter. For example, the FA developmental curve in the left ILF had a particularly low intercept and high derivative and the one in left SLF had a particularly high intercept and low derivative. For MD, we observed an initial increase and later decrease (Fig. 2B shows an example bundle) - a trajectory which was best captured by the second degree polynomial models (2nd degree polynomial model significant in 17 of the 20 evaluated bundles, 2nd degree polynomial model outperformed linear model in all bundles). Similar to FA, both the intercept and the derivative of MD development varied across bundles, suggesting non-uniform development of MD across the white matter. For example, the MD developmental curve in the left SLF had a particularly low intercept and a high derivative, the one in the forceps major (FMa) had a particularly high intercept, and the one in the right ATR had a particularly low derivative. When we fit separate linear models for each individual, we found that FA developmental slopes are dependent on GA at first scan (p = 0.04, Fig. 2C shows an example bundle), but not on the time interval between scans (delta GA, p=0.76). In contrast, MD developmental slopes were dependent on GA at first scan (p=0.007, Fig. 2D shows an example bundle) and on the time interval between scans (delta GA, p=0.02).

Conclusion

Here we evaluated the longitudinal microstructural development of 20 white matter bundles within individual fetuses and observed opposing non-linear trajectories for FA and MD. These findings align with prior work1,2 evaluating fetal white matter development at the group level, which reported an initial decrease in FA followed by a sharp increase close to term as well as an initial increase in MD followed by a decrease closer to term. These observed developmental patterns likely reflect progressive changes in tissue organization and axonal maturation during late gestation. Moreover, the variability in intercepts and instantaneous slopes across bundles that we observed highlights a spatially non-uniform maturation across the white matter with some bundles developing earlier and more steeply than others. This matches previous reports suggesting that sensorimotor bundles may develop earlier in gestation than other bundles3. One key benefit of the current study is its longitudinal design, which enabled us to reveal that the slopes of FA and MD development within individuals vary with the fetuses gestational age at first scan, suggesting that the non-linear trajectories seen on the group-level also characterize white matter development in individuals.
