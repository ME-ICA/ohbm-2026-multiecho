Introduction

Adolescence represents a sensitive window of development encompassing significant neurodevelopmental change. Myelin content in white matter tracts and intracortical gray matter increases over the course of this time, scaffolding circuit refinement and contributing to the maturation of cognitive and emotional functioning. Recent evidence also suggests that increases in myelination may close periods of heightened neuroplasticity in multi-modal association cortical regions during adolescence, as well as increase along key association white matter tracts involved in cognitive and emotional processing. While patterns of myelination during development have been extensively studied in non-human animal models, studies of myelination in developing human youth often have focused either on myelination either along white matter tracts or in intracortical gray matter, and have typically used lower-order moments of the tensor (mean diffusivity, fractional anisotropy) to assess tissue properties. In this project, we examine trajectories of mean diffusion kurtosis development, a myelin-sensitive metric obtained from higher order moments of the diffusion orientation distribution function, to delineate longitudinal trajectories of putative myelin development across gray and white matter boundaries. Leveraging data from the longitudinal Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study, we characterize a deep-to-superficial pattern of development that extends across tissue types.

Methods

ABCD participants completed scans at 4 longitudinal timepoints at 21 sites across the United States. Data were obtained from fast-track data processed as part of the ABCD-BIDS Community Collection (ABCC) release 3.1.0. All datasets with prerequisite T1w, dMRI, and field map images were preprocessed with QSIPrep v0.21.41 and postprocessed using the diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI) from DIPY as part of QSIRecon v1.0.0rc22. The present sample comprises participants with data at all four timepoints (N=6,172 scans; 46.2% F, ages 9-17) that successfully completed QSIPrep processing, QSIRecon post-processing, and had complete demographic information. Image quality metrics were produced as part of QSIPrep and captured dMRI image characteristics such as motion, signal-to-noise ratio, and dMRI–T1w registration. To exclude poor quality data, we omitted scans that failed checks for plausible tract geometry measures following processing and reconstruction. A subset of 3,101 postprocessed dMRI datasets across vendors were visually inspected and rated by highly trained reviewers. A multivariate classifier was trained to predict these manual ratings from the automated QSIPrep quality metrics and subsequently applied to the full sample. The classifier quality score showed strong correlations with manual ratings in a held-out dataset (r > 0.6 across scanner types) and was included as a covariate in subsequent modeling.

Analyses focused on mean kurtosis (MK), a metric that shows strong correlations with histological myelin content in both white matter and cortex3. We corrected for site-related technical variation using longitudinal ComBat-GAMM, an extension of LongComBat4 with a random intercept for participant. Participant age and sex assigned at birth were protected during harmonization as covariates of interest. Next, we assessed developmental change in MK using Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs). We first computed mean MK at the tissue level by separately applying MNI gray and white matter probabilistic segmentations to whole-brain MK maps. We then fitted two GAMMs to evaluate mean developmental change in MK in whole-brain cortical gray matter and white matter. Subsequently, we expanded analyses to the voxel level and applied ModelArray5 to iteratively fit GAMMs for each voxel in the brain across timepoints. GAMMs included a smoothed term for age and covariates including sex assigned at birth and the image quality score from the classifier, as well as a random intercept for each participant. We also ran a reduced model without the age term. We quantified the age effect size by computing the partial R2 value indexing improvement of model fit when age was included in the model. From each voxel-wise model, we then extracted the median predicted spline for the MK term across the full age range of the sample.

Next, we sought to characterize the spatiotemporal pattern of MK development across adolescence. Using ANTs registration, we computed a deformable registration between predicted voxel-level MK values at the youngest age in the sample (8.96) and the oldest age in the sample (17.70). This analysis yielded a warp field with vectors representing directional MK change for each voxel across the longitudinal age range. We visually inspected the warp field in order to understand directionality, magnitude, and patterning of regional MK variation across development.

Results

Results revealed that average MK values increased throughout the brain in both gray (F(2.791) = 3369, EDF = 2.791, p < .001) and white matter (F(2.772) = 3398, EDF = 2.772, p < .001) over the course of adolescence (Figure 1A; 1C). Subsequent voxel-wise GAMM models showed that age explained up to 44.8% of the variance in longitudinal differences in MK value (Figure 1B), with effects particularly concentrated in white matter underlying association cortex. Next, we sought to characterize the directional patterns of age-related change. Examination of vector fields capturing change in values between the youngest age in the sample and the oldest age in the sample revealed that spatiotemporal MK maturation unfolded in a deep-to-superficial manner across tissue boundaries (Figure 1D).

Conclusion

Myelination occurring during adolescence is implicated in the closure of sensitive periods of neurodevelopment, the stabilization of neural architecture, and underpins individual differences in behavior. Here, we present a fine-grained, within-person view of a non-invasive myelin-sensitive diffusion MRI metric, the mean kurtosis of the diffusion orientation distribution function. Our results suggest that increases in mean kurtosis observed during adolescence progress along a deep-to-superficial axis in an anatomically specific manner that transcends stereotyped boundaries between white and gray matter regions. Specifically, our findings show that the largest developmental changes in putative myelin content occur in white matter underlying association cortex, but that notable development also occurs in subcortical and cortical regions. Additional analyses as part of this ongoing project will further characterize region-specific rates of putative myelination, as well as individual differences in developmental trajectories and timing. These emerging findings contribute to a richer understanding of neurodevelopment and may have important implications for behavioral patterns emerging during adolescence, including the onset of psychopathology.
