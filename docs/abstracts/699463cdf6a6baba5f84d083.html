Introduction

Both the Electroencephalogram (EEG) and functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) are non-invasive methods to capture human brain activity, that can map the functional connectivity structure at the whole-brain scale. 7T-fMRI enables measuring sub-mm layer-dependent activity. We previously showed that a moderate connectivity correlation can be reliably reproduced across different setups, including at 7T [1]. Recently, we also demonstrated that sub-mm fMRI with simultaneous EEG acquisition is feasible [2]. Macrovascular venous signals contaminate layer-dependent BOLD fMRI [3], but their impact on the EEG-fMRI correlation is unexplored. In this study we want to explore the electrophysiological correlates of layer-dependent connectivity. 

Methods

Here we explored 13 eyes-open resting-state simultaneous EEG-fMRI (0.8mm, 2D-EPI) recordings using a 64 channel EEG (Brain Products) in a 7T Terra MRI Scanner (Siemens) [2] and compared it to our previous data of 72 participants from four different imaging centers [1]. T1 images were used to delineate 68 cortical regions of the Desikan atlas. EEG was filtered and projected into source space of these regions (Brainstorm). Using the equidistant approach of LayNII [4], we separated the cortical graymatter into 3 layers (surface, intermediate, and deep).  Average BOLD-timeseries were extracted for each region and layer. Functional connectivity was calculated between each regions’/layers timecourse (using the imaginary part of the coherency/iCoh for EEG and Pearson’s r for fMRI, Figure 1). EEG connectivity was calculated for each canonical EEG frequency band. Finally, the crossmodal correlation between EEG and fMRI whole-brain (layer-)connectivity strength was calculated (Figure 1).  

Results

Both fMRI and EEG connectomes were correlated to the connectomes of our previous 4 data sets (range(fMRI): r=0.71-0.74 EEG: r=0.29-0.65). Wenn comparing fMRI layer-resolved connectomes to classic connectomes the surface layer was more correlated to classic connectome than intermediate and deep layers (both intra-dataset r(surface/intermediate/deep)=0.95/0.98/0.99 and inter-dataset wise: r=0.65-0.70/0.69-0.74/0.70-0.75). We confirm our previous findings [1] that EEG connectivity is moderately correlated to fMRI connectivity (r~0.3), with the largest correlation between beta-EEG and fMRI (r=0.35). We further show that connectivities of surface, intermediate, and deep layers are moderately correlated to all EEG bands. The surface layer connectivity was less correlated to EEG than the intermediate and deep layers (e.g beta-EEG vs. fMRI surface/intermediate/deep r=0.33/0.34/0.35). 

Conclusion

We confirm that a 0.8-mm fMRI protocol can be used to derive reliable EEG-fMRI connectomes in line with our previous results at lower resolutions. We further show that this approach extends to cortical layers and that the mixing of macrovascular signals, dominant close to the pial surface, might contribute to a lower EEG-fMRI correlation. We observe an opposite effect when comparing layered connectivity to a classical one-layer approach: surface layer connectivity (vs. intermediate and deep layer) is most linked to a classical one-layer approach, in line with [3]. This hints that while vascular effects seem to drive a global one-layer connectivity approach, a potentially less biased signal of the deep layers seems to be more closely linked to EEG connectivity. Alternatively connectivity between deep layers might be genuinely more related to neuronal signals recorded by EEG. Future work will investigate the impact of removing unwanted venous signals to better understand the interactions between EEG and layer fMRI.
