Introduction

Mesoscale, layer-specific fMRI using vascular-space-occupancy (VASO) has been implemented primarily at ultra-high field strengths (≥ 7.0 T). While offering superior SNR, 7.0 T systems are largely confined to specialized research centers, limiting clinical translation and general accessibility. Although 3.0 T scanners are ubiquitous in cognitive and clinical neuroscience, most VASO implementations remain vendor-specific binaries, constraining cross-platform reproducibility and multi-site collaboration [1]. Pulseq, an open-source, vendor-agnostic framework for sequence development, offers a solution to this bottleneck. Here, we present the implementation of a 3D slice-saturation slab-inversion VASO (SS-SI-VASO) sequence at 3.0 T using Pulseq [2]. This work aims to democratize mesoscale fMRI, providing a reproducible and more accessible pathway for investigating laminar cortical dynamics on standard clinical hardware.

Methods

We developed a 3D SS-SI-VASO sequence in Pulseq (v1.4.2), utilizing a 3D-EPI readout. The sequence was first prototyped on a 5.0 T MRI (United Imaging uMR Jupiter) to verify portability and subsequently deployed on a 3.0 T MRI (United Imaging uMR890). The sequence employs slab-selective inversion with interleaved blood-nulled and BOLD image acquisitions. Acquisition parameters were: FOV = 172.8 × 172.8 × 24 mm³; voxel size = 0.8 × 0.8 × 1.2 mm³; 20 slices; TE = 29.3 ms; TI₁ = 802 ms (optimized for 3.0 T blood-nulling); slab TR = 1.47 s; effective TR = 4.5 s. To maintain temporal efficiency, we utilized in-plane GRAPPA (R=3) and 6/8 partial Fourier. A fully sampled reference scan was acquired for offline GRAPPA reconstruction, which included Nyquist-ghost correction via three-line navigators [3]. Validation employed a motor task (finger-tapping) consisting of a 7-volume baseline followed by 12 alternating activation/rest blocks. Data processing included NORDIC denoising [4], motion correction, nuisance regression, and BOLD correction. Cortical depth analysis was performed using the equi-distance approach in LayNii [5].

Results

Figure 1 displays individual-leve activation maps for VASO and BOLD contrasts, alongside manually defined ROIs in the primary motor cortex (M1, hand knob). Functional maps exhibited robust activation in the gray matter. Figure 1 (rightmost panel) and Figure 2 illustrate individual and group-level laminar profiles derived from 11 equi-distance depths. While BOLD profiles showed the characteristic draining vein bias towards the pial surface, VASO profiles at 3.0 T revealed a distinct laminar structure. Notably, comparing deep and superficial compartments revealed a “double-peak” activation profile in M1, consistent with input-output microcircuitry previously characterized at 7.0 T.

Conclusion

We present a successful Pulseq-based implementation of 3D SS-SI-VASO at 3.0 T. By achieving sub-millimeter in-plane resolution and functional sensitivity sufficient to detect laminar heterogeneity, we demonstrate that mesoscale fMRI is feasible on clinical field strengths without proprietary vendor constraints. This framework establishes a foundation for accessible, reproducible mesoscale fMRI, opening new avenues for translational research in neurological disorders.
