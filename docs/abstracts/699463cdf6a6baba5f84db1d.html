Introduction

Chemical Exchange Saturation Transfer (CEST) MRI detects dilute biomolecules by probing proton exchange between labile solute protons and bulk water. Amide proton transfer-weighted (APTw) imaging is an endogenous CEST contrast suitable for clinical applications due to its relatively high solute concentration, slow exchange rate (~30 s-1), and spectral separation from bulk water.1 However, clinical translation remains challenged by prolonged acquisition times, as densely sampled Z-spectra are required to capture weak CEST effects and correct for B0-induced spectral shifts across frequency offsets. 

Conventional acceleration using parallel imaging and compressed sensing achieve moderate speedup but face reconstruction accuracy limitations,² particularly critical for CEST where target signals represent only ~5% of total water signal. Deep learning-based reconstruction enables substantial acceleration. Supervised methods demonstrate superior accuracy but require extensive paired fully sampled datasets which are clinically impractical. Self-supervised learning methods eliminate the need for fully sampled reference data³ but may suffer from limited accuracy due to absent ground truth supervision, critical for preserving Z-spectral fidelity.

We propose a novel framework combining supervised and self-supervised learning with spectral-wise random encoding acquisition. Our approach leverages multi-offset volumetric CEST EPI with varying k-space sampling patterns to generate spectral-averaging data for pseudo-labels without additional scan time, while incorporating noise-augmented spectral-wise counterparts for robustness. The framework uses both pseudo-labeled and unlabeled datasets simultaneously by enforcing consistency between model reconstructions. This approach demonstrates improved reconstruction accuracy and preserved tissue contrast, enabling high-quality 3D CEST imaging with clinically feasible acquisition times.

Methods

Data Acquisition: 3D CEST EPI data were acquired on a 3T MRI system using pseudo-continuous RF saturation pulses and segmented EPI readout. Variable-density CAIPI sampling with acceleration factors of Ry x Rz = 3 x 2 yielded sixfold acceleration, reducing g-factor penalties through shifted phase encodings.4

Imaging parameters: FOV = 220×220×80 mm³, matrix = 110×110×40, resolution = 2 mm³ isotropic, TR/TE = 35/14 ms, flip angle = 20° with water excitation. CEST saturation used pseudo-continuous wave pulses (B₁ = 1.0 μT, duration = 299.8 ms, duty cycle = 99.9%, Ts1/Ts2 = 1.5/0.3 s, Td = 1.5 s). Z-spectra were acquired from −5.0 to +5.0 ppm (0.125 ppm intervals, 81 offsets). Total imaging time is 6 min 28 sec. CAIPI patterns were randomly varied across offsets following a spectral random-walk strategy.

Reconstruction Framework: The proposed hybrid framework integrates two complementary components. 

1) Spectral-wise SSDU: Unlike conventional SSDU using uniform partitioning across all frequency offsets, our approach employs distinct partition masks for each offset. K-space data were partitioned into Θ-set (data consistency) and Λ-set (loss calculation), ensuring balanced information utilization across offsets and maintaining spectral fidelity. 

2) Pseudo-supervised Learning: To provide reference guidance, we exploit CAIPI sampling pattern variation to generate pseudo-labels through offset-wise averaging. Complementary k-space coverage across different patterns enables approximate full k-space data by spectral averaging. This inherently co-registered pseudo-label enables supervised training without additional scan time. Noise augmentation (Gaussian noise, σ ∈ [0, 0.03]) was incorporated for robustness.5

The network employed a ResNet-based CNN architecture consisting of 10 residual blocks (two 3×3 convolutional layers, 64 channels, ReLU activation, xScale = 0.1), unrolled for 10 iterations with conjugate gradient data consistency. Coil sensitivity maps were estimated from pseudo-label data. Training was performed using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁴, batch size of 1, and 100 epochs.

Post-Processing: Post-processing consisted of three main steps: (1) exclusion of edge slices, (2) principal component analysis (PCA) denoising via singular value decomposition (SVD) using 40 principal components with soft-thresholding, and (3) calculation of Z-spectra as S/S₀. B₀ correction was performed using the water saturation shift referencing (WASSR) algorithm. APTw quantification was calculated using the magnetization transfer ratio asymmetry at 3.5 ppm: MTRasym(3.5 ppm) = 100% × [S(−3.5 ppm)/S₀ − S(+3.5 ppm)/S₀].

Results

Figure A presents representative Z-spectra from three tissue types: normal brain (green), tumor (red), and CSF (blue). Deep learning methods showed enhanced water saturation compared to RPCA, with deeper magnetization transfer at 0 ppm (dashed boxes), indicating improved sensitivity essential for accurate CEST quantification.

Figure B shows reconstructed CEST images at three offsets (−5, −2.5, 0 ppm) and corresponding APTw maps across different methods. All deep learning approaches demonstrated substantial noise suppression compared to RPCA. SSDU-based methods preserved anatomical details but showed blurring in upper and lower brain regions. Pseudo-supervised learning enhanced tissue contrast while removing edge artifacts but exhibited central aliasing.

The proposed method, integrating spectral-wise SSDU with pseudo-supervised learning, mitigated both peripheral blurring and central aliasing. While slightly smoother than SSDU methods, overall image quality was well preserved across all offsets.

APTw maps (bottom panel) confirmed these improvements. All deep learning methods generated more uniform maps than RPCA, with reduced noise and improved consistency across anatomical views (Cor, Sag, Ax). The proposed method demonstrated consistently high quality across all viewing planes.

Conclusion

We developed a hybrid reconstruction framework combining spectral-wise SSDU with pseudo-supervised learning for accelerated 3D CEST imaging. By leveraging varying CAIPI sampling patterns across frequency offsets to generate pseudo-labels and incorporating noise augmentation strategies, the proposed method achieved superior reconstruction quality compared to purely self-supervised approaches. The method successfully mitigated both peripheral blurring artifacts and central aliasing while maintaining spectral fidelity across all frequency offsets, enabling high-quality APTw imaging with sixfold acceleration and clinically feasible acquisition times.
