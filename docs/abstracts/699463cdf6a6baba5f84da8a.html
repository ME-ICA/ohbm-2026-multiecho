Introduction

Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) is a state associated with elevated dementia risk1. Deficits in the ability to encode and remember the relationship between discrete items in associative memory are pronounced in MCI2, reflecting vulnerability of binding processes. Metamemory, which is the self-awareness and beliefs about one’s memory3, may influence how individuals engage neural systems supporting memory4, but its role in MCI is unclear. Therefore, we examined whether behavioural sensitivity, neural responses, and pattern separability in associative memory vary across individual differences in metamemory in older adults with MCI.

Methods

Twenty-eight older adults with MCI (72.6±4.6y) completed fMRI during a mixed block/event-related face-place associative memory task. Images were acquired on a 3T Siemens Prisma (MB=4, TR=1000 ms, TE=30 ms, flip angle=40°, 2.5 mm isotropic). Metamemory was measured using the Multifactorial Memory Questionnaire (MMQ) that dissociates perceived Ability, Satisfaction, and Strategy use5. Preprocessing included an iterative method for echo-planar distortion correction and independent component analysis based denoising. Recognition sensitivity (d′) was estimated using signal-detection theory with loglinear correction. Cluster-corrected whole-brain contrasts in associative pairs versus items at encoding and recognition defined posterior visual regions of interest (ROIs) for percent signal change (PSC) extraction. Cross-validated Mahalanobis distance quantified pair-item pattern separability within visual ROIs. Multiple linear regression models predicted PSC from each MMQ domain, adjusting for age and sex, with secondary models adjusting for pair d′.

Results

Behaviourally, recognition sensitivity differed by stimulus type (faces d′=1.690, places d′=1.327, pairs d′=1.097). MMQ domains were not associated with recognition d′ for faces, places, or pairs (all p > 0.05). Whole brain contrasts (cluster-forming Z>1.65, cluster-corrected p<.05 FWE) identified posterior ventral and lateral visual ROIs. In preliminary ROI regressions, only MMQ-Satisfaction inversely predicted PSC in a visual ROI during pair recognition (β=-0.009, SE=0.004, p=0.029) and remained after adding pair d′ (β=-0.009, SE=0.002, p<0.001). Analyses of pair-item pattern separability are underway and will be reported alongside univariate findings.

Conclusion

Associative recognition is reduced relative to item recognition in MCI. Lower memory satisfaction was associated with higher visual engagement during associative recognition beyond behavioural performance. Integrating localization with distance-based representational metrics may provide a framework to test whether metamemory relates to how distinctly associations are coded beyond mean activation differences.
