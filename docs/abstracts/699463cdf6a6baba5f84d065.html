Introduction

General cognitive ability in childhood has been linked to differences in brain structure and connectivity in numerous studies.1 However, longitudinal examinations of links between early cognitive ability and brain development are limited.2,3 We examined how cognitive ability in childhood—indexed by matrix reasoning, fluid cognition, and crystallized cognition—may be associated with longitudinal development of gray and white matter structure and resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) at the whole-brain and regional/system level.

Methods

We used longitudinal data from 10,494 participants of the ABCD study (https://abcdstudy.org/; release 6.0) at baseline, 2-year, 4-year, and 6-year follow-ups. At baseline, cognitive ability was assessed using age-corrected standard scores for fluid and crystallized cognition from the NIH Toolbox, and total scaled matrix reasoning scores from the Wechsler Intelligence Scale for Children V. MRI acquisition was conducted across 21 sites using harmonized protocols,4 and images meeting quality control criteria preprocessed using openly documented procedures.5 Multimodal brain outcome measures were global (i.e., whole-brain) average cortical thickness, total cortical surface area, total cortical volume, fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), between-network rsFC, and within-network rsFC at each timepoint. Local measures were also examined at the level of Desikan-Killiany atlas regions (thickness, area, volume), AtlasTrack white matter tracts (FA, MD), or Gordon functional networks (rsFC). We first examined associations between baseline cognitive ability (n=3 variables; modeled separately) and change in global brain measures using linear mixed-effects models in R version 4.2.1 (covariates: sex, baseline income-to-needs ratio, scanner model, mean framewise displacement; random intercepts: participant ID, family ID, site; random slopes: participant ID). Significant global effects were followed up with 1) exploratory cross-sectional analyses at each timepoint, and 2) specificity analyses at the region/system level using the same longitudinal analytic framework as global models. False discovery rate (FDR) correction (p<.05) was applied within each modality and cognitive measure.

Results

Higher childhood fluid cognition was associated with faster contraction of gray matter area (p=.001) and volume (p=.038; Fig. 1a). Despite more rapid contraction, cross-sectionally those with higher vs lower baseline fluid cognition maintained higher area and volume across timepoints (Fig. 1b). For white matter, higher matrix reasoning related to slower increases in FA relative to those with mean or lower baseline matrix reasoning (p=.023; Fig. 1c); cross-sectionally, those with higher baseline matrix reasoning also had higher FA at the first three timepoints (Fig. 1d). For rsFC, lower values in all cognitive measures were associated with greater increases in between-network rsFC that were absent in those with higher baseline cognitive scores (p<.004; Fig. 1e). Cross-sectional differences were less pronounced for global between-network rsFC than structural measures (Fig. 1f). Regionally, differences in gray matter structural trajectories by cognitive ability were most prominent in the frontal lobe, while effects for white matter and between-network rsFC trajectories varied across distributed brain systems particularly higher-order cognitive and motor systems. There were no significant associations between baseline cognition and cortical thickness (range p=.070–.376), MD (p=.066–.936), or within-network rsFC (p=.389–1.000) development.

Conclusion

Across modalities, higher general cognitive ability in childhood was associated with slower growth rates for brain measures that normatively increased over adolescence, and more rapid decreases for brain measures typically showing an adolescent decline. Findings may be related to brain-sculpting effects of early cognitive stimulation and learning, or shared underlying factors (e.g., genetics) influencing both early cognition and later brain maturation. Future work is needed to shed light on mechanisms linking brain and cognitive features.
