Introduction

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is increasingly viewed as a disorder of brain circuit connectivity. While diffusion MRI (dMRI) is widely used to study white matter (WM) microstructure, existing methods face a trade-off: they either focus on microstructural metrics while neglecting macro-morphological information or model overall bundle shape at the cost of spatial precision. To address this, we propose an innovative analytical pipeline based on the Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping (LDDMM) framework, utilizing the Jacobian determinant as a direct morphological metric. In this study, we applied this framework to a cross-sectional dataset to validate its sensitivity and robustness in identifying MDD-specific WM morphological abnormalities.

Methods

We included a cross-sectional dataset comprising 308 MDD patients and 100 age- and sex-matched healthy controls (HC). Following standard preprocessing and whole-brain tractography, all subject’s streamlines were registered into a common space. As illustrated in Figure 1, a two-level clustering strategy was employed to homogenize fiber shapes (Fig. 1a). Specifically, the first level involved coarse clustering based on two anatomical atlases: the Yeo-7 network atlas1 and subcortical nuclei were parcellated to define regional connectivity using MRtrix tools 2. The second level consisted of fine clustering, wherein the cosine series method was utilized to extract even-indexed coefficients as feature vectors for K-means clustering, with the optimal cluster number determined via the elbow method. Subsequently, we cleaned the clusters and extracted representative centerlines using QuickBundles 3 (Fig. 1b). Based on the LDDMM framework (Fig. 1c), we registered each subject's fiber centerline to a standard geometric straight line (defined as the average normal line of the cluster). During this process, the Jacobian determinant (det(J)) was computed; this value reflects the local deformation required to "straighten" the curved bundle, thereby directly quantifying intrinsic geometric morphology (e.g., curvature and tortuosity). Finally, we compared det(J) values between MDD and HC groups at each point along the centerline using independent two-sample t-tests (FDR corrected, p<0.05). To improve the robustness of the results, we introduced a "highly significant bundle" criterion (requiring at least 4 contiguous significant points). We further compared our proposed method with the traditional FA-based approach.

Results

Under the stringent "highly significant bundle" criterion, the Jacobian determinant method revealed widespread and stable WM morphological abnormalities in MDD patients, demonstrating significantly superior sensitivity compared to traditional FA analysis (Figure 2). Specifically, the Jacobian determinant identified multiple anatomically continuous abnormal bundles concentrated in the connection pathways of the Control Network-Hippocampus, Default Mode Network-Amygdala, Salience Network-Hippocampus, and Somatomotor Network-Cerebellum/Brainstem. The observed damage in these regions is consistent with previous studies 4. For instance, we observed significant local compression in the Control Network-Hippocampus (t<−3, pcorr<0.03) and Default Mode Network-Amygdala (t<−2.5, pcorr<0.05) pathways. This directly indicates that MDD patients exhibit local morphological contraction within these key connection regions responsible for emotion regulation and cognitive control.

Conclusion

This study proposed a Jacobian determinant-based analytical framework to investigate WM morphological changes in MDD. Experimental results showed that this method has several notable advantages, including higher sensitivity, robustness, and physical interpretability. This work not only provides a powerful computational tool for advancing the investigation of the neurobiological mechanisms of depression but also opens new avenues for developing imaging biomarkers to predict treatment response and guide personalized medicine.
