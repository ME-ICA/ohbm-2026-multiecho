Introduction

Schizophrenia (SCZ) is a severe neuropsychiatric disorder that affects approximately 1% of the global population and ranks among the top ten causes of disability worldwide.[1] Previous neuroimaging studies have primarily focused on the pathological processes of cerebral cortex and subcortical structures,[2] while largely overlooking the cerebellum. Recently, cerebellum has been reported to be closely associated with cognitive deficits in SCZ.[3] However, it remains unclear how the cerebellum plays a role in the early stage of disease progression. In addition, individuals with SCZ exhibit significant heterogeneity in clinical phenotypes, which give rise to substantial challenges to clinical diagnosis, treatment, and drug development. However, there is no validated tool available for biological stratification in this population. Machine learning can identify subtypes with different trajectories of regional volume reduction by sequencing their biological characteristics.[4]

Methods

This study included multi-center data of structural magnetic resonance images (MRI) from a large-scale cohort including 1,588 patients with SCZ (638 females, age = 32.0 ± 12.0 years) and 2,341 healthy controls (HC) (1,041 females, age = 33.3 ± 13.6 years), primarily from Chinese hospitals. Standardized voxel-based morphometric analysis (VBM) was performed by the Computational Anatomy Toolbox (CAT12) (http://www.neuro.uni-jena.de/cat/). Cerebellum was further segmented into nine subregions. Morphometric feature (i.e., averaged gray matter volume [GMV]) was extracted from each subregion of the cerebellum. The GMV value was further transformed to z score (i.e., normative deviations from the average level of HC). By using Subtype and Staging Inference algorithm (i.e., SuStaIn) on z scores from nine cerebellar subregions, distinct trajectories (i.e., subtypes) of GMV reduction were inferred from individuals with SCZ. To characterize the GMV reduction pattern of each subtype, the mean GMV z-score for each subtype was calculated and further mapped to a brain template for visualization. ANOVA was used to compare the difference of clinical symptoms among three subtypes. Post-hoc analysis was conducted by two-sided independent-samples t test. To investigate the relationship between regional atrophy and clinical symptoms, Spearman correlation analysis was used to examine the correlation between the regional z-score and Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) scores. A flowchart is provided in the Figure 1. All participating sites obtained approval from their respective institutional review boards or ethics committees. Written informed consent was obtained from every participant.

Results

The optimal number of SuStaIn trajectories was determined at k=3 by cross-validation, indicating that there were three stable and different trajectories of cerebellar volume reduction. The three trajectories are described in Figure 2a. Trajectory 1 showed that the reduction of cerebellar subregion volume started in area 10, followed by the adjacent area 7b, and then expanded to the areas 8, 2, 9, 7, 6, 4_5, and finally reached the area 3. Trajectory 2 showed that the volume reduction started in area 3, then gradually expanding to the areas 4_5, 6, 1, 10, 9, 8, 7b, and finally reaching area 2. Trajectory 3 was characterized by starting in area 7b, followed by the adjacent areas 8, and then gradually expanding to the areas 2, 9, 1, 6, 4_5, 3, and finally reaching area 10. 

To characterize regional gray matter alterations specific to each subtype, we analyzed regional morphological z-scores (i.e., normative deviations from HC) across the cerebellar subregions. Figure 2b shows distinct patterns of cerebellar volume loss among the three subtypes. Comparison of clinical data showed significant difference among the three subtypes in depressive subscale score (F=3.8, p=0.023) and cognitive subscale score (F=4.7, p=0.008). Post-hoc analysis further revealed that the subtype 1 had worse depressive symptom (subtype 1 vs. subtype 3, p=0.007), but better cognitive symptom (subtype 1 vs. subtype 2, p=0.019; subtype 1 vs. subtype 3, p=0.008) (Figure 2c, d). The subregion area 9 exhibited a significant negative correlation with negative dimension of PANSS, only observed in subtype 3 (r=0.39, p<0.001) (Figure 2e).

Conclusion

This study identified three distinct trajectories of cerebellar subregional volume reduction from large scale brain MRI data in SCZ. Notably, these trajectories have distinct onset regions and spreading patterns in "pathological progression", highlighting biological heterogeneity in cerebellar pathophysiological processes, which may help for disease stratification and prognosis. This finding also promotes the shift from a "single group" of syndromes to new biotypes, opening up a novel perspective for investigating the pathological mechanisms of cerebellum underpinnings of SCZ.
