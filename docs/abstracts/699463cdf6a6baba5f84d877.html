Introduction

Epilepsy is one of the most common chronic neurological disorders. Many patients with temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) are reported as drug-resistant. Patients with drug-resistant TLE may be offered surgical resection of the temporal lobe; 60-80% achieve seizure freedom. White matter hyperintensities (WMH) are areas of white matter (WM) that appear bright on T2-FLAIR (Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery) MRIs and may be caused by underlying pathologies such as gliosis, myelin damage or damage to small blood vessels. Some studies have also shown links between WMH and epilepsy, including late-onset and newly-diagnosed adult epilepsy,1 but no clear links have yet been shown between the presence of WMH and patho-clinical aspects of epilepsy. The aim of our research was to apply an automated quantification technique to identify WMH to determine whether WMH are more prevalent in patients with refractory TLE compared to age-matched controls and whether WMH lesion burden is related to surgical outcome. Furthermore, we sought to explore whether relationships existed between seizure outcome and WM tract ‘disconnection’ through normative connectome analysis.

Methods

Patients with drug-resistant unilateral TLE underwent pre-operative imaging and neuropsychological assessment. MRI acquisition included 3D T1-weighted and 3D T2-FLAIR sequences at Medical University Bonn, Germany. 111 patients were included in this study and were matched with 94 healthy controls by biological sex and age (+/- 3 years). The same MRI sequences were acquired for the controls at the Medical University Bonn using the same parameters. No cognitive testing was acquired from the control group. Patients underwent a selective amygdalohippocampectomy and postsurgical seizure outcomes were evaluated using the Engel scoring system within 2-years of surgery. For this study, patients were grouped by surgical outcome as optimal (Engel I) or sub-optimal (Engel II-IV). DeepLesionBrain (DLB)2,3 was used to automatically segment WMH. It utilises deep learning algorithms and convoluted-neural networks to automatically identify and segment WMH from T1 and T2-FLAIR images. The number, location (periventricular, juxtacortical, deep white and infratentorial), and volume burden of WMH were automatically computed. WMH lesions were used by the DLB pipeline to estimate ‘disconnection probability’ of 64 normative WM tracts from the HCP1065 atlas.4 Statistical analysis between controls and patient groups was performed using Kruskal-Wallis tests, with post-hoc Dunn-Bonferroni testing.

Results

Patients with TLE had a significantly greater total number (p=0.03) and total volume burden (VB) (p<0.001) of WMH compared to controls. A significant increase in the number of WMH was found between patients with a sub-optimal surgical outcome compared to controls (p=0.04). Patients with optimal and sub-optimal outcomes had significantly increased total VB (p<0.001and p<0.001, respectively) compared to controls. When analysed according to location, the periventricular area was the only region to show a significant difference between the control and patient groups. Periventricular WMH were significantly increased in both the total number (p<0.001) and VB (p<0.001) in the patient groups compared to the controls. There was no difference between surgical outcome groups. No significant difference was noted between groups in the number or VB of WMH in the juxtacortical, deep white or infratentorial regions. The total number of tracts reported as disconnected was significantly increased in the patient groups compared to the controls (p<0.001). However there was no difference between patient outcome groups in the number of tracts identified as disconnected.

Conclusion

People with refractory TLE have a greater incidence of WMH than neurologically healthy individuals, particularly within the periventricular region. The presence of preoperative WMH did not have significance for postoperative seizure outcome. If WMH are considered a proxy for impaired brain health, then these findings are consistent with our previous work that reported changes in TLE generally regardless of postoperative seizure outcome.5 Further research is required to determine the role of WMH and its relationship with the pathophysiology of epilepsy.
