Introduction

Ultra-high-field (7 T) pediatric MRI offers finer spatial/temporal resolution and faster acquisitions improving tolerability, mitigating motion, and enhancing sensitivity to subtle structural anomalies. However, its deployment is limited by RF safety constraints and increased B1+ inhomogeneity, necessitating parallel transmission (pTx) for whole-brain excitation. We implemented a 7 T pTx protocol for high-resolution cerebellar anatomical and functional imaging at individual-level in school-age participants.

Methods

We employed pediatric pTx (16 Tx/32Rx head array) SAR control models[1] and universal and tailored dynamic shimming[2]. The protocol comprised whole-brain MP2RAGE (0.9 mm isotropic), cerebellar-slab MP2RAGE (0.6 mm iso), and 2D GRE/TSE of the vermis (0.27 mm in-plane, 1 mm slice thickness). To adapt to younger school-age children, per sequence time was limited to 6 min. fMRI used 3D-EPI (1.5 mm iso, TR = 2 s) and included a child-friendly finger-tapping block task :left/right button press to targets presented for 500 ms, with 1250-1750 ms interstimulus intervals (10 targets per block; 6 blocks per run; 2 runs). We also acquired 18 min of naturalistic movie-watching using four child-appropriate clips (two cartoons and two macaque-based documentary clips) to enable functional connectivity analyses (motor cerebral seeds to cerebellar voxel correlations; intra-cerebellar connectivity gradients). The protocol was tested in 4 typically developing children (6-12 y/o) and 4 young adults (18-21 y/o).

Results

Whole-brain MP2RAGE showed uniform contrast with little motion effect. Cerebellar excitation homogeneity was satisfactory and despite occurrence of motion artefacts with cerebellar selective higher resolutions – not clearly correlated to age – many images allowed delineation of individual folia up to third-fourth branching order, both visually (Figure 1A and 1B) and via intensity-based white-matter segmentation. Individual-level functional imaging repeatedly (all ages) revealed fine-grained (intra folium) direct motor activations (Figure 1B) so as canonical motor-to-cognitive cerebellar gradient of connectivity consistent with lower-field group reports, while cerebral-cerebellar hand-knob seed-to-voxel correlations were more variable across individuals and explicit in only 3/8 subjects (including one 12 y/o).

Conclusion

Beyond proof of feasibility and guarantee of safety, our pediatric-ready 7 T pTx workflow achieves uniform whole-brain excitation and submillimetric, cerebellum-optimized structural and functional imaging. Despite the possibility of some residual artifacts partly inherent to very-high resolution even in adults, these results lay the groundwork for precision phenotyping in clinical and neurodevelopmental applications.
