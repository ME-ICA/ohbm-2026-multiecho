Introduction

Emerging evidence suggests that the pathophysiology of depression involves dysfunction of the glutamatergic system, including abnormalities in the mechanisms regulating clearance and metabolism of glutamate (Glu), along with related cytoarchitectural and morphological alterations in several brain regions mediating the cognitive and affective processes central to depression, such as the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC). Recent meta-analyses of proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) studies point to lower Glu metabolism in patients with depression compared to psychiatrically healthy controls in portions of the ACC (Moriguchi et al., 2019). At the same time, extensive animal model data have shown that different types of environmental stress enhance Glu release and transmission in the ACC and cause significant structural effects, including dendritic remodeling, reduction in synapses, potential volumetric reductions, and phenotypic behaviors resembling those observed in depressed human patients (Cao et al., 2021). While one study has found that stress-induced glutamatergic adaptation in the rostral ACC is absent in unmedicated depressed adults (Cooper et al., 2021), it remains unclear whether first-line antidepressant treatment can reverse these effects. To address this gap, we recruited and measured ACC Glu in unmedicated adolescents and young adults with depression before and after a robust social stress test, using MRS before (at baseline) and again after a 12-week trial (at follow-up) of a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor.

Methods

Forty-one participants aged 14 to 21 (19.38 ± 1.70 years, 29 females) who were prescribed either fluoxetine or escitalopram and met the eligibility criteria for this study (see Coury et al., 2024 for more details) completed MR scans on the Siemens Magnetom Terra 7T system at the University of Southern California Stevens Neuroimaging and Informatics Institute. The scan protocol included standard imaging sequences with a 3-plane localizer, a 2D fast GRE coil sensitivity map for coil combination, and B0 and B1 maps to optimize the transmit gain for the subsequent scans. For MRS, a semiLASER sequence with a custom-made broadband excitation pulse and four refocusing pulses (Kaiser et al., 2022) was acquired for each voxel of interest with the following acquisition parameters: TE=29 ms (TE1/TE2/TE3= 9/10/10 ms), TR=4000 ms, NEX=64. Before acquiring MRS data, unsuppressed water acquisitions were used to calibrate the localization flip angles, and automated and manual localized shimming on the volumes of interest were performed until the water linewidth was below 30 Hz. Water-suppressed metabolite spectra (spectral width 12 kHz, 4096 complex points) were acquired with outer volume suppression. An unsuppressed water reference spectrum was obtained for eddy-current compensation and receive-channel reconstruction. Volumes of interest included the dorsal ACC and rostral ACC, each measuring 15x15x15mm3 and, at the baseline pre-stress scan, manually placed, with confirmation from the senior author. For the baseline post-stress scan (and for all follow-up scans), in-house MATLAB scripts were used to compute the coordinates for a specific ACC voxel by applying the transformation matrix needed to coregister the target scan with the baseline pre-stress MP2RAGE scan to the coordinates of the baseline pre-stress ACC voxel (Fig. 1A).

Processing of the T1-weighted MP2RAGE images and of the MRS was conducted using Osprey pipeline (version 2.8.1) implemented in MATLAB (R2022a; Mathworks, Natick MA). Basis sets for metabolite quantification were simulated with the SpinEvolution software, applying explicit coherence pathway selection to account for the action of the crusher gradient and using prior knowledge of chemical shift and J-coupling. The effects of fluid within each ACC voxel were corrected for by the respective fraction of non-fluid components (i.e., white matter, gray matter) based on tissue segmentation values derived from the T1-weighted images in Osprey. All metabolite concentrations were estimated with LCModel (version 6.3) using water-scaling based on tissue segmentation. Only voxels with relative Cramer-Rao lower bounds (CRLBs) < 20% for Glu and total creatine were considered for further analysis. See Fig. 1B for spectra and fit results from an exemplar subject.

Results

All results reported here are for the dorsal ACC (DACC) only. Out of the 41 participants with baseline data, 36 also had follow-up data with CRLB less than 20%. In the 14 days leading up to the MR scan, all but 9 reported taking their medication consistently for all 14 days: 4 participants reported taking 13/14 days, 2 reported taking 12/14 days, and 3 reported taking 10/14 days (mean: 13.44 ± 1.8 days). The average interval between pre-stress and post-stress sessions at baseline was 124.39 ± 20.23 minutes and, at follow-up, 116.69 ± 20.27 minutes. The average interval between baseline and follow-up assessments was 12.9 ± 2.34 weeks.

Using Pearson’s correlations, we found that age was not significantly correlated with Glu in the DACC at either baseline or follow-up (all ps>0.323). Using independent t-tests, we found that there were no sex differences in DACC Glu at either timepoint (all ps>0.178) and that antidepressant type (fluoxetine, escitalopram) and prescribed dose did not yield differences in DACC Glu at the pre-stress scan, post-stress scan, or in their differences (all ps>0.463). Using paired t­-tests, we found no effects of stress on DACC Glu at baseline (t(40)=1.125; p=0.267) but we observed a statistically significant stress-related upregulation of DACC Glu after 12 weeks of antidepressant treatment (t(35)=3.411, p<0.002; Fig. 2AB). Clinician-reported and self-reported depression scores significantly improved after antidepressant treatment (all ps<0.0001). However, stress-related changes in DACC Glu at either baseline or follow-up were not correlated with concurrent depression severity scores, perceived stress, or tension (all ps>0.069). Stress-related changes in DACC Glu at baseline were also not predictive of depression severity scores at follow-up (all ps>0.075).

Conclusion

Our findings replicate previous research (Cooper et al., 2021) by demonstrating that unmedicated depressed youth do not have an adaptive glutamatergic response to stress as measured in the DACC. We extend this important finding by providing initial evidence that effective antidepressant treatment may restore this response. Future studies will compare our current results in psychiatrically healthy controls and unmedicated depressed adolescents, and will also examine whether other antidepressants besides selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors also produce similar effects. Since most neurons and synapses in the ACC-based circuits that underlie core cognitive and affective processes are glutamatergic, these findings support the formulation that glutamate is a primary mediator of psychiatric pathology and may, potentially, serve as a common pathway for the therapeutic action of antidepressant agents.
