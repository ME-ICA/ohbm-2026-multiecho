Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder. Modelling the disease progression is pivotal for early detection, accurate prognosis, and treatment development; yet accurate modeling remains challenging due to the heterogeneity in disease trajectories among individuals. Disease Progression Modelling and Stratification (DPMoSt) is a recently developed probabilistic method to identify differential disease progression trajectories in heterogeneous (longitudinal) cohorts, based on specific biomarkers [1]. In the present study, we applied DPMoSt to longitudinal data in the Parkinson’s Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI) dataset, and validated the model on an external dataset from Stanford University. We included both imaging biomarkers (cortical thickness and subcortical volume) and clinical biomarkers (global cognition score, tremor score, and postural instability and gait disorder (PIGD) score).

Methods

Data of 146 PD patients from PPMI and of 125 PD patients from Stanford were included in the training and validation set, respectively. T1-weighted MR images of all subjects and visits were processed using FreeSurfer 7. We selected cortical thickness of 5 bilateral brain regions and subcortical volumes of 2 bilateral brain structures that were found to be affected in early stages of PD (fusiform gyrus, inferior temporal gyrus, superior and inferior parietal gyrus, precuneus, putamen, and amygdala [2]). All imaging biomarkers were harmonized using ComBat with subject ID and time since baseline scan as covariates [3]. The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) was included as a global cognition score. Tremor and PIGD scores were derived from OFF-stage Movement Disorder Society Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale part 3 (MDS-UPDRS-III) [4]. The model was applied using a monotonicity constraint on all biomarkers.

Results

Two distinct subtypes were identified in all cortical thickness biomarkers, but not in any of the subcortical volume biomarkers, except for the right putamen (Figure 1). The subtypes were also not identified in any of the clinical biomarkers. The ‘accelerated’ subtype was characterized by a stronger cortical degeneration in early disease stages, combined with a faster progression over time in most included cortical regions. The accelerated subtype also had a significantly lower MoCA score at the final visit compared to the other subtype. In the validation dataset this difference was no longer statistically significant. Higher reparameterized disease time in both subtypes combined was associated with higher Hoehn & Yahr disease stages.

Conclusion

The model discovered two subtypes primarily based on cortical thickness measures. This was validated in an independent cohort. The more severe subtype appears to predict faster cognitive decline. Future extensions of this study will include the inclusion of diffusion-based biomarkers as well as generalization and validation of findings using additional cohorts from the ENIGMA-PD consortium.
