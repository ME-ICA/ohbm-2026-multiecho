Introduction

Neurofeedback training (NFT) is an operant conditioning paradigm using feedback of particular brain activity via visual or auditory interferences. Participants learn how to tune brain activity on demand in an NFT and progressively improve their controllability of the brain activity through a repetitive NFT. Numerous studies have reported advantages of the NFT on improving several aspects of cognitive functions and ameliorating symptoms of psychiatric and neurological disorders. Specially, NFT of alpha activity (8-12 Hz) has been demonstrated in enhancement of memory in healthy and elderly people (Yeh et al., 2021). Our previous study demonstrated prominent equivalent current dipoles of NFT-induced alpha activity in the posterior cingulate cortex (PCC) (Hsueh et al., 2022). However, there is largely unknown the network modulation of the PCC for the alpha NFT using an fMRI experimental design. The present study aimed to identify the activated ROIs by block design of a 30-s alpha-neutral interleave design and to explore the changes of resting-state functional connectivity regard to the PCC for alpha NFT.

Methods

The experimental procedure was reviewed and approved by the research ethics committee of National Cheng Kung University. All participants were recruited from university students and had not participated in an NFT study in the past. The present study randomly assigned participants into the two groups. The Control group received a feedback of 4-Hz amplitude from 7-20 Hz bandwidths randomly. The Alpha group received a feedback of 8-12 Hz amplitude.

The entire experimental procedure contained three parts: memory test, NFT, and neurochemical measures for the Alpha group. The picture recognition task (PRT) was performed before and after NFT to assess memory performance. An NFT contained 12 sessions and performed trice per week. Each session started for 2 minutes baseline recording followed by 6 blocks (6 minutes for each block) which was interleaved with 1-minute pause to provide or strengthen the tips for participants and ascertain the EEG quality. Finally, Responders of the Alpha group performed the PRT, ROIs of alpha activity, and resting state analysis.

Brain activities were recorded from 3 pairs of Ag/AgCl electrodes located at 2.5 cm anterior and posterior to C3, Cz and C4, respectively. Bipolar recording was beneficial to reduce the possible artefacts of motion or eye blink. Amplitude spectra of bipolar EEGs were calculated using an FFT algorithm with a Hamming window on a second-by-second basis with no time overlapping. The feedback data contained two parts: the instantaneous information of a 1-s averaged amplitude and the cumulative waveform of all 1-s averaged amplitudes. Two parameters, i.e., the relative alpha amplitude and total duration of successful alpha epochs, were used to indicate training performance. The relative alpha amplitude was defined by the amplitude of 8-12 Hz normalized by its average amplitude of all 1-s baseline EEGs. When the relative alpha amplitude of 1-s EEG was higher than 1.5, thus the 1-s EEG segment was considered a successful event. All successful 1-s events within a session were cumulated as an index of the total alpha duration. Responder was defined as total alpha duration of the 12th session being higher than 95% confidence interval of the 1st-session total alpha duration.

After the NFT, Responders of the Alpha group were performed fMRI acquisition using 3 Tesla MR750 scanner of the GE company. Whole-brain BOLD functional images were obtained using single‐shot echo planar imaging with the following parameters: repetition time (TR)/echo time (TE)=2,000/35 ms; field of view (FOV)=240 mm×240 mm; matrix=64×64; slice thickness=4 mm; no gap; voxel size=3.75×3.75×4 mm. High-resolution anatomical T1 images were acquired using fast spoiled gradient recalled (FSPGR) sequence: TR/TE = 7.6/3.3 ms; FOV=224 mm×224 mm; matrix=224×224; slice thickness=1 mm; no gap; 166 sagittal slices. fMRI data processing and analysis of resting and activation states were performed using Data Processing & Analysis of Brain Imaging (DPABI) in MATLAB based on Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM 12). Regarding to Responders of the alpha NFT, an interleave alpha-fixation block design of 30 s each was performed to identify significant ROIs. Network analysis between the ROIs and resting state was evaluated in the Responders of alpha NFT. Functional connectivity (FC) was evaluated by seed-based correlation analysis using Pearson’s correlation coefficient. The time series of each seed was obtained from average BOLD signals within a sphere (radius=12 mm) centered to each ROIs. The correlation analysis was then performed in a voxel-wise way in the whole brain to obtain an r map. The r map within the study-specific brain mask was converted into a z map by Fisher’s r-to-z transform to improve the normality. The statistical map was corrected for multiple comparisons by the Gaussian random field (GRF) model with family-wise error (FWE) correction (voxel p<0.005, cluster p<0.05, two tailed).

Results

Baseline alpha amplitude throughout 12 sessions showed no differences in the two groups. Both the relative alpha amplitude and total alpha duration exhibited significant differences in the factors of the group (p<.01), session (p<.001), and group´session (p<.001). The Control group revealed no difference across 12 sessions in the two alpha parameters. In contrast, the Alpha group exhibited significantly higher alpha amplitude and total alpha duration for 5-12 sessions of NFT.

Accuracy of the PRT exhibited significant differences in the factors of group (p<.01), time (p<.001), and group´time (p<.01). In particular, the Alpha group showed significantly higher accuracy of the PRT than the Control group after NFT.

Significant activation regions of alpha upregulation via NFT included the right dorsal posterior cingulate cortex (rdPCC), right ventral posterior cingulate cortex (rvPCC), right visual cortex (rVC), and left visual cortex (lVC), which were considered as the ROIs here. The rdPCC and rvPCC exhibited a smaller cluster compared with those of rVC and lVC. Correlation coefficients between BOLDs of the four ROIs (rdPCC, rvPCC, rVC, and lVC) and the boxcar function were -0.08±0.04 (P=0.09), -0.08±0.04 (P=0.06), -0.32±0.06 (P<0.001), -0.34±0.06 (P<0.001), respectively. Regarding to the block design of alpha activation, time lag of the EEG alpha response was significantly shorter than those of the 4 ROIs’ BOLD response. BOLD activity of the rVC and lVC looked slightly faster than those of the rdPCC and rvPCC, but there was no significant difference among time lags.

Regarding to the rdPCC, the F-map shows the interaction effect (Group´Time) in the right middle temporal cortex (rMTC) (Figure). The post hoc analysis for the interaction cluster showed that resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC) values of the Ctrl group after NFT were significantly lower than those before NFT. By contrast, RSFC values of the Alpha group after NFT were significantly higher than those before NFT and those of the Ctrl group after NFT. Regarding to the rvPCC, there was a time effect that exhibited positive change in RSFC at the bilateral insula and rACC and negative change in RSFC at the left hippocampus. There was no significant effect regarding the rVC. Considered the lVC, the interaction effect appeared in the frontal eye fields (FEF). Regarding to the lVC, RSFC values of the right thalamus, right middle frontal gyrus (rMFG), and rACC in the Alpha group were significantly higher than those in the Ctrl group.

Conclusion

Our results demonstrated successful NFT of alpha activity in terms of relative alpha amplitude and total alpha duration. In addition, this study showed significant enhancement of the PRT’s accuracy. Moreover, this study 4 significant ROIs according to block design of alpha-fixation paradigms. Within these 4 ROIs, this study showed significant alteration of RSFC of the rTMC regarding to the rdPCC exclusively with significant interaction effect. This study extends our understanding of an alpha NFT in memory enhancement regarding with network modulation of the PCC.
