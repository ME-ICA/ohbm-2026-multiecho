Introduction

Transcranial light stimulation (tLS) is painless and non-invasive, and has been shown to enhance working memory capacity and modulate brain electrical activity1. However, its long-term effects on large-scale brain dynamics remain poorly understood. Here, we applied an 11-day right prefrontal tLS protocol to test whether repeated stimulation can reshape the aperiodic 1/f component of neural activity and improve visual working memory precision.

Methods

Twenty healthy adults (27.17 ± 3.63 years; 12F) received active transcranial light stimulation (tLS) for 11 consecutive days. Stimulation was delivered to the right prefrontal cortex (FP2, 10–20 system; 250 mW/cm²; 12 min/day). Resting-state EEG was recorded in the active tLS group every other day using a 64-channel Neuroscan Curry 9 system (extended 10–20 montage; 1000 Hz), with three 12-min eyes-closed runs per session (pre/during/post). A visual working-memory precision (WMP) task2 was administered before stimulation on day 1 and after stimulation on day 11.

EEG was preprocessed by using Brainstorm toolbox and standard procedures in MATLAB 2024a (down-sampling to 256 Hz; 1–50 Hz band-pass; 50 Hz notch; ICA artifact removal; bad-channel interpolation) and segmented into Pre, During, and Post epoches. Power spectra (1–40 Hz; Welch) were parameterized with Fitting Oscillations and One-Over F (FOOOF) approach to extract the aperiodic exponent3. For participants with T1-weighted MRI, we performed cortically constrained weighted minimum norm estimate (wMNE) source reconstruction and summarized source signals within Schaefer-100 parcels4 and Yeo-7 networks5 to obtain network-level aperiodic exponents (Fig. 1). WMP performance was quantified as mean absolute error (MAE) in a retro-cued orientation report task. Changes in the aperiodic exponent were analyzed with linear mixed-effects models, and MAE was compared pre vs. post within participants.

Results

Repeated right prefrontal tLS selectively modulated the source-level aperiodic exponent within the dorsal attention network (DAN). Across stimulation days, the DAN exponent showed a robust immediate decrease after each session (Post(i) < Pre(i)), a short-term rebound between sessions (Pre(i+1) > Post(i)), and a modest but significant long-term decline over the 11-day intervention, together indicating cumulative reconfiguration of aperiodic activity in this network (Fig. 2A). Other networks exhibited non-significant changes, supporting a DAN-selective effect of right prefrontal tLS. Behaviorally, participants exhibited a significant reduction (p = 0.002) in MAE in the retro-cued orientation-report task from Pre to Post (Fig. 2B), indicating that repeated tLS enhanced the precision of visual working-memory representations.

Conclusion

Our findings show that 11 days of right prefrontal tLS systematically reconfigured the aperiodic 1/f component of EEG within the dorsal attention network and improved visual working-memory precision. These results highlight the aperiodic exponent as a sensitive marker of multi-day neuromodulation and support tLS as a promising tool for non-invasive cognitive enhancement.
