Introduction

Adolescence is a sensitive period for brain development, as brain structure and function are significantly altered from pre- to post-adolescence. The trajectory of adolescent brain development has far-reaching implications on final adult brain topology and behaviour. A particularly salient influence on adolescent brain development appears to be that of puberty, during which there is a reorganisation of brain structure and function. Furthermore, sex differences in neurodevelopmental trajectories emerge during puberty and persist into adulthood. 

To capture the complex effects of puberty and sex on brain development, a longitudinal model is essential. Therefore, the current study employs a longitudinal rat model. This affords a variety of methodological advantages. Rodent development occurs over experimentally tractable timescales - rodents reach adulthood in less than three months, allowing for quicker, easier, and low cost collection of longitudinal data points. Furthermore, rodent models allow for carefully controlled conditions, thus enabling causal inferences. Finally, as functional brain networks and their features are largely conserved cross-species, there is a strong analogy between the networks identified using resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI; the methodology used in this dataset) approaches in humans and in rats. 

The current project presents an openly available sex-balanced dataset of 36 Wistar rats scanned at five timepoints spanning from juvenile to early adulthood, as well as accompanying seed-based analyses for connectivity validation. In sharing these data, we provide a dataset which can be used by future researchers for the charting of the development of functional brain networks, using a longitudinal rat model and rs-fMRI. 

Methods

Eighteen male and 18 female Wistar Han rats were weaned on postnatal day 21. Animals were housed in pairs and handled daily. Each cage had nesting material, a red polycarbonate tunnel and a red tall rat loft. Animals were kept in climate‐controlled rooms set to 22°C (±2°C) with relative humidity levels of 50%. Experimenters (two males) handled and carried out all procedures on the rats, while animal care staff were both male and female. 

Animals were handled daily, with puberty onset tracked, and the oestrous cycle tracked in females once puberty was reached via daily vaginal swab. Puberty onset was recorded as the day of vaginal opening in females and balanopreputial separation in males. Oestrous smears were stained using Wrights-modified stain and imaged at 10x and 20x using a light microscope.

A Bruker 7T BioSpec 70/30 scanner equipped with a 1H receive-only 8 x 1 rat surface array coil, 72mm transmit coil, BGA9S gradients (675mT/m) and Paravision 7 software was used for all imaging sessions. Animals underwent 5 MRI scans at approximately postnatal day 28, 35, 49, 70 and 91. Scans were acquired over 4 waves of longitudinal data collection, with 4 to 12 animals per wave. 

The StandardRat protocol^1 was followed for both anaesthesia/sedation and MRI scanning parameters (see Figure 1). Anaesthesia induction was carried out using 4% isoflurane in 1000ml/min oxygen and 400ml/min nitrogen. This was followed by a bolus of medetomidine (0.05mg/kg, s.c.). Animals were maintained at 0.4% isoflurane in 200ml/min oxygen and 400ml/min nitrogen, with a continuous infusion of medetomidine (0.1 mg/kg/h, s.c.) starting 15 minutes after the bolus. The EPI scan began 40 minutes after the bolus of medetomidine had been delivered. Isoflurane concentration was set reduced to 0.4% at least 10 minutes before starting the EPI sequence. Once scanning was complete, the continuous infusion was stopped and atipamezole (0.5mg/kg, s.c.) was delivered to reverse the effects of medetomidine. Respiration and temperature were monitored throughout the scan using a respiratory pillow and rectal probe, with body temperature maintained at 37°C using a water heating system connected to the MRI cradle. For the anatomical scan, a T2w turboRARE sequence was used with repetition time (TR) = 2500ms, echo time (TE) = 30ms, RARE factor = 8, 18 interleaved axial slices of 1 mm with 0.1mm gap, FOV = 25.6mm x 25.6mm, and matrix size of 128 x 128. For the functional scan, a gradient echo-echo planar sequence was used with the following parameters: TR = 1000ms, TE = 17ms, 18 interleaved axial slices of 1 mm with 0.1mm gap, FOV = 25.6mm x 25.6mm, and matrix size of 64 x 64.

Preprocessing was carried out using the open-source RABIES software^2. EPI scans were corrected for inhomogeneities then registered to the population average template using a non-linear registration. Confound correction was then carried out on the native space EPI timeseries and included voxelwise detrending, bandpass filtering (0.01-0.1Hz) and removal of 30 seconds from either end of the timeseries. Next, 6 rigid motion parameters and the global signal were regressed from the data. Finally, a spatial Gaussian smoothing filter was applied at 0.5mm full-width at half maximum. The output from this was then registered to the common space population average.

To determine functional connectivity, seed based analysis was carried out using 0.9mm spherical seeds located in the S1 barrel fields and anterior cingulate cortex following the approach outlined in^1. Functional connectivity was calculated as the Pearson’s correlation coefficient between regional time series. For each animal, connectivity was divided into four categories: specific (r S1bf left to right > 0.1 AND r S1bf left to ACA < 0.1); non-specific (r S1bf left to right > 0.1 AND r S1bf left to ACA > 0.1); no (r S1bf left to right [−0.1, 0.1] AND r S1bf left to ACA [−0.1, 0.1]); and spurious connectivity (remaining cases)^1

Results

A total of 179 functional scans were collected: 1 was excluded due to excess motion, 7 were excluded due to data loss/preprocessing issues. 

Across all sessions the mean respiration was 79.9 breaths per minute. The mean respiration was highest in the first session (postnatal day 28) at 86.2 breaths per minute and lowest in session 3 (postnatal day 49) with a mean respiratory rate of 74. 

Mean framewise displacement averaged across all sessions was 0.013mm, with a max framewise displacement of 0.0432mm across all sessions. Session 1 (postnatal day 28) recorded the greatest mean and max framewise displacement with 0.025mm and 0.062mm respectively, followed by session 2 (postnatal day 35) with 0.018mm and 0.052mm respectively. The mean temporal signal to noise ratio averaged across sessions in the cortex (barrel fields) was 50.6, with subcortical (striatum) temporal signal to noise measuring at 49.2. 

Across all included scans (n=171), 64.9% scans displayed specific connectivity, 20.5% displayed no connectivity, 12.9%  displayed spurious connectivity, while 1.7% displayed non-specific connectivity. Session 3 (postnatal day 49) displayed the greatest specific connectivity with 83.3% of scans at this timepoint meeting the threshold. Conversely, session 1 (postnatal day 28) had the lowest specific connectivity with 40% of scans reaching the threshold. Conclusion

This project describes a sex-balanced developmental dataset of 36 Wistar rats scanned at 5 timepoints spanning from juvenile to early adulthood. The longitudinal rs-fMRI data is supplemented with daily oestrous tracking in females and day of puberty onset for both sexes, making the dataset the first of its kind. Compared to existing publicly available rs-fMRI data in adult rats, the data present here is of similar or higher quality^1.
