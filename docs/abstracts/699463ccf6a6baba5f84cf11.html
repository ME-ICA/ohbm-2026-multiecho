Introduction

       In neuroimaging research, standardized anatomical brain templates serve as a cornerstone for achieving consistent spatial normalization. Such templates enable detailed cross-population comparisons—for example, between Western and Eastern populations—and enhance the precision of structural brain atlas analyses for precision medicine applications[1]. However, current brain templates often lack population specificity, resulting in limited ability to capture fine neuroanatomical details, particularly in sub-millimeter resolution morphometric analyses. Moreover, during the acquisition of raw MRI data, high-frequency Rician noise often degrades image quality, further affecting subsequent image processing steps such as segmentation and surface reconstruction. The Adaptive Optimized Non-Local Means (AONLM) denoising approach effectively suppresses such noise while maintaining the integrity of anatomical structures in the image[2]. This study presents a validated pipeline wherein T1-weighted images from 105 healthy subjects are first fused via nonlinear registration to construct a population-specific brain template, followed by AONLM denoising. Subsequent post-processing yields volumetric and surface-based measures used to assess the anatomical fidelity of the constructed brain atlas.

Methods

    Submillimeter-resolution T1-weighted images were acquired on a Siemens 3T wideband MRI scanner using an MPRAGE sequence (TE/TR/TI = 2.34/2800/1500 ms; acquisition time = 12:50 min), at 0.5 mm isotropic resolution. Processing steps included skull stripping, bias field correction, AONLM denoising, and tissue segmentation[2]. At the individual level, axial slices were visually inspected to evaluate gray-white matter contrast. For population-level analysis, T1-weighted images from 105 participants were fused through nonlinear registration and intensity averaging to generate a sub-millimeter resolution brain template. To further enhance anatomical contrast and suppress Rician noise while preserving fine structural details, Adaptive Optimized Non-Local Means (AONLM) denoising was applied to the fused population template[2]. Subsequent post-processing and structural optimization were performed using the SPM/CAT12 toolbox[3]. Comprehensive morphometric analyses were conducted, including measurements of total intracranial volume (TIV), gray matter (GM), white matter (WM), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) volumes, and intensity inhomogeneity. In addition, cortical surface reconstructions were used to extract key surface-based metrics such as cortical thickness, Euler number, and surface defect area. To ensure robustness and reliability of the template for downstream analyses, multiple quality control metrics were systematically evaluated, including surface topology consistency, bias field correction accuracy, defect rate, and denoising effectiveness.

Results

    Figure 1a presents an axial T1-weighted MRI image from a healthy participant, showing a comparison between the raw image prior to denoising and the corresponding image after applying Adaptive Optimized Non-Local Means (AONLM) denoising. [2]The left panel displays the original axial T1-weighted image from a neurologically healthy subject. Upon visual inspection, the image exhibits prominent Rician noise, which compromises anatomical clarity and results in blurred cortical boundaries. The right panel shows the same slice after AONLM denoising. The processed image demonstrates enhanced visibility of subcortical structures such as the lateral ventricles and putamen, as well as improved delineation of gray-white matter boundaries. Importantly, the denoising process effectively suppresses high-frequency noise without loss of anatomical detail. This visual comparison clearly illustrates the capability of AONLM to preserve fine-scale neuroanatomical features while reducing noise, supporting its applicability in submillimeter-resolution structural neuroimaging pipelines, such as population-based brain atlas construction.

          Figure 1b presents axial slices from a submillimeter-resolution anatomical brain atlas, constructed by nonlinearly registering and integrating T1-weighted MRI scans from 105 healthy individuals.[1] The population-averaged template was generated through groupwise nonlinear registration and fusion of individual images, followed by denoising using the Adaptive Optimized Non-Local Means (AONLM) algorithm. The resulting brain template demonstrates high anatomical symmetry and fine preservation of neuroanatomical details, with clearly delineated subcortical nuclei, major white matter tracts, and cortical folding patterns. This preprocessing pipeline significantly enhances the structural clarity of tissue boundaries, including the visualization of white matter fibers, subcortical structures, and cortical folding morphology. The population-specific atlas exhibits high-fidelity structural features and is well-suited for applications in morphometric analysis, brain parcellation, and connectivity studies. As an East Asian-specific brain template, it ensures both anatomical accuracy and computational reproducibility, making it a valuable standard reference for population-level neuroimaging pipelines. 

     Figure 1c illustrates a brain template generated by nonlinearly averaging and fusing magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) data from 105 participants, followed by denoising using Adaptive Optimized Non-Local Means (AONLM). Subsequent image quality evaluation using CAT12[3] revealed excellent denoising performance, with a noise reduction rate of 96.25% and intensity uniformity of 91.33%. Local variations in cortical thickness were captured through 3D surface reconstructions, providing quantitative validation of the template’s post-denoising quality. Volumetric measurements showed a total intracranial volume (TIV) of 1787 cm³, gray matter (GM) volume of 715 cm³(relative volume=40%), white matter (WM) volume of 736 cm³(relative volume=41.2%), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) volume of 336 cm³(relative volume=18.8%). The mean cortical thickness was measured at 2.41 mm. The structural robustness of the template was further confirmed through surface-based topological analysis, yielding an Euler number of 38 and a surface defect area of only 2.23%. This figure presents a comprehensive surface-based morphometric quality assessment of a T1-weighted brain template constructed from 105 healthy individuals. The template demonstrated good topological integrity for submillimeter-resolution morphometric analysis, with precise alignment of anatomical boundaries shown in the segmentation overlays. These multi-metric evaluations confirm the anatomical validity and applicability of the template for population-level studies involving cortical thickness modeling and cross-subject registration in structural neuroimaging.

Conclusion

This study establishes a validated framework for constructing a population-specific T1-weighted brain template at submillimeter resolution for healthy East Asian individuals. Given that most conventional brain templates are derived from Western populations, their limitations in anatomical resolution and population representativeness must be addressed. In this work, we integrated isotropic 0.5 mm T1-weighted MRI scans from 105 healthy Taiwanese participants. The images were first processed through groupwise nonlinear registration and fused into a unified space, followed by Adaptive Optimized Non-Local Means (AONLM) denoising. This pipeline ensures that the resulting brain template achieves consistent alignment between signal homogeneity and anatomical accuracy, thereby providing a reliable standard for morphometric analyses in East Asian cohorts.

At the individual level (Figure 1a), AONLM denoising effectively suppressed Rician noise while preserving fine neuroanatomical details. The resulting images showed improved gray-white matter contrast and sharper delineation of cortical and subcortical structures, laying a solid foundation for downstream analyses such as morphometry, segmentation, and surface reconstruction. At the population level (Figure 1b), T1-weighted images from 105 healthy Taiwanese participants were aligned through groupwise nonlinear registration and fused into a unified template. This process yielded anatomically coherent results with high spatial fidelity. AONLM further enhanced tissue boundary clarity by reducing residual noise and signal inhomogeneity. The final brain atlas clearly revealed subcortical nuclei, cortical folding, and major white matter tracts, supporting applications in brain parcellation, cortical thickness estimation, and cross-subject registration. In Figure 1c, the atlas was further evaluated using the CAT12 toolbox for surface-based morphometric quality assessment. These results confirmed the structural and topological integrity of the reconstructed surfaces, reinforcing the biological plausibility and anatomical consistency of the final template.

In summary, this study confirms the biological plausibility and structural stability of the constructed brain atlas. As outlined in the Introduction, the reliance on generalized anatomical models predominantly based on Western populations presents inherent limitations. Our findings directly address this gap by advancing a population-specific brain template tailored for East Asian individuals, thereby promoting anatomically and culturally relevant morphometric neuroimaging. The proposed atlas not only serves as a structurally faithful reference for East Asian cohorts but also establishes a scalable and reproducible framework applicable to other populations, clinical cohorts, and varied scanner protocols. Notably, this study employed submillimeter-resolution isotropic 0.5 mm imaging—a submillimeter scale rarely achieved in population-level templates—combined with the rigorously validated AONLM denoising technique to ensure robust image quality. The validation results further demonstrate its potential for precision imaging quality assurance and its utility in structural MRI applications, including precision medicine, population neuroscience, and cross-subject anatomical modeling. This study presents a high-fidelity, anatomically precise brain template for the East Asian population, specifically derived from Taiwanese individuals, acquired at an isotropic submillimeter resolution of 0.5 mm. It establishes a robust foundation for standardized structural analyses in cross-population research. Moreover, this atlas offers a valuable resource for the next generation of submillimeter-resolution morphometric neuroimaging studies and marks a significant step forward in advancing population-diverse human brain mapping and precision medical imaging.
