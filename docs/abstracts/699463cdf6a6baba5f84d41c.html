Introduction

Resting-state fMRI time series exhibit temporal autocorrelation that often extends across long ranges of time. These long-memory (LM) properties are not well characterized by the short-memory noise models commonly used in fMRI analysis pipelines.1  Instead of treating temporal persistence only as a nuisance, LM modeling leverages the scaling behavior of BOLD fluctuations through the spectral decay parameter α, where larger α reflects stronger long-range temporal dependence.2 Although LM characteristics have been examined at the region level or in reduced spatial settings,1 their full-brain distribution and relationships with individual-level covariates remain largely unknown.

We introduce a voxelwise wavelet-Bayesian pipeline that enables spatial mapping of α across the brain. At the subject level, we use a wavelet-domain representation to estimate α efficiently from the temporal covariance structure.1,2 At the group level, we relate these α maps to subject-level covariates using a composite-basis dimension-reduction strategy that enables scalable Bayesian spatial modeling and implicitly accounts for spatial dependence across voxels. LM modeling is rarely incorporated into standard fMRI analysis workflows, and this pipeline provides information that is complementary to functional connectivity: connectivity captures spatial coupling, whereas LM modeling characterizes intrinsic temporal organization within each location.

We apply this framework to the ADHD-200 dataset3 to evaluate whether spatial variation in LM dynamics relates to age, ADHD symptom severity, and medication status in children and adolescents.

Methods

We analyzed N = 355 participants (ages 7–18) from the ADHD-200 preprocessed Athena repository.³ Preprocessing included motion correction, slice-timing correction, nonlinear normalization to MNI space, and spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM), following the published pipeline.³ Resting-state BOLD time series were time-centered prior to modeling. To characterize long-range temporal dependence, we applied the discrete wavelet transform (DWT) to each voxel’s time series, obtaining a multiscale representation of BOLD fluctuations.² Under a long-memory process, the variance of wavelet coefficients decays approximately linearly on the log scale across scales, yielding an approximately diagonal covariance structure in the wavelet domain that enables fast computational estimation of covariance parameters.¹ An overview of the three-stage analysis pipeline is shown in Figure 1.

We estimated voxelwise long-memory parameters (α) using Bayesian inference implemented in R. For each time series, we specified Beta priors on α and constructed the likelihood from scale-specific wavelet-domain variances.¹ Posterior inference was performed using MCMC, yielding subject-specific α maps that provide voxelwise estimates of temporal persistence across the whole brain.

To relate the subject-specific α maps to population-level covariates while accounting for spatial structure, we used a composite-basis dimensionality-reduction strategy.4 Principal components were first extracted within anatomical ROIs to capture localized spatial variation in the α maps, and these ROI-level components were combined through a second global PCA to construct 411 spatial basis functions representing major whole-brain patterns of α variability. For each subject, α maps were projected onto this basis to obtain reduced-dimension coefficients. We then fit a Bayesian regression model relating α to age, ADHD severity, medication status, and their interaction, generating posterior distributions for regression coefficients associated with each basis function.

To obtain voxelwise effect estimates, we back-projected the full MCMC samples from basis space to the full brain. Statistical significance was evaluated using joint posterior credible bands combined with a spatial cluster-extent threshold (larger or equal than 50 voxels) to control for false positives. For comparison, we performed frequentist voxelwise regression using identical design matrices and cluster criteria, providing a check on the robustness of the Bayesian findings through comparison with a frequentist voxelwise model.

Results

Voxelwise long-memory parameter (α) maps exhibited clear spatial variability across subjects. At the group level, Bayesian regression identified significant associations between α and age after adjustment for ADHD symptom severity, medication status, and their interaction. Significant age-related effects were identified in medial temporal lobe regions, including bilateral hippocampus (Figure 2A). These effects were observed as spatially contiguous voxel clusters surviving joint posterior credible band thresholding and a minimum cluster size of 50 voxels.

Voxelwise inference based on the frequentist regression model revealed age-related associations with a similar anatomical distribution (Figure 2B). Although the frequentist analysis yielded fewer voxels exceeding the significance threshold overall, the largest significant clusters were spatially consistent with those identified under the Bayesian framework. Overlap maps highlighting voxels deemed significant by both approaches showed concordant clusters within medial temporal lobe regions, including bilateral hippocampus (Figure 2C), indicating agreement between the two inferential strategies despite differences in modeling assumptions and uncertainty quantification.

No widespread voxelwise associations were detected between α and ADHD symptom severity or medication status after correction for multiple comparisons. Any effects related to these covariates were limited in spatial extent and did not survive joint posterior credible band thresholding or frequentist cluster-level correction under the specified criteria.

Back-projection of the full MCMC samples from the reduced-dimensional basis to voxel space yielded voxelwise posterior effect estimates. Inference was conducted using joint posterior credible bands and a minimum cluster size of 50 voxels. The agreement between Bayesian and frequentist results provides evidence for the robustness of the identified age-associated spatial patterns.

Conclusion

This study presents a scalable Bayesian framework for voxelwise characterization of long-memory dynamics in resting-state fMRI and their association with subject-level covariates. By combining wavelet-domain estimation of long-memory parameters with composite-basis spatial modeling, the proposed pipeline enables whole-brain inference that would be computationally prohibitive using fully voxelwise approaches.

Applied to the ADHD-200 dataset, the framework identified statistically significant age-related associations in medial temporal lobe regions, including bilateral hippocampus, with consistent spatial patterns observed across Bayesian and frequentist analyses. These results demonstrate that long-memory properties of resting-state fMRI signals exhibit meaningful spatial variation that can be related to individual differences using a statistically grounded modeling framework.

Long-memory analysis provides information that is complementary to standard functional connectivity approaches, which focus on spatial coupling between regions. The proposed framework offers a flexible and general approach for studying intrinsic temporal organization in large neuroimaging datasets and can be extended to other populations, covariates, and imaging modalities.
