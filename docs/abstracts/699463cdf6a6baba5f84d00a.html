Introduction

Infant primates, while immature, already exhibit the ability to identify visual information, including sensitivity to contrast, global motion, and form.^1 Understanding how much of the visual system’s architecture is in place before birth is key to determining what neural infrastructure supports postnatal visual function and its development. The vertical occipital fasciculus (VOF) is a major white matter tract that connects the dorsal and ventral visual streams and is commonly observed across primate species.^2–5 Despite its significance in integrating spatial and categorical information, its development remains largely unexplored. We used diffusion MRI (dMRI) on ex vivo macaque fetal brains to test whether the spatial organization of occipital white matter tracts, including VOF, is already established before birth.

Methods

Ex vivo dMRI data were acquired from four fetal macaque brains at gestational days (GD) 100, 111, 121, and 160, and from four postnatal brains at postnatal days (PD) 185, 200, 235, and 246. All scans were carried out on a 9.4 Tesla Bruker AV3HD scanner running ParaVision 6.0.1, equipped with a BGA12S-HD gradient set with a 660 mT/m maximum gradient strength and a 72 mm quadrature bird cage coil. Brains were soaked in 0.02% Gadavist in phosphate-buffered saline for two weeks prior to imaging to reduce the tissue T1 and facilitate accelerated imaging. Brains were then placed in a sealed bag with a perfluorocarbon oil to match magnetic susceptibility and eliminate background signal. Diffusion images were acquired using a 3D segmented spin-echo EPI sequence (TR/TE  220/40 ms) with Stejskal-Tanner diffusion gradients (δ/Δ = 6.5/14 ms). 8 b=0 s/mm2 images and 150 total diffusion directions over multiple shells (b=1000, 2000, 4000, 6000 s/mm2) were collected at 156 μm isotropic voxel size. After correction for eddy-current distortion and motion using FSL, we fit a diffusion tensor model to dMRI data in each voxel to generate diffusion-encoded color (DEC) maps using MRtrix3. We visually examined occipital white matter tracts, including the optic radiation, forceps major, and VOF, using the DEC map in each dataset to assess whether each tract was identifiable. 

Results

At GD160, corresponding to the late third trimester, DEC maps revealed a prominent vertical white matter tract lateral to the optic radiation with a superior-inferior diffusion axis (Figure 1), consistent with the adult VOF shown previously.^2 The optic radiation and forceps major were also evident at this prenatal age. When comparing DEC maps across gestational and postnatal days, the VOF was not clearly visible at or before GD121, whereas the optic radiation and forceps major were identifiable even at GD100 (Figure 2). All occipital tracts studied were identifiable in every postnatal brain. 

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that the VOF emerges prenatally, suggesting that anatomical wiring supporting the dorsal and ventral visual streams is established before birth. In addition, these results indicate a distinct developmental timeline for occipital white matter tracts, with the VOF emerging later than the optic radiation and forceps major.
