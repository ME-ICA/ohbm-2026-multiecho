Introduction

Neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE) remains a leading cause of morbidity and long-term neurodevelopmental disability, affecting ~2.4 per 1,000 live births in Canada1. Therapeutic hypothermia (TH) improves survival and reduces the severity of acute brain injury; however, many treated neonates still present with brain injury. Disruption of early microstructural maturation within limbic regions may contribute to later impairments in cognitive, memory, and behavior. 

Advanced MRI techniques provide sensitive tools to characterize early brain development following injury. Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) metrics, including fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD), reflect microstructural organization and maturational processes, while T2* mapping offers complementary information related to tissue composition, iron deposition, and inflammatory processes. Together, these modalities enable longitudinal assessment of subtle injury effects and recovery trajectories during the critical early postnatal period. 

Sildenafil, a phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor, has demonstrated neuroprotective and neurorestorative effects in preclinical models, including functional recovery and hippocampal neurogenesis following hypoxia-ischemia2. However, its impact on early brain microstructural development in human neonates remains unknown. In this study, we assessed DTI and T2* changes in the hippocampus and amygdala during the first postnatal month in HIE neonates treated with TH, with or without adjunctive sildenafil, compared to uninjured controls. We hypothesized that HIE would disrupt limbic microstructural maturation, and that sildenafil treatment would be associated with partial normalization of these developmental trajectories, particularly within the hippocampus and amygdala. 

Methods

We conducted a prospective neuroimaging study of healthy neonates and neonates with HIE treated with TH. Eligible patients received whole-body cooling to 33.5°C within 6 hours of birth for 72 hours, followed by gradual rewarming. A subset of HIE neonates with persistent brain injury received a 7-day enteral sildenafil course (every 12 hours from day 2 to day 9). Neonates were assigned to three groups: no brain injury (NoBI), HIE with TH alone (BI+TH), and HIE with TH plus sildenafil (BI+TH/SI). 

MRI scans were acquired at Days 2, 10, and 30 on a 3T scanner. DTI used a single shot echo planar imaging sequence (TR/TE 5938/69 ms; 32 directions; b=0/750 s/mm²; 46 axial slices, 1.8 mm thickness). Preprocessing included motion and eddy-current correction. FA and MD were calculated using generalized q-sampling imaging 3. T2* mapping used a 3D multi-echo gradient-echo sequence (TR=58 ms;  6 echoes with TE=5–55 ms; echo spacing=10 ms; flip angle 15°; 2 mm slices). Voxelwise T2* maps were computed in MATLAB using mono-exponential decay and maximum likelihood estimation. 

Hippocampal and amygdala regions of interest were extracted using the Automated Anatomical Labeling (AAL) atlas and mapped to each participant’s native diffusion space via template-based normalization to the developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP) template, followed by manual quality control and correction. Mean FA, MD, and T2* values were extracted for each region. Values were analyzed using repeated-measures generalized linear mixed models with unstructured covariance, adjusting for gestational age at birth, scan timing, and baseline injury severity. Sidak-adjusted post-hoc comparisons were applied for significant effects. This modeling framework accommodated incomplete longitudinal data while appropriately accounting for within-subject correlations. 

Results

We analyzed 242 MRI scans from 127 neonates: 65 NoBI, 42 BI+TH, and 20 BI+TH/SI. Neonates in the BI+TH/SI group had more severe baseline injury. T2* mapping was available only in a subset of neonates (31 NoBI, 24 BI+TH, 20 BI+TH/SI).  

In the hippocampus, FA did not differ between groups on Day 2. By Day 10, both injured groups showed lower FA than NoBI (NoBI: 0.17 ± 0.01 vs. BI+TH: 0.16 ± 0.01, p=0.001; BI+TH/SI: 0.15 ± 0.02, p=0.001). By Day 30, FA in BI+TH/SI increased (0.17 ± 0.01) compared to BI+TH (0.15 ± 0.01, p=0.001), approaching NoBI (0.18 ± 0.01), consistent with a sildenafil associated effect. On Day 2, MD was lower in BI+TH/SI than NoBI (1.20 ± 0.12 vs. 1.34 ± 0.11, p=0.01). MD remained elevated in BI+TH on Days 10 and 30, whereas BI+TH/SI nearly normalized by Day 30.

In the amygdala, FA did not differ between groups on Day 2 and Day 10. By Day 30, FA was significantly reduced in BI+TH compared to NoBI (0.14 ± 0.01 vs. 0.17 ± 0.02, p<0.001), while BI+TH/SI showed intermediate values (0.16 ± 0.02). MD on Day 2 was lower in BI+TH/SI relative to NoBI (1.07 ± 0.10 vs. 1.17 ± 0.06, p=0.04). 

T2* declined over time in both hippocampus and amygdala across all groups, consistent with expected maturational changes; however, no significant between-group differences were detected.

Conclusion

Neonatal hypoxic-ischemic brain injury disrupted early microstructural maturation of the hippocampus and amygdala, as reflected by alterations in DTI metrics. Compared with uninjured neonates, neonates with HIE and brain injury demonstrated altered FA and MD values in both limbic regions 4. Adjunctive sildenafil treatment was associated with partial normalization of these microstructural trajectories, most notably in the hippocampus5. By sensitively capturing early injury-related alterations and subsequent microstructural evolution, DTI metrics provide complementary insight into limbic system development during a critical postnatal window. These findings offer the first human evidence suggesting a potential neurorestorative effect of sildenafil on hippocampal microstructural maturation following neonatal hypoxic-ischemic injury and support further investigation of early targeted interventions to preserve limbic development in neonates with HIE.
