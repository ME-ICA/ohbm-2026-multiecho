Introduction

Human individuals go through evident psychological changes throughout their lifetimes, starting from infancy, through extensive learning, peak of cognitive capabilities and their decline in old age. Developmental studies of the human brain quantify the concepts of maturation and aging into concrete measures such as grey matter volume and thickness or white matter myelination and connectivity1. They allow to associate these anatomical measures with phenomenological observations and to study the biological backbone of psychology. As the result, developmental ages of particular brain systems were found, indicating, for example, a posterior-to-anterior gradient, present not only in grey matter development2, but showing also as earlier maturation of sensorimotor pathways than frontal association fibers3. Similarly, determination of normative anatomical values allow to distinguish healthy aging from premature neurodegeneration. These anatomical studies are complemented by functional ones, based mainly on functional MRI and M/EEG, the former with high spatial precision and the latter high temporal one. None of them measures effective connectivity, i.e. quantifies causality directly. Similarly, neither can provide directionality of connections or signal propagation latency. Here, we address these gaps and report lifespan age-driven evolution of the whole-brain effective connectivity measured with intracerebral stereoelectroencephalography (sEEG) electrodes that combine spatial resolution of MRI with temporal one of M/EEG.

Methods

In order to assess the age-dependence of the human brain effective connectivity, we analysed the
F-TRACT4 (https://f-tract.eu) database that gathers intracerebral recordings of ~1200 patients with drug-resistant epilepsies, who in the course of preparation to brain resection surgery underwent sEEG implantation and stimulation in the awake state. Appearance of a significantly large evoked potential in a response to stimulation reveals effective connectivity between the stimulated and the recorded sites that can be far apart. Electrodes are implanted differently in different subjects, according to their individual medical condition. Although connectivity probing in an individual is sparse, integration of recordings from a large number of patients allows for measurement of connectivity for the whole-brain. The nature of this connectivity is probabilistic; for each connection we found a ratio between the number of responses with significant evoked potentials over the number of all responses (stimulations). The brain was divided according to Lausanne parcellation into 33 cortical and limbic parcels, and a connection for each parcel pair was found. Furthermore, since sEEG is characterized with a combination of excellent spatial (~1 mm) and temporal (~ 1 ms) resolutions, we were able to measure signal propagation latencies between stimulated and recorded sites. Importantly, thereby derived connectivity measures can be asymmetric, meaning that they capture differences between connections from some site A to B, and from B to A.

The F-TRACT cohort was divided into twenty overlapping groups spanning ages from 19 months to 65 years old (see Figure 1). Connectivity characteristics were computed for each group separately along with 95% confidence intervals. In this manner, we obtained time evolution for each connection. The age differentiation entailed sample size reduction: in order to mitigate it, we took the two following steps. First, we considered hemispheres together, dividing connections to only intra- and inter-hemispheric. Second, we integrated all connections per parcel, separately the afferent and the efferent ones. In this way we arrived to four (afferent, efferent and inter-, intra-hemispheric) age-dependent averaged probabilistic connectivity values (connection strengths) per parcel, additionally supplemented with corresponding signal propagation latencies. Finally, per each connection per parcel we report age at which it reaches maximum. Our study is completed with graph analysis of the whole brain effective connectivity network. We study age-dependence of local and global efficacy, average path length, clustering coefficient and small-worldness.

Results

As indicated in Figure 2A, the obtained intra-hemispheric connection strengths increase in childhood and adolescence, and decrease of in the later stage of life. The maxima form a coarse posterior-to-anterior gradients, indicating that the maturation occurs first in the occipital lobe and last in the frontal lobe (Figure 1B, left column). This finding is in accordance with earlier results2,3. We, nevertheless, additionally report asymmetry, i.e. separately afferent and efferent averaged connectivity strengths. Interestingly, we do not observe a consistent increase of inter-hemispheric connectivity with age. Similarly, no significant changes to network measures were observed. All these results are reported per each parcel / lobe and will be available online along with full connectivity matrices per each age group.

Conclusion

We present the first human brain atlas of age-related evolution of effective connectivity derived from electrophysiology, i.e. from intracerebral stimulation. Our results are in concordance with earlier studies and they uncover unknown so far charactericstics, such as directionality of connections and signal propagation latency. They can be interpreted in the psychological and anatomical context and they will be shared online.
