Introduction

Very premature birth occurs during a critical period of brain development, when white matter connections are still growing and starting intense maturation. This makes infants' brains particularly vulnerable to white matter injury. Aside from major lesions such as intraventricular haemorrhage or cystic periventricular leukomalacia, there is growing evidence that more subtle and widespread disturbances in WM microstructure substantially contribute to subsequent cognitive and motor impairments1. The sensorimotor network is one of the first systems to develop and is particularly vulnerable to perinatal complications. While diffusion MRI (dMRI) provides quantitative biomarkers of white matter integrity2, reliably measuring specific tracts in preterm infants is technically challenging due to tissue immaturity, small brain size and rapid developmental changes. This is further complicated by the various brain abnormalities frequently observed in preterm infants. The aim of this study was twofold: (i) to implement an automated, lesion-robust tractography pipeline applicable across the full range of preterm brain anatomies, even in the presence of macroscopically heterogeneous lesion patterns; and (ii) to characterise the influence of major perinatal risk factors on global and tract-specific WM microstructure at term-equivalent age (TEA), with a specific focus on tracts involved in the sensorimotor network.

Methods

We prospectively collected and analysed 3T-MRI clinical data from 111 consecutive babies born very or extremely preterm (gestational age at birth: 24–32 weeks), thus presenting with a wide variety of brain lesions typically associated with prematurity (Fig.1), and being at high risk of neurodevelopmental disorders. The babies were scanned at term-equivalent age (TEA, 38–43 weeks PMA). T2-weighted (T2w) anatomical images were acquired in three orthogonal planes with 0.8 mm in-plane resolution and 2 mm slice thickness, then reconstructed into a single isotropic volume using the NiftyMIC super-resolution framework. A hybrid segmentation approach combining DrawEM and iBEAT was applied to these super-resolved T2w volumes, enabling accurate whole-brain parcellation. Diffusion-weighted imaging was obtained using a single-shell EPI sequence (b=1000 s/mm², 42 directions, four b=0 images and three reversed-phase b=0 volumes) and processed using neonatal-optimised pipelines (DrawEM-iBEAT version 2.0-FSL version 6.0.7.13-MRtrix3 version 3.0.3) including motion, eddy-current and susceptibility distortion correction. A key point of this study was to obtain a robust registration of individual images to the template despite the brain anatomical specificities of our population. We optimized this registration by creating brain masks from segmentations of super-resolved T2w images, which enabled us to remove part of the CSF. Individual dMRI images without diffusion weighting (b=0) were then coregistered to T2w images, which were themselves registered to the Schuh3 neonatal template. The registrations were conducted using Ants 2.5.3 with finely tuned parameters. Besides, following the pre-processing of dMRI data, multiple fibre orientations were estimated with BEDPOSTX with a two-fibre model. Probabilistic tractography was conducted using baby-XTRACT4, ensuring consistent reconstruction of five tracts essential to the sensorimotor system: the corticospinal tract (CST), superior thalamic radiation (STR), frontal aslant tract (FAT), forceps minor (FMI), and middle cerebellar peduncle (MCP).  For each tract, DTI metrics of fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) were extracted using a weighted average over each tract and were used as microstructural markers of white matter maturation, reflecting processes such as axonal organisation, neurites density and the onset of myelination in the neonatal brain.

Besides, nine clinical factors were recorded for each infant, to target well-known risk factors related to pregnancy, birth and neonatal complications: sex, monochorionic twin pregnancy, category of gestational age (GA) at birth (<26 weeks (G1), 26–27weeks+6days (G2), and >=28weeks (G3)), small-for-gestational-age (SGA) weight, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, invasive ventilation, necrotizing enterocolitis, prolonged parenteral nutrition, and neonatal sepsis. We also collected the Kidokoro radiological score dedicated to preterm cohorts5. Linear models tested associations between DTI metrics (either on global WM or each tract of interest) and the set of factors, adjusting for PMA at MRI. Additional models included DTI metrics in the global WM as a covariate of non-interest instead of PMA, to disentangle global WM to tract-specific effects. FDR correction controlled for multiple comparisons.

Results

Tractography succeeded for nearly all infants (108–111 successful reconstructions per tract), demonstrating the robustness of the pipeline even in the context of ventriculomegaly, heterogeneous WM signal abnormalities, and variable lesion patterns and locations. The cohort displayed marked anatomical variability, yet group probability maps showed coherent tract anatomy, validating the approach (Fig.2.A.). As expected, FA increased and MD decreased in the tracts with advancing PMA at MRI.

Global WM analyses indicated that male sex, SGA status, neonatal sepsis, and high Kidokoro WM abnormality scores were consistently associated with reduced FA and/or elevated diffusivities.

At the tract level, CST and STR were the most sensitive tracts to risk factors. SGA, prolonged parenteral nutrition, and severe WM abnormalities were associated with higher MD and lower FA in these tracts (Fig.2.B.).

Importantly, after adjusting for global WM integrity, several associations persisted, particularly in CST and STR, supporting the presence of tract-specific vulnerabilities beyond diffuse dysmaturation.

Conclusion

Using a fully automated and lesion-resilient neonatal dMRI pipeline, we reliably reconstructed key sensorimotor tracts in a large cohort of very preterm infants. Our findings reveal that perinatal factors influence WM maturation through both diffuse and tract-specific mechanisms. Male sex, SGA weight, and sepsis were associated with global WM vulnerability, while the CST and STR showed heightened sensitivity to nutritional and clinical complications, including prolonged parenteral nutrition. These results suggest that early-life adversities differentially shape maturation within distinct components of the sensorimotor network. Tract-level microstructural characterization may therefore enhance early risk stratification and pave the way for targeted neuroprotective and rehabilitative strategies in infants born very premature. This approach will also be applied to MRI data acquired at 2 months of corrected age (CA) in a subgroup of 39 babies, in order to correlate the longitudinal evolution of MRI measurements with their neurodevelopmental follow-up up to 42 months of CA.
