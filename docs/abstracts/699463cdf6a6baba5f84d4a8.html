Introduction

Gestational brain development is rapid and structurally complex, making accurate in utero characterization from magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) critical for monitoring fetal growth. Spatiotemporal brain atlas construction captures average anatomical structures and physiological features across populations, providing anatomical priors for understanding normal and abnormal early brain development. Traditionally, Symmetric Groupwise Normalization (SyGN) from the Advanced Normalization Tools (ANTs) library has served as the standard for iterative atlas construction. However, grounded in pairwise and group-wise registration optimization, SyGN is computationally intensive, often requiring days for large-scale datasets. Furthermore, its reliance on discrete time points and affine alignment not only restricts temporal resolution and introduces bias but also neglects individual variability. Consequently, these limitations hinder the ability to capture the complex non-linear morphological changes typical of the fetal brain, particularly during the third trimester. To address these limitations, recent research has therefore focused on developing deep learning-based frameworks, such as CINeMA1, Atlas-GAN2, and Aladdin3, to enhance accuracy, efficiency, and versatility. However, in this study, our quantitative benchmarking demonstrates that these deep learning methods fail to surpass the traditional SyGN framework on fetal brain atlas construction, highlighting the need for improved methodologies.

Methods

Data Acquisition. With IRB approval and written informed consent, 2D T2-weighted scans in the axial, coronal, and sagittal planes were acquired from 484 pregnant women (20-38 weeks gestation) with normal fetal brains. The NiftyMIC algorithm was employed for motion correction and reconstruction, yielding 3D brain volumes at 0.8 mm isotropic resolution. A pretrained segmentation model was employed to generate masks for white matter (WM), cortical gray matter (CGM), deep gray matter (DGM), cerebellum, brainstem, ventricles, extra-axial cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and corpus callosum. Of 484 fetal 3D MRI scans, 419 were used for atlas construction and 65 for atlas quality evaluation.

Benchmark Baselines. Five state-of-the-art (SOTA) atlas construction methods were evaluated: the traditional SyGN and four deep learning-based methods (CINeMA, Atlas-GAN, Aladdin, and Deepali4). SyGN iteratively warps subjects to an initial template via non-rigid registration. The template is updated by averaging these warped images until convergence, yielding a stable population representation. CINeMA is an implicit neural representation (INR) framework where subject-specific latent codes and conditioning variables modulate a multilayer perceptron, mapping spatial coordinates to continuous image intensities and tissue probabilities for fast, resolution-agnostic generation. Atlas-GAN reformulates registration and atlas estimation as an adversarial game. Through generator-discriminator interplay, Atlas-GAN can synthesize high-quality anatomical structures under flexible covariates. Aladdin obviates affine pre-registration, employing CNNs and a novel pairwise alignment loss to jointly predict the population-average atlas and diffeomorphic velocity fields. Deepali is an open-source, GPU-accelerated library utilizing an image pyramid strategy for multi-resolution alignment between source and target images. In our work, a group-wise registration scheme based on Deepali is adopted to efficiently construct atlases. For ANTs, Atlas-GAN, and Deepali, which cannot directly estimate tissue segmentation maps of the mean atlas, ground-truth segmentations from training images were warped to the atlas space, and atlas tissue labels were generated via majority voting.

Metrics. (1) Avg Dice: the average Dice coefficient of segmentation labels deformed from the atlas to target images, reflecting registration accuracy; (2) EFC: Entropy Focus Criterion to assess the sharpness of reconstructed atlases; (3) Avg ||Def|| : the mean L2 norm of deformation fields; (4) Centrality: the L2 norm of the mean deformation field, where smaller values indicate a more central and less biased atlas for comparable registration accuracy; (5) Avg |Jφ(p)| : the mean absolute determinant of the Jacobian matrix Jφ(p) of deformation fields φ mapping the atlas to individual images, where voxels p with |Jφ(p)| ≤ 0 indicate local folding, while |Jφ(p)| ∼ 1 corresponds to smooth local deformations.

Results

Qualitative comparison revealed distinct differences in atlas and tissue segmentation quality across methods (Fig. 1). CINeMA constructed atlases that were visually sharp with clearly preserved structural details, outperforming the other methods. SyGN and Atlas-GAN yielded atlases that were broadly similar to each other, with relatively high anatomical fidelity, but with less fine detail than CINeMA. In contrast, the atlases generated by Aladdin exhibited noticeable distortions of anatomical structures, while those produced by Deepali appeared comparatively blurred with poorer delineation of structural boundaries.  

Growth trajectory modeling5 revealed varying alignment with normative data (Fig. 2A). The atlas-derived volumes from Deepali and Aladdin deviated more markedly from the normative mean with poorer goodness-of-fit, while those from CINeMA, Atlas-GAN, and SyGN demonstrated better alignment, consistent with the qualitative observations in atlas appearance (Fig. 1).

Quantitative metrics corroborated the visual assessments (Fig. 2B). CINeMA’s lowest EFC (0.9721) confirmed its visual sharpness. Atlas-GAN achieved the highest segmentation accuracy (Avg Dice=0.9034), marginally outperforming CINeMA, while Deepali scored lowest (0.8644). SyGN exhibited the minimal bias with the lowest deformation norms (Avg ||Def||=636.13; Centrality=383.56), whereas Aladdin showed the highest deformation costs. Nevertheless, all methods preserved topology with average Jacobian determinants (Avg |Jφ(p)|) approximating 1.

Conclusion

Quantitative benchmarking reveals that current deep learning methods do not consistently outperform the traditional SyGN framework in fetal brain atlas construction, underscoring the need for further methodological refinement.
