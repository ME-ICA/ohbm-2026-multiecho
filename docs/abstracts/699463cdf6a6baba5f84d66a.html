Introduction

Fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) MRI is the most sensitive structural sequence for detecting white matter hyperintensities (WMH), core imaging biomarkers of cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) with established links to cognitive decline, stroke, and dementia.[1] Large, longitudinal, multicenter studies of WMH often follow the same participants over many years, during which protocols have inevitably evolved from 2D to 3D FLAIR.[2] This protocol drift creates major barriers for harmonized pooled analyses because 2D and 3D FLAIR differ in spatial resolution and image contrast, leading to variable WMH segmentation. Conventional methods not designed for such heterogeneity perform poorly across scan protocols.[3] To address this critical gap, we develop a novel deep learning (DL)-based synthetic contrast framework that harmonizes FLAIR across scan protocols, enabling highly repeatable WMH measurements and robust WMH tracking over time. 

Methods

In a prospective longitudinal study, we acquired both 2D and 3D FLAIR and T1-weighted images (n=130) at baseline from a cohort of patients with CSVD. A subset of patients underwent test-retest repeatability scans (n=14) separated by ~1 month. Another subset of patients had longitudinal follow-up scans (n=42) ~3 years after the baseline. The paired 2D/3D FLAIR provides subject-level ground truth for 3D to 2D harmonization, enabling direct DL-driven mapping between the two protocols. After excluding images with severe motion artefacts, the baseline scans were split into training (n=72), validation (n=8), and test (n=50) sets. We developed a transformation network using a diffusion model with the shifted-window vision transformer as the backbone. This network learned complex non-linear conversions from 3D FLAIR + T1 to 2D FLAIR. The synthetic 2D FLAIR is compatible with existing 2D-based WMH segmentation tools.[4] This DL synthetic 2D FLAIR model was also applied to the test-retest and longitudinal data.

Results

Using paired 2D and 3D FLAIR in the test set, we compared native 3D FLAIR without harmonization to DL-generated synthetic 2D FLAIR using structural similarity index measure (SSIM) and Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) for WMH segmentation. The conversion markedly improved both image similarity and WMH segmentation accuracy (SSIM: 0.768 ± 0.032 vs. 0.900 ± 0.028, p < 10⁻⁷; DSC: 0.576 ± 0.149 vs. 0.826 ± 0.064, p < 10⁻⁷). In the repeatability subset, intraclass correlations (ICC) between baseline 2D FLAIR WMH volumes and those with and without conversion were 0.953 and 0.969, respectively, closely matching the ICC of 0.975 between repeated 2D FLAIR acquisitions. For assessment of longitudinal WMH volume change, using the first 2D FLAIR as baseline, synthetic 2D FLAIR yielded a strong correlation with ground-truth longitudinal measurements derived from follow-up 2D FLAIR (R =0.913, p <10-16), versus a much weaker relationship for non-harmonized 3D FLAIR (R =0.399, p<10-2). These results demonstrate that the proposed harmonization framework enables robust and accurate longitudinal WMH quantification across different FLAIR protocols.

Conclusion

We present the first DL-based 2D and 3D FLAIR harmonization for robust longitudinal WMH measurement across heterogeneous FLAIR MRI protocols. Our framework provides a practical, scalable solution to harmonizing mixed 2D/3D FLAIR protocols within individuals in large longitudinal studies, without requiring modification or retraining of downstream WMH segmentation models. By generating high-fidelity synthetic 2D FLAIR from 3D acquisitions, we allow integration of legacy 2D datasets with modern 3D imaging, facilitating reliable WMH quantification across protocol transitions and enabling generalizable, protocol-agnostic imaging of CSVD. Performance of the synthetic 2D FLAIR for WMH segmentation matches inter-rater reliability of manual annotation, supporting clinical validity. While the current framework targets 3D-to-2D harmonization, future developments could extend to bi-directional conversion, making this approach adaptable for studies utilizing dominant 3D protocols. Collectively, this work sets a new standard for accurate, multimodal longitudinal neuroimaging in cerebrovascular research.
