Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a prevalent and severe mental illness1 characterized by brain network dysfunction detectable through resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI)2. Although deep-learning (DL) models show superior performance in MDD prediction, recent studies reports conflicting findings regarding methodological choices: some showed decreased performance with harmonization3, while others reported varying performance across brain atlases4, In light of this inconsistency, we hypothesize that disease severity prediction is influenced by an interaction between harmonization techniques and atlases selection. 

Methods

We analyzed rs-fMRI data of 835 MDD patients (aged 20-65) from the Rest-meta-MDD dataset5, parcellated by four atlases (AAL, Dosenbach, Craddock, and Power atlases). Functional connectivity matrices for each atlas were computed using Pearson correlation and divided into training (80%), validation (10%), and testing (10%) subsets via age-based stratified sampling. ComBat harmonization was estimated from the training subset and applied to the remaining subsets to mitigate inter-site effect. Three DL architectures were evaluated: (1) a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with 3 hidden layers with batch normalization.; (2) a graph neural network with 3 convolutional layers and PairNorm regularization (GNN); and (3) an attention-enhanced GNN incorporating self-attention mechanism (GNN-Att). All models were trained by the Adam optimizer and ReLU activation. To statistically evaluate atlas and harmonization effects on model performance of predicting disease severity, data-splitting was repeated 30 times. Model performance was evaluated using Root-mean-square error (RMSE) and Pearson's correlation coefficient (r) between true and predicted severities. Considering variability observed across 30 resamples, statistical comparisons were performed using linear mixed models (LMM) with three factors (harmonization, atlas, model) and Bonferroni corrected post-hoc tests (p < 0.05).

Results

Separate LMM analyses for RMSE and r showed significant main effects of model on RMSE (F = 14.33, p < 0.001), and atlas on r (F=14.40, p < 0.05), harmonization on r (F=10.63, p < 0.05). Notably, significant model × harmonization interactions emerged for both RMSE (F=11.44, p < 0.05) and r (F=24.12, p < 0.0001), confirming our hypothesis. Post-hoc tests revealed that MLP benefited significantly from harmonization for both metrics (RMSE: p < 0.003; r: p < 0.001), with Dosenbach and Power atlases outperforming AAL and Craddock for RMSE (p < 0.05). In contrast, GNN models showed inferior performance while applying harmonization, though GNN-Att demonstrated improved r ( p = 0.003). Atlas effects remained consistent across GNN models: Power significantly outperform other atlas for RMSE (p < 0.05) and exceeded AAL for r in GNN-Att (p<0.001).

Conclusion

This study demonstrated architecture-specific responses to harmonization, with MLP benefiting from it while GNN models performed better without it. Notably, GNN and GNN-Att exhibited distinct performance patterns, highlighting the importance of model-specific methodological optimization. Across all DL architectures,, functional-based atlases (Dosenbach and Power) consistently achieved superior prediction, suggesting that functionally-defined parcellations better capture neural signatures relevant to MDD severity than anatomically-based atlases.
