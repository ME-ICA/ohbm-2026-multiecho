Introduction

Alcohol intake has been confirmed as a risk factor for the development of mental disorder, yet the specific biological mechanisms underlying this association remain unclear. Fractional anisotropy (FA), a component of diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), serves as a core indicator for quantifying the integrity of brain white matter microstructure. This study employs a two-step Mendelian randomization (TSMR) approach to investigate whether FA of brain white matter is a key mediating factor between alcohol intake and the development of mental disorder, thereby providing new biological evidence for the mechanistic study of alcohol-related mental disorder.

Methods

All analyses in this study were based on publicly available genome-wide association study (GWAS) meta-data. The summary data for alcohol intake-related GWAS was sourced from the GWAS & Sequencing Consortium of Alcohol and Nicotine Use (GSCAN),1 excluding data from UK Biobank and covering approximately 200,000 individuals. The summary statistics for 48 brain FA phenotypes were obtained from the Oxford Brain Imaging Genetics Server (BIG40),2 covering approximately 33,000 subjects. The summary data for GWAS of mental disorder was sourced from the Finnish Genetics Project (FinnGen).3

Genetic variants strongly associated with exposure (P < 5×10⁻⁸) were screened as instrumental variables. Variants from high linkage disequilibrium (LD) regions (r² > 0.001 within a 1000 kb range) were excluded based on the 1000 Genomes Project European reference panel. Moreover, the variants strongly correlated with alcohol intake were exclude from the instrumental variables used in the second stage of MR screening.

The main analysis was conducted using the Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW) method. Additionally, MR Egger regression, weighted median method, and weighted mode method were employed as sensitivity analyses. In the TSMR, β₁ was defined as the total effect of alcohol intake on mental disorders, β₂ was defined as the causal effect of alcohol intake on brain FA phenotypes, and β₃ was defined as the causal effect of brain FA phenotypes on mental disorders. The mediating effect was ultimately calculated using the product of coefficients method (mediating effect = β₂ × β₃), and the mediating proportion was estimated by (β₂ × β₃) / β₁. All statistical tests were conducted with a significance level of P < 0.05. All MR analyses were employed using the TwoSampleMR package in R software.

Results

An increase in alcohol intake can lead to an elevated risk of developing mental disorders (β = 0.462, SE = 0.113, P = 4.54×10-5; OR = 1.587, 95% CI: 1.271~1.981), and it can also result in a decrease in the FA value of the right anterior limb of the internal capsule (β = -0.304, SE = 0.126, P = 0.016). Conversely, a lower FA value in the right anterior limb of the internal capsule increases the risk of developing mental disorders (β = -0.113, SE = 0.047, P = 0.017; OR = 0.893, 95% CI: 0.814~0.980). The mediating proportion of the FA value in the right anterior limb of the internal capsule was 7.45%. Conclusion

This study, through a two-step Mendelian randomization analysis, revealed that the fractional anisotropy of the right anterior limb of the internal capsule plays a mediating role in the causal pathway between alcohol intake and mental disorder. It suggests that alcohol intake may indirectly increase the risk of developing mental disorders by reducing the integrity of white matter microstructure in this brain white matter.
