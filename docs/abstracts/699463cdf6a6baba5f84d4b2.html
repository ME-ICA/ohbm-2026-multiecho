Introduction

Recent advances in fMRI have pushed data acquisition toward extremes of spatial and temporal resolution, including whole-brain imaging at 0.8 mm and single-slice imaging sampled every 40 ms. Such datasets contain rich structure distributed across millions of voxels and time points, making exploratory, data-driven analyses particularly important [1]. In this regime, simple voxel-wise correlations are a powerful tool because they are well suited to probe structure across a wide range of temporal and spatial scales without committing to a specific generative model. Voxel-wise correlations can rapidly expose functional networks, reveal spatial coherence, highlight non-neuronal coupling driven by physiology, motion, or imaging artifacts.

However, the scale of modern fMRI data renders conventional voxel-wise correlations computationally intractable. A whole-brain 0.8 mm dataset contains ~4.3 million voxels (170×226×112), corresponding to ~18.5 trillion pairwise correlations. Storing this matrix at float32 precision alone would require ~74 TB of disk space. Storing or visualizing this matrix is far beyond the capabilities of typical neuroimaging labs. As a result, current pipelines compress fMRI data into regions of interest (ROIs). While effective computationally, this reduction conflicts with the fine grained nature of fMRI and is driven by practical constraints rather than theoretical considerations. Whether important information is lost by abandoning voxel-level analyses remains an open question.

Here we introduce an alternative approach that removes these computational barriers: real-time, on-demand voxel-wise correlation analysis integrated into a tailored graphical user interface (GUI). LayNii IDA (Integrated Discovery Application) is an optimized software for interactive voxel-wise operations, enabling rapid interrogation of functional structure and efficient quality control workflows (e.g. [2]) without requiring large compute clusters or coarse ROI strategies.

Methods

LayNii IDA is implemented as a stand-alone program within the LayNii software suite [3] and is available for MacOS, Linux, and Windows (https://github.com/layerfMRI/LayNii/releases). The computational backend is written in C++, and the graphical interface is built using Dear ImGui. Here, we introduce and demonstrate the new interface that implements voxel-wise correlations. This useful functionality allows users to surf time series datasets, viewing instantly updating seedbased correlation maps as the mouse moves. This feature is similar to AFNI's InstaCorr [4, 5], however our program is a fully independent, from-scratch implementation with a fundamentally different optimization strategy tailored for extreme fMRI datasets. Specifically, LayNii IDA avoids full-dataset precomputations; instead, correlations are computed on demand only for the actively visualized slices. As a result, the only substantial waiting time occurs during the initial data loading from disk to RAM, after which the GUI interactions are instantaneous, enabling a highly responsive exploratory workflow. Additionally, due the sparser nature of higher-correlation patterns in these high-res datasets, the overlay color scheme differs from the AFNI GUI's, to preserve more sensitive and spatially narrow patterns, independently of the structural grayscale underlay.

We demonstrate the capabilities of LayNii IDA using four datasets:

1. Whole-brain 0.8 mm iso. resolution acquired at 7 T during movie watching; publicly available, acquisition and processing described in [6].

2. Partial brain coverage at 0.39 mm iso. resolution during passive 30s block-designed checkerboard viewing [7].

3. Single-slice (transversal and sagittal) fMRI at 3 T with 3 mm iso. resolution at TR = 40 ms (25 Hz) during resting state; provided by Ben Inglis (https://practicalfmri.blogspot.com/2025/06/single-slice-epi-to-visualize-brain.html).

Upon loading each dataset, real-time correlations can be enabled from the GUI and explored. Volumes of fMRI data are underlaid in grayscale, and voxel-wise correlations are overlaid using the red and blue color channels. Positive correlations are mapped linearly onto the red channel and negative correlations onto the blue channel. This direct color channel mapping provides absolute, unthresholded visual feedback [8]. The full correlation range (–1 to 1) is always represented as an exact mapping of correlation value, without underlay-blending or colormap scaling that could obscure extreme correlation values.

Results

Figure 1 A shows voxel-wise correlations computed from the 0.8 mm isotropic fMRI dataset during movie watching, using a seed placed in Heschl’s gyrus. The resulting map reveals coherent, spatially precise correlations across the auditory cortex. B shows voxel-wise correlations from the 0.39 mm isotropic fMRI dataset acquired during a checkerboard stimulation task, using a simple on/off model with a 1-TR lag. Robust activation patterns within the visual cortex emerge immediately. Together, these examples illustrate how rapid voxel-wise correlation mapping enables instant exploratory inspection of functional specificity and data quality across tasks and resolutions. See https://youtu.be/ZFsBljNOcyw?si=eJNrdgjA8JdsFjhJ for a video showcase.

Figure 2 A shows voxel-wise correlations seeding from a voxel in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), highlighting motion-related CSF artifacts that propagate across the dataset. B voxel-wise correlations seeding from a voxel in the superior sagittal sinus, revealing venous drainage–related physiological artifacts. These examples demonstrate how seed placement can be used diagnostically to expose different classes of physiological and non-neural signals which is important for artifact detection and careful interpretation of the fMRI data.

Conclusion

LayNii IDA is a new tool designed specifically for interactive exploration of fMRI datasets at extremes of spatial (e.g., 0.8–0.39 mm isotropic) and temporal resolution (e.g., TR = 40 ms). Its central feature is real-time, voxel-wise correlation mapping, enabling immediate, data-driven interrogation of functional structure and data quality. By eliminating the need for full-dataset precomputation (e.g. as done in AFNI’s InstaCorr program), LayNii IDA substantially reduces memory demands: if a computer can load the time series, it can compute the voxel-wise correlations instantly with no further wait time. This design choice allows modest or older hardware (4–8 GB RAM) to handle submillimeter whole-brain data, making voxel-wise analyses accessible to small institutes, individual researchers, and students. For example, standard laptops from the past decade can perform interactive voxel-wise exploration of 0.8 mm isotropic whole-brain datasets (e.g. [6]). By removing computational barriers, LayNii IDA enables rapid, data-driven exploration of functional structure and facilitates efficient quality-control procedures that would otherwise require heavy compute resources or coarse ROI approaches.
