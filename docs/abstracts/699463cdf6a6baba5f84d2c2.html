Introduction

The autonomic nervous system (ANS) is at the interface of the central nervous system (CNS) and the organism. It is composed of the parasympathetic (PNS) and the sympathetic nervous system (SNS). These two systems are composed of mixed nerves. These two systems have classically antagonistic effects. Overall, the SNS has an accelerating role while the PNS has a moderating role with an equilibrium between both systems. An imbalance of these two systems may favor the development of pathologies. The complex network of connections in ANS allows a permanent/fine regulation of the innervated organs: information is transmitted from the periphery to the CNS by afferent pathways, integrated in the central autonomic nervous system (CAN), and then from the CNS to the organs by efferent pathways [1]. The central representation of the autonomic nervous system (ANS), known as the central autonomic network (CAN) has been explored in human subjects since the advent of neuroimaging tools, such as PET and fMRI and remains controversial: which cortical regions are related or modulated by the ANS ? Since PNS can be derived from ECG recordings, and intracranial EEG (iEEG) activity is considered as the gold standard to record brain activity, we took the unique opportunity of simultaneous iEEG and ECG recording in epileptic patients, to address the question of the brain regions involved in cardiac autonomic control.

Methods

Patients, protocol and data acquisition: Forty-five patients presenting focal drug-resistant epilepsy eligible for surgery underwent standard pre-surgical evaluations using intra-cerebral recordings at the epilepsy centers of five University Hospitals in France. They were proposed to participate to a research protocol approved by local ethics committees and signed informed consent. While participating to studies involving decision-making tasks, the iEEG and ECG were simultaneously recorded (Fig.1). For the purpose of this study, the iEEG data (up to 256 contacts) used here were acquired in the context of two previously published studies [2]. Each dataset had a total duration of 24.2 +/- 10.7 min per session with a 512Hz sampling frequency and two or three sessions were performed by each patient. Each patient also had an MRI anatomical image that allows accurate location of each electrode cortical implantation.

Data processing and statistical analysis: Among the 45 datasets, 38 could be analyzed. From the iEEG data, the γ-band was extracted in the 50-150Hz band at each bipolar montage and its time variation was evaluated by computing mean Υ power during 1-min long sliding windows. From the ECG, the R-peaks were detected to compute the R-R intervals that form the tachogram. After correction for missing peaks, a 1-min long sliding window was applied to evaluate the mean relative HF power (HFν) that reflects the time-course of its parasympathetic activity. It has been shown that despite this short duration, the HFν estimation remains robust [3]. For each recording site, a linear model between HFν and mean γ power time courses could then be computed providing a β-value. Statistical analysis was then conducted in two steps. At recording site level, using permutation tests, it was possible to derive the probability to obtain the β-value. At brain parcel level, we first sought for all the recording sites located in it, using the MarsBar Brain atlas parcellation scheme. Note here that the left and the right parcels were pooled together given that no hemispheric differences were found in the regions of sympathetic regulation nor in the parasympathetic regulation except in the amygdala and that this enhances the number of degrees of freedom. The parcel was further retained for statistical testing if at least 4 patients presented electrodes in that parcel and at least a total of 12 recording sites were located in it. Permutation testing was thus used to check for the significance in the parcel. Significance at parcel level is considered for p-values < 0.05.

Results

Contacts covered most of the neo-cortex (but not the cuneus) and poorly the deep brain regions (thalamus, caudate, pallidum and accumbens). A significant negative correlation between HFν-HRV and mean γ power were found in most of the parcels related to somatosensory (Sdl, Sv), motor (Mdm, Mdl, Mv), auditory (STCc, MTCc, MTCr), visual (VCm, VCl) and olfactory (OFCvl) functions, as well as in ventral inferior parietal cortex (iPCv), in dorsomedial prefrontal cortex (PFCdm), in cingulate regions (MCC and PCC) and in posterior insula (pIns) (Fig.2, red regions). In these regions, the number of significant recording sites and the number of subjects were higher than 13 and 6 respectively, while the p-value < 0.01. Regions with a p-value < 0.05 (and above 0.01) were located in the premotor cortex (PMdm, PMdl and Pmrv) and in the prefrontal cortex (PFrd) (Fig.2, light red regions). This last region of the prefrontal cortex presents a positive correlation with HFn while the correlation is negative in all other regions. The negative correlation indicates a decreasing influence of the parasympathetic system on iEEG activity. And finally, we report few regions for which there are less or equal than 4 subjects with significant correlation (p<0.01) in these regions (Fig.2, hatched red regions). They are located in the somatosensory cortex (Sdm), in the visual cortex (VCs, VCrm), in the superior parietal (SPC) and in the cingular cortex (ICC). Those with less than 4 subjects with significant correlation (p<0.05) are also reported (Fig.2, hatched light red regions).

Conclusion

Intracranial EEG recordings present a unique opportunity to better understand the cortical autonomic network organization. In the present study, the analysis of correlation of iEEG activity in the broadband g power and of parasympathetic parameter HFν-HRV allowed the direct determination of brain parcels whose activity is modulated by the parasympathetic system. This is the first direct demonstration of the involvement of regions of the CAN. The results reported in the present study mainly confirm regions found in the fMRI literature in meta-analysis of task related studies [4] and in resting state fMRI study [5] except the anterior insula, the amygdala and the hippocampus, which are considered as pertaining to the core of the CAN. The present study elicited additional regions which appears modulated by the parasympathetic activity, that are related to the somatosensory cortex, the motor and premotor cortices, the visual cortex, the auditory cortex as well as in the ventral orbitofrontal cortex. The correlation is negative in all these regions, as was found in the previous literature, reflecting the concomitant down-regulation of the neuronal activity with increased parasympathetic activity. Interestingly, there was a lack of correlation in the anterior insula contrary to the current literature and in the hippocampal formation that was remaining controversial.
