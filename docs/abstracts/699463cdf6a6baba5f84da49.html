Introduction

Autism is a disorder affecting information processing across multiple functional domains, exhibiting distinct differences in perception, emotion, and higher cognitive development compared to typically developing children^1,2. These differences suggest potential developmental anomalies in the functional hierarchy^3. Early research points to abnormal connections between low-level sensory processing and higher cognitive functions in autistic children, potentially causing imbalances in information processing, which affect behavior and learning^4. However, the exact developmental patterns of the functional hierarchy in autistic children are still unclear.  Understanding these patterns could reveal neural mechanisms specific to ASD, aiding in early diagnosis and targeted interventions. This study aims to investigate the developmental patterns of functional hierarchy in children with ASD and typically developing children, and to identify potential developmental abnormalities in children with autism.

Methods

We employed a large functional MRI dataset comprising 476 children aged under 13 years old from the Shanghai Autism Early Development (SAED) Cohort, including 200 NC children (aged 1.04-12.93 years, 42.0% males), 276 ASD children (aged 1.67-12.87 years, 79.3% males). We performed gradient decomposition analysis^5 on the voxel-level functional connectivity matrices of different age groups and plotted scatter plots of the core gradients. To explore the developmental changes in the global topography and regional gradient scores of the functional connectivity gradient, we also performed an cross-participant age sliding window analysis. Subsequently, we calculated the convex hull areas of different networks in the core gradient score scatter plots, which reflects the degree of dispersion or aggregation of various brain networks within the functional hierarchical structure. We also calculated the Euclidean distance between different networks in the scatter plot of core gradient scores to illustrate their proximity within the functional hierarchical structure. Their correlations with age were then assessed to examine potential developmental abnormalities in the functional hierarchy structures of children with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD). Finally, for each voxel, we calculated the age at which the core gradient reached its peak to generate a maturation map. We then compared the average gradient maturation age across different networks between ASD and typically developing (NC) children. 

Subsequently, to precisely identify regions exhibiting nonlinear developmental abnormalities, we performed a voxel-wise analysis using a General Linear Model (GLM) to examine the relationship between the Primary Functional Gradient (PG) score and age. The model included terms for Age, Age2 (to capture nonlinear trajectory), Group (ASD/NC), Sex (as a nuisance covariate), and their respective interaction terms (Age × Group, Age2 ×Group). The Age2 ×Group interaction term was the primary focus, as its significance pinpoints regions where the developmental trajectory shape fundamentally differs between the two groups, which underlies the PG trajectory inversion. Statistical significance was determined using a non-parametric permutation test (n = 10000) and corrected for multiple comparisons across the whole brain using Gaussian Random Field (GRF) theory (Voxel-level P < 0.001, Cluster-level P < 0.05). The resulting significant cluster was analyzed further to characterize the specific PG developmental trajectory in the ASD cohort, including fitting the quadratic curves and calculating the instantaneous slope (dG / dAge) over the age range to quantify the directional error in maturation rate.

Results

In early and mid-childhood, the visual-sensorimotor gradient dominated in both the NC and ASD groups (Fig. 1A-B). In late childhood (11.0-13.0 years), the NC group exhibited a transition to the primary-to-transmodal gradient, which was less evident in the ASD group (Fig. 1A-D). Age sliding window analysis (Fig. 1E) revealed that in the NC group, the explanation ratio of the visual-sensorimotor gradient gradually decreased (t = -2.44, p < 0.05), while the explanation ratio of the primary-to-transmodal gradient gradually increased (t = 4.94 , p < 0.01), becoming dominant around age 12. In contrast, the explanation ratio of the visual-sensorimotor gradient in the ASD group gradually increased (t = 2.03, p < 0.05), while the primary-to-transmodal gradient does not display significant age-related changes (t = -0.585, p = 0.561). Furthermore, the range of the primary-to-transmodal gradient in the ASD group showed a reduction related to age (t = -2.92, p < 0.01). 

Furthermore, we found that the sensorimotor network (SMN), dorsal attention network (DAN), and ventral attention network (VAN) in the ASD group were abnormally developed in the core gradient structure (Figure 2A-B). Specifically, in the ASD group, the convex hull area of the somatomotor network increases significantly with age, whereas no clear age trend is observed in the NC group. The convex hull areas of the DAN and VAN networks decrease significantly with age in the NC group but remain stable in the ASD group. We also found that the inter-network distance between DAN and VAN increases with age in the NC group (t = 2.21, p < 0.05), whereas in the ASD group, this distance decreases significantly over time (t = -2.16, p < 0.05).

Finally, significant differences were observed between the ASD and NC groups in the functional maturation patterns of the first two core gradients, with pronounced differences in the default mode network (DMN), SMN, subcortical regions (SUB), and VAN (Figure 2C-D).

Building on the network-level developmental alterations observed in Figure 2A-D, Figure 2E further examined whether these hierarchical abnormalities were associated with localized deviations in regional gradient trajectories. A significant Age² × Group interaction was identified in the right posterior middle temporal gyrus (pMTG), a region classified within the dorsal attention network (DAN), surviving voxel-wise multiple-comparison correction (Voxel-level P < 0.001, GRF-corrected P < 0.05). Regional principal gradient values in this locus exhibited distinct age-related trajectories across groups. In the NC group, gradient values increased with age and reached a plateau, forming an inverted U-shaped developmental profile. In contrast, the ASD group showed a U-shaped trajectory, characterized by an initial increase followed by a subsequent decrease across development. Consistent with these trajectory differences, instantaneous slope analysis revealed group-specific patterns in age-dependent change rates. The NC group showed a progressively increasing and predominantly positive slope with age, whereas the ASD group exhibited a decreasing slope that transitioned from positive to negative values during childhood. Given that the principal gradient indexes the hierarchical axis from primary sensory-motor regions to transmodal association cortex, these slope patterns indicate a progressive shift toward the transmodal end of the gradient in the NC group and an age-related shift away from the transmodal end in the ASD group. Together, these findings link the network-level gradient abnormalities to a localized inversion of developmental directionality in a temporo-occipital attentional region, providing a regional substrate for the altered hierarchical maturation observed in ASD.

Conclusion

These findings provide converging evidence that children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) exhibit atypical developmental organization of the cortical functional hierarchy. By examining age-related changes in the principal functional gradients, our results demonstrate that the maturation of large-scale brain organization in ASD deviates from the canonical developmental trajectory observed in typically developing children.

In the neurotypical group, development was characterized by a gradual transition from a visual–sensorimotor-dominated gradient structure in early and mid-childhood toward a primary-to-transmodal hierarchy in late childhood, accompanied by increasing dominance and expanding range of the primary-to-transmodal gradient. This shift reflects the progressive differentiation and integration of higher-order association systems along the cortical hierarchy. In contrast, children with ASD failed to exhibit this normative transition. Instead, the visual–sensorimotor gradient remained increasingly dominant with age, while the primary-to-transmodal gradient showed attenuated age-related change and a reduced hierarchical range, indicating altered large-scale cortical organization.

At the network level, these abnormalities were particularly evident in the sensorimotor, dorsal attention, and ventral attention networks, which showed atypical developmental patterns in their spatial embedding within the core gradient structure. The absence of normative age-related separation between attentional networks in ASD further suggests disrupted hierarchical differentiation among systems supporting attentional control and sensory integration.

Crucially, these network-level alterations were linked to localized deviations in regional developmental trajectories. A focal region in the right Middle Temporal Gyrus (MTG) within the dorsal attention network exhibited a significant Age² × Group interaction, characterized by an inversion of developmental directionality along the primary-to-transmodal gradient. Instantaneous slope analysis revealed that, whereas typical development involved a progressive shift of this region toward the transmodal end of the hierarchy, ASD was marked by an age-related shift in the opposite direction. This finding highlights how global alterations in functional hierarchy can manifest as regionally specific disruptions in developmental dynamics.

Together, these results suggest that ASD is associated with altered timing and direction of cortical hierarchical maturation, particularly affecting the integration of attentional and higher-order association systems. Rather than reflecting a uniform developmental delay, the observed patterns point to a reorganization of neurodevelopmental trajectories that may underlie core cognitive and perceptual features of ASD.
