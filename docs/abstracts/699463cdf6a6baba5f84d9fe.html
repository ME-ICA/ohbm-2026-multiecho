Introduction

Neuroimaging-derived brain age models quantify how closely an individual’s brain structure aligns with normative aging trajectories, with higher brain age gaps (BAGs) indexing accelerated aging linked to vascular burden, reduced cognition, and mortality.1 Recent deep-learning approaches provide regional brain age estimates, enabling detection of spatially distributed aging patterns that reflect heterogeneity in neurostructural vulnerability.2,3 This spatial precision is critical for identifying intermediate phenotypes that may signal early susceptibility to neurodegenerative processes.

Plasma phosphorylated tau at threonine 217 (p-tau217) has emerged as a sensitive and minimally invasive biomarker of Alzheimer-related tau pathology. Although p-tau217 rises with age and predicts cognitive decline in clinical cohorts, its structural brain correlates in community-dwelling adults without cognitive diagnoses remain poorly understood. Aging itself produces widespread cortical change, making it unclear whether elevated p-tau217 corresponds to patterned deviations in brain aging that might influence cognitive performance.

Integrating plasma biomarkers with regional brain age phenotypes offers a mechanistic framework for identifying neural pathways that distinguish resilience from vulnerability. Here, we used a validated deep-learning model to derive regional brain age maps in adults aged 20–86 years without neurological or cognitive diagnoses. We examined whether p-tau217 was associated with cognition directly, or indirectly through expression of common spatial patterns of accelerated aging. We hypothesized that (1) higher p-tau217 would relate to poorer cognition, (2) expression of association-cortex aging patterns would correspond to lower cognitive performance, and (3) p-tau217 would relate to cognition indirectly via regional brain aging.

Methods

A total of 518 neurologically healthy, community-dwelling adults aged 20–86 years were included. All had T1-weighted MRI; 196 also had plasma p-tau217 and MoCA data. Participants were recruited through the University of South Carolina Aging Brain Cohort or drawn from a public neuroimaging repository to ensure broad age coverage. Standard 3T MPRAGE protocols were used. Exclusion criteria included prior stroke, neurodegenerative disease, severe medical or psychiatric illness, BMI > 42 kg/m², and non-native English for cognitive analyses.

Regional brain age was estimated using BrainStructureAges2, a validated deep learning pipeline that generates parcel-level age maps from T1-weighted MRI. The method uses an ensemble of 3D U-Net models trained to reproduce expert manual parcellations (BrainCOLOR protocol), segmenting each brain into 132 regions. A second U-Net ensemble predicts chronological age directly at the voxel level from raw T1 images, learning multiscale intensity-, shape-, and curvature-derived features without relying on predefined morphometric measures. Age predictions from overlapping subvolumes are averaged to produce a spatially continuous 3D age map, which is then upscaled and aggregated into regional brain age estimates. The model was trained and validated on thousands of MR images spanning ages 5–85 and includes a built-in bias-correction step that removes systematic age-related prediction bias. Brain age gaps (BAGs) were computed for all cortical regions.

To characterize common spatial patterns of brain aging, we applied principal axis factor analysis with varimax rotation to the cortical BAG correlation matrix for all 518 participants. Six orthogonal gradients were retained. Participant-level factor scores indexed expression of each aging pattern and were used in downstream models.

Plasma p-tau217 concentrations were measured in 196 participants using the Simoa ALZpath assay and log-transformed.

Cognitive performance was assessed with the MoCA. In participants ≥60 years, MoCA was predicted from the six brain age gradients to identify aging patterns most associated with cognition; two gradients were retained for mediation.

Linear regression tested associations between p-tau217, demographic variables, and cognition. Mediation models evaluated whether p-tau217 related to MoCA indirectly through expression of the two aging gradients, adjusting for age, sex, education, and dementia family history. Bias-corrected bootstrapping (10,000 iterations) provided confidence intervals. Exploratory analyses tested MoCA subdomains and repeated mediation including QRISK3 cardiovascular risk.

Results

Plasma p-tau217 increased with age and decreased with education (adj. R² = .139, p < .001). In the full plasma sample (N = 196), MoCA was significantly predicted by age (negative) and education (positive), but not by p-tau217. Among adults ≥60 years (N = 71), p-tau217 was not directly associated with MoCA.

Factor analysis revealed six reproducible brain age gradients explaining 95.1% of variance: frontal, dorsal, ventral, left frontotemporal, right frontotemporoparietal, and parietal. In older adults, two gradients, right frontotemporoparietal and bilateral parietal, were associated with lower MoCA.

Higher p-tau217 was associated with greater expression of the right frontotemporoparietal aging gradient (β = .259, p = .015). Both aging patterns were individually related to poorer cognition. Mediation analysis showed a significant indirect effect of p-tau217 on MoCA via right frontotemporoparietal aging (β = −.118, p = .008), consistent with full mediation, whereas the parietal pathway was nonsignificant. Exploratory models showed indirect effects on executive functioning, attention, and language. Results were unchanged when QRISK3 cardiovascular risk was included. No significant pathways were observed in adults aged 20–59.

Conclusion

In a large community-dwelling adult cohort without cognitive diagnoses, plasma p-tau217 was not directly associated with cognition but was linked to the expression of a specific accelerated aging pattern centered in right frontotemporoparietal regions. This aging pattern, in turn, was associated with lower cognitive performance, demonstrating that p-tau217 relates to cognition indirectly through regional structural aging rather than through global cognitive decline.

These findings identify brain age gradients as intermediate phenotypes linking peripheral tau-related pathology to cognitive function prior to overt impairment. They suggest that early vulnerability to tau-related processes may manifest as patterned deviations in neuroanatomical aging rather than generalized atrophy. Integrating plasma biomarkers with regional brain age phenotypes may provide a mechanistic framework for distinguishing individuals who remain resilient from those at elevated risk for cognitive decline. This approach may ultimately improve early detection and stratification in preclinical Alzheimer disease.
