Introduction

Malformations of cortical development cause lifelong disability and underlie neurodevelopmental disorders such as autism, cerebral palsy, and epilepsy. Because cortical folding patterns are highly heterogeneous, population-based normative models obscure subject-specific deviations, motivating frameworks that estimate an individual’s expected developmental trajectory from MRI. Cortical folding produces extreme, highly non-linear changes in the brain over a short period, which vary substantially across individuals and make realistic transformations difficult to predict. Physics-informed neural networks exploit biomechanical priors to disentangle phenotypic trends from natural variation: although folding patterns are heterogeneous, tissue mechanics are largely universal. Da Silva et al.1 propose regionally adaptable regularisation that imposes personalised deformations per individual and brain region. However, their growth is modelled as isotropic volumetric change derived from large segmentations, whereas cortical expansion is strongly anisotropic, limiting its ability to guide long-range folding. Therefore, adaptable regularisation directly on the cortical surface is required. Here we introduce NEUBORN+, a biomechanically-informed deformation framework for capturing the complex growth and folding involved in perinatal cortical development. We estimate personalised, spatially varying growth fields from anatomically constrained multimodal surface matching (aMSM),2 which regularises a cortical surface mesh and drive deformations of the surrounding volume. We evaluate NEUBORN+ on a longitudinal registration task in perinatal MRI by deforming a younger brain to its older follow-up. NEUBORN+ achieves biomechanically realistic deformations, with improved cortical alignment compared to learning-based baselines.

Methods

NEUBORN+ frames growth modelling as a learning problem: a volumetric U-Net3 takes as input two longitudinal scans, I1 and I2. To encode extreme changes in shape, the model learns multi-resolution fields ϕ = ϕ4 ◦ ϕ3 ◦ ϕ2 ◦ ϕ1 and, to avoid self-intersections, we model these as diffeomorphisms defined by stationary velocity fields. Spatially adaptive regularisation is applied to the moving mesh to up-weight cortical surface alignment and enforce a biomechanical model of growth. Brain tissue is modelled as a neo-Hookean hyperelastic material,4 whereby mechanical equilibrium after growth is found by minimising the strain energy of each triangle, t, in the mesh computed as in Equation 1 in Figure 1. Here, F ∈ ℝ2×2 represents the two-dimensional deformation gradient of each triangle of the transformed anatomical surface ϕ(S1), projected into the tangent plane to constrain deformations to the surface. The growth tensor Fg ∈ ℝ2×2 is the expected growth, computed as Fg = d−1/2I, where d is the area change of mesh faces, calculated following aMSM alignment. We minimise the elastic deviation Fe away from the prescribed growth Fg . The shear modulus μ (set to μ = 1) penalises distortions in shape, the bulk modulus κ (set to κ = 100μ) penalises changes in area, and Je = det(Fe) is the Jacobian determinant. To further upweight the importance of aligning the cortex, a mean squared error loss, ℒMSE (ϕ(S1), S2) is imposed between the deformed surface and the target S2, where vertex correspondence between source and target cortical meshes is known from aMSM. In addition to surface losses, NEUBORN+ incorporates a (mutual information) image matching term, ℒM I (ϕ(I1), I2), which measures the overlap of ϕ ◦ I1 and I2 while remaining robust to the rapid contrast changes that accompany perinatal development. A final diffusion-based regularisation loss, ℒsmooth, encourages smooth deformations by penalising the spatial gradients of the displacement field u = ϕ − I over N voxels. Our dataset consists of longitudinal T2-weighted MRI from 92 preterm neonates, where the first time point was acquired shortly after preterm birth and the second at term-equivalent age. Acquisition was performed on a 3T Philips Achieva scanner with a 32-channel neonatal head coil using a fast spin-echo sequence. Images were processed with the dHCP structural pipeline and linearly aligned using FSL’s FLIRT to remove global scaling. Subsequently, white and pial surfaces were extracted. A total of 64 subjects were allocated to the training set, 9 were reserved for hyperparameter tuning, and 19 were held out for testing.

Results

We demonstrate that the model deforms cortical anatomy in a manner consistent with biomechanical expectations by comparing vertices in key folds to their corresponding target vertices (Figure 2, B). The model flexibly deforms the cortex, with the predicted white-matter surface (inner boundary of the cortex) closely aligning with the target (Figure 2, A). The level of detail in the deformation (Figure 3, B), particularly around regions where entirely new folds emerge, is greatly increased relative to state-of-the-art methods (i.e. VoxelMorph5), as biomechanically expected growth guides the model on where folds should appear and which regions of tissue should grow.

Conclusion

A digital twin of the brain’s morphology is a highly coveted goal for both diagnostic and research purposes. However, a deformation model capable of capturing long-range, realistic deformations remains central to realising this technology. Here, we demonstrate a deformation framework that is accurate around the cortex while also deforming the whole brain volume, providing a foundation for forward modelling an individual’s brain. The framework is built on a biomechanical growth model that can impose personalised growth directly on the cortex as well as within the volume, enabling future work on personalised normative models. Such personalised expected trajectories could anticipate malformations and forecast clinical outcomes, while deviations from the expected trajectory could serve as biomarkers of malformations of cortical development.
