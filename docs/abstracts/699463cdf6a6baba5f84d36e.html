Introduction

Characterizing the development of cortical folding patterns during the critical prenatal period is challenging but essential to detect early abnormal neurodevelopmental disorders including epilepsy, schizophrenia and autism spectrum disorders.1 While fetal MRI enables direct observation of gyrification as it progresses, technical limitations including fetal motion and rapidly changing anatomy have constrained most studies to qualitative assessments. Previous works have reported that cortical folds emerge in successive waves following a hierarchical organization: primary folds (major anatomical landmarks), secondary folds (intermediate structures), and tertiary folds (finest convolutions), each with distinct developmental timing and clinical significance.1 Precise quantitative characterization of their evolution has been quantified in preterms,2 however, their precise description throughout pregnancy remains to be established.

Methods

We establish normative models of developmental trajectories of cortical folding patterns using Spectral Analysis of Gyrification (SPANGY)3 applied to multi-center fetal brain MRI data, enabling quantitative assessment of cortical folding at multiple spatial scales across gestational age. As detailed in3 SPANGY decomposes the mean curvature of cortical surface meshes onto eigenfunction bases, partitioning the resulting spectral components into seven frequency bands (B0-B6), of which bands B4-B6 capture 92.8% of folding-related structures.3 We applied SPANGY to 508 fetal MRI scans from two independent imaging centers spanning 20-39 weeks gestational age. 216 from MarsFet dataset (dataset acquired in La Timone hospital in Marseille, France), 292 from dHCP dataset (Open source dataset provided by the Developing Human Connectome Project). Ethical approval was obtained from all centers (La Timone: Aix-Marseille University N°2022-04-14-003). We then performed multicentric data harmonization using Combat GAM to account for site effects and scanner related artifacts in each dataset.4 We developed normative models5 for each fold frequency (B4, B5 and B6), characterizing typical developmental trajectories and inter-individual variability across the complete prenatal period.

Results

Normative models quantified specific growth patterns for each spectral band, capturing developmental trajectories, inter-individual variability, and 95% confidence intervals across gestational age. Spectral power initially concentrates in band B4, which predominates from 21 to 32 weeks with an inflection point around 26wGA and a linear growth pattern until 39wGA. Band B5 shows a superlinear expansion with accelerated growth rates starting around 32wGA, overtaking B4 to become the most prevalent band through the term. Band B6 exhibits exponential growth from 34 weeks through the remainder of gestation. Multi-center validation demonstrated robust generalizability of SPANGY metrics and normative references across diverse scanning environments and populations. These models establish reference curves that define expected folding development for each band, providing a quantitative basis for assessing individual deviations from typical developmental patterns (see fig.2). 

As illustrated in fig.1, SPANGY's frequency-based decomposition naturally aligns with successive waves described in folding theory. Note however that the SPANGY decomposition enables us to distinguish the subparts within each fold contributing to the three frequency bands (B4, B5, B6), which provides a different perspective compared to the historical classification of sulci as primary, secondary or tertiary.1 This distinction becomes evident when examining the evolution with age for specific sulci such as the central sulcus, the superior temporal sulcus, or the inferior frontal sulcus in fig.1. SPANGY thus allows for a more detailed analysis that could be instrumental for better detection and characterization of cortical malformations.

Conclusion

This study represents the first application of SPANGY to a large multicentric dataset of fetal brain imaging, establishing for the first time normative models for the development throughout pregnancy of three frequency bands explaining more than 95% of the gyrification. By quantifying trajectories across two independent datasets, we provide a validated framework for detecting frequency-specific folding dynamics.
