Introduction

Understanding collective neuronal dynamics is important for computational neuroscience. Detailed spiking neuron models capture biophysical mechanisms at the cost of scalability and direct links to macroscopic signals such as EEG or fMRI. Mean-field models address this by reducing large networks to population-level variables, enabling the study of large-scale dynamics. Phenomenological Mean field models such as the Epileptor^1 have been highly successful in reproducing key brain dynamics, but do not provide information on underlying biophysical mechanisms.

To address this, Rabuffo et al.^2 developed a biophysically inspired mean-field model derived from a Hodgkin–Huxley type neuron with explicit potassium ion dynamics^3. Being able to link ion dynamics to large-scale brain activity, this model provides insight into how slow ionic processes shape excitability and pathological activity. While parameter regimes associated with epileptic dynamics have been identified, this research aims to extend this framework to reproduce resting-state whole-brain activity.

Here, we systematically explore the dynamical repertoire of the biophysically inspired mean-field model introduced by Rabuffo et al.^2. To complement the mean-field analysis, we simulate the corresponding spiking neural network version of the model,

allowing us to simultaneously evaluate dynamics at the macroscopic level and the underlying SNN. Moreover, we demonstrate the use of inference to uncover underlying parameters based on large-scale activity alone. Finally, the model has been extended to a neural field model from one node to simulate whole-brain high-resolution brain activity.

Being able to map macroscopic brain activity and its dynamic trajectories from health to pathology to underlying biophysical mechanisms is key to understanding disease  pathways and revealing novel treatment. This work is a first step towards a biophysically interpretable model of large-scale brain activity.

Methods

The biophysically inspired mean-field model we use is based on the reduction introduced by Rabuffo et al.^2, which applies the Ott–Antonsen reduction framework to a Hodgkin–Huxley-type neuron with slow ionic dynamics. The single-neuron system is divided into fast variables like membrane potential and potassium conductance gating variable and slow variables like intracellular potassium concentration variation, and extracellular potassium buffering. In the population limit, the voltage distribution is approximated by a Lorentzian ansatz, and the cubic membrane dynamics are represented through a piecewise-quadratic form. Moment-closure assumptions yield a five-dimensional mean-field model that retains explicit ionic currents, sodium–potassium pump dynamics, and synaptic coupling. Full model equations and parameters follow Rabuffo et al.^2 and Depannemaecker et al.^3.

To characterize the model’s dynamical repertoire, we performed a two-dimensional sweep across external potassium bath concentration (K_bath) and synaptic strength (J). For each parameter pair, the mean-field model was simulated for 12 seconds. Voltage traces were processed using simple feature metrics (variance, spike count, inter spike interval (ISI)), and regimes were assigned to qualitative categories (e.g., tonic firing, bursting, seizure-like activity). These classifications were then verified by running the SNN simulation build using the single neuron model. We used a network of 3000 neurons, all-to-all coupled, to validate the results. Synaptic coupling was scaled accordingly for SNN and all other parameters are single neuron level description, hence identical. Measures like Spike Synchrony, coefficient of variation of ISI[MC3]  and mean firing rate were calculated using the raster output from the simulation. 

We used simulation-based inference (SBI)^4 to assess which biophysical parameters can be recovered from voltage dynamics. SBI was implemented via Sequential Neural Posterior Estimation using the sbi toolbox. Uniform priors were defined over physiologically plausible ranges of K_bath and J. From each simulated trace, we extracted handcrafted summary statistics capturing baseline behavior, stimulation response, and post-stimulus activity (mean/variance features, spike counts, spike timing, and ISI-based descriptors). The neural density estimator was trained on batches of simulated data across multiple rounds, yielding approximate posteriors for each parameter.

Finally, to explore the model’s applicability to large-scale brain dynamics, we embedded the mean-field model into a 20,000-node high-resolution structural connectome^5. Connectivity weights and delays were taken from a standard distance-dependent SC matrix of a particular individual. This prototype implementation allows us to test seizure initiation and spreading under the influence of local and global connectivity forming the basis for future virtual-epilepsy-twin applications.

Results

Using the parameter sweep over K_bath and J, and based on the features we can divide the 2-D plane into distinct behavioral regions using threshold-based criteria on voltage variance, peak counts, and simple adaptation metrics. Here, we have shown two example voltage traces for selected points. We classified the observed behaviors into seven categories: 1) depolarized, unresponsive activity; 2) sustained ictal-like oscillations with large amplitude; 3) tonic firing; 4) bursting activity; 5) asynchronous irregular firing; 6) seizure-like activity; and 7) sustained low-amplitude ictal-like oscillations. This mapping provides a structured overview of how membrane potential dynamics vary with the two parameters. In Figure 2, we have shown bursting activity in the top row and sustained low-amplitude ictal-like oscillations in the bottom row.

Similar patterns were observed at the level of the corresponding SNN. Also, these patterns have been obtained using SNN simulation and we have provided the following raster plot in Figure 2 (b) and (e) to show the correspondence. 

Using one example, we further demonstrated the possibility to use this model in a high-resolution whole-brain modeling framework to generate large-scale brain dynamics (Shown in Figure 1 (c)).

To assess which model parameters can be reliably identified from mean field level voltage dynamics, we applied Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) to all eight  parameters (potassium bath concentration, synaptic coupling, membrane capacitance, gating time constants, mean and width of heterogeneity distribution, diffusion factor, conversion factor) using a set of summary statistics involving mean and variance of the membrane potential, spike count, interspike interval. Sensitivity analysis revealed a clear hierarchy: K_bath and J showed high normalized sensitivity, while parameters such as diffusion factor, and conversion factor had negligible influence on the posterior. Correlation analysis further indicated compensatory interactions among several parameters, including mean of lorentzian, membrane capacitance, and gating time constants, reflecting degeneracy typical of biophysical models.

Guided by these findings, we repeated the inference using only K_bath and J. In this reduced space, SBI yielded tightly concentrated posterior distributions centered around ground-truth values, demonstrating robust recoverability when restricting inference to the most informative parameters. For the above mentioned two examples, we have shown well that the posterior distribution captured the true parameter using violin plot in Figure 2 (c) and (f).

Conclusion

In this work, we used a biophysical mean field model which provides an interpretable link between cellular mechanisms and large-scale brain dynamics to simulate activity at the scale of spiking-networks, neural mass and whole-brain dynamics. Our work is a comprehensive assessment of a mean-field model that takes into account biophysically known ionic mechanisms which might help to shed light on dynamical principles governing health and disease. 

The sweep over the K_bath-J plane illustrates how network excitability is a function of extracellular potassium concentration and coupling. In this case, a model can switch in a natural way between spiking, bursting, and oscillations characteristic of a seizure. Note that these transitions have very good correspondence with those in a spiking neural network consisting of single neuron models.

The second goal of this work is to examine if the information available in voltage dynamics is sufficient for constraining biophysical parameters using simulation-based inference. The results are encouraging where SBI successfully recovered two relevant parameters K_bath and J, over a broad set of conditions. Other parameters, in contrast, were linked with high uncertainty and correlations, which represent a degeneracy in parameter space.

Lastly, we implemented our model to a 20,000-node high-resolution whole-brain network as a prototype application. This is the first application of biophysical model at such a high resolution, which, in terms of exploring the disease of epilepsy, is a very promising direction because now we can examine the effect of potassium buffering the onset of seizure activity, how the epileptogenic activity spreads along the both global and local connections, and how focal perturbations, such as stimulation or pharmacological, might maintain the network into a stable state.
