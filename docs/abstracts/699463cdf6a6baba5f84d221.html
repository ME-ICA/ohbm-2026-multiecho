Introduction

Adolescence is known to be a crucial development period for future adult mental health. During this very sensitive window, the interplay between environment and genetics informs behavioral development. Disruption of this process, either because of adverse experiences or genetic vulnerability, can lead to the emergence of problematic behaviors such as depression or conduct disorders. These precursors to adult mental illness manifest themselves in adolescence as internalizing and externalizing behaviors. Previous research has shown that these behaviors are associated with some neuronal changes, few consider how the dual developmental trajectories of comorbid problematic behaviors may be associated with neuronal development during adolescents.

Methods

A parallel process latent class growth (ppLCGA) model, leveraging the longitudinal cohort (N=11k, 4 timepoints, aged 9-12) of the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study, was used to model the dual developmental trajectories of internalizing and externalizing behaviors. Scores for these behaviors from subjects were used for the observations across time, and level of ACEs, four PRS principle components analysis (PCA) measures, site, sex, race, and area deprivation were included as time-invariant predictors for the trajectory models. Dual linear and quadratic trajectories for one to five classes were compared, with best model fit determined by bayesian information criteria, high entropy values, approximate likelihood-ratio tests, and meaningful trajectories. Fractional anisotropy (FA) measures were calculated from subjects’ diffusion images (dMRI) and included in an independent component analysis (ICA) to extract 100 components.  Subject loadings (N=9644 subjects) from these components were included in a multivariate analysis of covariance (MANCOVA) to explore differences in FA based on ppLCGA group membership. FA regions significant in the MANCOVA were included in a repeated measures linear mixed effects model along with site, sex, and adi.

Results

The fit indices showed a 4-class model best fit the dual trajectories in this cohort. Adolescents were classified into the following subgroups: Low Level Behaviors (86%), Increasing Behaviors (4%), Decreasing Behaviors (5%), and High Level Behaviors (5%). The MANCOVA results revealed significant group differences (f = 1.32, p<0.0002) for 16 FA components. Of these, five regions (thalamus, anterior thalamic radiation, corticospinal tract, and superior longitudinal fasiculus (slf) 2) had significant longitudinal group differences in the linear mixed-effects models and three had significant group:sex interactions (slf 1 and 3, and arcuate fasiculus) for subjects with persistently high or increasing problematic behaviors.

Conclusion

While a large percentage of this cohort exhibit low level behaviors across early adolescence, the subjects who experienced high or increasing levels of problematic behaviors had significant differences in their neuronal maturation compared to their peers. Altered maturation was seen in regions of the brain responsible for salience attribution and emotional processing, suggesting the differences in behavior when compared to their peers could be driven by misattribution or dysregulation in response to their environment. These biomarkers, in conjunction with the observed behaviors, could prove critical as treatment targets.
