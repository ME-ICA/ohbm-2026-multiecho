Introduction

Background:

KBG syndrome (MIM #148050) is a rare neurodevelopmental disorder caused by pathogenic variants in ANKRD11 gene. The clinical phenotype includes global developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder (ASD), attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, variable intellectual disability, and distinctive craniofacial and skeletal anomalies. Intracranial cysts, particularly posterior fossa arachnoid cysts (PFACs), have been reported in KBG syndrome and in other ASD-related genetic conditions such as ARID1B and FOXP1. Although often incidental, PFACs may influence neurocognitive function, and a few reports suggest improvement after surgical decompression.

Here, we report a 10-year longitudinal follow-up combining combining neuropsychological assessment, social functioning, ASL perfusion imaging, resting-state functional MRI, and diffusion MRI in a male patient with KBG syndrome and severe ASD diagnosis. We explore the neural mechanisms underlying long-term improvement.

Methods

Methods:

The patient, a right-handed male carrying KBG syndrome and important ASD symptoms, presented a PFAC and underwent cyst drainage at age 12. Neuropsychological assessment and multimodal brain MRI, including areterial spin labeling (ASL) measures of regional cerebral blood flow (rCBF) at rest, were performed before surgery, one year after surgery and ten years after surgery. At the ten years timepoint resting-state fMRI and diffusion weighted imaging were also obtained.

We performed whole brain voxel-wise analysis on ASL-MRI images, comparing our patient to 8 healthy age matched controls at each time point (before, one year and ten year after surgery) using the linear model within SPM12. We also performed advanced post-operative analyses using resting-state fMRI and diffusion weighted imaging at the ten years time point comparing our patient to twelve healthy age-matched controls.

Results

Results:

Pre-operative ASL analysis comparing our patient to controls revealed significantly reduced rCBF at rest (p < 0.05, FWE corrected) localized in the left superior temporal sulcus (STS), a key region for social cognition. One year after drainage, post-operative ASL analysis indicated normalization of rCBF in the STS (no significant differences compared to controls). Neuropsychological testing at this time showed normalization of IQ scores (+2 SD verbal index), improved executive functions (planning +3 SD, emotional regulation +1 SD), and verbal memory (+3 SD). Social behaviors also improved, with better eye contact and reduced ASD symptom severity (CARS scores improved 10 points).

At the ten-year follow-up, cognitive gains were sustained. The patient presented average IQ, superior verbal abilities, improved executive and social functioning, and reduced autistic traits (CARS improved another 4.5 points). Particularly, rCBF at rest in the STS remained not significantly different from controls. Exploratory resting-state fMRI analyses revealed strengthened STS functional connectivity with temporal networks. Likewise, exploratory diffusion imaging analysis showed increased fiber density and bundle cross-section in the left temporal lobe and corticospinal tract, consistent with compensatory neuroplasticity, though not surviving correction for multiple comparisons.

Conclusion

Conclusion:

This case provides the first long-term evidence of cognitive and social improvement associated with reversible temporal hypoperfusion following PFAC drainage in KBG syndrome with ASD. The convergence of neuropsychological, perfusion, and connectivity findings suggests functional reorganization of temporal networks after decompression. Although causality cannot be firmly established, these results highlight the potential contribution of posterior fossa pathology to neurodevelopmental outcomes and support further investigation into targeted surgical and rehabilitation strategies in genetically defined ASD.
