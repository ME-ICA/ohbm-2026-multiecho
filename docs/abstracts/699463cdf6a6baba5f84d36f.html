Introduction

Introduction

Individuals with 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (22q11DS) exhibit a markedly increased risk for psychosis, yet the developmental neural mechanisms distinguishing resilient from vulnerable trajectories remain unclear.1 Functional connectome “fingerprinting”, which quantifies how uniquely reproducible an individual’s functional connectivity (FC) profile is across time, provides a powerful framework for characterizing maturation of brain network identity and stabilty.2 Typical development progresses from highly flexible adolescent connectivity toward increasingly stable, hierarchically integrated networks in adulthood. Whether this maturation of network stability is altered in 22q11DS, and whether such alterations relate to the emergence of positive psychotic symptoms, remains unknown.3 Here, we combined longitudinal connectome fingerprinting, within-subject edgewise stability (ICC), and a novel joint FC–ICC principal component decomposition to identify developmental trajectories of large-scale network organization in 22q11DS. We asked whether individuals with and without positive psychotic symptoms follow distinct maturational trajectories, and whether alterations in how functional networks stabilize and reorganize across development are linked to emerging psychotic symptoms.

Methods

Methods

Participants. Data included 111 individuals: 56 with a confirmed 22q11.2DS diagnosis (28 females, age 8.1–34.0) and 55 healthy controls (30 females, age 7.4–33.2). Participants completed 2–5 longitudinal visits, yielding 307 scans (160 in 22q11.2DS, 147 in controls). The study was approved by the cantonal ethics committee and was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki.

Clinical Assessment. Psychotic-symptom stratification used the Structured Interview for Prodromal Symptoms (SIPS), including Unusual Thought Content (P1), Suspiciousness (P2), Grandiose Ideas (P3), Hallucinations (P4), and Disorganized Communication (P5). Five individuals were excluded due to missing data or age below the validated range. Among the remaining 51 22q11DS participants, PPS+ status was assigned if any positive-symptom item scored ≥3 at any visit; otherwise participants were labeled PPS–. This yielded PPS+ = 40 (101 scans) and PPS– = 17 (47 scans).

MRI Acquisition and Preprocessing. Resting-state fMRI was acquired on a 3T Siemens Trio (n=220) and 3T Siemens Prisma-fit (n=87). Preprocessing used SPM12 and DPARSF libraries. Functional images were realigned, coregistered, and smoothed (6 mm FWHM). Structural images were segmented and normalized via a study-specific DARTEL template. Time series were extracted from 232 regions (200 cortical + 16 subcortical), ^4 resliced to fMRI resolution. Nuisance regression included 12 motion parameters plus white-matter and CSF signals. The first five volumes were removed, data were band-pass filtered (0.01–0.1 Hz), and motion scrubbing applied at FD > 0.5 mm (removing the flagged frame, the previous one, and next two). Regional time courses were z-scored, and Pearson correlations yielded a 232×232 FC matrix per scan.

Longitudinal Connectome Fingerprinting. Individual dentifiability was quantified as the correlation between each participant’s FC across successive visits (Iself) and contrasted with correlations to age-matched others (Iothers). Prediction accuracy (PredAcc) reflects the proportion of cases in which Iself exceeded corresponding Iothers across subjects and within windows.

Edgewise stability (ICC). Similar to fingerprinting, we computed the edgewise ICCs of the FCs from subjects’ consecutive visits that fell within the same window. 

Moving-Average-Window Framework. Ages were divided into overlapping windows (5-year width, 1-year step). Within each window we collected Iself, Iothers, PredAcc, and ICC, enabling age-resolved trajectories. Group differences (controls, PPS–, PPS+) were tested using linear mixed-effects models with PredAcc or ICC as dependent variables and group, age, age², and group-by-age interactions as predictors.

Joint FC–ICC decomposition. To identify shared spatial axes of developmental change, FC and ICC matrices from each window were concatenated and submitted to PCA. The first three components were retained; FC and ICC loadings were mapped back onto networks, and component scores were analyzed longitudinally.

Results

Results

Fingerprinting Reveals Altered Maturation in 22q11. Controls showed relatively low but stable PredAcc across adolescence, with a mild decrease peaking near age 18 followed by a gradual adult increase (Figure 2A). In contrast, both deletion groups exhibited overall higher PredAcc throughout development. PPS(–) demonstrated a initial high start, a large decrease with a nadir around age 20 and a subsequent increase, while PPS(+) expressed a similar but more flattened shape. Pairwise comparisons confirmed significant group differences between Controls vs PPS(–) and Controls vs PPS(+) (Tukey-adjusted p < .0001), with no difference between PPS groups (p = 0.27).

ICC Corroborates Reduced Maturational Stability in Deletion Carriers. Dynamic stability of functional connectivity (ICC) largely paralleled the PredAcc trajectories (Figure 2B). Controls showed an increasing profile occurring slightly earlier than the PredAcc trajectory. PPS(–) showed a similar shape as the PredAcc curve, whereas PPS(+) exhibited a flatter trajectory with reduced age-related variation. The Control vs PPS(–) contrast remained significant after correction (p < .0001); Control vs PPS(+) was not (p = 0.51). PPS groups differed reliably (p = .0017).

Principal Components. To elucidate the Fingerprinting and ICC results a principal component analysis was conducted on FC and ICC matrices jointly across all participants and time points (variance explained: 46.9%). Loadings were decomposed into FC and ICC contributions, focusing on the first three distinct maturation dimensions (Figure 2E). These components captured distinct but complementary dimensions of developmental organization: PC1 Sensory-Association Axis (Global hierarchical integration), PC2 Emotional-Cognitive Balance (Aﬀective-Sensory Integration) and PC3 Executive-Sensory Control (Fronto-Striatal-Sensory Integration).

Typical development. Controls showed reduced identifiability during adolescence followed by a steady increase into adulthood. PC1 scores increased with age, reflecting consolidation of hierarchical integration. PC2 shifted from affective-sensorimotor dominance toward balanced affective-cognitive coordination. PC3 remained stably positive, reflecting efficient executive-sensory coordination.

22q11DS without positive psychotic symptoms (PPS–). PPS– participants began with abnormally high identifiability, suggesting premature stabilization, followed by a pronounced adolescent decrease and partial adult recovery. Their PC1 trajectory showed an initial decline with partial restoration of hierarchical stability; PC3 increased with age, suggesting strengthening of executive-sensory integration. 

22q11DS with positive psychotic symptoms (PPS+). PPS+ individuals showed no adolescent reorganisation and minimal improvement with age. Their trajectory in PC1-PC2 space was irregular and non-directional, lacking coherent hierarchical or emotional-cognitive maturation. Critically, PC3 became progressively negative, reflecting disorganised executive influence and unstable sensory-attention coupling. This deviates sharply from both HC and PPS– groups.

Group dissociation. Integrating all three components in three orthogonal axes revealed distinct developmental gradients. Controls followed a rather smooth and directed pathway. PPS(–) diverged globally but directed (PC1 and PC2) and maintained some hierarchical recovery (PC3). PPS(+) lacked a coherent maturational direction (PC1-PC2) and showed a hierarchical worsening (PC3) along development.

Conclusion

Conclusion. This study provides a multiscale characterisation of functional connectome maturation in 22q11DS, revealing two distinct developmental deviations. PPS– individuals undergo delayed but pronounced compensatory reorganisation, partially restoring hierarchical and executive-sensory integration. In contrast, PPS+ individuals exhibit fundamentally irregular and non-directional developmental trajectories, failing to engage adolescent reorganisation and showing progressive disruption of top-down coordination.

By integrating FC strength and temporal stability into a shared PCA framework, we uncover separable axes of developmental maturation and identify combinations of disrupted hierarchical, affective-cognitive, and executive-sensory alignment that might distinguish vulnerability from resilience. These results suggest that psychosis risk in 22q11DS may reflect disrupted developmental sequencing rather than a simple delay or attenuation of maturation. Such instability may point toward a systemic dyscoordination of the neurodevelopmental milieu, reflecting a loss of developmental timing, potentially linked to disruptions in the synaptic pruning, myelination, interneuron maturation, and neuroimmune processes previously implicated in psychosis.4,5 More broadly, our approach demonstrates how functional fingerprinting can move beyond identifiability toward a dimensional neurodevelopmental model linking maturation trajectories to emerging clinical outcomes.
