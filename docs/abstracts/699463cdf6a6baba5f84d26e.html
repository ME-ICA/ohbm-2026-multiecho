Introduction

Cholinergic degeneration is a hallmark feature of Lewy body disease, including Parkinson’s disease (PD) and dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB). It occurs early in the course of both PD and DLB and is related to and predictive of cognitive decline. Most previous studies have focused on analyzing volumetric changes in the cholinergic basal forebrain or cortical PET markers of cholinergic activity. Less is known about the integrity of the main cholinergic white matter pathways, especially in prodromal disease stages. Isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) constitutes a remarkably specific risk factor for Lewy body disease with most iRBD patients eventually developing an overt alpha-synucleinopathy in the form of PD or DLB (and less commonly multiple system atrophy). However, time to phenoconversion can vary greatly between individuals and there is an increasing interest in developing markers of conversion risk for more accurate prognosis and, eventually, stratification of patients for clinical trials. In this study, we present a comprehensive analysis of cholinergic white matter tracts, including pathways from the basal forebrain to the cortex as well as pedunculopontine-thalamic projections, to characterize cholinergic pathway integrity at the prodromal stage and investigate how it relates to phenoconversion.

Methods

Data were obtained from the Parkinson’s Progression Markers Initiative (https://www.ppmi-info.org), including 146 iRBD patients and 102 controls. We used T1-weighted MR images to estimate basal forebrain volume and DTI to study the integrity of three main cholinergic pathways: lateral and medial pathways from the nucleus basalis of Meynert (NBM) to the cortex and cholinergic pathways from the pedunculopontine nucleus (PPN) to the thalamus.
All statistical analyses were performed in a Bayesian framework using R. We report posterior distributions of the parameter estimates (PE) with the median and 95% credible intervals.
Basal forebrain volumes and DTI metrics along the white matter pathways were compared between the groups using Bayesian linear mixed models with fixed effects for diagnosis, hemisphere, diagnosis*hemisphere interaction, and covariates for age, sex, and education. The mean DTI metric from a white matter control mask was included as an additional covariate in analyses of DTI metrics.
Associations between imaging metrics and cognitive scores were investigated within the iRBD group using Bayesian linear mixed models with an additional covariate for disease duration.
In the iRBD group, to examine associations between cholinergic imaging metrics at baseline and time to conversion to PD, we used Bayesian Cox proportional hazards models with right-censoring for participants who had not converted at the last follow-up visit, including fixed effects for imaging metric, hemisphere, imaging metric*hemisphere interaction, and covariates for age, sex, years of education, disease duration and DTI metrics from the white matter control mask.

Results

The iRBD and control groups were similar in age and years of education. The iRBD group had a higher proportion of male participants compared to the control group and slightly increased UPDRS motor scores at baseline. During follow-up, 12 of the iRBD patients converted to PD and one patient converted to DLB after a median time of 1.3 years (range: 0.3 - 3.2 years). Due to the low number of DLB converters, we combined PD and DLB for the phenoconversion analysis.
Fractional anisotropy along the PPN-thalamus pathway was significantly reduced in iRBD vs. controls (PE: median=-0.48, 95% CI=[-0.73,-0.22]) with a diagnosis*hemisphere interaction (PE: median=0.25, 95% CI=[0.03,0.47]), indicating that the reduction was stronger for the left hemisphere. No group differences were found for the DTI metrics along the NBM-cortical pathways or basal forebrain volume (all credible intervals overlapping with zero). Within the iRBD group, higher integrity of the NBM pathways was associated with better performance on a cognitive test of executive function/processing speed at baseline.
In iRBD, increased mean diffusivity along PPN-thalamic projections was related to an increased risk of future phenoconversion to an overt α-synucleinopathy (hazard ratio=2.0, 95% CI= [1.3,3.3]). No associations with phenoconversion were found for the DTI metrics along the NBM-cortical pathways or basal forebrain volume.

Conclusion

This study offers new insights into early alterations within the complex structure of the cholinergic system in Lewy body disease, highlighting that the cholinergic projections from the brainstem to the thalamus may be affected even earlier than the basal forebrain cholinergic system and might serve as a sensitive marker for increased risk of phenoconversion in iRBD.
