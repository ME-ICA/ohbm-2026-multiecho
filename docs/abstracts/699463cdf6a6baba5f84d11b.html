Introduction

The corpus callosum (CC) is the largest white-matter (WM) structure in the human brain and plays a central role in interhemispheric communication, as well as in cognitive processes such as memory and olfaction. Alterations of the CC have been linked to a range of neurological and psychiatric disorders, including epilepsy, autism, and schizophrenia. Although the CC has distinct boundaries in the sagittal plane, it lacks clearly defined borders with WM of the left and right hemispheres. Consequently, most structural MRI studies restrict CC segmentation to predefined midsagittal slices. Existing work frequently addresses head-pose normalization and segmentation independently1, which can lead to error propagation and biased measurements of CC size and shape. Moreover, local morphological variations are associated with different diseases and remain of broad research interest, indicating the importance of robust and sensitive tools that capture both global and regional CC changes. To meet this need, we present a comprehensive CC analysis pipeline2 that (i) determines the mid-sagittal plane for CC analysis, (ii) localizes the anterior and posterior commissures (AC and PC), (iii) segments CC and fornix, (iv) computes morphometric features including area, length, curvature, and thickness, and (v) performs geometric sub-segmentation (Fig. 1).

Methods

We develop and evaluate our methods on a dataset of 280 T1-weighted brain MRIs from 7T, 3T, and 1.5T scanners of the HCP, ADNI, OASIS, ABIDE-II, MIRIAD, la5c, Rhineland-, and in-house studies. Additionally, we use a subset of the PREDICT-HD dataset (predict Huntington’s disease, 12686 cases) for evaluation. Our method determines the mid-sagittal plane based on a centroid registration of FastSurfer’s  cortical segmentation labels to Talairach space. On the mid-sagittal plane and adjacent slices, two dedicated deep learning models determine the location of AC & PC and then segment the CC. Combining the CC and AC & PC information, our method derives an arbitrary number of corpus callosum thickness estimates along the anterior-posterior axes, subdivides the CC using a novel geometry-aware subdivision scheme, and produces six shape descriptors. We evaluate our method by comparing it against the state-of-the-art3,4 5 in:

  1. A rater comparison of mid-sagittal planes.

  2. Mean and median localization distances for randomly sampled and challenging cases.

  3. Segmentation performance on randomly sampled and challenging cases, measured by the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) and Hausdorff distance.

  4. Statistical power in a group comparison of Huntington’s disease patients and controls.

  5. Test-retest reliability using paired scans captured during the same scan session for both Huntington's disease patients and controls.

Results

Our method outperforms state-of-the-art methods across the board. We show, for example, the segmentation performance in Fig. 2a, where FastSurfer-CC not only demonstrates high accuracy (=high DSC scores) but is also least affected when moving from randomly sampled cases (Fig. 2a top) to challenging cases (Fig. 2a bottom). In Fig. 2b, we demonstrate the power of the whole pipeline in finding thickness differences between Huntington’s disease patients and controls. Our method outperforms the only other analysis tool capable of generating these granular local difference maps (CCseg4) by finding more differences at higher significance. Finally, the metrics derived by our method show high test-retest reliability, as indicated by high intraclass correlation coefficients (ICC) shown in Fig. 2c. Estimates of thickness, area, and circularity are close to one, indicating nearly perfect agreement across scans.

Conclusion

We introduce FastSurfer-CC2, a tool for comprehensive morphometric CC analysis that demonstrates robustness, reliability, and sensitivity across our experiments. The tool rapidly produces local thickness estimates and corpus callosum shape descriptors across multiple mid-sagittal slices. By localizing the AC and PC, it additionally provides an anatomically guided brain orientation that is consistent with common standardization procedures such as rigid Talairach alignment. FastSurfer-CC is being integrated into the FastSurfer toolbox (https://github.com/Deep-MI/FastSurfer), enabling broad and seamless use within standard structural MRI processing workflows.
