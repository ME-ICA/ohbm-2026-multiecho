Introduction

Frontotemporal degeneration (FTD) is widely recognized as a progressive neurodegenerative disease. However, converging evidence hints at neurodevelopmental effects in genetic forms of FTD. The three most common genetic groups where autosomal dominant variants cause FTD include C9orf72, GRN, and MAPT, which are active during typical neurodevelopmental processes. A recent study of young adult FTD variant carriers showed differences in brain volumes and better executive function compared to familial non-variant carriers.1 However, longitudinal trajectories may more sensitively capture FTD-related neurodevelopmental vs. neurodegenerative changes than cross-sectional approaches. This study thus aimed to examine longitudinal trajectories of brain volumes, executive function, and plasma biomarkers in young adult carriers compared to familial non-carriers, as measures of neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative outcomes of FTD-causing variants.

Methods

This longitudinal cohort study comprised participants aged 18-30 years from the FTD Prevention Initiative, with sites across Europe, Canada, and the USA (Table 1). Genetic groups included C9orf72 (47%), MAPT (30%), and GRN (23%). Participants underwent T1-weighted MRI, neuropsychological testing, and blood draws for plasma biomarker collection and genotyping. Preprocessing for T1-weighted scans was conducted in FreeSurfer v7.4.1,2 and included N3 bias-field correction and intensity normalization, skull stripping using watershed segmentation, affine Talairach registration, white matter and pial surface reconstruction with spherical surface registration, cortical parcellation using the Desikan-Killiany atlas, and subcortical volumetric segmentation using FreeSurfer’s probabilistic atlas. Regions of interest for all genetic groups included volumes of the total brain, medial orbitofrontal cortex, and frontal pole. Additional regions of interest differed by genetic group: C9orf72 included the thalamus, insula, and rostral anterior cingulate cortex; GRN included the insula, rostral anterior cingulate cortex, and putamen; and MAPT included the medial anterior temporal lobe, encompassing the hippocampus, amygdala, parahippocampal and entorhinal cortices, and temporal pole. An executive function composite score3 was calculated from these measures: animal fluency, letter F verbal fluency, number span backward, and trail making tests-A and -B. Linear mixed-effects models were computed to assess longitudinal outcomes between groups. Fixed effects included a group by age interaction, sex, scanner (brain volume models), and education (executive function models). Outcomes included brain volumes, executive function composite scores, and plasma biomarkers of neurodegeneration via NfL and GFAP levels. Random effects accounted for between-subject variability nested within family membership. Models were computed per outcome and genetic group.

Results

Variant carriers (n=147) and familial non-carriers (n=113) did not differ in age (mean±SD, 25.9±3.2 years), sex (55% female), or number of visits (2.1±1.7). In C9orf72, there was a significant effect of age on medial orbitofrontal cortex volume trajectories, which differed between repeat expansion carriers and familial non-carriers with small-to-medium effect size (b=-126.4, SE=61.9, p=0.046, f2=0.07). C9orf72 non-carriers showed expected age-related declines (-116.2 mm3/year, 95% CI [-219.6, -12.7], whereas carriers showed relatively stable trajectories between 18 to 30 years old (+10.1 mm3/year, 95% CI [-53.4, 73.6]) (Fig. 1a). Compared to non-carriers, C9orf72 carriers also had smaller average thalamic volumes with large effect size (b=-1145.9, SE=337.3, p=0.0023, f2=0.46) (Fig. 1b), and smaller average total brain volumes with medium effect size (b=-40336.1, SE=19370.2, p=0.047, f2=0.17) (Fig. 1c), though with relatively stable trajectories between 18 to 30 years and no significant group x age interactions. Young adult GRN mutation carriers had larger rostral anterior cingulate cortex volumes than non-carriers with large effect size (b=-471.8, SE=206.8, p=0.040, f2=0.40) and relatively stable trajectories between 18 to 30 years. For MAPT, there were no conclusive differences in total brain, cortical, or subcortical volumes between groups or over time. No longitudinal differences were observed between carriers and non-carriers in executive function, or plasma NfL or GFAP for any genetic group.

Conclusion

C9orf72 and GRN variants are linked to brain volume differences that are stable and persist across 18 to 30 years old, suggestive of neurodevelopmental origins. The absence of longitudinal differences in plasma NfL and GFAP in young adult mutation carriers may also reflect the occurrence of neurodevelopmental rather than neurodegenerative processes. Similar executive function performance between groups raises questions about compensatory and resiliency mechanisms during young adulthood. Future longitudinal study of youth variant carriers and non-carriers (younger than 18 years) is necessary to determine whether compensatory mechanisms are active, and to disentangle neurodevelopmental vs. early neurodegenerative changes in FTD.
