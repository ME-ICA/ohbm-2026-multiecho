Introduction

The Alzheimer’s Disease Research Center (ADRC) program includes over 30 centers across the USA. The SCAN project (Standardized Centralized Alzheimer’s and related dementias Neuroimaging) began in 2020 and standardizes methods for acquisition, quality control, and analysis of MRI and PET across the ADRC network. Collected images and analyses are shared via the National Alzheimer’s Coordinating Center (NACC) and LONI, and they are linked with richly characterized, harmonized clinical, psychometric, fluid biomarker, genetic, and neuropathology data available at NACC.  

Methods

SCAN includes an unusually high proportion of participants with atypical AD phenotypes (e.g. PCA, PPA, dysexecutive) and non-AD etiologies including FTLD, DLB, CBD, and vascular brain injury, and those with multiple etiologies. SCAN imaging methods are also identical to those in ADNI, CLARiTI, HEAD, US POINTER, MCSA, and others, facilitating meta-analyses across major public datasets.

Results

SCAN currently (September 2025) includes de-identified and de-faced imaging data from 6645 participants, including 7499 MRI exams from 5691 participants and 6249 PET exams (amyloid, tau, and FDG) from 3474 participants. Roughly 60% of SCAN participants are cognitively unimpaired, 20% have MCI, and 16% have dementia. 29% of the cognitively impaired individuals had a predominantly non-amnestic initial presentation, and 16% have suspected primarily vascular etiology. 74% of participants have longitudinal data from more than one visit. The median age is 72 years old, and 59% are female. Nearly 30% identify as ethnoracial groups typically underrepresented in the USA.

Conclusion

SCAN imaging data are available to all qualified researchers, providing images and standardized ATN imaging biomarkers for a richly characterized, highly heterogeneous group of cognitively unimpaired individuals, and those with AD, non-AD, and multiple dementia etiologies.
