Introduction

Non-suicidal self-injury (NSSI), defined as the deliberate harm to one’s own body without suicidal intent, has emerged as a pressing public health issue with significant mental and physical health consequences1. NSSI shows high prevalence during adolescence and represents a strong marker for future mental health issues 2.

Emotion dysregulation has been proposed as a core feature contributing to the development and maintenance of NSSI1. The effective selection and implementation of emotion regulation strategies are critical for adaptive emotion regulation3, but deficits in these processes have been observed in individuals with NSSI4. Acceptance, a strategy widely used in therapeutic interventions, has been shown to successfully facilitate the regulation of negative affect in both experimental and clinical settings5. However, it remains unclear whether adolescents with NSSI experience difficulties when applying the acceptance strategy and whether such difficulties are associated with altered neural activity. The present study aims to address this question by combining functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) with a naturalistic emotion regulation paradigm (Fig. 1).

Methods

Twenty-five adolescents with NSSI behaviors and twenty-five healthy control (HC) adolescents were included in the analysis. MRI data were analyzed using SPM12. Preprocessing was conducted using the standard pipeline. At the first level, a general linear model was specified for each participant. Task conditions (acceptance of negative video clips, react/view to negative or neutral clips) were modeled as separate regressors and convolved with the canonical hemodynamic response function. Motion parameters and depression level measured by the Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) were included as nuisance regressors. Individual contrast images comparing conditions of interest (e.g., Acceptance > React) were generated. At the group level, two-sample t-tests were conducted to compare NSSI and HC participants. Statistical significance was evaluated at a voxel-wise threshold of p < 0.001 (uncorrected) with cluster-level FWE correction at p < 0.05.

Correlations between the average activation within each cluster significantly differing between the groups and emotion regulation difficulties in everyday life, including Nonacceptance of Emotional Responses and Limited Access to Emotion Regulation Strategies (measured by the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale, DERS), were analyzed separately (FDR corrected). Functional connectivity was assessed using a generalized psychophysiological interaction (gPPI) approach with a 6-mm sphere centered at the right amygdala peak (MNI: 20, -8, -22) as the seed, based on prior findings from a naturalistic emotion regulation task in young adults employing the acceptance strategy.

Results

Negative clips elicited significantly higher negative emotions compared with neutral clips, and adolescents with NSSI reported stronger negative feelings than HC participants even during the neutral clips (Fig. 2a). During negative emotion experience, HC participants showed significantly greater activation in the temporal cortex than adolescents with NSSI (Fig. 2b). When employing the acceptance strategy, adolescents with NSSI exhibited heightened activation in regions associated with both emotion reactivity (e.g., right amygdala, insula) and emotion regulation (e.g., right dlPFC, dmPFC, vlPFC) (Fig. 2c). Difficulties in emotion regulation were positively associated with activation across multiple clusters (Fig. 2d). Additionally, adolescents with NSSI showed significantly reduced functional connectivity between the right amygdala and the left dlPFC.

Conclusion

Adolescents engaging in NSSI exhibited abnormal neural responses during both negative emotion experience and the use of the acceptance strategy. The hyperactivation observed during acceptance may reflect increased cognitive demands required for these adolescents to implement the strategy, which is further supported by the positive associations between brain activation levels and emotion regulation difficulties. The reduced functional connectivity between regions implicated in emotion reactivity (amygdala) and emotion regulation (dlPFC) may play a key role in this dysregulated processing.
