Introduction

Cochlear implants (CIs) are the treatment of choice for severe to profound hearing loss. Despite progress in CI technology, there is considerable variability in outcomes. The function and plasticity of central auditory pathways contribute to this variability. Direct intracranial recordings in CI patients are methodologically difficult and exceedingly rare.1 Spectrally degraded sounds presented to normal-hearing listeners can approximate the CI’s input to the central auditory system.2 Previous intracranial electroencephalography (iEEG) studies used spectrally degraded (noise-vocoded) speech tokens in a phonetic behavioral task.3,4 These studies described responses across stimulus conditions, cortical processing stages, and task performance, and established the importance of the dorsal auditory cortical stream for processing of spectrally degraded speech. Spoken sentences are more ecologically relevant than isolated phonemic tokens and engage additional processing stages, including anterior temporal lobe and prefrontal cortex. Thus, the current study sought to further characterize differential responses to clear and vocoded speech using sentence stimuli.

Methods

Participants were adult neurosurgical epilepsy patients undergoing chronic iEEG monitoring. English speech sentences (duration 1.5-3.7 s) were degraded using a 3-band noise vocoder.5 Clear and vocoded stimuli were presented via earphones during a visual target detection task. Cortical activity was recorded using depth and subdural iEEG electrodes. Electrode coverage included regions within superior temporal plane, superior temporal gyrus, dorsal and ventral auditory-related areas, prefrontal, and sensorimotor cortex in both hemispheres. Analysis of iEEG data focused on event-related band power (ERBP) in canonical bands (theta through high gamma). Differences between responses to clear and vocoded sentences were established in two windows (0-1 s re: sentence onset and -0.5...0.5 s re: sentence offset) using cluster-based permutation tests.

Results

Differential responses (ERBP augmentation or suppression) to clear and vocoded sentences occurred across multiple iEEG frequency bands. Clear- and vocoded-preferred responses typically manifested as greater ERBP augmentation in higher iEEG bands (gamma, high gamma) and greater suppression in lower bands (theta, alpha, beta). These responses were broadly distributed and could persist throughout the sentence duration (Fig. 1). Vocoded-preferred responses were less common than clear-preferred responses, with an overall greater involvement of posterior/dorsal compared to anterior/ventral regions in both hemispheres. The superior temporal plane, posterior portion of the superior temporal gyrus, lower bank of the superior temporal sulcus, and supramarginal gyrus had the highest proportion of vocoded-preferred responses. Within prefrontal cortex, vocoded-preferred responses were rare and typically manifested as low frequency ERBP suppression.

Conclusion

The results are consistent with previous work that used simple speech tokens and emphasize the role of auditory cortex and the dorsal stream in processing degraded speech at the sentence level. Clear-preferred responses are likely driven by spectral complexity and intelligibility. Vocoded-preferred responses may reflect processing demands associated with challenging listening conditions. These responses emerge at early cortical processing stages and can engage the dorsal stream without extending into prefrontal cortex. Cortical regions that are preferentially activated by vocoded speech may have diagnostic and prognostic utility and present potential targets for neuromodulation-based CI rehabilitation strategies.
