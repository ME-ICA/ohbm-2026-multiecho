Introduction

WHO data indicates that stroke is the second leading cause of death globally. Ischemic Stroke (IS) accounts for the highest proportion of cases. Acute Hyperglycemia (AHG) is a common complication of IS, occurring even in patients without a history of diabetes. Research shows that non-diabetic IS patients with AHG have a worse prognosis than those with a history of diabetes. Clinical trials using traditional glucose control have largely failed to improve outcomes, highlighting the need for better prognostic tools. The modified Rankin Scale (mRS) is the gold standard for measuring post-stroke functional recovery. Early and accurate prediction of long-term mRS scores is crucial for formulating individualized treatment and rehabilitation plans.

This study utilizes diffusion MRI (dMRI) to non-invasively analyze cerebral white matter integrity. While the negative impact of AHG is acknowledged, the specific regions of white matter damage and the translation of imaging features into clinical prediction tools require further study. This research integrates dMRI and Machine Learning (ML) to investigate the pathology and prognosis of IS complicated by AHG. The specific objectives are divided into three parts: First, utilizing Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI) to investigate specific areas of white matter microstructural damage in IS patients with AHG and analyze the potential impact on brain function. Second, assessing the efficacy of high b-value images synthesized using Deep Learning (DL) techniques compared to original low b-value images to validate the clinical value of synthetic imaging. Finally, combining these imaging features with the Random Forest (RF) algorithm to establish a functional prognosis prediction model aimed at accurately predicting mRS scores six months post-stroke, providing clinicians with an objective auxiliary tool for diagnosis and treatment.

Methods

Research Data Source

This study analyzed 89 Ischemic Stroke (IS) patients from the Taipei Medical University Clinical Research Database, utilizing data from Shuang Ho Hospital. Microstructural analysis employed diffusion imaging metrics. Voxel-based Analysis (VBA) used DTI metrics including Fractional Anisotropy (FA), Apparent Diffusion Coefficient (ADC), Axial Diffusivity (AD), and Radial Diffusivity (RD). For high b-value data, NODDI1 metrics Isotropic Volume Fraction (FISO) and Orientation Dispersion Index (ODI) were included. Fixel-based Analysis (FBA)2 calculated Fibre Density and Cross-section (FDC) to investigate nerve fiber changes.

Image Generation Workflow

A Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) was trained to generate b2000 diffusion images from b1000 images. Accuracy was verified using Structural Similarity Index, Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, and Mean Absolute Error across DWI raw images, Quantitative Anisotropy, and Fiber Density. Tests confirmed high restoration of microstructural features. The model generated b2000 images for the 89 patients, which were included with original b1000 images in VBA and FBA statistics.

Image Processing and Statistical Analysis

Preprocessing used MRtrix33 and Synb0-DISCO4. VBA analyzed DTI metrics. Patients were grouped by acute hyperglycemia (admission glucose >180 mg/dL or fasting >126 mg/dL). The final cohort included 38 IS-non-AHG and 26 IS-AHG patients. TFCE analysis assessed group differences. b2000 images were used for NODDI metric analysis via TFCE. FBA calculated FODs and FDC values, followed by Connectivity-based Fixel Enhancement statistics. The TractSeg atlas localized nerve bundle differences. Primary study areas were defined as regions where significant voxels or fixels (p < 0.05) exceeded 5%.

Machine Learning Model Construction

Mean FDC, DTI, and NODDI values in TractSeg regions, combined with pre-stroke and six-month post-stroke mRS scores, served as features for a Random Forest (RF) prognosis model. RF Feature Importance prevented overfitting by selecting key features with the highest weights. 44 patients with complete follow-up data were trained using Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation. A hierarchical strategy used b1000 and b2000 data for three models: Model A distinguished full recovery (mRS=0) from symptoms. Model B distinguished slight (mRS=1) from moderate-to-severe (mRS=2-5) disability. Model C distinguished mild-to-moderate (mRS=1-2) from moderate-to-severe (mRS=3-5) disability. b1000 and b2000 data trained Model A. Symptomatic b2000 data trained Model B. Cases predicted as moderate-to-severe by Model B were refined by Model C; if Model C predicted mild-to-moderate, the final mRS was set to 2.

Results

Demographics and Clinical Characteristics

The cohort included 38 patients in the IS-non-AHG and 26 in the IS-AHG for imaging statistical analysis. Regarding baseline demographic characteristics, independent t-tests showed no statistically significant difference in age between the two groups (p = 0.053). Chi-square tests indicated no significant difference in sex ratio (p = 0.895). To strictly exclude the influence of potential confounding factors, age and sex were included as covariates in VBA and FBA statistical analyses. Regarding blood glucose levels, the IS-AHG group had significantly higher average admission emergency blood glucose compared to the IS-non-AHG group (p < 0.001), which is consistent with the grouping definition. These results demonstrate good comparability between the two groups in baseline characteristics.

Diffusion Imaging Statistical Analysis

This study first investigated the impact of AHG on cerebral white matter microstructure in IS patients. The statistical analysis results for VBA and FBA are shown in Figure 2(a). In the statistical analysis of b1000 images, compared to IS-non-AHG, the IS-AHG exhibited significant microstructural disruption in multiple white matter regions (p < 0.05). Specifically, regarding DTI metrics, the IS-AHG showed significantly decreased FA accompanied by significantly increased AD, ADC, and RD in the rostral body (CC_3) and splenium (CC_7) of the corpus callosum, thalamic-occipital (T_OCC), striato-occipital (ST_OCC), optic radiation (OR), and fornix (FX). Regarding FBA metrics, the IS-AHG showed significantly reduced FDC values (p < 0.05) in fiber bundles related to motor and sensory functions, with affected areas primarily including the corticospinal tract (CST), thalamic-precentral (T_PREC), striato-precentral (ST_PREC), superior thalamic radiation (STR), and striato-postcentral (ST_POSTC). Analysis using GAN-generated b2000 images showed a significant decrease in FISO in the IS-AHG (p < 0.05; indicating IS-non-AHG was significantly higher than IS-AHG). Regarding the distribution of significant white matter tract regions, b2000 VBA and FBA results were largely consistent with b1000, but specific changes in extent were observed. In most affected brain regions, the significant range of FA and AD abnormalities detected in b2000 images was more extensive than in b1000, while the significant range for ADC and RD was reduced, such as in the FX, OR, ST_OCC, and T_OCC. The significant range for FDC in the STR also showed a shrinking trend.

Machine Learning Prognosis Prediction Model Performance

To evaluate the combined impact of white matter damage on functional prognosis, whole-brain white matter fiber tract regions were used as input features for the model, rather than limiting them to statistical analysis results. Utilizing the RF algorithm, a hierarchical mRS prediction model was constructed combining imaging features from different b-values. Detailed performance metrics for each stage are summarized in Figure 2(b)-2(d). In Model A, the model trained with b1000 features performed best. With min_samples_leaf set to 5, it achieved an Accuracy of 0.94 and an AUC-ROC of 0.846, demonstrating effective differentiation between full recovery and symptomatic patients. In subsequent severity differentiation, the advantages of different b-values were complementary. In Model B, the model trained with b2000 imaging features performed better (accuracy = 0.889, AUC-ROC = 0.923); whereas for Model C, the model trained with b1000 features stood out (accuracy = 0.889, AUC-ROC = 0.958). Based on these single-model performance differences, a three-class hierarchical model was constructed, using the b1000-trained Model A for initial screening, followed by combining the b2000-trained Model B and b1000-trained Model C for disability severity determination. The integrated model achieved an improved overall accuracy of 0.944, maintaining a high average overall precision across classes of 0.96 ± 0.07.

Conclusion

This study integrates diffusion MRI analysis, generative deep learning, and machine learning techniques, confirming that acute hyperglycemia has a significant destructive effect on the white matter microstructure of ischemic stroke patients. The results indicate that IS-AHG patients exhibit significant reductions in FA and FDC across multiple key nerve fiber tracts. These microstructural abnormalities are highly correlated with specific neurological functional deficits. According to previous relevant studies, damage to the CC_3 and CC_7 may lead to obstructed interhemispheric information transfer and cognitive coordination disorders; abnormalities in the OR affect the patient's visual processing capabilities; damage to the CST explains the decline in reaction speed and motor coordination; and disruption of the T_OCC and T_PREC involves a decline in sensory and cognitive integration functions. From a neuroanatomical perspective, these findings specifically elucidate why stroke patients with concurrent acute hyperglycemia have a poorer prognosis.

Furthermore, this study confirms the clinical value of image generation technology and machine learning. High b-value images synthesized using GANs were not only highly consistent with original images but also demonstrated unique advantages in statistical analysis. The b2000 images exhibited broader sensitivity in detecting FA and AD abnormalities while providing more convergent and specific lesion localization in ADC and RD metrics, effectively filtering out interference from free water diffusion. Combined with advanced metrics like FISO, this enhanced detection sensitivity for lesions, providing richer pathological information than traditional imaging.

The hierarchical prediction model proposed in this study successfully integrates the advantages of multi-modal features. The features relied upon by the prediction model are not entirely limited to specific brain regions affected by AHG, suggesting that post-stroke prognostic recovery is a complex process involving whole-brain network integrity. Results show that b1000 and b2000 images exhibit complementarity in different prognostic classification tasks; b1000 is more suitable for distinguishing between asymptomatic and symptomatic cases as well as extremely severe cases, whereas b2000 performs better in distinguishing moderate disability. This adaptive model design demonstrated excellent accuracy (0.944) in predicting the mRS score six months post-stroke.

In summary, this study not only reveals the specific pathological landscape of exacerbated cerebral white matter injury caused by acute hyperglycemia but also proposes an innovative framework combining deep learning image optimization and machine learning precise prediction. This provides an important objective basis for clinical diagnosis, prognosis assessment, and treatment decision-making in ischemic stroke.
