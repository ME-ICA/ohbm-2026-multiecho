Introduction

MRI performed at different field strengths across the entire spectrum of ultra-low-field (0.064 T) up to ultra-high-field (7 T) has distinct advantages: ultra-low-field MRI is more accessible, cost-effective, and easier to implement,1 whereas ultra-high-field MRI (field strength > 3 T), offers higher signal-to-noise ratio, resulting in superior image resolution, and contrast.2 Large datasets that include paired acquisitions across different MRI field strengths are particularly valuable for a range of computational applications. For example, developing super-resolution methods to improve the quality of low-field images depends on having matched data from higher field strengths. Likewise, datasets spanning multiple field strengths are essential for creating algorithms that generalize reliably, ensuring that artificial intelligence models such as those used for detecting epileptogenic lesions perform robustly across varying image qualities and acquisition protocols.

Methods

At our center, MRI across the entire spectrum of currently available MRI field strengths were deployed in the presurgical evaluation of people with epilepsy and a reference group of healthy participants. MRI scans were performed for research purpose at 0.064 T (Hyperfine Swoop portable MRI system) and 7 T (Siemens Healthineers 7Tplus), alongside imaging at 3 T as part of the clinical routine (Philips Achieva/Intera) and for research purposes (Siemens Healthineers MAGNETOM TrioTim). Datasets were considered eligible if participants had MRI acquisitions at a minimum of two field strengths. All eligible participants were invited to provide written consent for the publication of anonymized raw MRI data. Datasets for which consent was granted were curated and converted to BIDS format. All subjects had at least one 3D T1-weighted scan per available field strength, and most structural imaging series include FLAIR/T2-weighted sequences largely in accordance with the HARNESS protocol for epilepsy assessment.3Structural scans were defaced for anonymization. In addition, a subset of the 7 T sessions also includes research sequences for quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) and chemical exchange saturation transfer (CEST) mapping. QSM maps were calculated in accordance with the recommendations of the ISMRM (International Society for Magnetic Resonance in Medicine) electromagnetic tissue properties study group's 2024 recommendation.4 CEST maps include semisolid magnetization transfer, relayed nuclear Overhauser effect, amides and amines.5 These data are provided as derivatives with the dataset. For all participants, a set of relevant demographic and clinical covariates was compiled to accompany the imaging data.

Results

The curated dataset contains MRI scans of 100 people with epilepsy (380 sessions, median age at MRI 31 years, range 14-73, female:male 47:53) and 45 healthy controls (107 sessions, median age at MRI 26 years, range 20-50, female:male 24:21). See Fig. 1 for overview. 7 T QSM maps are available for 117 subjects, CEST maps are included for 89 individuals. The dataset is publicly available on OpenNeuro.

Conclusion

We present a dataset containing paired MRI acquisitions across the full range of currently available field strengths, collected from individuals with focal epilepsy undergoing presurgical evaluation as well as from healthy controls. The dataset comprises defaced structural MRI scans, QSM and CEST maps and is publicly accessible for download.
