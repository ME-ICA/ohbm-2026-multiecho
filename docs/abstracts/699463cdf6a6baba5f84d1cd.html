Introduction

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration’s (FDA) approval of Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy (SNT) in 2022 for depression marks a shift towards personalized neuromodulation guided by functional MRI (fMRI). SNT identifies personalized stimulation target through individualized FC pattern, specially, the anticorrelation between a deep emotion related region and a superficial functional zone, typically the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), offering a potential advantage over conventional anatomically defined targeting strategies1,2. However, the precision of this targeting approach depends on reliable FC estimates, particularly their strength and spatial reliability. 

Traditional analyses based on fast fourier transform (FFT) methods are limited for characterizing fMRI signals, which are inherently non-stationary3. In contrast, the Wavelet Transform (WT) offers superior time-frequency localization and holds promises for yielding more robust and reliable FC4. Yet, the influence of specific wavelet parameters, including mother wavelet types, decomposition level, on FC reliability, especially its spatial reliability, remains largely unexplored. 

Therefore, the present study systematically evaluates multiple wavelet implementation parameters, derives functional connectivity from wavelet coefficients, and identify the optimal configuration that maximizes test-retest reliability. Specially, we evaluated both the strength and spatial reliability of the FC-guided stimulation target derived from this wavelet-based approach against conventional FFT-based methods.

Methods

This study used data from the the ECEO dataset, which includes resting state fMRI scans collected under eyes-closed (EC) and eyes-open (EO) conditions, along with high-resolution 3D-T1 structural images. Twenty-one participants were scanned twice (Visit 1 [V1] and Visit 2 [V2]). Standard data preprocessing was performed except that the filtering step for wavelet-based FC utilized wavelet transform. Specifically, two wavelet-transform frameworks were systematically evaluated: the Maximal Overlap Discrete Wavelet Transform (MODWT) and the Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT). Within each frameworks, we assessed multiple Daubechies wavelet families (including the Daubechies Extremal Phase and Daubechies Least Asymmetric families) with filter lengths ranging from 2 to 20. FC was computed from scale-specific wavelet coefficients corresponding to canonical frequency bands (Scale 1: 0.125–0.25 Hz; Scale 2: 0.06–0.125 Hz; Scale 3: 0.03–0.06 Hz; Scale 4: 0.015–0.03 Hz). Test-retest reliability was quantified using intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) between V1 and V2 FC maps for each wavelet parameter configuration. The proportion of voxels with ICC ≥ 0.4 was then compared between wavelet-based FC (Wavelet-FC) and FFT-based FC (FFT-FC). A ratio greater than 1 indicates higher reliability for Wavelet-FC. Subsequently, the wavelet optimal wavelet configuration (based on reliability) was applied to access FC consistency across five emotion-related effective regions of interest (ROIs), including the posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC), pregenual anterior cingulate cortex (pgACC), and nucleus accumbens (NAc)2,5. Furthermore, the spatial stability of the FC-guided stimulation targets within the DLPFC was compared between CWT-db2-FC and FFT-FC, as well as between two target-definition algorithms: the watershed-peak method and the watershed-center of gravity (COG) method.

Results

Results demonstrate that Wavelet-FC consistently outperforms FFT-FC across all frequency bands in scales 1–4. Among all parameter combinations, CWT with the db2 filter (CWT-db2) yielded the highest reliability in the vast majority of scenarios (Figure A) and was selected for subsequent analyses. Using five seed ROIs, CWT-db2 consistently produced a higher voxel count exceeding the ICC ≥ 0.4 threshold across scales 1–4, demonstrating superior reliability in both connection strength and spatial extent. Regarding spatial stability of FC-guided potential stimulation targets, CWT-db2-FC showed smaller inter-session Euclidean distances (Eds) than those from FFT-FC, indicating greater target reproducibility, although this difference did note reach statistical significance (Figure B).

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that the choice of wavelet method, mother wavelet type, and filter lengths significantly influences the reliability of FC estimation through wavelet transform. Wavelet-based FC, particularly CWT with db2 filter, offers superior reliability compared to conventional methods, supporting its potential as a more robust foundation for connectivity-informed neuromodulation. Moreover, the improved spatial stability of FC-guided potential stimulation targets under the CWT-db2 framework aligns with the goals of personalized therapies like SNT. This work thus provides empirical support for integrating advanced time–frequency methods into clinical neuroimaging pipelines aimed at enhancing treatment precision and reproducibility.
