Introduction

Juvenile fibromyalgia (JFM) is a chronic pain condition characterized by widespread pain, fatigue, sleep disturbances, and attention and memory problems, affecting 2–6% of youth, primarily adolescent girls1. Adolescents with JFM report reduced tolerance to non-painful sensory stimuli and showed hyperactivation of sensory processing regions and default mode network (DMN) in response to these stimuli during multisensory stimulation2.  Interestingly, adult FM patients show abnormal functional connectivity between somatosensory/nociceptive regions and higher-order association cortices3,4. However, it remains unclear how these complex sensory-to-cognitive interactions take place in JFM. Here, we investigated adolescents with JFM using functional connectivity analyses designed to identify brain connectomics streams between networks, offering insight into neurobiological mechanisms that may contribute to fibromyalgia from an early age.

Methods

Forty-six adolescent girls (16.44±1.11 years) diagnosed with JFM and forty-three healthy girls (16.09±1.06 years) completed validated symptom questionnaires and underwent resting-state functional MRI. The functional images were preprocessed using CONN Toolbox-20.b (www.nitrc.org/projects/conn) running on MATLAB-R2021a (Math Works Inc, Natick, MA). To investigate changes in brain connectomics relevant to sensory-modal interconnections in FM, we first created an association matrix and a bipartite matrix targeting brain regions vulnerable to FM and later we applied Stepwise Functional Connectivity (SFC), described by Sepulcre et al. (2012), to map regions connected to the seed areas at multiple functional step distances5 (Figure 1). Voxel-to-voxel association matrices were generated using 2,585 cortical voxels, calculating positive functional correlations and correcting for family-wise error (pFWE < 0.001). A bipartite matrix was then constructed to isolate connectivity between primary sensory regions (visual, somatosensory, and auditory) and Default Mode Network (DMN) nodes, retaining only inter-set connections using a bipartite filter. For visualization purposes, the bipartite matrix was plotted using a force-directed network graph layout (Gephi software; ForceAtlas2 layout). Using this bipartite matrix as input, we implemented an SFC analysis to quantify the propagation of connectivity from the six bilateral sensory regions across paths of different lengths (1 to 3 steps). Each step identifies regions that are progressively further from the seed in terms of network topological distance. Therefore, this approach allowed us to identify, for each voxel, the number of functional pathways linking it to the sensory systems at increasing link-step distances. The SFC weighted degree maps were generated for each path length. Using SPM12, we performed within- and between-group t-tests and a regression model in the JFM group to investigate the associations between self-reported symptom severity scores and functional connectivity SFC metrics (pFWE<0.05 at cluster-level and p<0.001 at voxel-level). The results were visualized using Connectome Workbench.

Results

We observed that JFM patients and healthy participants display similar topological patterns in their SFC patterns with no significant between-group differences. In the first step, connectivity extended from primary sensory regions (visual, auditory and somatosensory) to secondary sensory regions. In the intermediate step, connectivity extended from these secondary sensory regions -visual, auditory and somatosensory- to multimodal (multisensory) integration brain areas. In last steps, connectivity showed specific pathways from the multimodal integration areas to association cortices, particularly the DMN. However, we found that JFM patients with higher symptom severity showed decreased connectivity between bilateral secondary somatosensory cortices and the right anterior insula (peak-level T=5.11, pFWE<0.05 cluster-level) where the connectivity streams converge at multimodal integration areas (Figure 2). 

Conclusion

This study provides a detailed characterization of hierarchical sensory-to-heteromodal connectivity in adolescents with JFM. Our methodology disentangled connectivity propagation from primary sensory cortices through secondary and multimodal integration regions to heteromodal networks such as the DMN. By applying SFC to a bipartite-filtered matrix targeting FM-vulnerable systems, we were able to isolate sensory-to-DMN pathways with high topological specificity, capturing functional connections that traditional analyses cannot resolve. Moreover, we detected symptom severity–related disruption of connectomic streams of multisensory integration in the insula, suggesting that this area may be involved in the relay of the altered somatosensory processing and symptom expression in JFM.

Overall, these results demonstrate that this methodology is a potential tool for mapping the spatiotemporal propagation of functional pathways, offering new insights into the neurobiological mechanisms underlying juvenile fibromyalgia and guiding future longitudinal studies aimed at identifying early biomarkers of network reorganization in chronic pain.
