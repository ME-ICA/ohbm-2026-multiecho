Introduction

Prenatal alcohol exposure is a leading cause of neurodevelopmental disorders in the United States and frequently leads to long-lasting impairments in spatial memory and real-world navigation. Preclinical research shows that hippocampal and entorhinal circuits are highly vulnerable to alcohol teratogenicity. However, the neurophysiological mechanisms underlying spatial learning deficits in children with Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders (FASD) remain poorly understood. Bridging this gap requires translational approaches that capture real-world navigation behaviors while probing the functional integrity of underlying neural networks.

Methods

Children and adolescents (8-14 yrs of age) with and without FASD completed a virtual Morris Water Task (vMWT) during multimodal neuroimaging sessions. The MEG session measured theta-band (4-8 Hz) oscillatory dynamics supporting hippocampal navigation, while the fMRI session identified regions preferentially engaged during spatial navigation. In the vMWT, spatial navigation memory was tested through a combination of hidden platform blocks, where the participant relies on distal cues to find an escape platform hidden beneath the surface of the water, and visible platform blocks, where the participant navigates to a visible escape platform with the absence of distal cues. VMWT behavioral metrics included latency and path length. Here we examined MEG session data. After noise and motion preprocessing, analyses were focused on the initial 2 seconds of hidden-platform navigation. Source-level MEG activity was processed with a LCMV beamformer, and time-frequency decomposition examined theta (4-8 Hz) task-evoked oscillatory responses. Behavioral performance and neural dynamics were compared between groups to evaluate alterations associated with FASD.

Results

Early findings indicate that during the vMWT children with FASD demonstrate less efficient search patterns and take longer to find the hidden platform when compared to typically developing controls. FASD is associated with reduced task-evoked theta power during navigation. Group differences emerged in hippocampal and entorhinal regions. Across all participants, stronger theta responses predicted more efficient navigation. Together, these results point to disruption of navigation-related circuitry as a neural mechanism underlying spatial learning deficits following prenatal alcohol exposure.

Conclusion

This study integrates ecologically valid spatial navigation behavior with high-resolution multimodal neuroimaging to advance the understanding of navigation circuitry disruption in FASD. These insights can guide earlier identification and intervention strategies, ultimately supporting better long-term outcomes for affected children and families.
