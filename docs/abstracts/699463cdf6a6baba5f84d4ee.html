Introduction

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) is a chronic mental disorder characterized by recurrent obsessions and compulsions. Abnormal activity in cortico-striatal-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) circuit is considered to be closely associated with OCD, among which the striatum is one of the brain regions of intensive interest. Glutamate (Glu) and γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) mediate excitatory and inhibitory functions respectively; abnormalities of these two neurotransmitters and the imbalance in their ratio are also closely linked to the development of OCD. Based on this, some researchers have begun to explore the characteristics of GABAergic and glutamatergic functions in the striatum (including its subregions: the putamen, caudate nucleus and nucleus accumbens) among patients with OCD. However, these studies all have certain limitations, such as analyses restricted to a single subregion. To address this gap, the present study aims to employ whole-brain 3D magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging (3D-MRSI) to comprehensively quantify GABA and Glu levels across all three striatal subregions. This approach will enable us to explore differences in neurotransmitter levels between OCD and healthy controls (HCs), as well as their associations with OCD core symptoms.

Methods

Imaging technology: The SPectroscopic Imaging by exploiting spatiospectral CorrElation (SPICE）¹H-MRSI sequence was employed, which is characterized by high speed (completed within 8 minutes) and high spatial resolution (1.3×1.3×2.0 mm³).

Data Processing: Preprocessing was performed using the Freesurfer software. 

Statistical Methods: The independent samples t-test was applied for the analysis of MRS levels between groups. We used Pearson correlations to investigate the possible associations between clinical measures and MRS. In addition, mediation analyses were conducted by using PROCESS version 3.4.1 and 95% confidence interval (CI) was used to determine the significance of the indirect effect.

Results

Demographic and Clinical Scale Scores: No significant differences were observed between the OCD (n=17) and HCs (n=25) in terms of gender, age, IQ, and years of education (all p > 0.1).

MRS results: (1) GABA: There were no significant differences in GABA levels between the OCD and HC groups across all striatal subregions; (2) Glu: A significant difference in Glu levels was detected in the right putamen (Right-51) between the two groups (p < 0.05); (3) GABA/Glu Ratio: A significant difference in the GABA/Glu ratio was noted in the left nucleus accumbens (Left-26) between the two groups (p < 0.05).

Correlation Analysis: (1) Glu levels in the right putamen were significantly positively correlated with OBQ44 scores (r = 0.739, p < 0.001) and trait anxiety (STAI-T) scores (r = 0.606, p < 0.01), but showed no significant correlations with Y-BOCS total or subscale scores; (2) The GABA/Glu ratio in the left nucleus accumbens was significantly negatively correlated with STAI-T scores (r = -0.569, p = 0.017), whereas no significant correlations were found between this ratio and Y-BOCS total/subscale scores or OBQ44 scores (all p > 0.1).

Mediation Analysis: Glu levels in the right putamen exert a positive mediating effect on Y-BOCS total scores via STAI-T score [β = 1.65, SE = 0.94, 95% CI (0.36, 4.00)]; The GABA/Glu ratio in the left nucleus accumbens (NAc) exert a negative mediating effect on Y-BOCS total scores through STAI-T score  [β = 1.34, SE = 0.93, 95% CI (0.01, 3.62)].

Conclusion

Compared with healthy controls, OCD patients did not show significant abnormalities in striatal GABA levels. However, significant abnormalities were observed in glutamate levels in the right putamen and the GABA/Glu ratio in the left nucleus accumbens. Although these abnormal neurochemical factors are not directly correlated with the core OCD symptoms reflected by the YBOCS, they can exert a significant impact on OCD symptoms by influencing trait anxiety of the patients.
