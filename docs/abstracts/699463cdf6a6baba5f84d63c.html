Introduction

The hippocampus is critical for memory and is a key structure affected in numerous neurodegenerative diseases such as multiple sclerosis (MS). Most MS studies of the hippocampus have focused on volume measurements from 3D T1-weighted images that show overall reductions as well as in specific subfields. For example, a cohort of early relapsing-remitting MS participants showed bilateral reductions across all hippocampal subfields, notably within the cornu ammonis (CA) region.1 Human postmortem histology in progressive MS has demonstrated marked, but heterogeneous, demyelination in the hippocampus which may be relevant to cognitive decline.2 Diffusion MRI could investigate such micro-structural changes, but it is challenging to apply to the hippocampus given its small and complex, folded anatomy. High-resolution 1 mm isotropic diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) of the hippocampus in MS has shown elevated total hippocampal mean diffusivity (MD) in an adult population (n=43, 32-70 years) as well as focal regions of elevated MD that was not uniform across the hippocampus, suggesting regional demyelination and neuronal loss.3  However, it is not known how water diffusion is altered across hippocampal subfields in MS. An automated, U-net deep learning model called HippUnfold can segment the hippocampus into subfields which can be unfolded and ‘flattened’ for easier visualization and has yielded subfield-specific diffusion metrics in healthy controls of the Human Connectome Project database.4,5   The purpose here is to investigate diffusion changes of hippocampal subfields across the “lifespan”, including children, in MS compared to a normative cohort using 1 mm isotropic diffusion MRI.

Methods

MRI was acquired on a 3T Siemens Prisma with a 64-channel RF coil in controls (n=144, 79 females, 5-74 years) and MS participants (n=93, 69 females, 13-71 years; 77  relapsing-remitting MS (RRMS), 13 secondary progressive MS (SPMS), 3 primary progressive MS (PPMS); EDSS=3.1±1.9; disease duration=12±10 years). FLAIR (1.2 mm isotropic) and T1‐weighted (0.9 mm isotropic) images were used for lesion segmentation in MS (total lesion volume 11±14 cm3). Hippocampus diffusion data was acquired with single-shot EPI, 20 slices, 1.0 mm isotropic, TR/TE= 2900/74 ms, 10 b=0 s/mm2, b=500 s/mm2 with 10 gradient directions and 10 averages (100 total), and scan time of 5.5 min.3 Preprocessing consisted of denoising (MPPCA), correction for Gibbs ringing and eddy currents/motion (FSL), and tensor fitting in MRtrix3 to generate MD maps. Hippocampus subfields were segmented bilaterally into subiculum, cornu ammonis (CA) 1-4, and dentate gyrus (DG) on the mean b500 diffusion-weighted image using a custom module in HippUnfold.4 The segmentation outputs were visually assessed for quality check. MD was sampled on mid-surface hippocampal segmentations to limit partial volume effects. Volume and diffusion metrics were averaged across left and right hippocampus.

Unfolded MD maps were averaged across three age groups (younger: 13-20 years, n=31 controls, n=13 MS; middle: 21-50 years, n=60 controls, n=54 MS; and older: 51-71 years, n=26 controls, n=26 MS), while excluding voxels with MD greater than 2.0x10-3 mm2/s to minimize cerebrospinal fluid effects. Age trajectories of MD for total and subfields of hippocampus in controls were fit with quadratic and Poisson fits, with best fit determined by the smallest Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) value. The MD of each MS participant was then compared to the 95% control cross-sectional prediction bands. Group analysis between the three age cohorts (using only controls aged 13-71 years to match the MS ages) was performed with unpaired t-test, correcting for multiple comparisons (Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate at p<0.05).

Results

Hippocampal subfields were successfully segmented across the lifespan in both cohorts; only 13 controls and 7 MS participants were excluded due to poor image quality or lack of full coverage of the hippocampus. Averaged MD unfolded maps across the three age groups showed similar heterogeneity, e.g. lower MD in subiculum and higher MD in CA2/CA3, with evident changes with age and marked MD elevation (shown in red) in MS participants in all three age cohorts from hippocampal head to tail, mainly in CA2/CA3 (Figure 1A – top row controls, bottom row MS). Compared to controls, the MD of total hippocampus in MS participants was greater (p < 0.001) by 7.4% (age 13-20), 6.1% (age 21-50) and 6.9% (age 51-71).

In controls from 5 to 74 years, MD yielded Poisson-fit U-shaped age changes in total and four out of five subfields (CA2 was quadratic) with steeper drops during childhood/adolescence followed by minima ~37-47 years (total 41 years; subiculum 47 years, CA2 43 years, CA1 41 years, CA3 37 years, CA4/DG 37 years) and then slower increases with aging (Figure 1B-G, orange symbols). Compared to controls, the entire cohort of MS participants 13 to 71 years had significantly higher MD (p < 0.001) in the total hippocampus by 6.3% and all of the subfields with the largest percentage changes in CA2 (10%) and CA3 (9.4%) followed by CA1 (6.2%), CA4/DG (6.0%), and finally the subiculum (3.4%). Compared to normative prediction bands, the number of MS participants whose MD fell outside the normative range was 28 out of 93 for total hippocampus (24 RRMS, 4 SPMS), and within each subfield with the most participants was 47 in CA2 (38 RRMS, 7 SPMS, 2 PPMS), followed by 37 in CA3 (33 RRMS, 3 SPMS, 1 PPMS), 30 in CA4/DG (27 RRMS, 2 SPMS, 1 PPMS), 25 in CA1 (20 RRMS, 5 SPMS), and 17 in subiculum (14 RRMS, 3 SPMS) (Figure 1B-G, gray symbols). CA2 and CA3 showed the greatest increase in MD and number of participants outside the normative range. Similarly to RRMS, progressive participants (SPMS/PPMS) had the most participants outside the normative range in CA2; however, second on the list was CA1 for progressive MS as compared to CA3 for RRMS.

In controls from age 13 to 71 years, the mean volumes were: total hippocampus 3.5±0.43 cm3, subiculum 0.67±0.12 cm3, CA1 0.86±0.14 cm3, CA2 0.18±0.04 cm3, CA3 0.33±0.07 cm3, and CA4/DG 0.70±0.10 cm3. The MS participants (age 13-71 years) showed a -7.3% smaller volume (p <0.001) in total hippocampus including all subfields with the largest difference in CA2 (-10%), followed by CA1 (-8.3%), subiculum (-7.6%), CA4/DG (-6.8%), and CA3 (-6.0%). Amongst the three age groups, only young MS participants did not show significant total hippocampus volumetric changes compared to controls (p=0.26) whereas adult MS participants showed a 7.4% (middle age) and 8.4% (older age) reduction. Similar trends were found in all subfields, with only significant reductions in volume for adults. Recall from above that the children with MS had elevated hippocampal MD.

In controls, volume of the total hippocampus and three of five subfields (CA1, CA3, CA4/DG) best fit an inverted U-shaped quadratic model with a maximum at ~41-46 years of age whereas CA2 yielded a Poisson-fit inverted U-shaped fit peaking at 38 years, and the subiculum had no significant age-related changes (data not shown). MS participants fell between the normative predication bands with only ~1-6 participants falling outside the normative range in total hippocampus and all subfields. However, in total hippocampus and most subfields (excluding subiculum), most adult MS participants were below the normative mean in controls.

Conclusion

High resolution diffusion MRI of hippocampal subfields demonstrated significant diffusivity elevation in MS across the “lifespan” including children, whereas only adults showed volumetric reductions. A previous study in early RRMS (within 5 years of diagnosis) young adults found widespread hippocampal subfield atrophy, albeit at a lower proportional change in most CA regions (~-5%) with the greatest change in the subiculum (~-9%).1 Here, CA2 was found to show the greatest volume difference (-10%) in MS. Young MS participants (age 13-20 years) did not show significant differences in volume when compared to controls, although MD was significantly elevated, implicating that microstructural abnormalities are evident prior to atrophy or loss in volume.

A prior 1 mm isotropic DTI paper (with some overlap of adult MS participants) showed elevated MD in MS (~6%) over total hippocampus which was manually outlined.3 It was shown here that the elevated MD was observed in all five subfields over the entire MS “lifespan”, with the greatest changes in CA2 and CA3 regions suggesting a greater involvement of these regions. In addition to CA2, a greater number of progressive participants (SPMS/PPMS) fell outside normative prediction bands in CA1, which could implicate greater involvement in progressive diseased states. This latter observation is in agreement with human histopathology in progressive MS reporting pronounced demyelination in CA1 2; however, they report relative sparing of the CA2 region with little demyelination (only with isolated lesions) 2 which was found here to have marked DTI changes, although this could be attributed to other microstructural changes such as neuronal/axonal loss. Differential involvement of subfields was observed between MS cohorts (pediatric MS, RRMS, SPMS/PPMS), suggesting selective hippocampal involvement between MS phenotypes.
