Introduction

Glial cells have long been considered the benign support cells of the brain. Contemporary research has challenged this belief, uncovering the critical role that these cells play in modifying the brain's dynamics. Astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes and oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs) have disparate functions which together form a glial network that regulates synaptic function, metabolic support, and circuit stability across spatial and temporal scales that extend beyond fast neuronal signaling. 

The brain is a network composed of interconnected regions (i.e. the neural connectome). One organizational principle of the brain that has emerged from the network view is an energy-efficient embedding of its connections. That is, most are short-range, optimizing communication through nearby myelinated fibers or within cortical gray matter. However, as distance between regions increases (e.g., in larger brains), such short connections are insufficient to integrate information. The metabolic demands of forming connections spanning longer distances are thought to be a necessary cost to produce more complex behaviours. Glial populations regulate synapses and circuits at varying spatial and temporal (i.e. developmental) scales, suggesting that regional differences in glial composition may align with distinct patterns of macroscale connectivity. 

The cortical underpinnings of the macro-scale connectivity have been previously linked to distinct neuronal attributes, including neuron density, excitatory-to-inhibitory receptor ratios, dendritic spine count, and soma size1. While it is known that glial cells play a critical role in neurodevelopment, metabolic support and circuit regulation, their contribution to macro-scale connectivity has not been previously investigated. We hypothesize that variation in glial architecture and vascular blood supply reflect metabolic specialization across the cortex, and is associated with connectivity topology and connection length. 

Methods

Objective - Using acquired and open-source data, the goal of the current research was to characterize the metabolic and glial architecture of the macroscopic connections of the macaque brain to deepen empirical understanding of cellular principles organizing the primate connectome.

MRI data - In vivo MRI data was acquired in 17 adult macaques. Diffusion MRI data were acquired with b-values of 0 (11 repeats), 1.2 (90 directions), 2.5 (90 directions), and 4.0 (90 directions) ms/um2. Structural MRI data were processed using the CIVET-Macaque pipeline to generate cortical surfaces. CIVET-generated transformations were then used to register the D99 Macaque atlas into each subject's space (140 regions per cortical hemisphere). Diffusion tractography was then performed for each subject to measure structural connectivity between each cortical region of the D99 atlas using the MRtrix3 toolbox. Connectivity measures between regions include Spherical-Deconvolution Informed Filtering of Tractograms 2 (SIFT2) weights and average streamline length (figure 1B). To align with the spatial transcriptomic data, only left hemisphere cortical regions were considered. 

Macroscopic connectivity - To derive measures of macroscopic connectivity, a group-representative structural connectivity matrix was derived through a distance-dependent thresholding which preserves the individual connection-length distribution2. This has been previously shown to improve the signal-to-noise ratio of brain network analyses2. Using the group structural connectivity matrix, the average SIFT2 and streamline length of a region to all other regions it is connected to was calculated. The group connectivity matrix was then used to calculate network properties (figure 1B) of degree (number of connections a node has), betweenness centrality (nodes influence quantified by how often it lies on the shortest path), and the clustering coefficient (the interconnectedness of a nodes neighbours).

Spatial transcriptomics - An open-source dataset of 3D single-cell spatial transcriptomics of three adult macaque brains3 was used to quantify abundances of glial cells including astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes, and oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs) in regions from the D99 atlas (figure 1A). The dataset included transcriptomic profiles from approximately 1.5 million cells from the left hemisphere. 

Cerebral blood volume -  An open-source dataset of ferumoxytol-weighted MRI in 4 adult macaques4 was used to quantify cerebral blood volume (CBV). Specifically, a multi-echo gradient-echo image was obtained with and without ferumoxytol, and R2* was calculated for both images. The ferumoxytol-induced change in R2* is believed to be an indirect proxy of cerebral blood volume. We then averaged the change in cortical R2* (CBV) for each region of the D99 atlas (figure 1C). 

Statistics & assortativity- Correlations between glial cell-type abundances, macroscopic connectivity, and cerebral blood volume was quantified with Pearson’s R. Variogram-matching was used to generate 5000 permuted null maps with underlying spatial autocorrelation that matches the original map. Then p-values were calculated by comparing the observed correlation with the null distribution of correlations from the permuted maps. The p-values are presented with false-discovery rate correction using both the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure, and the more conservative Benjamini-Yekutieli procedure. For the glial and CBV features, we calculated the weighted assortativity of the macroscopic connectome. The assortativity describes the tendency for regions with similar features to connect with each other. A sensorimotor-to-association labelling of regions was derived from previous work5.

Results

Correlation analysis & assortativity - Figure 2A presents the correlation between all measures derived, as shown in figure 1. Astrocyte, OPCs, and microglia cell abundances were all significantly positively correlated with each other, while oligodendrocyte cell abundance had a unique spatial variation. A variety of macroscale connectivity measures were correlated with glial cell abundances. Astrocyte cell abundance was significantly correlated with the betweenness centrality, while microglia cell abundance was significantly correlated with the SIFT2 weights, average streamline length, clustering coefficient, and betweenness centrality. The OPC abundance was significantly correlated with all macroscopic connectivity features. We found that all the macroscopic connectivity features were significantly correlated with each other, suggesting that they are capturing similar features of the neural connectome. Finally, we found that cerebral blood flow was significantly correlated with both glial (astrocyte, microglia, and OPC) cell abundance, and macroscopic connectivity (SIFT2 weights, average streamline length, and degree). 

Using the weighted assortativity to quantify the tendency for regions with similar features to be connected, we found that astrocytes (R=0.65), microglia (R=0.54), and OPCs (R=0.63) were highly assortative, while oligodendrocytes (R=0.30) were less assortative. This suggests that regions with similar glial profiles were more likely to be macroscopically connected.

Connectivity length analysis - To capture the general variation in glial cell abundance, we performed a principal component analysis (PCA) using all the glial features. Figure 2B shows the first 2 principal components, which capture 62% and 26% of the variance, respectively. The astrocytes, microglia, and OPCs are the main loadings of the first principal component, while the oligodendrocytes mainly load onto the second component (figure 2B). We then binned the cortical regions into 5 groups based on their average length of connectivity (figure 2C). We found that regions connected on average by longer distances weigh more negatively on the first principal component, and more positively on the second principal component. We also found that mean CBV increases with increasing connection length. That is, regions that are on average connected by longer distances have increased cerebral blood volume. 

Sensorimotor-to-association analysis - A sensorimotor-to-association axis has been consistently identified across scales and with varying cortical features. In figure 2D, we show the correlation between the first two glial principal components and the mean CBV, with points stratified by their position on the sensorimotor-to-association axis. We first found a negative correlation between the mean CBV and the first glial principal component (figure 2A), suggesting that as a region has an increasing astrocyte, microglia, and OPC profile it has decreasing CBV. A positive correlation between the second principal component and the mean CBV was found, suggesting that as a region has an increasing abundance of oligodendrocytes it has increasing CBV. We then found that regions classified as sensorimotor appear to have more negative weightings on the first principal component, and more positive weightings on the second principal component. This suggests that such sensorimotor regions have a profile of low astrocyte, microglia, and OPC abundance, while having a higher abundance of oligodendrocytes. Conversely, regions classified as association have a relative profile of high astrocyte, microglia, and OPC abundance, while having a lower abundance of oligodendrocytes.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that macro-scale organization of the primate neural connectome is systematically associated with regional metabolic and glial architecture. Coordinated spatial variation in astrocyte, microglia, and oligodendrocyte precursor cell abundance is associated with macroscopic connectivity strength, topology, and average connection length, whereas oligodendrocytes display a distinct and less assortative organization. These findings indicate that metabolic and glial cell populations are more closely aligned with large scale network structure than cells underlying myelination.

Astrocytes, microglia, and OPCs exhibit high assortativity, indicating that cortical regions with similar glial profiles preferentially connect to one another. This reveals a principle of connectome organization based on cellular and metabolic similarity that extends beyond neuron centered models. In addition, variation in average connection length maps onto glial principal components and cerebral blood volume, suggesting that cortical regions have distinct metabolic demands that scale with its underlying connection length. We further identified an underlying motif of glial profiles related to a cortical region's position on the sensorimotor-to-association axis. Regions identified as more sensorimotor had a profile of low astrocyte, microglia, and OPC abundance, with high oligodendrocyte abundance. Regions identified as association had an opposing profile, with high glial cell abundance except for oligodendrocytes. Differences in sensorimotor-to-association cortices have been shown across a variety of features spanning different scales. Here we find that such an organization may indeed be reflected in the glial cell abundance. 

Together, these results support a model in which glial mediated metabolic support and vascular coupling constrain primate connectome architecture, shaping connectivity topology, strength, and connection length. By linking macro-scale connectivity to micro-scale glial and vascular features, this work provides a cellularly grounded framework for understanding primate connectome organization. Future work will look to examine histological measures of glial cells, where physical measures of morphology and density can be related to the structure of the macaque connectome.
