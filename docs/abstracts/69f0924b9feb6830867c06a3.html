Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a prevalent metabolic disorder associated with increased risk of cognitive impairment and dementia, yet its central mechanisms remain unclear. The glymphatic system supports brain waste clearance via cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)-interstitial fluid (ISF) exchange along perivascular spaces (PVS) under neurovascular unit (NVU) regulation, and may be vulnerable to microvascular and barrier dysfunction in T2DM. As a single imaging index cannot capture the multidimensional nature of this system (Taoka T et al.,2024), we developed a multimodal noninvasive MRI framework to characterize glymphatic-related alterations across macrostructural, microstructural and functional domains and to examine their associations with cognitive performance in T2DM.

Methods

Cognition was evaluated using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), Digit Span Test (DST) and Digit Symbol Substitution Test (DSST). MRI included T1-weighted (T1WI), T2-weighted and FLAIR images, diffusion-weighted images (DWI) and resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) from a 3T scanner (MAGNETOM Prisma, Siemens, Germany, a 64-channel head coil). Five complementary MRI-derived glymphatic-related metrics were used. From T1WI, choroid plexus volume normalized to total intracranial volume (CPV/TIV) was obtained by automated segmentation of the bilateral choroid plexus using a pretrained deep-learning model with manual quality control, to index cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) production and blood-CSF barrier integrity (Eisma JJ et al.,2024). Perivascular-space volume fraction in white matter (PVSVF-WM) was quantified by an in-house deep-learning based PVS segmentation, followed by exclusion of white-matter hyperintensities and free-water voxels, to reflect perivascular fluid burden and impaired interstitial clearance. From DWI, free-water fraction in white matter (FW-WM) was estimated using a bi-tensor free-water model to characterize extracellular-interstitial water accumulation (Pasternak O et al.,2009), and diffusion tensor image analysis along the perivascular space (DTI-ALPS) was calculated from direction-specific diffusivities in projection and association fiber regions to probe relative water diffusivity aligned with the perivascular direction (Taoka T et al.,2017). From rs-fMRI, global BOLD-ventricular CSF coupling (gBOLD-CSF) was derived using cross-correlation between cortical global BOLD signals and ventricular CSF time series to assess functional CSF inflow dynamics linked to neurovascular activity (Han F et al.,2021). Nine glymphatic-related features were grouped into five feature combinations and entered into support vector regression with nested cross-validation to predict MoCA scores, with performance assessed by Pearson correlation and R². Group differences and associations were tested using models adjusted for age, sex, education, body mass index, and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, with FDR-corrected P < 0.05 considered significant.

Results

We included 103 patients with T2DM and 78 healthy controls. Patients with T2DM showed significantly poorer performance on MoCA, DSST and DST. CPV/TIV, PVSVF-WM and FW-WM were all significantly higher in T2DM, whereas DTI-ALPS indices and gBOLD-CSF coupling were both significantly reduced. Cognitive performance was closely linked to glymphatic alterations. MoCA scores were negatively correlated with CPV/TIV and PVSVF-WM, and positively correlated with DTI-ALPS and gBOLD-CSF coupling. The all-five-index model achieved the best performance in predicting MoCA scores (R²= 0.32, r = 0.59), representing approximately 6% improvement over the best four-index model. 

Conclusion

Our framework reveals widespread NVU-glymphatic dysfunction in T2DM associated with cognitive decline, suggesting a potential pathophysiological substrate and candidate imaging biomarker for diabetes-related brain injury, but further longitudinal studies are warranted determine their prognostic value and causal relevance.
