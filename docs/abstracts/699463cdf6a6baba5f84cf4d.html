Introduction

Complete resection of the epileptogenic zone is important for achieving seizure freedom in epilepsy surgery.1 The extent of tissue resected can be quantitatively assessed through accurate delineation of the resection cavity on postoperative MRI. However, manual and semi-automated delineation methods are time-consuming and labour-intensive,2 while existing automated approaches often show limited generalisability.3 To address these challenges, we developed MELD-PostOp, a deep learning tool that automatically segments resection cavities from postoperative 3D T1w MRI alone. MELD-PostOp was trained on a large multicentre dataset of postoperative scans from the Multicentre Epilepsy Lesion Detection (MELD) project and an open-source dataset (EPISURG)2, and enables fast, reproducible, and generalisable delineation of resection cavities across large, heterogeneous cohorts.

Methods

MELD-PostOp was trained using the nnU-Net framework with a 3D full-resolution configuration trained for 2000 epochs. Model development followed an active learning approach (Figure 1). First, a prototype model was trained on 285 postoperative MRI scans with manually segmented cavity masks (Prototype Cohort). The prototype model was then applied to an additional 680 postoperative images (Inference cohort), and its predictions underwent visual quality control (QC). Cases that failed QC were manually refined, while cases that passed QC were retained. Both refined and QC-passed cases were combined with the Prototype Cohort to train the final model (MELD-PostOp Train Cohort, n=965). Performance was evaluated on a test cohort, stratified by site (n=50), and an Independent Test Site (n=53). Quantitative metrics included dice similarity coefficient (DSC) calculated between the predicted and ground truth masks, number of false positive clusters, and inference runtime. The performance of MELD-PostOp was compared against other open-source tools, Epic-CHOP and ResectVol.4,5 The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to compare the median DSC between models. Subgroup analysis assessed performance across age group, sex, pathology, resection lobe, and imaging parameters including isotropic resolution and field strength. Comparisons within subgroups were performed using pairwise Mann-Whitney U tests.  The correlation between DSC and volume of the ground truth resection cavity masks were analysed, using Spearman’s rank correlation test. Bonferroni adjustment was used to correct for multiple comparisons.  

Results

935 patients from 26 MELD sites and 133 patients from the EPISURG dataset were included in the study (total n = 1068). Malformation of cortical development (MCD) and focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) were the most prevalent pathologies in the study population (38.4%), followed by low grade epilepsy associated tumour (LEAT) (17.6%) and hippocampal sclerosis (HS) (17.2%). The temporal lobe was the most frequently resected (55.4%), followed by the frontal lobe (26.7%). Most MRI scans were acquired at 3T (75.9%) and were of isotropic resolution (71.8%). MELD-PostOp achieved a median DSC of 0.83 (IQR, 0.68-0.89) in the Stratified Test Cohort and 0.86 (IQR, 0.78-0.93) on the Independent Test Site, outperforming Epic-CHOP (0.61; 0.69) and ResectVol (0.53; 0.68) (Figure 2). MELD-PostOp detected all resection cavities (DSC>0) in the Stratified Test cohort (100% sensitivity) and 52 out of 53 resection cavities in the Independent Test Site (98% sensitivity), with a meaningful overlap (DSC>0.5) in 92% and 94.3% of cases respectively. MELD-PostOp produced minimal false-positive clusters (median FP clusters [IQR], 0 [0-0]). Mean runtime for MELD-PostOp was 17s per image, whereas Epic-CHOP and ResectVol required longer processing times of 612s (10 minutes; SD, 160s) and 3,205s (53 minutes; SD, 876s), respectively. Model performance remained high across clinical and imaging subgroups (DSC > 0.9), with best performance for hippocampal sclerosis (median DSC [IQR], 0.98 [0.95-0.99]) and temporal lobe resections (median DSC [IQR], 0.97 [0.90-0.98]). MELD-PostOp achieved significantly higher DSC at 3T than for 1.5T scans (corrected p < 0.05). A significant positive correlation was observed between the volume of the ground truth resection masks and the DSC (corrected p < 0.001).  

Conclusion

MELD-PostOp outperformed two established automated resection cavity tools (Epic-CHOP and ResectVol), delivering more accurate cavity delineation in less time per image. It demonstrated strong generalisability, with excellent performance (median DSC > 0.9) across diverse patients and imaging characteristics. There was no significant impact of sex, age, isotropic resolution, or surgical side on segmentation accuracy. Performance varied by histopathology, with highest accuracy in HS cases and temporal lobe resections, reflecting larger training volumes and the standardised neurosurgical resections used for HS. MELD-PostOp offers an accurate, efficient, and generalisable tool for postoperative cavity segmentation using only postoperative MRI, enabling large-scale quantitative outcome analysis and supporting reproducible neuroimaging research in epilepsy surgery.
