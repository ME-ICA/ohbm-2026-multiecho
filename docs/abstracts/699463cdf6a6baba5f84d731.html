Introduction

Stroke is the leading cause of acquired disability worldwide, with upper-limb sensorimotor impairments affecting ~80% of survivors (Hatem et al., 2016). Early prediction of recovery remains challenging, yet critical for individualized rehabilitation planning. Parieto-frontal connectivity is essential for sensorimotor integration and goal directed motor control, converging evidence links disruptions in these parietofrontal networks to post-stroke sensorimotor deficits. Supporting that goal-directed motor control contributes to post-stroke motor outcomes. However, how parietofrontal functional connectivity evolves from the acute to the chronic phase, and the extent to which early connectivity can predict long-term recovery, remain insufficiently understood. This longitudinal study aimed to investigate whether resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) within parietofrontal motor networks evolves over the first 6 months post-stroke and whether early connectivity predicts chronic-phase visuomotor performance.

Methods

Sixty first-ever ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke patients (30% women; mean age = 54.9 ± 12.8 years) with upper-limb paresis were assessed at 3 weeks and 6 months post-stroke. A visuomotor grip-force control task on the paretic hand, quantified maximal grip strength, precision and power grip accuracy. Structural and resting-state MRI were acquired to quantify corticospinal tract lesion load and functional connectivity. Preprocessing and denoising were conducted using SPM and the CONN toolbox. Right-sided lesions were flipped to the left hemisphere for group analyses. Seed-based rsFC analyses focused on bilateral anterior intraparietal sulcus (AIPS), caudal intraparietal sulcus (CIPS), ventral premotor cortex (PMv), primary motor cortex (M1), and supplementary motor area (SMA), examined ipsilesionally, contralesionally, and interhemispherically. Functional connectivity was analysed using parametric multivariate statistics (cluster-level p-FDR < .05; seed-level p-uncorrected < .05), controlling for age, sex, and lesion volume.

Results

ROI-to-ROI analyses demonstrated significant intra- and interhemispheric connectivity at each time point. Interhemispheric M1–M1 rsFC significantly increased from 3 weeks to 6 months (T(55) = –4.52, p-FDR = 0.000287; MNI (x, y, z) –38, –22, 54), indicating strengthening bilateral motor network during recovery. Average Fugl-Meyer Assessment for Upper Extremity (FMA-UE) scores improved from 33.2 ± 22.9 at 3 weeks to 45.6 ± 19.7 at 6 months, reflecting substantial motor recovery over time. Seed-to-voxel analyses revealed four significant clusters where early rsFC predicted 6-month visuomotor force-tracking error: (1) left SMA with right medial orbital gyrus (peak MNI (x, y, z) 20, 46, –12; k = 192; FDR-corr = .044; T = 4.61, Z = 4.20), (2) right M1 with right cerebellum (peak MNI 46, –60, –26; k = 261; FWE-corr = .024; T = 5.28, Z = 4.69) and (3) the right precentral gyrus (peak MNI (x, y, z) 50, 2, 52; k = 413; FDR-corr = 0.006; T = 3.98, Z = 3.70), and (4) right CIPS with left temporal pole (peak MNI –42, 12, –38; k = 502; FDR-corr = .001; T = 6.11, Z = 5.26). Stronger functional connectivity 3 weeks after stroke in connections between the LSMA-right medial orbital gyrus, RM1–right cerebellum, and RCIPS-left temporal pole was associated with increase in error in the visuomotor task at 6 months, indicating that heightened early coupling in these circuits may reflect maladaptive or inefficient network states. In contrast, stronger early connectivity between RM1 and the right precentral gyrus was related with decrease in error in the visuomotor task at 6 months. These findings highlight a differential role of parietofrontal and motor network interactions in shaping long-term recovery.

Conclusion

Interhemispheric motor connectivity, particularly between bilateral M1 regions, strengthens over the first 6 months following stroke, supporting bilateral motor system reintegration. However, this was not related to sensorimotor outcome. Early seed-to-voxel connectivity from parietofrontal and interhemispheric motor regions, including LSMA, RM1, and RCIPS, with distributed cortical and cerebellar areas, were strongly associated with long-term visuomotor performance, identifying potential functional biomarkers of recovery. These findings suggest a prognostic value of early motor network connectivity in both ipsi- and contralesional hemispheres for predicting individualized recovery trajectories and could inform targeted neuromodulation or training strategies aimed at optimizing post-stroke reorganization.
