Introduction

White matter (WM) and cortical folding are closely linked, exhibiting normal variability across individuals that reflects the diverse patterns of brain organization.1 However, identifying rare patterns remains a challenging task because they are largely hidden by the high inter-subject variability of cortical anatomy.2 In this work, we employed a β-Variational AutoEncoder (β-VAE) to model the variability in WM connectivity across the population, enabling the identification of individuals whose patterns deviate from the baseline through reconstruction error and latent space embeddings.

Methods

We used probabilistic tractography from 37,140 subjects in the UKBioBank database.3,4 Streamlines representing fibers were selected in the global tractogram using a mask surrounding the right Central Sulcus (CS). Next, a density map of the fiber endpoints was computed and then affinely transformed to ICBM09c referential (see Fig. 1-A/B). Then, we trained a depth-3 β-VAE2, which uses 3D convolutions, on 29,492 subjects (see Fig. 1-C/D). We evaluated the detection power of the β-VAE using an annotated subset of subjects with interrupted CS (n=183), serving as proxy of uncommon WM geometry.5 We evaluated whether this rare pattern can be detected, even when it is modeled as part of the normal variability of the population, therefore, 74 interrupted CS were added to the Train set. The remaining subjects were split into two sets.Validation 1 assessed reconstruction error differences between 18 interrupted CS and 7,373 non-interrupted CS (see Fig. 1-E), whereas Validation 2 was used to perform classification using a linear Support Vector Machine (SVM) and the β-VAE embeddings (see Fig. 2-A) from 91 interrupted and 92 non-interrupted CS.

Results

Fig. 1-E shows the reconstruction error distribution corresponding to the largest significant difference between interrupted CS and the rest of the subjects from Validation 1 (Mann-Whitney U). Fig. 2-A shows the results for classification based on embeddings between interrupted and continuous CS, with an Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) above 0.8 for most combinations of β and latent dimension D. Using the SVM, we grouped the subjects not annotated as interrupted CS into 20% probability bins and computed the corresponding average density maps (see Fig. 2-B), while the group average CS is overlaid in purple. A transition can be observed from a continuous CS to a more disorganized pattern, where the WM connectivity begins to invade the sulcal space, along with the merging of the CS with adjacent folding branches. We identified nine potentially new subjects not included in our annotated dataset (see Fig. 2-C, the first two subjects from left to right). 

Conclusion

We demonstrated that the model captures meaningful latent representations of WM connectivity, as evidenced by the significant difference in the reconstruction error, as well as the high AUC obtained. Furthermore, by using the probability bins derived from the SVM, we observed a population-level transition from a continuous, stable CS to a rarer, more disorganized pattern. In future work, we will incorporate a fiber-length threshold to focus on different profiles of local connectivity.
