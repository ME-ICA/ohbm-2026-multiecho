Introduction

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is the most common neurodegenerative disorder, marked by progressive cognitive decline. Although voxel-based morphometry (VBM) has been widely used to assess gray matter volume (GMV) alterations in AD, findings remain highly heterogeneous across studies, limiting the identification of robust structural abnormalities. To address this issue, we applied Seed-based d Mapping with Permutation of Subject Images (SDM-PSI) for a whole-brain voxel-wise meta-analysis, and integrated transcriptional and neurotransmitter mapping to characterize the multiscale neurobiological mechanisms underlying AD-related GMV alterations.

Methods

A whole-brain voxel-wise meta-analysis was conducted using SDM-PSI to identify differences in GMV between AD patients and healthy controls (HCs). Significant GMV clusters were assigned to canonical functional networks defined by the Yeo 7-network parcellation. We annotated these clusters using Neurosynth database to infer associated functional terms. The spatial correspondence between transcriptional data from the Allen Human Brain Atlas and GMV difference z-maps was assessed using partial least squares (PLS) regression, followed by gene set enrichment analysis. Spatial correspondence between the meta-analytic findings and 29 neurotransmitter maps was further assessed with the JuSpace toolbox.

Results

A comprehensive search up to April 16, 2025, identified 70 eligible VBM studies including 2,628 AD patients and 2,508 HCs. AD patients showed significant GMV reductions in the bilateral amygdala, parahippocampal gyrus, and hippocampus (p < 0.05, FWE corrected), primarily involving the Default Mode Network (DMN) and Frontoparietal Network (FPN) of the Yeo 7-network atlas. Sensitivity analyses confirmed high robustness of these findings, with the two main clusters reproducible in 100% and 96.1% of analyses. Heterogeneity across studies was generally low (I² = 29.81%) with minimal evidence of publication bias (Egger test p = 0.021). Neurosynth decoding indicated that atrophied regions were primarily linked to cognitive functions related to emotional processing (particularly fear) and facial expression recognition.. PLS-based transcriptional analysis revealed that genes associated with the spatial pattern of GMV atrophy were mainly enriched in cytoplasmic translation, ribosomal subunits, cadherin-mediated cell adhesion, and ligand-gated ion channel activity. Neurotransmitter mapping demonstrated that AD-related atrophy showed significantly negative spatial correlations with multiple systems, especially the dopaminergic system (DAT rho = -0.67, D1 rho = -0.53, D2 rho = -0.32, FDOPA rho = -0.49, all p < 0.001, FDR corrected) and serotonergic system (5-HT1a rho = -0.60, SERT rho = -0.64, all p < 0.001, FDR corrected).

Conclusion

Structural alterations in AD are characterized predominantly by limbic system atrophy, particularly within the temporal lobe, with pronounced involvement of the DMN and FPN. These alterations show spatial correspondence specific biological processes such as cytoplasmic translation and ligand-gated ion channel activity, as well as serotonin and dopamine neurotransmitter systems. The findings provide novel insights into the neuropathological mechanisms and molecular basis of AD.
