Introduction

Bowers et al. (1990) proposed the continuity model of intuitive decision-making, indicating that intuition is a graded process that evolves from implicit (preconscious) to explicit (conscious) processing (Bowers et al., 1990). The continuity model posited that intuitive decisions emerged through two interactive yet dissociable stages. The initial guiding stage involved a preconscious semantic activation, where distributed neural networks gradually accumulated evidence for potential solutions. In the integration stage, information became consciously available, facilitated by a gradual accumulation of the preactivated semantic representations (Zander, Öllinger, et al., 2016). 

Unfortunately, research on the neural mechanisms underlying the continuity model of intuitive decision-making remains sparse, with very few fMRI studies exploring this area. A study using the triads task proposed by Bowers et al. (1990) illustrated that the inferior frontal gyrus, orbitofrontal cortex, and anterior insula were involved in intuitive decision-making (Zander, Horr, et al., 2016). However, there is currently no literature that directly reports whether the guiding stage and the integration stage follow dissociable functional brain mechanisms.

Intuitive decision-making is an extremely rapid cognitive process that occurs almost in a timescale that exceeds the temporal resolution limits of fMRI, while fNIRS systems achieve sampling rates of 10-50 Hz, enabling precise tracking of its neural underpinnings. Therefore, this study addressed this gap by employing a novel combination of high-density fNIRS and staged behavioral paradigms to: (1) characterize the spatiotemporal dynamics of the guiding stage and integration stage from the perspectives of local brain activity, functional connectivity and dynamic neural patterns; (2) characterize inter-individual variability in the neurofunctional signatures of intuitive decision-making and elucidate their behavioral correlates.

Methods

1.    Participants

Forty-nine participants (mean age 21.2 years, SD 1.37, range 18-23) took part in the fNIRS experiment. Of these participants, four were excluded from further analysis after pre-processing due to excessive head motion.

2.    Stimulus selection

The stimulus set comprised 40 Chinese word triads (30 semantically coherent, 10 incoherent). An independent sample of 59 participants (age-matched to the fNIRS experiment cohort) completed two computerized ratings: (1) 10-point Likert-scale judgments of semantic cohesion for each triad and (2) 10-point associative accessibility Likert-scale of the ease of fourth-word retrieval. 

3.    Experiment design

By combining a semantic coherence judgment task with a subsequent word-stem completion task, we operationalized a graded mapping onto Bowers et al.’s (1990) continuity model of intuitive decision-making. We distinguished the guiding stage and integration stage of intuitive decision-making for coherent triads based on the behavioral performance patterns of participants. An overview of the experimental design is shown in Figure 1.

4.    fNIRS data acquisition

We employed two 16-channel LIGHTNIRS systems (Shimadzu Corp., Kyoto, Japan) to measure concentration changes in oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO), deoxygenated hemoglobin (HbR), and total hemoglobin using three wavelengths (i.e., 780, 805, and 830 nm), with a sampling rate was 13.33 Hz. We used a 48-channel array of optodes (16 light sources/emitters and 16 detectors) covering the dorsolateral frontal cortex and the inferior parietal cortex.

5.    Behavior data analyses

Trials were categorized into the guiding or integration stage of intuitive processes based on participants’ responses to both the semantic coherence judgment task and the associative word completion task. Paired samples t-tests were conducted to compare response times and accuracies between the guiding and integration stages for both tasks.

6 fNIRS data analyses

6.1 Preprocessing

We preprocessed the raw data using the NIRS_KIT toolbox (Hou et al., 2021) in MATLAB R2018b, including drift correction, artifact correction (TDDR method), and filtering (0.01~0.08 Hz, IIR method). 

6.2 Functional Activity Analysis

For each participant, general linear model (GLM) analyses were performed for each of the 48 channels to estimate the hemodynamic response. Statistical analyses were performed using the independent samples t-tests to evaluate the difference between the guiding stage and the integration stage on the beta value for each channel. FDR was subsequently applied to conduct multiple comparison corrections for t-tests for the fNIRS channels.

6.3 Functional Connectivity Analysis

For each participant, stage-specific functional connectivity matrices (48 × 48) were generated by computing pairwise Pearson’s r correlation coefficients between channels, yielding two distinct functional networks for each participant, representing the guiding and integration processes stages, respectively. We performed the independent samples t-tests to assess the between-group differences in inter-regional correlations. FDR correction was then applied to correct for multiple comparisons.

6.4 Multivariate Pattern Analysis

To identify spatially distributed and temporally dynamic neural patterns that discriminate between the guiding and integration stages, we employed multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) on HbO time series, leveraging the spatial and temporal resolution of fNIRS.

6.5 Connectome-based Prediction Modeling

We used Connectome-based predictive modeling (CPM) (Shen et al., 2017) to predict individuals’ behavioral indices from both intuitive decision-making processing stages based on the task-evoked functional networks in the word triad association task, which were obtained by correlating the time series of the whole task. Prediction models were trained and tested using a leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) method.

Results

1.    Activity differences between the two stages

To characterize stage-dependent neural activity patterns, we conducted FDR-corrected (p < 0.05) paired-sample t-tests between the guiding stage and the integration stage in the beta values of the GLM across all 48 fNIRS channels. This revealed distinct spatiotemporal activation patterns between the two stages. Significantly stronger HbO responses during the guiding stage were observed in the right middle frontal gyrus (MFG; channel 9: t(47) = -3.26, p = 0.002) and left medial superior frontal gyrus (mSFG; channel 25: t(47) = -2.39, p = 0.021). In contrast, the left inferior parietal lobule (IPL; channel 12) exhibited reversed activation patterns (t(47) = 3.36, p = 0.002), with greater engagement during the integration stage.

2.    Functional connectivity differences between the two stages

Five edges showed significant state differences after FDR correction. Specifically, during the integration stage, stronger functional connectivity was observed between the superior/middle frontal gyrus and the inferior parietal areas. In contrast, during the guiding stage, stronger functional connectivity was evident between the superior/middle frontal gyrus and the sensorimotor areas.

3.    Spatial and temporal dynamics of discriminative patterns

The word triad association task consisted of a semantic coherence judgment task part and an associative word completion task part, with an intuitive decision-making process usually occurring during the semantic coherence judgment phase. Accordingly, we exclusively report results from the first part in our analyses. The hemodynamic delay (500ms) associated with this part was explicitly incorporated into the analyses. After binary classification analysis distinguishing the guiding and integration stages, we identified statistically significant time points at 2295 ms and 5940 ms (FDR-corrected, p < 0.05). These temporal windows are interpreted as representing critical neural signatures of intuitive decision-making processes. We performed interpretability analysis by examining channel-wise feature weights at these timepoints to identify the most contributive features underlying the model’s predictive decisions. At the time point of 2295 ms, the left middle frontal gyrus exhibited a preferential encoding of the guiding stage (FDR-corrected, p < 0.05). At the time point of 5940 ms, the left superior medial frontal gyrus and the middle frontal gyrus showed a preferential encoding of the guiding stage, while the right middle frontal gyrus and the right supramarginal gyrus exhibited a significant classification toward the integration stage (FDR-corrected, p < 0.05).

4.    Behavior prediction based on the CPM 

CPM results indicated that the task-evoked functional network can effectively predict individual differences in the response accuracy in both the semantic coherence judgment task and the associative word completion task. A LOOCV approach was implemented to examine whether the task-evoked functional networks can predict a novel individual’s behavioral indices (i.e., response time and accuracy). The performance of the predictive models was assessed by Pearson correlation between predicted and observed behavioral indices. Results showed that the positive model was able to predict response accuracy in novel individuals (correlation coefficient between observed and predicted scores: r = 0.25, p permutation = 0.015). Based on the positive model, which reliably predicted individuals’ behavioral indices, we identified a contributing network in this prediction. This bilateral network was composed of three areas, which were composed of the left superior frontal gyrus, right precentral gyrus, and right inferior parietal areas.

Conclusion

This study provided novel evidence for stage-specific neural mechanisms underlying intuitive decision-making. First, the guiding phase predominantly recruits functional connectivity between superior/middle frontal regions and sensorimotor areas, reflecting their role in automatic semantic activation and response preparation. Second, the integration phase engages distinct fronto-parietal circuits involving these same frontal regions and the inferior parietal lobule, supporting their contribution to solution awareness and conflict resolution. Third, early guiding stage encoding was observed in the left middle frontal gyrus, while late phase processing was observed in the left medial superior frontal gyrus and the left middle frontal gyrus sustaining guiding representations, and right frontoparietal networks shifted to integration representations, suggesting complementary hemispheric specialization during intuitive decisions. Last, we identify a robust predictive network comprising the left superior frontal gyrus, right precentral gyrus, and right inferior parietal regions that reliably predicts individual differences in intuitive decision accuracy. These findings not only advance our understanding of the dynamic neural architecture supporting intuitive cognition but also establish the first functional connectivity-based biomarker for intuitive ability, opening new avenues for research into individual differences in non-deliberative decision processes.
