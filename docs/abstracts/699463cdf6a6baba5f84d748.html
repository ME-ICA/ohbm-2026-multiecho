Introduction

Adolescence is a critical developmental period marked by pronounced changes in social behavior and rapid maturation of brain white matter microstructure. Prosocial behaviors—including trust, altruism, and fairness—undergo substantial developmental refinement across adolescence, accompanied by increasing individual variability. Despite their importance for social adaptation, the neurobiological substrates underlying age-related changes in prosocial decision-making remain incompletely understood. Diffusion magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) provides a noninvasive means to characterize white matter development1. Although prior research has linked prosocial behavior to cortical structure and function2, evidence regarding the role of white matter microstructure remains scarce. To date, there is a lack of empirical work examining how developmental changes in white matter properties support prosocial decision-making during adolescence, a period characterized by profound social and neurobiological change. To address these limitations, the present study investigated multivariate associations among demographic factors (age and sex), behavioral measures of prosocial decision-making, and white matter microstructure in typically developing adolescents. Using multiset canonical correlation analysis (MCCA), we aimed to identify shared modes of covariation that capture age-related developmental changes linking distributed white matter networks to prosocial behavior across multiple economic decision-making contexts.

Methods

Eighty-six typically developing adolescents (10–16 years; 39 girls) participated in the study. Prosocial behavior was assessed using five well-established economic games: the Dictator Game, Prisoner’s Dilemma Game, Public Goods Game, Trust Game, and Ultimatum Game. These tasks provided multiple indices of trust, resource allocation, and giving behavior. dMRI data were acquired using a 3T Siemens PrismaFit scanner with a multi-shell protocol (6 b = 0 s/mm2 images; b = 1000 and 2000 s/mm2, 50 directions per b-value; 2 mm isotropic voxels). dMRI data were then preprocessed to correct for susceptibility-induced distortion, head motion, and eddy-current distortion using FSL. Then we identified tract-averaged fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) from major commissural, association, and projection pathways using Automated Fiber Quantification in Python (pyAFQ)3. Dimensionality reduction was performed separately for demographic variables (age and sex), behavioral measures, and diffusion metrics using principal component analysis. Multiset canonical correlation analysis was then applied to identify shared modes of covariation across these domains.

Results

The first canonical mode revealed an interpretable multivariate pattern linking demographic factors, prosocial behavior, and white matter microstructure. Age showed a strong negative correlation with the canonical variate, whereas the contribution of sex was comparatively modest. Behaviorally, multiple indices of prosocial decision-making—including trust, resource allocation, and giving behavior across economic games—loaded positively, indicating that this mode captured a task-general prosocial tendency rather than game-specific effects. With respect to white matter microstructure, this mode was characterized by widespread negative loadings of FA and positive loadings of MD across major commissural, association, and projection pathways. These associations were particularly pronounced in tracts connecting frontal, parietal, and limbic regions, including the anterior corpus callosum, arcuate fasciculus, superior longitudinal fasciculus, and cingulum. Together, these findings suggest that the first canonical mode reflects a global developmental pattern of white matter maturation covarying with prosocial decision-making across adolescence.

Conclusion

The first canonical mode identified in this study represents a strongly age-linked developmental dimension underlying the association between prosocial decision-making and white matter microstructure during adolescence. Rather than being driven by a single tract, this mode involved distributed fronto-parietal and limbic pathways, indicating that prosocial behavior emerges from coordinated maturation of large-scale white matter networks supporting social cognition and behavioral regulation. Although sex contributed modestly to this mode, age was the dominant organizing factor, suggesting that developmental stage plays a central role in structuring individual differences in prosocial decision-making. The present findings are therefore best interpreted as reflecting a coupled developmental process, in which age-related changes in white matter microstructure and prosocial behavior co-occur across adolescence. Rather than isolating a prosociality-specific neural substrate, this multivariate pattern highlights neurodevelopment as a key mechanism underlying variability in social decision-making during this period.
