Introduction

The dorsal striatum (comprising the caudate nucleus and putamen) and the globus pallidus play a central role in motor, cognitive, emotional, and integrative functions. However, their microstructural organisation and interindividual variability remain insufficiently characterised in a common stereotaxic space. Consequently, current functional and connectivity studies often lack the anatomical precision required to differentiate the contribution of distinct striatal and pallidal compartments. Although existing maps of subcortical nuclei based on in vivo MRI provide valuable macroscopic information, they lack cytoarchitectonic detail and do not include a subdivision of the striatum.

The aims of this study were therefore to examine the microstructure in cell body-stained sections of ten postmortem brains and delineate the borders of the dorsal striatum and globus pallidus, as well as their subregions; to generate probabilistic maps of these structures in stereotaxic reference space to provide a reference for neuroimaging studies; and to produce an ultrahigh-resolution map in the BigBrain template.^1

Methods

The subregions of the caudate nucleus, putamen, pontine fibres, and the external and internal globus pallidus were delineated microscopically on every 60th cell-body-stained section (with 1 μm in-plane resolution) of ten brains (five female, aged 37-85 years), including the BigBrain dataset (Figure 1). These delineations were based on cytoarchitectonic criteria, including cell size, neuronal density, cellular shape and arrangement, clustering, and fibre density laminae.

To generate high-resolution models in BigBrain space, we employed a deep learning-based tool to delineate the remaining sections.^2 All ten brains were used to create probability maps capturing intersubject variability in the volume and location of the areas. For this purpose, the brains were 3D reconstructed and superimposed in standard reference spaces (MNI Colin27 and MNI ICBM152).^3

Innovative tools, including texture analysis^4 and the Contour Proposal Network^5, were then applied to characterise the subdivisions in more detail. Texture analysis was performed on regions of interest derived from the delineations, using the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix method^4 to quantify spatial relationships and intensity patterns in greyscale images. We extracted 21 modified Haralick texture features, such as correlation, homogeneity, and dissimilarity, to characterise the tissue’s cellular architecture. Differences between striatal and pallidal subdivisions were assessed using an independent-samples Kruskal-Wallis test. In addition, neurons were segmented using a Contour Proposal Network based on Fourier descriptors, enabling precise measurements of the number, size, and morphology of neurons. The volumes of all regions were estimated using Cavalieri’s principle and compared between hemispheres and sexes using permutation testing, with a significance threshold of p < 0.05.

Results

We delineated the caudate nucleus, putamen and globus pallidus in both hemispheres over their entire extent. High-resolution 3D maps of the dorsal striatum and pallidum were generated, showing their shapes and neighbourhood relationships with high anatomical precision. Five structures were identified within the caudate nucleus: the lateral caudate, the medial caudate, the subcaudate nucleus, the caudate portion of the pontine fibres and the tangential layer. In the putamen, three structures were delineated: the medial, lateral and ventral parts. Additionally, we identified pontine components (the caudate pontine fibres and putamen pontine fibres) and pallidal subdivisions (the external and internal globus pallidus, separated by the medial and external medullary laminae). 

The dorsal striatum showed two major organisational principles: (1) dorsoventral gradients with respect to the density of medium-sized neurons, which decreased gradually from ventral to dorsal within both the putamen and the caudate nucleus. Cell counts confirmed significantly higher neuronal density in the ventral compared to the dorsal caudate (p < 0.001). (2) Distinct cell-cluster formations that dominate the dorsal caudate and putamen, but are absent in the ventral and caudo-ventral regions. 

There were no significant effects of sex on the shrinkage-corrected volumes for each nucleus as tested by permutation testing. However, the volume analysis revealed a significantly larger right than left caudate, driven primarily by the lateral caudate subregion. 

Intersubject variability is reflected in the probabilistic maps as part of the Julich Brain Atlas.^3 They will be made available via EBRAINS and other platforms. The probabilistic maps showed low regional variability, except in the caudate tail, where variations between brains were high. The maximum probability map of this region provides non-overlapping 3D representations of all basal ganglia subregions in MNI space.

Conclusion

This study presents the first high-resolution 3D cytoarchitectonic probabilistic maps of the human dorsal striatum and its subregions, as well as the pallidal nuclei. These maps allow for more detailed characterisation of the microstructure, revealing microstructural gradients and cell-cluster patterns that may reflect distinct modes of intrinsic processing. By capturing interindividual variability in a common MNI space, the resulting maps provide a novel link between histological microarchitecture and neuroimaging. This enables improved localisation in fMRI, PET, and diffusion MRI, and offers an anatomically grounded framework for neurosurgical targeting and computational modelling.
