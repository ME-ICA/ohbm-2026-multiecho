Introduction

Introduction: White matter (WM) integrity is critical for all brain functions. In humans, WM integrity can be quantified using diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), most commonly through fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD). In individuals with diabetes and obesity, disruptions of WM integrity were found in specific brain tracts, as indicated with reduced FA and greater MD values1,2. 

Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) is a non-invasive neurostimulation method, influencing brain function and behavior. Typically, anodal tDCS enhances cortical excitability, while cathodal tDCS has opposite effects. Previous evidence suggests that anodal tDCS enhances FA in regions adjacent to the genu of the corpus callosum3, a tract reported to exhibit reduced FA in obesity2.

Recent work from our group showed that it is possible to stimulate a brain network important for eating behavior regulation (i.e. the hypothalamus appetite-control network) using network-targeted tDCS (net-tDCS). Thereby, anodal net-tDCS yielded the most robust effects, showing greater cognitive control and lower food intake compared to sham4,5. 

However, the underlying neural mechanisms of net-tDCS remain unknown. For this, our study investigates whether net-tDCS targeting the hypothalamus appetite-control network alters microstructural integrity in persons with normal weight. 

Methods

Methods: We enrolled 33 individuals with normal-weight (16 male) in a double-blind, placebo-controlled, within-subject study employing net-tDCS followed by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Participants (19-57 years of age; BMI range from 19.4 to 24.7 kg/m2) completed two 25-min active net-tDCS and one sham session in a counterbalanced order on separate visits. 

Active net-tDCS included cathodal and anodal stimulation of the hypothalamus-appetite control network, as previously described4. In short, electrode position and stimulation intensity were determined using the Stimweaver™ algorithm, resulting in a 12-channel optimized montage. The target network was defined based on the correlation strength of the hypothalamic resting-state FC network from the Neurosynth website (https://neurosynth.org/locations/6_2_-10_6/). During anodal net-tDCS, brain regions with a positive functional connectivity (FC) to the hypothalamus were stimulated, regions with a negative FC were inhibited. For cathodal net-tDCS, this paradigm was reversed. Stimulation was applied with a network-targeted tDCS device (Starstim® 32, Neuroelectrics Barcelona S.L.U., Barcelona, Spain), which was controlled by the corresponding NIC 2.0 software (NIC2 v2.0.11). A maximum of 4 mA of total injected current was used, with each electrode contributing up to 2 mA in absolute terms. A reference electrode to carry the return current was placed on the participant’s ear.

Directly after stimulation, imaging was performed on a Siemens Prisma 3.0 T MRI with a standard 64 channel head coil (Magnetom Prisma, Siemens Healthcare GmbH, Erlangen, Germany). Diffusion images were acquired based on the acquisition scheme dMRI_dir98_AP of the Human Connectome project with the following parameters: TR=3230 ms, TE=89.2ms, voxel size: 1.5x1.5x1.5 mm3, slices: 92, FoV: 210 mm, 98 directions, multiband factor= 4 and two non zero b-values (1500 s/mm2, 3000 s/mm2). Additionally, a T1-weighted image was acquired as anatomical reference (TR = 2300 ms, TE  = 2.98 ms). Diffusion data was analyzed with the ACID toolbox (https://bitbucket.org/siawoosh/acid-artefact-correction-in-diffusion-mri) implemented in SPM12. Preprocessing included FSL eddy distortion correction, adaptive denoising and Rician bias correction. Because we acquired diffusion images with a second higher b-value, a diffusion tensor model was expanded by the kurtosis tensor. We used nonlinear least squared method for model fitting DTI. FA and MD maps were computed for further analyses. For group analyses, tissue specific smoothing was performed to minimize partial volume effects. Regions of interests (ROI) were 3 predefined tracts (genu of the corpus callosum, retrolenticular part of the internal capsule, and anterior limb of the internal capsule), based on our target network4 and previous findings on reduced FA in persons with obesity and diabetes1,2. Second-level statistical analyses were performed using full-factorial models with the factors sex and stimulation condition and age as a covariate. Whole-brain results were thresholded at p<0.001 uncorrected and p<0.05 family-wise error (FWE) small volume corrected (SVC) for each ROI. Bonferroni-Holm correction was used to correct for the number of ROIs. Significance levels were set at p<0.05. Figure 1 depicts the study design.

Results

Results:  We observed significant greater FA values after anodal compared to sham net-tDCS in the right genu of the corpus callosum (t = 4.37, pFWE<0.05, SVC), the left retrolenticular part of the internal capsule (t = 4.14, pFWE<0.05, SVC), and the right anterior limb of the internal capsule (t = 4.37, pFWE<0.05, SVC). Moreover, we could observe significant lower MD values after anodal compared to sham net-tDCS in the center of the genu of the corpus callosum (t = 4.11, pFWE<0.05, SVC). No differences in FA and MD were observed for cathodal compared to sham net-tDCS. See Figure 2 for an overview of the results. 

Conclusion

Conclusion: Anodal net-tDCS, applied to enhance neuronal excitation within brain regions important for eating behavior regulation, increased FA and lowered MD in tracts previously linked to metabolic dysregulation. The results suggest improved microstructural integrity directly after stimulation. Future research is needed to investigate the durability of these effects and their behavioral relevance. Additionally, it remains to be determined whether net-tDCS could serve as a potential therapeutic application in metabolic diseases.
