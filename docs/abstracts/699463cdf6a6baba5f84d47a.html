Introduction

Epidemiological evidence highlights the substantial global burden of severe psychotic disorders like schizophrenia (SCZ), psychotic bipolar disorder (PBD), and schizoaffective disorder (SAD). Advancing their nosology requires understanding both heterogeneity (individual variability) and homogeneity (shared features) to enable precise diagnosis and therapy. Current diagnosis relies on subjective phenomenology, leading to high misdiagnosis rates with serious clinical consequences. Deciphering these dimensions is crucial for establishing biological diagnostic criteria.

Electroencephalography (EEG) during an auditory oddball task captures real-time synaptic activity and elicits biomarkers like the P300 potential. Task-modulated functional connectivity (FC) further reveals brain network disruptions. While the FCs in psychotic patients in principle contain both subject-specific heterogeneous features as well as homogeneous features reflective of disorder pathology, traditional FC analysis often conflates both components within a single integrated measure. 

In addition to macro electrophysiological depictions, it is equally crucial to incorporate multiscale micro genetic and cell-specific transcriptomic assessments to establish whether the decomposed features are genetically controlled and thus possess distinct transcriptional signatures. While, there remains limited exploration into the extent to which genetic and neurochemical factors contribute to variations in FC profiles in different subspaces of psychotic disorders.

Herein, to isolate disorder-specific homogeneity from individual heterogeneity, we applied Common Orthogonal Basis Extraction (COBE) to decompose FCs into shared and individualized subspaces. Thereafter, individualized and shared FCs were separately utilized to achieve accurate symptom prediction, psychotic disorder classification, and to provide molecularly validated biomarkers, paving the way for biologically-based diagnosis and targeted therapies.

Methods

Participants. This study utilized data from the Bipolar-Schizophrenia Network on Intermediate Phenotypes consortia. The discovery cohort comprised 187 patients with psychotic bipolar disorder (PBD), 182 with schizophrenia (SCZ), and 129 with schizoaffective disorder (SAD) from B-SNIP1. An independent replication cohort included 95 PBD, 235 SCZ, and 174 SAD patients from B-SNIP2. Diagnoses were confirmed via the Structured Clinical Interview for DSM-IV Disorders. Symptom severity was assessed using the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) and Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF). Standardized protocols were used across recruitment sites. The study received ethical approval, and all participants provided written informed consent.

Experiment and data acquisition. Participants performed an auditory oddball task involving 567 standard (1000 Hz) and 100 target (1400 Hz) tones. They were instructed to press a button upon detecting a target tone. EEG was recorded using 64-channel arrays. Preprocessing included re-referencing, bandpass filtering (0.5-45 Hz), epoching (-200 to 800 ms), baseline correction, and artifact rejection. After preprocessing, data from 401 patients in the discovery cohort and 471 in the replication cohort were retained for analysis.

FC Decomposition by COBE. Cortical activity was source-localized using sLORETA, parcellated into 238 regions of interest. Phase-Envelope Coupling (PEC) was calculated to estimate frequency-specific oscillatory synchronization between regions, resulting in a large-scale cortical FC matrix for each participant. The COBE was applied to decompose the raw FC matrices into two components: a shared subspace containing common FC patterns across patients within that group, and an individualized subspace containing participant-unique features. This separation aimed to isolate disorder-homogeneous from subject-heterogeneous neural signatures. 

Psychotic Severity Prediction Based on Individualized FC Features. A combined Connectome-Based Predictive Modeling (CPM) and LASSO regression framework was used. Edge-wise correlations between individualized FC features and clinical scores (PANSS and GAF) identified predictive connections. LASSO regression then built a regularized model for symptom severity prediction, validated via five-fold cross-validation. Predictive performance was compared against models using raw and shared FCs. 

Three-Class Classification Based on Shared FC Features. A multiclass Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifier with ten-fold cross-validation was implemented to differentiate PBD, SCZ, and SAD. Discriminative features were the weights of the shared FC patterns. Feature selection was performed per fold based on F-scores. Performance was evaluated using accuracy, recall, precision, and F1-score. Comparative classification was performed using raw FC, individualized FC, and P300 amplitude features. 

Neurotransmitter Receptors and Transporters Analysis. Spatial correlations were computed between regional shared/individualized FC patterns and the cortical densities of 19 neurotransmitter receptors/transporters, derived from a published PET atlas. Significance was assessed using spin-based spatial permutation tests (5,000 iterations) with FDR correction. 

Molecular Profiles Associated with Inter-Psychosis Differences. Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression modeled the covariance between cortical gene expression data (from the Allen Human Brain Atlas) and inter-group differences in shared FC patterns (F-maps). Metascape was used for KEGG pathway and Gene Ontology enrichment analysis of PLS1+/− gene sets. Cell-type and cortical layer specificity of these gene sets were evaluated by overlapping them with markers from single-cell and laminar transcriptomic studies, using permutation testing.

Statistical Analyses. Group differences in FCs and P300 were assessed using independent sample t-tests and one-way ANOVA, with FDR correction for multiple comparisons.

Results

Individualized FCs Reflect Heterogeneity and Predict Symptom Severity

The COBE decomposition isolated individualized FC patterns capturing subject-specific variations. Using these individualized FCs within a combined CPM and LASSO regression framework enabled significant prediction of overall symptom severity (PANSS total score) for each disorder: PBD (r=0.23, p=0.004), SCZ (r=0.19, p=0.026), and SAD (r=0.22, p=0.03). Predictive models based on individualized FCs consistently outperformed those using raw or shared FCs, which failed to predict PANSS scores. The spatial topology of the most predictive individualized FC connections differed among disorders, involving left-lateralized fronto-parietal linkages in PBD, cross-hemispheric parietal connections in SCZ, and fronto-parietal/parieto-occipital connections in SAD. At the network level, predictive features primarily involved between-network connections (e.g., DMN-AN for PBD).

Shared FCs Reflect Homogeneity and Enable Disorder Classification

The shared FC subspace, derived from the first three common COBE components, contained group-common patterns. While all disorders showed strong SN/SMN-centered architecture, significant inter-group differences were identified. Compared to PBD, SCZ showed reduced connectivity between the DMN and SSHN. SAD exhibited weaker connectivity between VN and AN, between DMN and FPN/SN compared to PBD. Differences between SCZ and SAD involved DMN connections with SSHN/VN/DAN. These differences were more pronounced in shared FCs than in raw FCs. 

A multiclass SVM classifier using shared FC features achieved 79.3% accuracy in differentiating PBD, SCZ, and SAD, significantly outperforming classifiers using raw FCs (44.2%), individualized FCs (24.7%), or P300 amplitudes (43.7%). Confusion matrices revealed SCZ was diagnosed most reliably (reliability ~1.0), followed by PBD (0.81), while SAD was less distinguishable (0.4), aligning with its known diagnostic complexity.

Molecular Correlates of FC Patterns

Neurotransmitter Receptor Densities: Regional shared FCs across all disorders showed significant negative spatial correlations with serotonin (5-HT1A, 5-HT2A, and 5-HT4) and dopamine (DAT) receptor densities, and positive correlations with cannabinoid (CB1) receptor density. Regional individualized FCs for PBD and SCZ correlated positively with serotonin, acetylcholine (VAChT), and dopamine (D2) systems, and negatively with the CB1 system. Inter-psychosis differences in shared FCs (F-map) were positively correlated with 5-HT1B and CB1 densities, and negatively correlated with densities of 5-HT1A, 5-HT4, 5-HTT, VAChT, D2, and H3 receptors (see Fig. 2).

Transcriptomic Associations: Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression linked inter-psychosis FC differences (F-map) to cortical gene expression patterns from the Allen Human Brain Atlas. The first PLS component (PLS1) explained significant variance (34.56%, p<0.001) and its weighted gene expression map correlated spatially with the F-map (r=0.59, p<0.001). Genes negatively associated with PLS1 (PLS1−, n=1,028) were enriched for synaptic and behavioral biological processes (e.g., "synapse organization"). Genes positively associated (PLS1+, n=2,323) were enriched for metabolic and transport processes.

Conclusion

Decomposing FCs via COBE successfully isolated clinically relevant dimensions: individualized FCs captured heterogeneous, symptom-predictive features, while shared FCs reflected disorder-homogeneous signatures enabling accurate diagnostic classification. These neurophysiological patterns were further validated by distinct associations with multiscale molecular architectures, including neurotransmitter systems and cell-type-specific gene expression.
