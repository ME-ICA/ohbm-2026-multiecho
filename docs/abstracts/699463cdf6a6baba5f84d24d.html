Introduction

Diffusion MRI (dMRI) tractography is a valuable tool for pre-surgical planning, helping to avoid surgical white matter tract injuries.1 At Royal Children's Hospital, Melbourne, Australia, expert-guided manual ROI-based tractography using multi-shell diffusion weighted MRI (dMRI) has been instrumental in visualizing the optic radiations (OR) for epilepsy and brain tumor surgeries, supporting both pre-surgical planning and intraoperative guidance to preserve visual function. However, multi-shell acquisition poses a barrier to widespread adoption due to scanner hardware limitations and restricted clinical scan time at many centers. This study investigates whether reduced dMRI protocols, such as halved acquisition time or single-shell sampling at low or high b-values, can produce OR tractography of comparable quality to those reconstructed using a multi-shell protocol, for surgical use.

Methods

Patients

Nineteen children (median age 9.79 years, IQR 6.84–12.45; 9 females) with drug-resistant focal epilepsy or brain tumors who underwent temporal, parietal, or occipital resections at the Royal Children’s Hospital were included. Histopathology comprised 5 low-grade gliomas, 4 gangliogliomas, 3 dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumors, 4 hippocampal sclerosis, and one each of cavernous hemangioma, encephalocele, and tuberous sclerosis complex. All patients underwent OR tractography for pre-surgical planning and intra-operative image guidance.

 MRI

Images were acquired using a 3 Tesla Siemens Biograph scanner, included a T1-weighted MPRAGE sequence (TR/TE = 1900/2.69 ms, 0.8 mm³ isotropic voxels), and a 2-shell diffusion-weighted MR image (dMRI) sequence acquired using multi-band EPI (2.3 mm³ isotropic voxels, TE/TR = 114/4000ms, 11 interleaved b0s, b = 1000 s/mm², 30 directions, and b = 3000 s/mm², 60 directions), plus a reverse-phase b0 pair. Total acquisition (TA) =7.5 minutes. This original dataset is referred to as dMRI scheme A (original-2-90).

Four experimental dMRI schemes with reduced protocols were derived from scheme A (Figure 1): 

  • dMRI scheme B (halved-2-45): 5 b0s, b = 1000 s/mm², 15 directions, and b = 3000 s/mm², 30 directions, TA = 3.75 minutes
  • dMRI scheme C (high-b-60): 5 b0s, b = 3000 s/mm², 60 directions, TA = 5 minutes. 
  • dMRI scheme D (high-b-45): 5 b0s, b = 3000 s/mm², 45 directions, TA = 3.75 minutes.
  • dMRI scheme E (low-b-30): 5 b0s, b = 1000 s/mm², 30 directions, TA = 2.5 minutes. 

MRI pre-processing

T1-weighted images were aligned to dMRI using rigid body registration using ANTs. All dMRI data were pre-processed with MRtrix3 and FSL, to correct for imaging noise, Gibbs ringing artefacts, motion and susceptibility distortion, and bias field inhomogeneity. Lesion segmentations were performed manually by an experienced neurosurgeon, using 3D T1 as the anatomical reference, with contrast-enhanced T1, T2, and FLAIR sequences overlaid to improve lesion delineation accuracy.  

Tractography

Optic radiation tractography from the operating hemisphere was performed using the iFOD-2 probabilistic algorithm in MRtrix3.2  Fiber orientation distributions (FODs) were estimated using multi-shell multi-tissue (MSMT) CSD3 for schemes A and B, and single-shell 3-tissue (SS3T) CSD4 for schemes C, D and E. Expert-guided tracking region of interests (ROIs) defined on the original data were applied to each reduced dMRI scheme space for consistent tracking. All other tractography parameters were held constant. For each experimental dMRI scheme, OR tractography was repeated 10 times. All tract masks were in the original imaging space for comparison. Analysis 

Tracts from the original 2-shell scheme (A), used in surgical planning, served as the reference. Dice Similarity (DS) scores were computed to quantify spatial overlap between reference OR tracts and those generated from each reduced scheme (B-E). To estimate inherent variability in the reference, scheme A was repeated 10 times and DS scores were calculated against the original tract.

ANOVA comparisons of DS scores were conducted using a linear mixed model with subjects as a random effect, followed by pairwise t-tests (significance threshold p < 0.05).

To increase clinical relevance, we also compared tract-to-lesion (TTL) distances across schemes. The TTL distance was defined as the Euclidean distance from the OR tract mask to the lesion, computed using a distance map from Convert3D. TTL comparisons also used the same ANOVA and pairwise testing framework.

Effect sizes (Cohen’s d) were reported for all analyses, with thresholds of ≤ 0.2, > 0.2 to < 0.8, and ≥ 0.8 defined small, medium and large effect sizes, respectively.

Results

Visual assessment showed that schemes  B, C and D produced OR reconstructions closely resembling scheme A, while scheme E shows notably noisier, and less reliable in reconstructions. Figure 1 illustrates an example case, with the white arrow indicating the basal temporal tumor.

 Dice Similarity scores 

The top panel of Figure 2 shows the DS score distributions comparing each reduced scheme with scheme A; the bottom panel of Figure 2 summaries the DS scores. Median DS scores were similar across schemes A-D, while scheme E showed substantially lower values. A linear mixed-model ANOVA confirmed significant differences between schemes (p < 2.2e-16). Pairwise comparisons between all reduced schemes with scheme A were significant, with medium effect sizes for schemes B-D (0.4-0.55) and a large effect size for scheme E (~3).

Most pairwise comparisons between reduced schemes were also significant, except between schemes B and C (small effect sizes < 0.07). Within the high-b schemes (C vs D), increasing directions improved DS scores slightly (small effect size < 0.07). Schemes with the same 45 directions (B and D) performed comparably. Comparisons involving scheme E consistently showed large effect sizes (> 2).

 Tract-to-lesion distances

The middle panel of Figure 2 shows TTL distance distributions, with summary statistics shown in the bottom panel. The TTL distances were similar between schemes B and A. Schemes C and D produced slightly larger TTL distances than scheme A, suggesting tract under-reach (i.e. false-negative). In contrast, scheme E produced smaller and negative TTL distances compared to scheme A, indicating overestimation of tract extent (i.e. false-positive).

ANOVA showed significant differences among schemes. Pairwise comparisons were all statistically significant; however, effect sizes were very small (< 0.05) for all comparisons except those involving scheme E, which showed medium effect sizes (~ 0.3).

Conclusion

Our experiments showed that for CSD-based OR tractography used in pre-surgical planning in children with epilepsy and brain tumors, reduced dMRI schemes-either by halving the 2-shell protocols (scheme B) or using high-b-value single-shell (schemes C and D)- produced significant differences in tract spatial overlap compared to the original 2-shell scheme. However, these quantitative differences did not translate into clinical meaningful differences in TTL distances, as all effect sizes were very small. This suggests using these tractography for surgical decision-making would be unaffected in these cases. A key commonality across schemes A-D is the use of at least 45 total diffusion gradient directions.

Increasing the number of gradient directions from 45 to 60 in the high-b-value schemes (C and D) did not produce meaningful changes in DS scores or TTL distances, consistent with prior CSD simulations showing minimal modelling gains beyond 45 directions.5 Likewise, we found no meaningful performance differences between the halved 2-shell scheme (B) and the high-b-value single-shell scheme (D) with the same gradient directions, supporting previous findings on the comparable performance of MSMT-CSD and SS3T-CSD models (Dhollander et al., 2016).

In contrast, the reduced low-b, low direction scheme (E) produced substantially lower DS scores and significantly different TTL distances compared with all other reduced schemes and the original 2-shell protocol, with large effect sizes indicating robust evidence. This finding supports technical recommendations for the SS3T-CSD and any CSD-based models: high b-values (> 2500 s/mm²) or high angular resolution (≥ 45 directions) data are required for accurate FOD estimates to resolve crossing fibres (https://3tissue.github.io/doc/ss3t-csd.html). At low b-values (1000 s/mm²), insufficient suppression of extra-axonal signal degrades FOD accuracy.

Clinically, this indicates that low-b, low-direction data (scheme E) should not be used for CSD-based OR tractography in paediatric epilepsy or tumour surgery. This scheme risked overestimating tracts (false positives), which could mislead surgeons and risk incomplete resections with unacceptable neuro-oncological outcomes.

 In conclusion, this study shows that reduced diffusion MRI schemes can do well at delineating the OR while guided by expert-guided tracking ROIs. Despite differences in DS scores, halved the 2-shell protocol or using a single high-b-value shell schemes did not produce clinically meaningful differences in OR tractography for surgical planning, compared to the original 2-shell protocol. We recommend using acquisition schemes with ≥ 45 diffusion directions. Conversely, low-b, low-direction data should be avoided, as it introduces tractography errors that could mislead surgeons and risk lesion under-resection. These findings provide preliminary guidance for developing time-efficient dMRI protocols that preserve OR tractography quality in epilepsy and tumour surgery. Validation in larger and different surgical cohorts and across other clinically relevant tracts is warranted.
