Introduction

Developmental dyscalculia (DD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder marked by persistent difficulties in acquiring and using numerical skills, affecting academic achievement and daily functioning. These deficits are associated with altered brain structure and function, particularly in the intraparietal sulcus (IPS) and broader parietal, frontal, and insular systems.^1 Longitudinal studies indicate that individuals with DD show improvements in numerical performance over time, yet persistent deficits remain, as reflected in both behavioral outcomes and brain-based measures.^2 While localized activations in DD are well documented, fewer studies have examined functional connectivity within numerical networks. A key unresolved question is how brain regions involved in numerical processing exert directed influence on one another. In previous work, we addressed this question using effective connectivity, and demonstrated that number-order processing engages the right pre-supplementary motor area (preSMA) as an orchestrating hub, with DD showing altered modulation of connectivity to the right ventral premotor cortex from the right IPS, right preSMA, and right dorsal anterior insula.^3 In the present study, we examined long-term changes in effective connectivity in DD. Given the developmental shift of symbolic number processing from right- to left-hemispheric dominance and their central role, we focused on the bilateral preSMA and IPS. To our knowledge, this is the first study of developmental changes in effective connectivity in DD. We hypothesized that longitudinal improvements in symbolic number processing would involve increased engagement of the left IPS and altered interhemispheric connectivity between the IPS. We further expected that improvements in number-order performance would be accompanied by increased self-inhibition of the bilateral preSMA, reflecting reduced reliance on cognitive control due to more automatized processing.

Methods

Seventeen individuals with a diagnosis of developmental dyscalculia (DD; 14 females) performed a symbolic number fMRI task at two time points separated by an average of 4.5 years. The task included two conditions: number order (deciding whether triplets of Arabic digits were sequential) and number identification (deciding whether the number "2" was present). DD diagnosis was based on a comprehensive neuropsychological assessment at both time points including numeracy, attention, working memory, and intelligence.

MRI data were acquired on a 3T GE Signa scanner (GE Medical Systems, USA) with an 8-channel head coil. Whole-brain functional images were collected using an interleaved gradient-echo EPI sequence, and high-resolution structural images were obtained using a T1-weighted 3D fast spoiled gradient echo (FSPGR) sequence. Preprocessing was performed with SPM12 (Wellcome Trust Centre for NeuroImaging, UK) in Matlab (R2017b, The MathWorks, USA). Functional images were slice-time corrected, realigned, and unwarped, with the six motion parameters included as regressors. Structural images were co-registered to the mean functional image. Age- and gender-matched tissue probability maps generated via the CerebroMatic toolbox were used for segmentation and normalization to MNI space. Normalized functional images were resampled to 2 mm³ and smoothed with a 6 mm FWHM Gaussian kernel. High-pass filtering was applied at 180 s.

Effective connectivity was assessed using dynamic causal modeling (DCM) in four regions of interest (ROIs): left [MNI: −30 −46 36] and right IPS [30 −52 36], and left [−7 −4 48] and right preSMA [6 10 50]. ROI time series were extracted using SPM’s volume-of-interest function, taking the principal eigenvariate within a 6 mm sphere centered on participant-specific local maxima within an 8 mm search radius, corrected for motion. Participants lacking task-related activation in all four ROIs at both time points (i.e., T1; T2) were excluded, resulting in a final sample of 13 individuals (10 females; T1 mean age = 9.8 years; T2 mean age = 14.4 years). The DCM model space was fully connected, including bidirectional extrinsic connections and self-connections. The task, comprising all trials from both conditions, was defined as the driving input for all regions. A one-state bilinear DCM with mean-centered inputs estimated endogenous (A-matrix) parameters representing connectivity common across conditions, and modulatory inputs (B-matrix) were specified for both conditions on all self- and between-region connections.

Longitudinal changes were analyzed using Parametric Empirical Bayes (PEB). A group-level covariate coded T1 as 0 and T2 as 1, with commonalities reflecting the T1 group mean and the covariate capturing T2 changes. Bayesian model reduction (BMR) pruned parameters not contributing to model evidence, and Bayesian model averaging (BMA) provided weighted parameter estimates, conducted separately for A- and B-matrices. Only parameters with posterior probability >95% were interpreted. Predictive validity was assessed via leave-one-out cross-validation within PEB. Parameters with long-term effects were evaluated both as groups for common connectivity, number-order modulation, and number-identification modulation, and individually by correlating predicted and observed time-point labels using point-biserial correlation.

Results

Behavioral performance

For the number-order condition, accuracy increased significantly from T1 (mean = 63.5%, SD = 17.9) to T2 (mean = 83.1%, SD = 14.4; paired t(12) = -4.66, p < .001). Response times decreased significantly from T1 (mean = 2.0 s, SD = 0.48 s) to T2 (mean = 1.6 s, SD = 0.27 s; paired t(12) = 3.675, p = .003).

For the number-identification condition, accuracy increased from T1 (mean = 94.0%, SD = 5.1) to T2 (mean = 98.3%, SD = 0.7; paired t(12) = -2.63, p = .022). Response times also decreased from T1 (mean = 1.3 s, SD = 0.31) to T2 (mean = 0.9 s, SD = 0.17; paired t(12) = 4.49, p < .001).

Effective connectivity (parameters with posterior probability > 95%)

Common connectivity across task conditions (A-parameters):

Across task conditions, all four regions showed reduced self-inhibition relative to the prior (default) self-connections, indicating increased regional responsiveness within the network (left IPS = −0.34; right IPS = −0.15; left preSMA = −0.33; right preSMA = −0.34).

Long-term effects revealed further reduced self-inhibition in the right IPS (−0.35), whereas self-inhibition increased in the left preSMA (0.24). In addition, connectivity from the left IPS to the right IPS (−0.19 Hz), left preSMA (−0.34 Hz), and right preSMA (−0.33 Hz) was reduced over time.

Modulation by number order (B-parameters):

During number-order processing, the left IPS (−0.92) and bilateral preSMA (left = −1.71; right = −1.92) exhibited reduced self-inhibition. Additionally, increased connectivity was observed from the right IPS to the right preSMA (0.49 Hz) and from the right preSMA to the right IPS (0.91Hz) and to the left preSMA (0.59 Hz).

Long-term effects showed increased self-inhibition both in left and right preSMA (1.92; 1.62). Connectivity increased from the left IPS to the right IPS (0.87 Hz) and to the right preSMA (0.67 Hz), whereas connectivity from the right preSMA to the right IPS was reduced (−0.94 Hz).

Modulation by number identification (B-parameters):

During number identification, reduced connectivity was observed from the right IPS to the left preSMA (−0.41 Hz). In contrast, increased bilateral connectivity was identified between the left IPS and right preSMA (0.68 Hz; 1.38 Hz). The left IPS and right preSMA also showed increased bidirectional connectivity (0.74 Hz; 0.46 Hz), and connectivity from the left preSMA to the right IPS (0.58 Hz) and to the right preSMA (0.68 Hz) was increased.

Long-term effects were characterized by reduced connectivity from the left preSMA to the right IPS (−1.28 Hz), as well as reduced interhemispheric connectivity between the left and right preSMA (−0.86 Hz; -1.13 Hz).

Predictive validity (leave-one-out cross-validation):

Only connectivity parameters with posterior probability > 95% were entered into the predictive analyses.

For common connectivity, parameters did not predict time-point membership when considered jointly. When examined individually, connectivity from the left IPS to the right preSMA significantly predicted long-term change (r(24) = 0.34, p = .043).

For modulation by number order, parameters significantly predicted time-point membership when considered jointly (r(24) = 0.43, p = .014). Individually, self-connections of the left preSMA (r(24) = 48, p = .006) and right preSMA (r(24) = 0.35, p = .039) significantly predicted long-term change.

For modulation by number identification, parameters significantly predicted long-term change when considered jointly (r(24) = 0.38, p = .027). Individually, connectivity from the left preSMA to the right IPS significantly predicted time-point membership (r(24) = 0.43, p = .014).

Conclusion

Our longitudinal study provides novel evidence that effective connectivity within the bilateral IPS and preSMA undergoes measurable changes during the developmental course of DD. Behavioral performance improved over time in both number-order and number-identification tasks, indicating improvement and maturation of numerical skills. At the neural level, common connectivity across task conditions revealed reduced self-inhibition in all regions, indicating increased responsiveness within the network. Long-term effects revealed reduced self-inhibition in the right IPS, indicating increased responsiveness within the network. In contrast, no increased responsiveness (i.e., reduced self-inhibition) was observed in the left IPS, which was contrary to our expectation.

For number-order processing, confirming previous results, the bilateral preSMA showed reduced self-inhibition, indicating higher responsiveness during modulation, alongside increased connectivity between the right preSMA and right IPS. Longitudinally, connectivity increased from the left IPS to the right IPS and right preSMA, highlighting the growing importance of the left IPS during adolescence. Additionally, increased self-inhibition of bilateral preSMA supports our hypothesis that these regions may play a reduced role in cognitive control as neuronal representations of numerical information may become relatively more efficient, despite persistent deficits. These connectivity patterns likely underlie the observed behavioral improvements at T2, consistent with prior studies showing long-term gains in DD, though performance remains below that of typically developing peers.

For number-identification, long-term effects included reduced connectivity between left and right preSMA and from the left preSMA to the right IPS, suggesting a more streamlined and efficient network. Leave-one-out cross-validation demonstrated that several of these parameters, particularly involving the left IPS and bilateral preSMA, had predictive validity for long-term behavioral outcomes, both jointly and individually. Together, these findings suggest that improvements in numerical performance in DD in the longterm are supported by increased efficiency within key regions and dynamic reconfiguration of inter-regional interactions.

Comparative studies with typically developing peers are required to determine whether these patterns reflect a delayed maturation of the network or an alternative, gradually improving, yet still inefficient effective connectivity profile in DD.

In sum, our results demonstrate that developmental gains in DD are accompanied by selective and condition-specific changes in effective connectivity within the IPS–preSMA network, highlighting the neural mechanisms supporting improved numerical performance.
