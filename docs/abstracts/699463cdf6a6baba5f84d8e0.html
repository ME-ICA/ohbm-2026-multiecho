Introduction

When a speaker describes a face and a listener forms a mental image, effective information transfer requires that the listener correctly comprehends the speaker's intended message, which should produce similar neural representations across brains. Traditional hyperscanning studies^2 quantify inter-brain coupling using correlation-based metrics, which rely on first-order correspondence, directly correlating either temporal signals or spatial voxel-wise activation patterns between two brains. For spatial pattern correlation, voxel activation values are arranged into vectors, and first-order correlation is computed between them. This approach focuses on the overall envelope of the vectors, while the spatial adjacency relationships between voxels are not considered. Topological analysis^1 provides a complementary perspective by capturing how spatial adjacency relationships between voxels are organized into structure. We hypothesized that successful representation transfer from speaker to listener would manifest as shared topographic structure in spatial activation patterns, detectable through persistent homology.

Methods

Forty-six participants (23 same-sex dyads: 5 male pairs, 18 female pairs) completed a face-related communication task. During each trial, senders viewed and verbally described face images to receivers within 16 seconds based on experimenter-provided hints. Receivers then had 6 seconds to form mental images of the described faces. Sender-receiver roles alternated in a pseudo-randomized order, yielding 46 sender-receiver dyads for analysis. 

Hyperscan MRI data were acquired simultaneously using two 3T MRI scanners (Magnetom Verio, Siemens) with standard 32-channel phased array coils. Functional images were collected using a T2*-weighted gradient-echo EPI sequence with multiband acceleration (TR = 1,000 ms, voxel size = 2 × 2 × 2 mm³). Anatomical images were acquired using a T1-weighted MP-RAGE sequence (voxel size = 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.8 mm³).

To examine whether the topographic structure of neural activation during receiver's mental imagery corresponds to that during sender's verbal encoding, we applied persistent homology analysis across 400 cortical regions defined by the Schaefer2018 atlas. Within each region, every voxel has an activation value and a three-dimensional spatial coordinate, together forming a topographic map where activation values represent "elevation." Using superlevel set filtration, we begin at the highest elevation and gradually lower the threshold. High-activation voxels first appear as peaks, and spatially adjacent high-activation voxels form clusters (connected components). As the threshold continues to decrease, clusters gradually expand, and previously separate peaks may merge. We computed H₀ (connected components) to extract structural features of this topographic map: how many distinct peaks exist, how prominent each peak is (the elevation range from emergence to merger), and the hierarchical relationships among peaks. Specifically, from each persistence diagram, we extracted nine statistical features: number of topological features, total persistence, mean persistence, standard deviation of persistence, maximum persistence, persistence entropy, mean birth time, mean death time, and mean midlife. Euclidean distances between sender-receiver feature vectors were computed to quantify topographic dissimilarity.

We compared real pairs (same dyad, same trial) against pseudo pairs (mismatched dyads) using linear mixed-effects models, with pair type (real vs. pseudo) as the fixed effect of interest, controlling for experimental condition. Subject and trial were included as random effects. Statistical significance was determined via Monte-Carlo permutation tests (3000 iterations) with FDR correction (q < .05).

Results

Real communicating pairs showed significantly stronger topological correspondence than pseudo pairs in the left orbitofrontal cortex, corresponding to Brodmann area 11/12 (pair type coefficient = -0.59, p < .0001, FDR-corrected). The negative coefficient indicates reduced feature-based distances for real pairs relative to pseudo pairs, demonstrating that receiver's imagery-related activation patterns preserved the geometric structure present during sender's verbal encoding in this prefrontal region.

Conclusion

Persistent homology offers a novel approach for probing inter-brain representational alignment during communication. Unlike first-order methods that that compare activation vector envelopes, this approach captures how spatial adjacency relationships between voxels are organized into topographic structure. Our findings implicate the orbitofrontal cortex in sustaining coherent topographic structure across communicating individuals, facilitating the transformation from verbal description to mental visualization.
