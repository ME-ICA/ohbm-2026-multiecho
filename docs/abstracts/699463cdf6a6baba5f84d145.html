Introduction

Achieving seizure freedom in drug-resistant temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) depends critically on the precise delineation of the epileptogenic zone (EZ). While neuroimaging advances have improved localization, 30–40% of patients still fail to achieve seizure freedom, highlighting the need for more accurate presurgical evaluation tools. Subtraction Ictal SPECT Co-registered to MRI (SISCOM) is a standard technique but is limited by reliance on arbitrary thresholding (e.g., 2 standard deviations) and susceptibility to physiological variations in regional cerebral blood flow. To address these limitations, Ictal-Interictal SPECT Analysis by Statistical Parametric Mapping (ISAS) was developed to provide voxel-wise statistical comparison against a normal control database. Although previous studies have suggested ISAS's utility, few have rigorously compared its prognostic value against SISCOM specifically at the "sublobar" resolution—a scale crucial for tailored resections—in a homogeneous TLE cohort. This study aims to bridge this gap by comparing the localizing accuracy, interobserver reliability, and predictive value for surgical outcomes of ISAS versus SISCOM. 1,2

Methods

Subjects & Data Acquisition: We retrospectively analyzed 60 consecutive patients (33 males, mean age 32.8 ± 11.9 years) with drug-resistant TLE who underwent resective epilepsy surgery and had a minimum follow-up of 1 year (median: 93 months). All patients underwent both ictal (mean injection latency: 30.1 seconds) and interictal Tc-99m-ECD SPECT scans.
Image Processing: SISCOM analysis was performed using ANALYZE 7.5 software with a standard threshold of 2 standard deviations. ISAS was conducted using BioImage Suite, utilizing a voxel-based analysis against a healthy control database to generate z-score maps (threshold p < 0.05 uncorrected).
Blinded Review: Two blinded reviewers independently identified the most prominent hyperperfusion regions across 40 predefined brain subregions (e.g., temporal pole, mesial temporal, lateral temporal) using the image review tool we developed (Fig. 1).
Statistical Analysis: Localization concordance with the actual surgical resection site was scored at both lobar and sublobar levels. Interobserver agreement was assessed using Cohen’s kappa (κ). Logistic regression was employed to evaluate the independent prognostic value of ISAS and SISCOM concordance in predicting Engel Class I seizure freedom. Comparison with other modalities (MRI, ictal scalp EEG, interictal FDG-PET) was also performed.

Results

Interobserver Reliability: ISAS demonstrated significantly superior reliability compared to SISCOM. Cohen’s kappa for the most prominent localization was 0.88 for ISAS (Excellent) versus 0.69 for SISCOM (Good). Across all six ranked hyperperfusion clusters, ISAS maintained statistically significant agreement (p < 0.001), whereas SISCOM showed poor consistency in lower-ranked clusters.
Localization Accuracy:ISAS achieved higher concordance with the resection site than SISCOM at both the lobar (88.7% vs. 71.7%, p < 0.001) and sublobar levels (79.7% vs. 60.9%, p < 0.001). (Fig. 2)
 Comparison with Other Modalities: In sublobar localization, ISAS (79.7%) outperformed not only SISCOM but also High-Resolution MRI (72.5%), Ictal Scalp EEG (59.1%), and Interictal FDG-PET (55.0%). Only intracranial EEG (performed in 18 patients) showed higher accuracy (100%).
Prognostic Value: Among the 60 patients, 47 (78.3%) achieved Engel Class I outcomes. Logistic regression analysis identified ISAS concordance as the only significant predictor of seizure freedom. ISAS concordance significantly predicted Engel I outcome at both lobar (β = 0.418, p = 0.003) and sublobar levels (β = 0.301, p = 0.041). In contrast, SISCOM concordance failed to predict surgical outcome at either level (Lobar: p = 0.178; Sublobar: p = 0.566), suggesting that SISCOM-defined foci may be less specific to the true epileptogenic zone.

Conclusion

This study provides robust evidence that ISAS offers superior diagnostic performance over conventional SISCOM in the presurgical evaluation of TLE. ISAS minimizes subjective interpretation errors, resulting in higher interobserver reliability and greater anatomical precision. Crucially, sublobar concordance of ISAS—but not SISCOM—is a significant independent predictor of post-surgical seizure freedom. These findings advocate for the routine implementation of ISAS as the primary SPECT post-processing method to optimize surgical planning and improve outcomes in patients with drug-resistant epilepsy.
