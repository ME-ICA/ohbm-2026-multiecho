Introduction

Salient risk factors for Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and accelerated cognitive decline in middle-aged and older-adults include age, beta-amyloid burden (Aβ), and presence of APOE-e4 genetic variation (Livingston et. al, 2020). Differential atrophy patterns in the medial temporal lobe (MTL) is another important risk factor (Shi et. al, 2009), yet it remains unclear how age and AD risk-factors may influence rate of volumetric decline in MTL sub-regions, particularly those of the hippocampus subfields. Automatic segmentation of MTL regions, especially the hippocampus and surrounding regions, has shown great promise in many aging and lifespan studies (Wisse et. al, 2014; 2017). We explored 4-year longitudinal change in MTL sub-region volumes (via semi-automated segmentation) and modifying effects of age, APOE e4, and beta-amyloid burden on individual rates of change across the adult lifespan. 

Methods

Participants: N = 172 healthy, cognitively normal participants (age 20-94 years) from Waves 1 and 2 of our ongoing longitudinal study, the Dallas Area Longitudinal Lifespan Aging Study (DALLAS); Cognitive testing and neuroimaging administered at baseline and 4-year follow-up.

Genetics Testing: Saliva was collected using Oragene kits; APOE genotype (rs429358 and rs7412) was assessed with TaqMan SNP Genotyping assays.

MRI Acquisition: Single 3T Phillips Achieva whole-body scanner with 32-channel head coil, SENSE encoding; Ultrahigh-resolution (0.42 x 0.42 x 2mm3) T2/PD weighted (T2w) sequence, MTL slab aligned along the longitudinal axis of the hippocampus; T1-weighted MPRAGE (T1w) sequence, 1mm3 isotopic, 160 sagittal slices

Positron Emission Tomography (PET) Acquisition: Single Siemens ECAT HR PET scanner: Participants administered 18F-florbetapir tracer onsite: Static PET scan (50 mins uptake period, ~20 post-uptake mins scan)

PET Processing: PET images coregistered to T1w images; 7 ROIs for amyloid deposition (i.e., anterior cingulate, posterior cingulate, precuneus, lateral temporal, lateral parietal, middle frontal, and inferior frontal); Binding counts extracted for each ROI, and then normalized by binding counts in the cerebellar gray matter to produce standardized uptake value ratios (SUVR); averaged across ROIs to form mean cortical amyloid SUVR.

MRI Processing: T1w is coregistered to MTL T2w, and reoriented to RIA format; Segmentation and volume of subregions computed via ASHS software (Yushkevich et al., 2015). Custom atlas used to reflect adult lifespan, HC subfield ROIs manually traced for 30 study participants at Wave 1, used to train ASHS template and estimation (Foster et al., 2020).

Results

After MTL segmentation, 2 linear mixed effects models (LMEs) tested the relation of baseline age, time, amyloid SUVR (Model 2) or APOE e4 (Model 3) and their interactions as predictors of MTL volume change. Result 1: Test of age and time effects on MTL (Model 1). MTL volumes decreased at different rates across the lifespan. All regions exhibited a significant association between volume and time after around age 40, except for CA1/2 region, where age and time were never significantly associated. Result 2: AD biomarker effects on MTL shrinkage. Model 2: APOE. MTL rate of shrinkage depends on age, region, and APOE genotype. APOE e4 carriers demonstrated age-accelerated entorhinal loss relative to e4 negative group. E4 carriers tended to exhibit and accelerated loss of ERC volume in older ages as compared to their e4 negative peers. Model 3: Amyloid SUVR. MTL shrinkage depends on beta-amyloid burden. We observed a significant time x amyloid SUVR interaction which did not (significantly) depend on MTL region, whereby individuals with higher beta-amyloid burden are more likely to exhibit steeper MTL volume loss across 4 years.


Conclusion

Reliable age-accelerated volume loss was detected in all regions but CA1-2; APOE e4+ carriers demonstrated age-accelerated entorhinal volume loss relative to e4- group; higher Aβ-burden was associated with steeper MTL volume loss. Findings highlight that AD risk-factors influence MTL aging, even in cognitively-healthy adults across 4-years of aging. Specifically, we found that age-accelerated volume loss was steeper in the entorhinal cortex in e4+ group but not in other MTL sub-regions. These findings are in line with prior findings which suggest that ERC, compared to other regions, seems to decline faster and is more vulnerable to age-related decline and AD pathogenesis (Olajide & Chapman, 2021). Additionally, steeper rate of MTL volume loss was found in individuals with greater amyloid burden, irrespective of age and MTL region, suggesting that rather than targeting regions of MTL over time like APOE, amyloid’s effects may be more general. Future studies should investigate how these biomarkers and their interaction predict age-related decline in cognition. Given the multitude of factors involved in aging and AD process, it is worth also exploring the effects of amyloid burden with other biomarkers, like inflammation and tau accumulation.
