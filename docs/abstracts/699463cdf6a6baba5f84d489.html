Introduction

Schizophrenia is a highly heterogeneous psychiatric disorder with substantial overlap in etiology and phenotype with other psychiatric conditions, raising questions about whether it reflects a single disease entity or a cluster of related neurobiological alterations1. Aberrant attentional processing and impaired self-other distinction have been consistently implicated in schizophrenia, with converging evidence pointing to dysfunction in large-scale cortical networks such as the default mode network (DMN), frontoparietal network (FPN), ventral attention network (VAN), and somatomotor network (SOM)2-3. Infraslow resting-state brain dynamics, including quasi-periodic patterns (QPPs), have emerged as a promising non-invasive marker of large-scale network organization and may provide complementary information beyond static functional connectivity4. In the present study, we detected QPPs using complex principal components analysis and found subtle but consistent differences in DMN amplitude and inter-network correlations during the QPP waveform in the patient group. 

Methods

Resting-state fMRI data were analyzed from 160 participants (68 controls, 92 patients with schizophrenia), approximately matched for age and sex (see figure 1). Scans were obtained at IKEM (Institute for Clinical and Experimental Medicine, Prague) on a 3 Tesla Siemens Trio scanner. Structural scans were MPRAGE T1 with 2300 ms TR, TE = 4.6 ms. Functional scans were T2* weighted, TR = 2000 ms and TE = 30 ms, flip angle 90 degrees. Data were preprocessed using the CONN toolbox standard pre-processing pipeline (alignment, slice-timing correction, motion correction, smoothing) and parcellated into 246 regions using the Brainnetome atlas. Time series were bandpass filtered (0.01–0.1 Hz) prior to complex principal components detection. Complex principal component analysis (cPCA) was applied at the group level (controls, patients, and combined) for qualitative inspection, and on a subjectwise basis to characterize individual infraslow dynamics. (Original code available here: https://github.com/tsb46/complex_pca).  A group-derived cPCA template was used exclusively for post hoc sign and phase alignment of subjectwise components. Network-level waveforms, amplitudes, and internetwork correlations were computed for the dominant quasi-periodic component. Group differences were assessed using t-tests and ANOVA, with multiple comparisons controlled using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate.

Results

Patients exhibited subtle but significant alterations in QPP dynamics compared to controls. Specifically, increased internetwork correlations during the Quasi-Periodic Pattern were observed between VAN-DAN, VAN-SOM, and SOM-FPN, alongside decreased coupling between SOM and DMN. Network amplitude analyses revealed greater QPP-related activation in DMN, VAN, and SOM in patients. No group differences were observed in explained variance across the top ten components.

Conclusion

These findings indicate network-specific alterations in infraslow brain dynamics in schizophrenia, particularly involving the DMN, VAN, and SOM. Increased DMN amplitude during the quasi-periodic pattern is notable given prior evidence of reduced DMN suppression in schizophrenia during cognitive processing, suggesting that altered infraslow dynamics may reflect a complementary mechanism underlying previously observed task-based and resting-state abnormalities. Changes in VAN and SOM-centered internetwork coupling further point to disruptions in attentional reorienting and sensorimotor integration, consistent with core features of the disorder. Importantly, these effects were subtle but consistent between groups and were detectable using subjectwise complex PCA, despite no group differences in explained variance.

Beyond these specific findings, the present work highlights the utility of complex PCA as a tool for characterizing dynamic, infraslow organization of large-scale brain networks at the individual level. While atlas-based parcellation approaches remain the norm, growing evidence suggests that individual-specific functional organization may be critical for capturing clinically meaningful variability in psychiatric populations. Future studies could integrate subjectwise complex PCA with individualized parcellation or surface-based approaches to improve sensitivity to heterogeneity within schizophrenia and across related disorders5. More broadly, combining infraslow dynamic measures with clinical, cognitive, or genetic data may help clarify whether quasi-periodic patterns represent trait-like features, state-dependent markers, or potential targets for stratification in translational research.
