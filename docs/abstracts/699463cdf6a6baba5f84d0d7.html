Introduction

Empathy is fundamental to prosocial behavior and social interactions.1 However, current EEG studies suffer from low ecological validity: over 80% of pain-empathy ERP studies use static images,2 which poorly approximate real-life responses. EEG microstate analysis addresses this limitation. Microstates segment EEG into distinct topographies3 associated with resting-state networks,4 characterizing brain dynamics with millisecond resolution ideal for naturalistic stimuli.

This study examines empathy's neural representation using microstate analysis with film clips, validating this approach by comparing microstates with established ERP components (N1, N2, P2, P3)5 and investigating whether microstate features predict state empathy, trait empathy, and prosocial behavior.

Methods

Thirty-five healthy Chinese college students (17 males, 16 females; age: 22.47±1.85 years) completed the study, with 33 analyzed for EEG-behavioral data. Trait empathy was assessed using the Interpersonal Reactivity Index (IRI-C; Cronbach's α = 0.86), and prosocial behavior through a donation task (¥0–60) (Fig. 1a).

Participants underwent EEG recording while viewing: (1) 48 trials of 3-second video clips showing cotton swabs (neutral) or needles (pain) touching faces (Fig. 1b); (2) film clips from Aftershock including 40-second neutral and 216-second empathy-evoking segments depicting suffering (Fig. 1c).

EEG was recorded using a 64-channel system at 1000 Hz, preprocessed in EEGLAB (1–40 Hz, downsampled to 500 Hz). Mean amplitudes were calculated for N1, N2, P2, and P3 components. For microstate analysis, k-means clustering identified four canonical microstates, with temporal parameters (duration, occurrence, coverage, transition probabilities) extracted. Paired t-tests with Bonferroni correction and correlation analyses examined associations between neural indices, trait empathy, and prosocial behavior.

Results

Empathy-related Microstates: Empathy elicited distinct microstate reconfigurations reflecting network-level changes. Microstate analysis revealed four distinct topographical configurations. Compared to neutral conditions, empathy processing showed: (1) shorter MSC duration (57.8 vs. 63.3ms; t(34) = -3.037, p = 0.020, Cohen's d = -0.51); (2) shorter MSD duration (55.0 vs. 61.5ms; t(34) = -3.400, p = 0.008, d = -0.57); (3) higher MSB occurrence (4.6 vs. 3.9/s; t(34) =3.594, p = 0.004, d = 0.61) (Fig. 2a). No differences emerged for coverage or transition probabilities.

ERP Components and Microstate-ERP Associations: Microstates converged with established ERP markers of empathic processing. Empathy conditions elicited larger P2 amplitude in central-parietal region (t(34) = 3.156, p = 0.003, d = 0.53) and larger P3 amplitude in occipital region (t(34) = 2.629, p = 0.013, d = 0.44) compared to neutral conditions. MSB occurrence positively correlated with P2 differential amplitude (r = 0.393, p = 0.019) (Fig. 2b), indicating convergence between microstate dynamics and ERP markers.

Association with Behaviors and Traits: Microstate features predicted both prosocial behavior and trait empathy. MSC duration negatively predicted donation amount (r = -0.272, p = 0.035). MSA duration positively correlated with IRI scores (r = 0.249, p = 0.044), while MSB coverage showed negative correlation (r = -0.365, p = 0.037). P2 differential amplitude negatively correlated with IRI scores (r = -0.622, p < 0.001) (Fig. 2c).

Conclusion

This study identified distinct microstate signatures: increased MSB occurrence and reduced MSC and MSD durations, reflecting dynamic reconfiguration of visual, salience, and dorsal attention networks. Critically, our findings suggest that distinct microstate-ERP configurations correspond to core empathy processes: MSB coupled with P2 underpins attentional-affective sharing, MSC supports motivational-action tendencies, and MSA is related to trait empathy.

The present work establishes EEG microstates as sensitive and ecologically valid neural markers of empathy using naturalistic film paradigms, capturing the dynamic unfolding of state empathy and enabling a finer-grained distinction between state and trait empathy.
