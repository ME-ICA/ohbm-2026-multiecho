Introduction

Understanding the relationship between molecular architecture and macroscale brain function is central to systems neuroscience and biological psychiatry. However, comparing molecular and macroscale features in the human brain remains a challenge. In vivo molecular measurements currently rely on positron emission tomography (PET), which is impractical for widespread or multi-tracer use, creating a critical need for surrogate markers based on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Previous studies have proposed spatial constraint principles across brain imaging modalities to bridge brain structure, function, and metabolites (Pang et al., 2023; Wan et al., 2024). Here, we present EigenBrain, a spatially constrained framework that integrates cortical geometry, structural wiring, and functional organization to link MRI-derived connectomic eigenvectors with molecular PET maps. 

Methods

We used manifold learning to obtain low dimensional connectomic eigenvectors of geodesic distance, structural connectivity, and functional connectivity, respectively derived from structural, diffusion-weighted, and resting-state functional MRI. Group-level reconstructions of 20 neurotransmitter receptor and transporter maps from Neuromaps (Hansen et al., 2022; Markello et al., 2022) were performed using MRI-based eigenmaps from the Human Connectome Project (HCP, unrelated subjects N = 94). Individual-level reconstructions were performed for cerebral metabolic rate of glucose (CMRglc, Munich dataset, N = 20), amyloid-β accumulation, and tau deposition (Dallas dataset, N = 61) from OpenNeuro. To generalize the framework, we developed a deep learning model to predict CMRglc maps from structural and functional MRI, trained on the Munich dataset and evaluated on the external Monash dataset (N = 24). Across all analyses, datasets were used systematically for group modeling (HCP), individual reconstructions (Munich and Dallas), and external validation (Monash).

Results

Using the MRI-derived eigenmaps, we reconstructed 20 group-level neurotransmitter receptors and transporter maps explaining 92-98% of variance. When applied to individuals, subject-specific reconstruction of CMRglc achieved 91–95% variance explained. The deep learning model generalized to the Monash dataset with moderate to strong spatial correlations between ground-truth and predicted CMRglc maps (r = 0.516-0.721). Before applying the framework to amyloid-β and tau (Dallas dataset), we aimed to clarify whether  amyloid-β and tau are directly associated with  brain function, and how their relations are constrained by geodesic distance and structural wiring. We found that Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Score (ADAS) was related to amyloid-β standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR) in the left lateral superior parietal lobule (5L) and tau SUVR in the right parieto-occipital sulcus (POS2). These two regions’ functional relation to ADAS decreased with the geodesic distance (r = -0.621 and -0.506) and increased with high-wiring structure (r = 0.431 and 0.226). The individual reconstructions of amyloid-β and tau achieved 91–97% and 83–95% variance explained, respectively, which was not related to the ADAS.

Conclusion

Our EigenBrain framework introduces a biological bridge between molecular architecture and macroscale brain function and shows excellent reconstruction for group-level neurotransmitter maps and individual-level CMRglc, amyloid-β, and tau maps. It offers promising applications to neurodevelopment and neurodegeneration with the potential to inform individualized molecular profiling for precision therapeutic targeting.
