Introduction

Brain activity at slow time scales is highly dynamic, and understanding the factors that influence such fluctuations is important to understand the flexible nature of brain and cognition. Recent work investigating human and animal brain data, using various methodological approaches, and comparing different behavioral states, suggests that brain activity recordings presents slow travelling waves of activity propagation from unimodal to transmodal areas. However, the factors that modulate this propagation and how they impact macroscopic measures of brain activity remain little known. Following our previous work on the effect of catecholaminergic tone on activity propagation as measured with fMRI (Mäki-Marttunen & Nieuwenhuis 2025), we aimed to further examine whether the modulation of travelling wave activity would be affected by another brain feature that presents a strong topological component: the distribution of neuromodulatory receptors. Given the evidence that psilocybin, a psychedelic agent acting on serotoninergic receptors, affects functional connectivity measures obtained from whole-brain data, and that the presence of travelling waves over the principal gradient are bound to shape the measures of functional connectivity, we aimed to assess the hypothesis that psychedelics would modulate the propagation of travelling waves over the landscape of serotoninergic receptors and result in the changes in functional connectivity previously associated with psilocybin.

Methods

We tested our hypotheses using pharmacological 3T fMRI data of 7 highly sampled healthy adults during rest and task performance from the Psilocybin Precision Functional Mapping open database (Siegel et al. 2024). Participants underwent a series of baseline sessions, and two drug sessions with psilocybin and methylphenidate administered in a counter-balanced design. Psilocybin is a 5HT2a agonist leading to a strong psychedelic state. fMRI data followed standard preprocessing and further filtering (0.001-0.05 Hz). Slow propagation of activity was detected around peaks of global signal as a delay profile following the spatial pattern of the principal gradient (Gu et al. 2021). Speed, number and ratio of travelling waves in different directions was calculated and compared statistically. We calculated a novel measure of energy of propagation throughout the principal gradient.

Results

We found that the pharmacological manipulation was associated with faster travelling waves, and that faster waves correlated with stronger global functional connectivity and a more contracted principal gradient, two commonly reported findings in fMRI studies of psilocybin and psychedelics in general. The energy of propagation was larger under psilocybin, and it was related to the topographical distribution of 5HT2a receptors.

Conclusion

Our results confirm that neuromodulatory tone affects travelling wave propagation, and that this propagation shapes functional connectivity features, highlighting that psychedelic effects on fMRI functional connectivity measures could be explained by a modulation of brain activity propagation over the receptor landscape at slow time scales.
