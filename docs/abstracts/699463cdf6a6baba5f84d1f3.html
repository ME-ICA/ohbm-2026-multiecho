Introduction

Previous studies in patients with Parkinson's disease show specific problem with comprehension of syntactically complex sentences such as noncanonical sentences. Our aim was to found weather comprehension for noncanonical sentences will improve after real tDCS as compared to the pre-intervention state and to identify the neural correlates of tDCS-induced improvement.

Methods

A total of 24 Czech Parkinson's disease subjects (12/12 real/sham stimulation) participated in home-based tDCS stimulation study. Before and after 10 sessions of 20 minutes of tDCS 2 mA over left inferior frontal gyrus the participants performer fMRI task (3.0T Siemens Prisma). We analyzed their performance of a Test of sentence comprehension (ToSC) adjusted for fMRI using Wilcoxon test for behavioural data and whole-brain GLM analysis for fMRI data (cluster level inference, initial cut-off p<0.001, FWE-corrected with p<0.05). 

Results

On the behavioural level we have noticed some trend towards improving performance after real stimulation sessions in noncanonical task (Accuracy: before: 76.7 ± 14.7 and after: 80.4 ± 13.0). After real stimulation, there was a significant difference in activation in the left fusiform gyrus for noncanonical complex sentences vs. canonical complex sentences. 

Conclusion

Home-based tDCS stimulation might have some positive effects on comprehension of syntactically complex sentences. We also captured a difference after real stimulation in activation for noncanonical complex sentences vs. canonical complex sentences but more subjects are needed in order to precisely identify the neural underpinnings of this difference.
