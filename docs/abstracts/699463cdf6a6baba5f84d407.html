Introduction

Previous evidence suggests that the brain’s intrinsic connectivity networks (ICNs) may develop at different rates and at different developmental stages. These developmental network changes are reflected in gray matter architecture and can be detailed using structural covariance MRI. Alterations in structural architecture and connectivity of brain networks in autism spectrum disorder (ASD) have been reported previously; however, the development of these network differences during the transition to adulthood has not been extensively studied. We applied structural covariance MRI (scMRI) to examine the developmental trajectory of network architecture in ASD from childhood into adulthood to determine when and where departures from typical development occur.

Methods

We applied scMRI utilizing an existing database of males with and without ASD aged 11 to 35 years (n=150). Data were binned by age (11-15, 16-20, >20, n=50 per group). To identify group and age differences in whole-brain gray matter covariance, 4mm seed regions of interest (ROIs) were constructed from known hubs of three ICNs implicated in ASD (default mode (DMN), executive control (ECN), and salience (SN) networks). Structural covariance networks (SCNs) were thresholded at family-wise error (FWE) corrected p<0.05 and used to qualitatively compare across diagnostic and age groups. Second-level direct group contrasts (p<0.05, masked to the global map at p<0.05FWE) were performed using one-tailed T-tests. We further evaluated inter-nodal trajectories across our sample using Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) in addition to multilayered convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to determine contributions of inter- and intra-network influences on overall network topology and ASD diagnosis.

Results

SCNs in ASD participants were spatially restricted in early adolescence and expanded with age. ASD networks were more spatially restricted than typically-developing control (TDC) networks in early adolescence and early adulthood. ASD participants also displayed different network covariance patterns than TDC participants across development and included additional regions, and excluded key regions present in TDC networks. Quantitative group contrasts revealed increased cross-network covariance between the DMN hub and ECN regions in ASD in adolescence, which transitioned to altered covariance patterns with DMN regions in adulthood. During adulthood, divergent covariance patterns were seen within the SN.

Adults with ASD exhibited increased cross-network covariance between ECN and DMN hubs and regions in the canonical salience network. ASD adults also showed increased cross-network covariance between ECN hubs and DMN regions and vice versa. Autistic adults also exhibited increased covariance between cerebellum and cerebral cortex that were not observed in typically developing adults. Similar structural covariance deviations were also observed in autistic adolescents, although cerebellar alterations were not apparent before adulthood.

GAM models demonstrated differing inter-network relationships primarily between DMN, SN, and ECN nodes, and longitudinal covariance trajectories in ASD varied by severity. CNNs revealed influence of drivers of change across the sample, and diagnostic prediction, and reflected early influence of visual and language networks and later influence of SN and DMN.

Conclusion

Autistic participants exhibited structural covariance alterations in multiple large-scale brain networks during the transition to adulthood.

Altered developmental trajectories and reduced segregation of brain networks in ASD persist into adulthood.

Alterations in covariance between the ECN, DMN, and SN network hubs differs in ASD, as a function of severity.

Differences in network topology influence diagnosis of ASD at differing stages of developmental ontogeny.
