Introduction

The locus coeruleus is a small brainstem nucleus that synthesises and releases noradrenaline. Together, the locus coeruleus–noradrenergic system plays a key role in adaptive behaviour. In human neuroimaging atlases, the locus coeruleus is treated as a single structure,1 despite convergent work from animal models showing heterogeneity within the locus coeruleus that is consistent with a modular organisation.2 Characterisation of sub-regions in small subcortical nuclei has previously relied on post-mortem data. However, high-quality neuroimaging data and data-driven pipelines are now making this possible in vivo. In particular, multimodal MRI can provide proxy measures for different aspects of cellular microstructure and myelination, which can be used to characterise sub-regions within subcortical nuclei.3 Here, we apply a data-driven technique to structural and diffusion MRI data to parcellate sub-regions of the human locus coeruleus.

Methods

We used multimodal MRI data from 50 randomly selected subjects from the Human Connectome Project Young Adult data release.4 The data included minimally pre-processed 3T structural (T1w and T2w) and multi-shell diffusion scans. MRtrix3 was then used to further preprocess diffusion data and construct diffusion tensor parametric maps (fractional anisotropy, FA; axial diffusivity, AD; radial diffusivity, RD; mean diffusivity, MD). Multi-shell multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution was then used to compute fibre orientation distributions (FODs), and subsequently directionally-encoded colour maps (DEC). Probabilistic anatomically constrained tractography first generated 10 million streamlines using dynamic seeding from whole-brain FODs. This was complemented by a further 10 million streamlines generated by seeding from a binary pons mask to boost the density of streamlines traversing the locus coeruleus. The two tractograms were then concatenated into a 20 million streamline tractogram, where individual streamline cross-section multipliers were optimised using SIFT2. Subject parametric maps and tractograms were warped to a tissue-unbiased population template space using non-linear warping through the ANTs toolbox and were then used to compute track-weighted (TW) contrast maps for boosting SNR. This resulted in ten parametric maps for each subject (FA, TW-FA, TW-AD, TW-RD, TW-MD, TW-T1w/T2w, TW-FODamp, DEC-r, DEC-g, DEC-b) at 0.7mm isotropic super-resolution (Figure 1).

An established locus coeruleus atlas1 was subsequently non-linearly registered to the template space to define the spatial boundary for parcellation. We then extracted measures corresponding to the voxels within the locus coeruleus across each of the ten parametric maps for every subject. Principal component analysis (PCA) reduced the dimensionality of the combined dataset, from which we selected the first 4 principal components that individually explained a minimum of 5% of the total variance, and together accounted for at least 75% of the total variation in intensity values. Bimodal k-means clustering then parcellated voxels of the locus coeruleus into clusters exhibiting similar and consistent properties across the principal components selected. We then performed several reproducibility and validation steps, including repeating the PCA and clustering, and replicating the procedure using alternative locus coeruleus atlases.  

Results

Our data-driven parcellation identified two symmetrical and spatially contiguous sub-regions across both sides of the locus coeruleus (Figure 2C). There was a clear mediolateral organisation between the two parcels spanning the rostro-caudal longitudinal axis of the locus coeruleus (Figure 2D). Ten independent replications of the PCA and k-means clustering consistently resulted in a highly similar organisation of the parcels, with a dice coefficient matrix of 0.996±0.004. The spatial organisation of the parcellation was also conserved when the analysis was repeated using alternative locus coeruleus atlases. Taken together, the replication steps indicated a robust and consistent separation between the two parcels. Consistent with this, paired sample t-tests revealed statistically significant differences in voxel intensity values between the two parcels across all T1, T2, and diffusion MRI-derived parameters (pfdr > 0.001), suggesting underlying neuroanatomical differences between the regions represented by the two parcels. 

Conclusion

This work produced a data-driven parcellation of the human locus coeruleus. The parcellation remained consistent across replications and validation in alternative locus coeruleus atlases. The identified parcels were symmetrical, with spatially contiguous voxels, which is suggestive of a biologically plausible organisation — perhaps consistent with a more tightly packed “core” of locus coeruleus neurons and a reduced density of neurons in the periphery.5 This work advances neuroimaging of the locus coeruleus, with the prospect that future investigations can establish the functional significance of these parcels and their respective vulnerability to neurodegenerative changes.
