Introduction

Functional MRI (fMRI) is widely used in cognitive neuroscience but is sensitive to many noise related issues, ranging from head movement to hardware artifacts, which are exacerbated for multi-site and longitudinal aging studies. Preprocessing pipelines aim to reduce these issues, yet choices vary—manual/semi-automated approaches (SAP) offer control but are time-consuming and less reproducible, while automated pipelines (AFP), like fMRIPrep, improve accessibility and multi-site consistency but may struggle with older adult brains due to default templates. Task design also matters - block designs are more robust than event-related designs. The current study compares SAP and AFP within one site and AFP across two sites using a hybrid task-switching paradigm (Basak et al., 2018; Nashiro et al., 2018) in older adults to assess robustness and replicability of activation patterns.

Methods

We recruited 133 healthy older adults (65–85 years) from two sites: University of Texas at Dallas (UTD, n = 62) and University of Iowa (UI, n = 71). Groups did not differ in age, education, MoCA scores, or sex distribution. Exclusion criteria included neurological disorders, MoCA < 23, MRI contraindications, and sensory impairments. All participants provided IRB-approved informed consent.

Task
Participants completed a hybrid task-switching fMRI paradigm combining block and event-related designs. Blocks included Single-task and Mixed-task conditions; event-related trials varied by switch status and stimulus-response compatibility. Contrasts computed were Global Switch Cost (GSC), Mixing Cost (MC), Local Switch Cost (LSC), and Compatibility Switch Cost (CSC).

Imaging Acquisition
UTD scans were acquired on a Philips Achieva 3T scanner; UI scans on GE Discovery 750W and SIGNA Premier 3T scanners. Both sites used similar EPI parameters (TR = 2000 ms, TE = 30 ms, voxel size ≈ 3.4 × 3.4 × 4 mm). T1-weighted MPRAGE sequences provided structural images.

Preprocessing Pipelines
UTD data were processed using two pipelines:

  • Semi-automated pipeline (SAP): FSL-based, manual skull-stripping (BET), study-specific template creation, motion correction (MCFLIRT), nonlinear registration (FNIRT), spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM), ICA-AROMA denoising.
  • Automated pipeline (AFP): fMRIPrep (v20.2), ANTs-based skull-stripping and normalization, FreeSurfer segmentation, slice-timing correction (AFNI), motion correction (MCFLIRT), ICA-AROMA denoising, spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM).

UI data were processed using AFP only. All datasets were converted to BIDS format before processing through the pipeline. 

GLM Analysis
Six whole-brain GLM analyses were conducted in FSL FEAT: block and event-related designs for UTD-SAP, UTD-AFP, and UI-AFP. High-pass filtering was applied (block = 380 s; event-related = 100 s). First-level contrasts modeled task conditions with a single-gamma HRF and motion regressors. Group-level analyses used cluster thresholding of Z > 3.1, p < .01. All preprocessed images were fitted with identical task-switching contrasts, and the resulting brain maps were compared both qualitatively and quantitatively.

Cluster Overlap
Dice similarity coefficients quantified overlap between clusters from different pipelines (UTD-SAP vs UTD-AFP) and sites (UTD-AFP vs UI-AFP). Overlap was computed for each contrast (GSC, MC, LSC, CSC).

Results

Significant activation clusters were identified for both block (GSC) and event-related contrasts (MC, LSC, CSC) across all three sets of analyses. Block design (GSC) showed robust bilateral frontoparietal activations (MFG, PCG, IPL/SPL), occipital regions, cerebellum, and insula across pipelines and across sites. UTD analyses additionally revealed subcortical activations (thalamus, caudate), absent in UI data. Automated pipeline (AFP) produced larger clusters than semi-automated (SAP), but the site differences were more pronounced than the pipeline differences.

Results from the event-related contrasts were more variable than block-design GSC contrast. Mixing costs (MC) resulted in similar regions as GSC but smaller clusters; AFP maps were larger than SAP. For LSC, large bilateral clusters spanning frontal, parietal, occipital, SMA, and visual regions were observed; UI uniquely showed putamen and nucleus accumbens. For CSC, significant clusters were observed for UTD (ACC, SMA, occipital, cerebellum), but were completely absent in UI.

Cluster overlap: Dice similarity coefficients quantified overlap between clusters from different pipelines (UTD-SAP vs UTD-AFP) and sites (UTD-AFP vs UI-AFP). Overlap was computed for each contrast (GSC, MC, LSC, CSC). Thus, we could calculate both within-site cluster overlap and within-pipeline cluster overlap. 

Our cluster overlap analyses show that block design contrasts resulted in more consistent outputs (avg. overlap ~59%) than event-related contrasts (avg. ~36%). Additionally, both GSC and LSC showed comparable cluster overlap for within-site vs within-pipeline.

Conclusion

Researchers face multiple choices in fMRI preprocessing, and even automated pipelines require oversight. Our findings show that while semi-automated pipelines (SAP) offer slightly greater spatial specificity—likely due to study-specific templates—they are time-intensive. Automated fMRIPrep (AFP) produced highly comparable results to that of SAP, albeit with larger clusters and significant time savings, making it preferable for large datasets and multi-site collaborations. Differences between sites were as impactful as differences between pipelines, underscoring the need to account for scanner variability and participant characteristics in multi-site studies. Block design contrasts were more robust and consistent across sites and across pipelines than event-related contrasts, which showed greater variability, particularly for Compatibility Switch Cost (CSC) that was absent in the UI data. Subcortical and hippocampal activations were more reliably detected in UTD data, possibly due to scanner differences and age-related factors like iron accumulation affecting BOLD signal. For older adult fMRI samples, AFP could be improved by incorporating study-specific templates, since SAP with study-specific template led to a more robust pattern. Overall, AFP is recommended for efficiency and reproducibility, but pipeline choice should remain project-specific, considering design type, sample characteristics, and collaboration needs.
