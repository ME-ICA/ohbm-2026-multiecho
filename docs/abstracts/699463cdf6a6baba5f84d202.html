Introduction

Language functions rely on a distributed network of specialized brain regions, yet the extent to which aging alters this functional organization remains uncertain. Recent evidence indicates that subtle language deterioration can precede the clinical onset of neurodegenerative diseases by decades, suggesting that self-compensatory mechanisms may mask functional decline. While the "brain age gap" has emerged as a promising biomarker for accelerated aging, most studies rely on whole-brain resting-state fMRI, which may lack sensitivity to domain-specific network changes. Task-based fMRI specifically elicits relevant circuits, yet language-specific connectomes remain underexplored in brain age research. Furthermore, predicting age from functional imaging is methodologically challenging: while structural MRI models often achieve Mean Absolute Errors (MAE) of 3–4 years, functional models typically lag behind (MAE ≈ 5–9 years in lifespan cohorts), with outcomes heavily dependent on feature selection and modeling strategies. To address these challenges, this study systematically evaluates a broad matrix of connectivity metrics—incorporating both static and dynamic approaches alongside distance-based measures—within individually localized language networks. By leveraging an AutoML strategy (TPOT) to automatically optimize predictive pipelines and explainability tools to interpret results, we characterize age-related reorganization in the language system. We introduce the \textit{network-based language aging gap (N-LAG)}, a novel index defined as the discrepancy between chronological and predicted age based exclusively on language network connectivity, offering a targeted biomarker to capture early, network-specific aging processes.

Methods

Sample and Data Acquisition

This study utilized the open-access Neurolingua1 dataset (N=437; age 18-82 years, mean=35.98; 51% female). Participants were healthy adults with no history of neurological or psychiatric disorders. Functional MRI data were acquired during a language localizer task2 designed to isolate the language comprehension network using visual and auditory sentence stimuli. The dataset included sequences with repetition times (TR) of 2.0s and 2.4s. All participants provided informed consent under approved ethical protocols.

Preprocessing and Connectivity Estimation

Images underwent standard preprocessing including slice-timing correction, realignment, normalization to MNI space, and global signal regression. A band-pass filter (0.01–0.2 Hz) was applied; the upper cutoff was specifically selected to satisfy the Nyquist theorem for the maximum TR (2.4 s) in the sample. BOLD time series were extracted from 116 regions of interest (ROIs) defined by the AAL atlas and z-normalized. To systematically evaluate how connectivity definitions influence brain age prediction, we computed connectivity matrices using 12 distinct metrics. These included similarity measures (Pearson correlation, Cross-correlation, Mutual Information, Coactivation) and distance-based measures (Euclidean, Manhattan, Earth Mover’s Distance, Dynamic Time Warping). Coactivation matrices were computed using three thresholds (0.5, 0.9, and 0.95), while Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) was applied with three window sizes (20, 60, and 100 s) to capture temporal dynamics at different scales.

AutoML Pipeline and Evaluation

We employed an Automated Machine Learning (AutoML) strategy using the Tree-based Pipeline Optimization Tool3 (TPOT) to optimize regression architectures. Connectivity matrices were vectorized into 6,670 features (upper triangle). For each of the 12 connectivity metrics, we executed 20 independent optimization runs (20 generations, population 25) using stratified 6-fold cross-validation. This resulted in 2,880 candidate models. Performance was assessed on a hold-out test set (15%) using a custom composite score that integrates normalized MAE, RMSE, and R2 to provide a balanced evaluation of accuracy and error. The final model was selected based on this score and computational efficiency.

Explainability

We applied SHAP4 (SHapley Additive exPlanations) with a KernelExplainer to interpret the optimal model. To handle computational complexity, we generated a background dataset using 35 K-means centroids derived from the training data. We calculated feature importance (edge-level) and nodal strength (region-level) to identify the specific language network components driving age prediction. The Network-based Language Aging Gap (N-LAG) was calculated as the difference between predicted and chronological age (ypred - yreal). 

N-LAG Validation

Finally, to examine the behavioral relevance of the N-LAG, we assessed its association with verbal and non-verbal intelligence using the Kaufman Brief Intelligence Test (KBIT-2). Since not all participants completed the KBIT, this analysis was performed only on the subset of individuals with available KBIT scores. We used a Bayesian correlation analysis to evaluate the relationship between N-LAG and cognitive performance, reporting Pearson’s r and Bayes Factors (BF10) to provide a robust measure of statistical evidence beyond conventional thresholds.

Results

Model Performance and Methodological Comparison

We systematically evaluated 12 different connectivity metrics combined with an AutoML optimization approach and found clear differences in performance among the metrics. The most accurate predictions were achieved using Euclidean distance matrices, reaching an R² of 0.848, a mean absolute error (MAE) of 4.82 years, and a root mean squared error (RMSE) of 6.69 years. This accuracy is better than typical benchmarks, which range from 5 to 9 years of MAE, showing that focusing on task-based language network connectivity is effective.

A key finding is that distance-based metrics outperform standard correlation-based measures when predicting age from the language network. Looking at the top 100 pipelines, 60% used Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) matrices, 18% used Euclidean distance, and 11% used Manhattan distance. Overall, nearly 90% of the best models relied on distance-based metrics. This suggests that age-related changes are better captured by measures reflecting signal differences or temporal misalignment between regions, rather than simple linear synchrony. In contrast, Earth Mover’s Distance (EMD) performed poorly, with the best EMD-based model achieving an R² of only 0.02.

Optimal Pipeline Architecture

 The top-performing pipeline, identified through TPOT’s evolutionary search, used a stacked ensemble design. It starts with MaxAbsScaler normalization, followed by VarianceThreshold feature selection. The main predictive component is a FeatureUnion that combines the original features with predictions from a Linear Support Vector Regressor, which was tuned with a high regularization value. These combined features are then fed into a K-Nearest Neighbors regressor with 25 neighbors and distance-based weighting. Importantly, although this architecture was originally evolved using DTW matrices, it generalized well to Euclidean distance matrices, where it achieved the best overall performance. This shows the robustness of the AutoML-derived architecture.

Explainability 

Preliminary analyses using SHAP are starting to reveal which functional connections contribute most to the model’s predictions. Early results suggest that predictive information is spread across both intra- and inter-hemispheric connections.

N-LAG Validation

To evaluate the behavioral relevance of the Network-based Language Aging Gap (N-LAG), we examined its relationship with cognitive performance in participants under 40 years old, where model predictions were most reliable. Cognitive performance was measured using the KBIT-2 Vocabulary and Matrices subtests. Bayesian correlation analyses showed negative trends between N-LAG and cognitive scores. For KBIT Vocabulary (N=12), the correlation was about -0.28, and for KBIT Matrices (N=21), it was about -0.30. Although the Bayes factors indicate that the evidence is currently weak due to the small sample, the consistent negative trend supports the idea that a younger effective brain age, reflected by a lower N-LAG, is linked to better cognitive performance. Individuals with a negative N-LAG, suggesting resilient aging, tended to score higher on both verbal and non-verbal intelligence tests. This indicates that N-LAG captures meaningful differences in the functional integrity of the language network beyond chronological age.

Conclusion

This study establishes the Network-based Language Aging Gap (N-LAG) as a robust, domain-specific biomarker for quantifying functional aging within the language system. Through a systematic benchmarking of 12 connectivity metrics, we show that distance-based measures, including Euclidean, Manhattan, and Dynamic Time Warping, clearly outperform standard correlation-based approaches. The resulting prediction error, with a mean absolute error of approximately 4.8 years, reaches a level comparable to state-of-the-art structural MRI brain age models. These findings challenge the widespread reliance on Pearson correlation in functional connectomics and indicate that information related to signal magnitude and temporal misalignment captures critical aspects of aging that are often missed by conventional analyses.

The AutoML-driven framework further reveals that the language connectome undergoes systematic and predictable reorganization across the lifespan. The strong performance of Euclidean and DTW-based representations suggests that aging in language networks is better described in terms of changes in geometric and temporal distances between regional signals, rather than solely by alterations in linear synchrony. Importantly, preliminary analyses linking N-LAG to behavior indicate cognitive relevance: individuals with a “younger” functional language network, reflected by a negative N-LAG, tend to show higher performance in both verbal and fluid intelligence measures. This pattern supports the interpretation of N-LAG as an index of neural resilience within the language system.

Although additional validation in larger and more diverse samples is necessary to strengthen the behavioral conclusions, the present results underscore the sensitivity of task-based fMRI for capturing network-specific aging effects. By integrating interpretable machine learning with a targeted functional focus, the N-LAG framework offers a promising approach for detecting subtle functional decline before the emergence of overt clinical symptoms. This opens new avenues for early identification and potential intervention in neurodegenerative conditions that primarily affect communication and language functions.
