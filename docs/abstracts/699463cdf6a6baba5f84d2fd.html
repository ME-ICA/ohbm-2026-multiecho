Introduction

Isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) is a specific subtype of prodromal alpha-synucleinopathy, characterized by the loss of physiological atonia and the presence of dream-enactment behaviors during REM sleep. It represents the “body-first” subtype of alpha-synucleinopathy, in which alpha-synuclein aggregation is thought to originate in the autonomic nervous system and subsequently spread to the central nervous system via the vagus nerve and brainstem. Neuropathological and neurophysiological studies have documented damage to and dysfunction of widespread neuronal networks within the brainstem in iRBD. However, most morphometric studies to date have focused on whole-brain analyses and relied on conventional MPRAGE images, which offer suboptimal sensitivity for brainstem structures, resulting in heterogeneous and often inconsistent findings.1,2

In this study, we performed voxel-wise analyses comparing iRBD patients with healthy controls using MP2RAGE images and a processing pipeline optimized for the detection of brainstem-specific differences.

Methods

The study included 27 iRBD patients (3 females; mean age 64 ± 8 years), and 40 healthy controls (13 females; mean age 59 ± 10 years). MP2RAGE data acquired at 3T system (Siemens Skyra) were processed using SPM25, CAT12 v12.9 and the SUIT toolbox v3.7 to enable sensitive voxel-wise analyses of the brainstem. T1-weighted UNI images were used for subject-specific isolation of the posterior fossa, followed by non-linear normalization into SUIT space. Gray and white matter tissue density maps were obtained using CAT12 segmentation pipeline and resliced into SUIT space using SUIT-derived deformation parameters. Quantitative T1 maps derived from MP2RAGE were resliced into SUIT space to preserve quantitative signal integrity. All SUIT-normalized maps were smoothed with a small isotropic Gaussian kernel (3 mm FWHM). Voxel-wise group comparisons were performed within a brainstem mask using general linear models including age and sex as covariates. Statistical inference was based on permutation testing with threshold-free cluster enhancement (TFCE) with 5000 permutations and family-wise error (FWE) correction at p<0.05. Locations of significant clusters were retrieved using the BrainstemNavigator atlas (https://www.nitrc.org/projects/brainstemnavig).

Results

In iRBD compared to controls, higher grey matter tissue density was observed in bilateral ventral tegmental area and pedunculopontine nuclei, left subcoeruleus, left viscero-sensory-motor nuclei complex, right inferior olivary nucleus and several regions in medullary and pontine reticular formation. In controls compared to iRBD, higher white matter density was observed in bilateral ventral tegmental area (p<0.05 FWE corrected; Fig.1).

Conclusion

Our results demonstrate widespread brainstem alterations in patients with iRBD. Counterintuitively, we observed higher grey matter density in iRBD compared to controls. This finding was accompanied by lower white matter density in the ventral tegmental area in iRBD relative to controls, a pattern that may indicate reduced myelin content in this region in iRBD. Such an interpretation is consistent with previously reported increases in magnetic susceptibility within the ventral tegmental area in iRBD.3,4 Neuropathological studies have described mild neuronal loss and gliosis in several brainstem nuclei in post-mortem examinations of iRBD patients.5 It is therefore unlikely that the observed increase in grey matter density reflects compensatory neuroplastic changes. Instead, we speculate that reduced myelin content may increase the probability of affected regions being classified as grey matter in voxel-based analyses.

In conclusion, voxel-based morphometry using MP2RAGE is capable of detecting widespread brainstem alterations in iRBD. Affected structures include the ventral tegmental area, pedunculopontine nuclei, subcoeruleus region, and the medullary and pontine reticular formation.
