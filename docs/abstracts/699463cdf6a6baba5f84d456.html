Introduction

Chemical Exchange Saturation Transfer (CEST) MRI, including Amide Proton Transfer (APT) and creatine-weighted (Crw) imaging, provides molecular contrast by detecting exchangeable protons on metabolites and macromolecules through their interaction with bulk water. 1 APTw images enable indirect mapping of protein content, pH, and microstructural changes in brain and spinal cord tissues. Creatine is a central metabolite in cerebral energy metabolism, and its alterations are linked to aging, neurodegeneration, tumors, and impaired bioenergetics. 2 High resolution Cr and APT mapping has numerous clinical applications.  APTw and Cr maps can be generated by measuring exchangeable protons near 3.5 ppm and ~1.8–2 ppm respectively. 

Conventional CEST methods are limited by long scan times, motion sensitivity, and high RF power deposition and these issues are amplified at 7 T. Commonly used Cartesian encoding based MRI CEST approaches require repeated scans across multiple offset saturating frequencies, resulting in long exam times and are not feasible for translation to clinical studies. Non-cartesian trajectories such as radial stack-of-star (SoS) trajectories enable aggressive undersampling for an accelerated acquisition and are inherently motion-insensitive. We developed a motion-robust, power-efficient 3D CEST framework using 3D radial Stack-of-Stars (SoS) and low-power saturation pulses to simultaneously generate APTw, Crw, and MTRasym contrasts from highly undersampled kspace while preserving magnetization preparation. 

The goal of this study is to determine the feasibility of acquiring APTw and Crw maps using novel imaging 3D radial SoS sequence at 7T. Our results demonstrate significant step towards high resolution CEST imaging for maximum brain coverage in clinically relevant scan time. 

Methods

All experiments were performed using Siemens 7T Terra. X and 8Tx/32Rx head coil. Four healthy subjects participated in the study. The sequence consists of a magnetization transfer (MT) preparation block followed by 3D SoS radial readout (Fig. 1). All data were acquired using a single-shot acquisition, i.e., one saturation block followed by rapid acquisition. The 3D SoS implemented here applies an additional phase-encoding gradient in the slice direction (centric encoding) to acquire all slices with high SNR. The MT preparation block consisted of 30 hard pulses (95 ms duration, 5 ms inter-pulse delay) with crusher gradients of 11 mT/m applied during each delay. Sixty-four frequency offsets were acquired to generate the Z-spectrum. The offsets were non-uniformly sampled from –300 to +300 ppm, with dense spacing near water between –6 to +6 ppm and wider spacing at distant offsets. B1 power was 0.50 μT for all RF pulses. The image acquisition was single shot with TE = 2.01 ms, flip angle = 5°, 12 slices, slice thickness = 4 mm, FOV = 256, 256 readout point and 64 radial projections. The inter-pulse repetition time was 4 ms with total acquisition time of 3 sec for complete 3D dataset. TR for one offset was 8 sec (which included 3 sec saturation time +3 sec image acquisition and 2 sec recovery delay) resulting in total scan time of and 8.5 minutes. 

Image reconstruction was done using the Berkeley Advanced Reconstruction Toolbox (BART) with ESPIRiT coil calibration, RING gradient delay correction, and compressed sensing with 0.01 l1-wavelet regularization. 3,4 Bo correction was applied voxel-wise with an externally calculated Bo map derived directly from radial images at two echo times, TE1=2.01 and TE2=4.02 ms.

MTRasym, APTw, and Crw maps were calculated after B0 correction in MATLAB (MathWorks, Natick, MA, USA): After Bo correction, Z-spectra were normalized Z=  Ssat/S0  with S0 the unsaturated image without magnetization preparation. Magnetic transfer ratio asymmetry ( MTRasym ) for each frequency offset was then calculated as: MTRasym (∆ω)=Z(-∆ω)-Z(∆ω), computed on paired ± ppm points within the in-vivo range (we report 0–20 ppm). ∆ω is the offset resonance frequency from water in ppm (3.5 ppm for APT and 2 ppm for Cr). 

Results

CEST Z-spectra from ROIs in the Grey Matter (GM) and White Matter (WM), in two different slices are shown in Figure 2. Z-spectrum is nearly symmetric, and signal amplitude decreases close to zero around 0 ppm offset frequency (Fig. 2a) demonstrating high effectiveness of the implemented saturation block. The positive and negative offset frequencies are marked with green and red markers, respectively. Bo-correction did not affect the z-spectrum significantly, as the z-spectrum is densely sampled around 0 ppm (Fig. 2b).

The MTRasym plots for GM and WM ROIs are shown in Fig. 2c and 2d, respectively. MTRasym curves reveal negative and consistent asymmetry for 3D across slices. APTw values were more negative with clearer GM/WM contrast (Fig. 2e). Overall, our 3D radial-CEST acquisition provided stability and uniform APTw and Crw contrast across slices.

Conclusion

In this work, we studied the feasibility of rapid, motion-robust APT and Creatine CEST imaging at 7T using 3D radial stack-of-stars trajectory with low-power saturation. Our 3D stack-of-stars (SoS) sequence leverages radial sampling for motion insensitivity and image quality, while improving SNR through an additional gradient in the slice direction. Furthermore, all slices are acquired with single-shot acquisition, i.e., one saturation block followed by rapid acquisition of the entire slab. 

Fast and reliable CEST imaging is a highly desired goal to advance clinical applications in neuropathology but has not gained widespread use due to long acquisition times and limited reproducibility due to low SNR. We have overcome this by fast and robust under-sampled 3D sequence radial acquisition approach. Here we used 64 spoke acquisition that successfully preserves the CEST contrast during the readout generating reliable CEST images. In case of full kspace acquisition, the magnetization saturation contrast will be lost during the later part of the acquisition block, reducing contrast and SNR. 5 An alternate approach is to use segmented acquisition with full kspace which will result in increased scan time. 

Given the importance of imaging APTw and creatine-weighted CEST contrasts for probing molecular and metabolic information beyond conventional MRI, this work represents a significant step toward high resolution CEST imaging for maximum brain coverage in clinically relevant scan time with advanced acquisition and reconstruction. In future implementation of our sequence, we would extend the readout to a 3D koosh-ball trajectory that is ideal for whole-brain or tumor coverage and to further reduce motion effects. Future work can apply our radial CEST imaging to other structures, such as the spinal cord, heart, and skeletal-muscular tissue, and can be considered for clinical applications such as cancer, multiple sclerosis, and schizophrenia.
