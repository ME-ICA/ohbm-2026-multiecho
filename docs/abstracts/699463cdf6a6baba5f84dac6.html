Introduction

Multimodal neuroimaging provides complementary information about brain structure and function, but many datasets suffer from missing modalities, limiting statistical power and biasing analyses. Traditional imputation or subject exclusion strategies are suboptimal, particularly when modalities are highly heterogeneous, such as structural MRI and functional network connectivity (FNC). Recent generative models offer promising alternatives, yet many approaches require paired data or struggle to preserve biologically meaningful variability. Here, we propose a GAN-guided diffusion framework for bidirectional translation between T1-weighted MRI and FNC, designed to leverage both paired and unpaired data while preserving clinically relevant patterns in Alzheimer’s disease (AD).

Methods

We developed a conditional diffusion model augmented with adversarial learning and cycle consistency to enable cross-modality translation between T1-weighted MRI and FNC. The framework integrates (i) a cycle-consistent GAN to generate synthetic conditioning samples when paired data are unavailable, and (ii) an adversarial diffusion process that progressively denoises samples toward the target modality using a small number of diffusion steps. Subject-aware and cycle-consistency losses were incorporated to preserve individual-level variability and prevent collapse toward population averages. The model was evaluated on Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) data (1,709 subjects; 8,372 samples), using NeuroMark-derived FNC and structural MRI. Performance was compared against conditional DDPM and CycleGAN baselines.

Results

Figure 1 illustrates the results generated by the proposed model. The model produces high-fidelity synthetic modalities that closely match real data in both structure and variability. For T1 synthesis, the model achieved higher PSNR (24.95) and SSIM (0.86) compared with baseline methods, while FNC synthesis showed a stronger correlation with real connectivity patterns (r = 0.65). Mean and variance analyses demonstrate preservation of subject-level variability rather than simple averaging. Importantly, the generated T1 and FNC data reproduce clinically meaningful group differences among cognitively normal, mild cognitive impairment, and Alzheimer’s disease groups, despite the absence of diagnostic labels during training. These results indicate that disease-relevant information is embedded in the learned cross-modal mappings.

Conclusion

In summary, we introduce a GAN-guided diffusion framework for bidirectional translation between structural MRI and functional connectivity that effectively handles missing modalities and heterogeneous data representations. By leveraging both paired and unpaired samples, the model produces anatomically and functionally realistic synthetic data while preserving clinically relevant group differences. This approach offers a robust alternative to traditional imputation methods and has potential applications in multimodal data augmentation, biomarker discovery, and studies where full multimodal acquisition is infeasible.
