Introduction

Structural MRI plays an important role in identifying characteristic patterns of neurodegeneration (e.g. cortical thinning) that support syndromic clinical diagnosis, but MRI signatures of pathology-associated appearance features of FTLD are lacking. Tissue microstructural changes can subtly change intracortical MRI appearance based on pathology.1 Textural features may provide earlier identification of disease or provide faster localization of pathology. Based on prior work,1 we hypothesize that high resolution MRI sequences, which capture iron-rich gliosis, or iron that accumulates in and activates glial cells, may be informative for defining FTLD biomarkers.

Ex vivo T2*w MRI of autopsy-confirmed FTLD provides exactly such a unique opportunity for defining informative biomarkers, given its ability to obtain ultra-high resolution data that can localize pathologic tissue for further analysis.2 Visual inspection of ex vivo T2*w MRI reveals abnormal appearing gray matter hypothesized to correspond to specific underlying biological processes such as increased iron deposition and gliosis.1,3 However, manual identification of pathological regions is time consuming, qualitative, and subjective. Radiomics analysis based on textural features is a potentially promising method for identifying regions of pathology, like iron-rich gliosis, in FTLD but to date its application to high resolution ex vivo MRI has been limited.

Our group has created a radiomics pipeline, a novel use of gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) discretization to create quantitative ex vivo MRI features. We demonstrate that GLCM features correlate with semiquantitative ratings of FTLD pathology, providing a foundation for a novel biomarker strategy to predict whole-brain maps of FTLD pathological features.

Methods

We investigated 28 individuals enrolled in Clinical Core and brain donation programs through the Penn Frontotemporal Degeneration Center and Penn Alzheimer’s Disease Research Center (Figure 1C). Twenty-five participants met clinical criteria for behavioral variant frontotemporal degeneration (bvFTD) and 3 were cognitively unimpaired (CU). Post-mortem autopsy procedures are previously described4. In the bvFTD cohort, 11 participants had a neuropathological diagnosis of FTLD-TDP, 10 with FTLD-Tau, and 4 with AD neuropathological change; the three CU participants had minimal pathology.

MRI preprocessing and rating:

Following formalin fixation, ex vivo MRI of one brain hemisphere included a 0.3 mm isotropic T2w (TE=0.382s, TR=3s) and 0.16mm isotropic T2*w FLASH sequence (TE=0.02701s, TR=0.06s). T2w MRI was manually AC-PC aligned. Point annotations were manually placed by a trained expert in 7 anatomic regions of interests (ROIs). FLASH MRI was aligned to the T2w MRI by applying the same rotation matrix.

An expert blinded to the diagnosis manually rated 7 ROIs using solely the T2*w FLASH scan on an ordinal scale between 0-3 (no pathology, mild, moderate, severe pathology), including midfrontal cortex (MFC), orbitofrontal cortex (OFC), anterior-inferior temporal cortex (AITC), motor cortex (MOT), angular gyrus (ANG), insular (INSULA), and visual cortex (VIS). Normal cortex was considered to have 2-3 homogeneous, well-defined layers. GM pathology ranged from granular/speckling cortex to hypodense, laminar bands. All ratings of VIS were set as no pathology (0) and therefore removed from all subsequent analyses.

Radiomics feature mapping

A custom segmentation model for FLASH MRI labeled gray matter (GM) and white matter (WM).2 We used N4 bias field correction on FLASH, then used our segmentation as a prior for intensity non-uniformity correction in the SPM package, to remove inhomogeneity artifact. Radiomics analysis was performed on only GM tissue by iterating in all 3 directions of the image to create feature maps for GM in FLASH MRI using PyRadiomics.5 To force dynamic bin sizes during the GLCM discretization step we used binCount = 64 to fit the range of intensities within an input image window to a constant number of discrete levels. Every sliding kernel has custom discretization, such that levels are not comparable between kernels, to force GLCM dependent features to be relative to contrast instead of intensity.

We chose a kernel radius of 31 voxels (kernel size of 63x63x63 voxels i.e. 10mm), and stride of 5 voxels to reduce computation and maintain a window that extends from pial surface to white matter. Discretization to generate the GLCM was done per kernel, and outliers (>4 standard deviations from mean) per kernel were set to the appropriate extrema to avoid biasing dynamic bins. Only GLCM and first order features were generated. We remapped feature values per kernel back to image space to create feature maps.

Statistical analysis

We presented feature maps overlayed on MRI images to an expert who suggested which features overlapped with obvious pathology. The human expert identified 6 GLCM feature maps that appeared to be useful based on a small subset of hemispheres: contrast, joint entropy, joint energy, difference entropy, inverse difference moment/homogeneity (IDM), and sum entropy.

For each of the 6 radiomic features the measurements within a 5mm spherical neighborhood of each point annotation were averaged. Spearman rank correlation was used to correlate each feature with semi-quantitative expert ratings of pathology, adjusted for multiple comparisons using false discovery rate. A multivariate ordinal regression model was fit based on features that reached significance in multiple ROIs. We pooled data from all ROIs and included disease duration and age of death covariates. We used the fitted model to predict a pathology rating across all brain voxels. Evaluation was done via 7-fold cross validation across participants.

Results

Univariate associations: As shown in Figure 2A, correlation of feature values with ordinal ratings showed that many of the ROIs had significant relationships. Moreover, directionality of correlations matches the expected trend for all the features; all entropy features, as well as contrast, measure randomness and variability and accordingly correlate to higher pathology ratings. Inverse difference moment (IDM), and joint energy measure aspects of smoothness and negatively correlated to pathology. Of note, sum entropy appeared to show lower correlation to ratings compared to other features.

ROIs showed differing numbers of significant rating-feature correlations (see Figure 2).  Specifically, all 6 radiomic features correlated with AITC pathological ratings. 5 out of 6 radiomic features evaluated correlated with pathology in the MFC, OFC, and insula ROIs, which are known anatomic regions affected in bvFTD. Sum entropy did not correlate with pathological ratings in these ROIs.  In ANG, we similarly observed correlations with pathological ratings for contrast, IDM, and Difference entropy but to a lesser extent Joint entropy and not Sum entropy. We did not observe significant correlations between radiomic features and motor cortex pathological ratings, consistent with this ROI being relatively spared in bvFTD.

Multivariate models. Since our univariate analyses revealed significant associations, we generated a composite ordinal regression predictor that was fitted with 5 of 6 features (dropping Sum Entropy due to low significance values) by concatenating all ROIs and with disease duration and age at death as covariates. We ran a 7-fold cross validation across subjects and showed a Spearman rank correlation to actual predictions of 0.648±0.086. In a multivariate model, contrast (p<0.001), IDM (p=0.022), and difference entropy (p<0.001) showed significance to the model in a Wald test, while all other feature variables did not reach significance (all p>0.05). This likely indicates significant collinearity between some of the input features.

Whole-Brain Prediction. A full-brain prediction map was generated for each participant and was used for qualitative assessment, with examples shown in Figure 2B. We observed widespread patterns of pathology across the whole brain consistent with end-stage disease at autopsy. Critically, the most focal predicted pathological regions are in frontal and temporal regions, consistent with bvFTD patterns of disease. Non bvFTD brains with minimal pathology, such as the primary age related tauopathy individual in Figure 2B, did not have a similar pattern of disease identified. We also observed regions predicted as pathological that may be due to artifacts like signal drop out as well as visual cortex regions not typically involved in bvFTD that requires additional investigation. Predictions for highest pathology cluster in regions with expected textural change, such as laminar iron degeneration or “speckling” in the GM. Increased randomness and variability in GM generally correlated to pathology. We are currently validating these predictions with pathological ratings in unseen participant MRIs.

Conclusion

Our work measures GM textural features in individuals with bvFTD and show that it correlates to iron-rich gliosis pathology ratings from a human expert. We can identify and quantify the textural features that a rater uses to qualitatively identify GM pathology, and the derived features correlate to semiquantitative ratings of pathology. This provides an alternative to more traditional macrostructural measures of neurodegeneration such as cortical thickness.

                  Our prediction maps and feature maps for many brains show significant regional patterns. MFC, OFC, and insula are ROIs most impacted by FTLD and accordingly had high correlations to features. AITC and ANG are more impacted in other variants of FTLD, though our bvFTD samples had predominantly frontally-mediated executive/behavioral features , some individuals later in disease developed language features reflecting spread of disease progression to these anatomic regions. Further work is required to elucidate how regional predictions relate to pathological features.

                  Features such as contrast and difference entropy capture high local intensity variability within tissue, which can indicate presence of hypointense laminar banding. In contrast, joint entropy and sum entropy performed worse due to capturing longer-range variability such as gradual texture changes whereas the human expert ratings reflect sudden contrast changes in GM microstructure. Additional work may be necessary to compare variability of features between ROIs.

In contrast to quantitative MRI techniques such as quantitative susceptibility mapping, quantifying subtle pathology in non-quantitative MRI is a difficult task, but our work shows a potential usage of radiomics to overcome such challenges. Improper analysis may easily prevent comparison of regions even within the same brain, due to intrinsic unitless nature of voxel intensities. By using GLCM discretization per kernel, and a dynamic bin width via a constant bin count, we further allow our textural analysis to be agnostic to arbitrary voxel intensities, to capture intensity contrast relative to a specific region.

Though data presented here are limited to GM regions, we also have labels for additional tissue types, including WM. Many ROIs appear to have white matter hyperintensities possibly correlating to demyelination, motivating a future extension of our work. Based on neuropathological data, future work will correlate histopathology stains to regional patterns and textural features to identify biological correlates for texture. We also plan to extend the work beyond clinical bvFTD to additional FTLD variants to show generalizability of results. Registering prediction and feature maps to a common template could identify differential spatial topography for pathological phenotypes. We plan to explore additional radiomic features as well, and compare against deep learning-based feature maps.

Our work opens interesting new directions for utilizing iron-sensitive structural MRI sequences to quantify textural measures of degeneration. We show for the first time that ex vivo MRI radiomics can correlate to FTLD semiquantitative pathology ratings, and can be used to make prediction maps for possible pathology across the entire brain.
