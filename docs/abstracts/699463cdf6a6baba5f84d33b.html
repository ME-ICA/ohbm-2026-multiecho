Introduction

The simultaneous acquisition of EEG-fMRI signals is a neuroimaging technique that aims to combine the high temporal resolution of EEG and the high spatial resolution of fMRI to obtain an accurate and dynamic mapping of brain activity. However, recording EEG under fMRI has a price, as it dramatically affects the EEG signal due to the strong MRI magnetic field B0 and switching of gradients. This causes two main types of artifacts: gradient artifacts and motion artifacts. Motion artifacts are caused by the fact that a closed electrical circuit in a magnetic field induces an electric current (Lenz-Faraday law of induction). Motion can be generated by cardio-ballistic activity, the participants' movements, or vibrations due to the helium pump.
In theory, if there are no movements in the MRI tube during recording, MR pulse gradient artifacts can be corrected1, since the EEG is synchronized with the MRI clock.
In this study, we record and study motion artifacts in different types of experiments. This will determine the principale cause of noticeable artifacts, especially the Helium pump one.

Methods

For this study (NCT05107232), we designed a protocol to measure MR-related artifacts and the Helium pump artifacts on EEG signals, in order to study their impact.
To do so, two types of data were acquired: an Alive Phantom and a Phantom.
The Alive Phantom2 is a participant who wears an insulating layer between the scalp and the EEG cap to isolate the spontaneous electrical brain activity. The purpose of this isolation is to mimic a standard EEG-fMRI sequence, recording everything except the electrical brain activity, leaving the EEG-fMRI recording only artifacts.
The purpose of the phantom is to remove any motion-related artefacts generated by small movement of the participant and cardiobalistic activity. The recorded artefacts would not be due to human activity.
Here, both the Alive Phantom and the Phantom are equipped with four Carbon Wire Loops (CWL) to record any movements of the electrodes in the magnetic field3.

Data were acquired on a 3T MRI scanner (Magnetom Prisma, Siemens Healthineers), using a 64-channel MR-compatible BrainAmp EEG cap with an ECG channel and four CWL from BrainProducts. For the Alive Phantom, the participant was instructed not to move and holders were placed around the head, as for a classical EEG-fMRI acquisition.
For the phantom acquisition, we used an MRI sphere phantom with a 17cm diameter on which we placed the same MR-compatible EEG cap equipped with CWL.
The phantom was then positioned in the MRI at the same position as the head of the Alive Phantom.

The Alive Phantom had two 2-minute resting state acquisitions: 1/ EEG-fMRI resting state recording with the He pump turned ON. 2/ EEG-fMRI resting state recording with the He pump turned OFF.
For the Alive Phantom and the Phantom we did the following 2-minutes runs : 1/ EEG recording without fMRI sequence and the Helium pump turned OFF.
2/ EEG recording without fMRI sequence with the Helium pump turned ON.

The data were processed using Brain Vision Analyzer (BVA). All EEG signals, alive phantom and phantom ones, were down-sampled at 500 Hz and filtered with a high-pass filter at 0.1 Hz in order to remove slow artifact amplitude modulation4.

For the Alive Phantom, the gradient correction of EEG-fMRI recordings was done using the AAS approach implemented in BVA.
After that process, the power spectral density of all EEG and CWL signals (from Alive Phantom and Phantom) was computed and compared.

For this preliminary study, we performed the following analysis: the CWL's Phantom with no MRI acquisition with the Helium pump turned on versus turned off: the purpose of this comparison is to observe whether or not the Helium pump can be seen on CWL in the absence of gradient, cardiobalistic, and other motion-related artefacts.

Results

Primary outcome:
The phantoms' CWL recording without MRI acquisition with the Helium pump turned on, demonstrated a significant artifact in the power spectral density spectrum at 47 Hz that does not appear when the Helium pump is turned off (Figure 1.A). Since there is no gradient artifact nor cardio-ballistic artifacts in those phantom recordings, the Helium pump artifact is the only source of differences between the recordings. The Helium pump is then what causes the peak at 47 Hz in the power spectral density of the Phantom's CWL.

Secondary outcome:
Alive phantoms' CWL during EEG-fMRI recording with the helium pump turned on after gradient artefact correction showed, as expected now, a spike at 47 Hz in CWL power spectral density (Figure 1.B). Also, all the frequencies (in blue, Alive Phantom's CWL with pump off), different from the red plots (Phantom's CWL with pump off), are due to cardiobalistic artefacts and other movements from the participant.

To make sure that the Helium pump artifact was not a special case in our experiment, but rather a broader observation, another EEG-fMRI acquisitions dataset5 using a 3T Verio MRI scanner was processed. The power spectral density of those EEG signals showed the same artifact at 47 Hz.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we performed a phantom and an alive phantom acquisition in this study to characterize motion artifacts on the EEG acquired in MRI.
The observations on the Alive Phantom EEG-fMRI acquisition showed the effect of the Helium pump on CWL signals responsible for a significant spike at 47 Hz in the gamma band.
Furthermore, the analysis proved, thanks to the Alive Phantom, that the CWL does not only capture human movement artifacts (cardioballistic, breathing,...) but also the artifact induced by the Helium pump at 47 Hz. The Helium pump causes vibrations in the MRI tube that create movements in the magnetic field.
