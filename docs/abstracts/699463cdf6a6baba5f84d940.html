Introduction

Infancy represents a period of exceptionally rapid brain development, characterized by profound functional changes and the progressive maturation of connectivity between functional systems. These evolving spatiotemporal patterns establish the neural foundation for emerging behavioral and cognitive capacities. Previous studies of infant brain functional development either focused on network (system)-wise maturation, or on inter-network and whole-brain functional connectivity (FC) topology, though largely confined to static FC. However, inherent FC dynamics, such as FC flexibility,1 are critical for environmental adaptation and behavioral maturation. The Quasi-Periodic Patterns (QPPs) algorithm is dynamic FC method for identifying recurring spatiotemporal brain activity patterns in a data-driven, pattern exploration manner, and it may reflect a systemic, fundamental mechanism that contributes to both static and dynamic FC.2 Characterizing QPPs developmental trajectories in infancy may reveal core neurobiological processes underlying early brain maturation. In the present study, we leveraged a large cross-population resting-state fMRI dataset (of infants aged 0-3 years) to identify QPPs that govern whole-brain functional activities and characterize their early developmental trajectories. We focused on age-related changes in default mode network (DMN)-centered dynamic interactions, aiming to elucidate the neural foundations of emerging self-awareness and higher-order cognitive development.

Methods

Data and Preprocessing

We analyzed 573 resting-state fMRI scans from 492 infants aged 0-3 years from the Baby Connectome Project (BCP) and the Chinese Baby Connectome Project (CBCP).3,4 After careful quality control, data with good quality were divided into eight age bins: 0-5, 3-8, 6-11, 9-14, 12-17, 15-23, 18-29, and 24-36 months (n = 317, 136, 114, 141, 121, 118, 151, 104). Infant-dedicated preprocessing was conducted based on established pipelines.5 Preprocessed data were parcellated into 400 regions of interest using the Schaefer’s atlas and mapped to Yeo's seven networks atlas, with mean fMRI time series extracted for each region.

QPPs Calculation

QPPs were identified from preprocessed fMRI 4D data using a template-matching algorithm that detects recurring spatiotemporal patterns (usually spanned about 30 s) through iterations. Multiple random initializations were used to generate robust QPPs. To account for phase variability across subjects, individual QPPs were then aligned to a group template anchored at peak left posterior cingulate cortex (a core DMN region) activity. After extracting the principal QPP (i.e., QPP1), its effect was regressed out and the above procedure was repeated to identify the secondary QPP (QPP2)^5.

Developmental Analysis

To characterize age-related changes in brain functional network dynamics, we quantified temporal relationships between the DMN and other networks (particularly task-positive networks, TPN, consisting of dorsal attention network, DAN, and frontoparietal network, FPN) within each QPP cycle. We computed phase differences and correlation patterns across the age bins to identify developmental trajectories of large-scale network coordination.

Results

For QPP1, the brain regions that are negatively correlated with the DMN are initially restricted to the sensorimotor network (SMN), then gradually expand to high-level function-related regions including the DAN and FPN, the core constituents of the TPN. This progressive pattern shift during 0-3 years of age likely reflects the maturation of externally-oriented attention/control systems and their functional disassociation from the internally-focused DMN, suggesting gradual establishment of the foundation for flexible attention switching and cognitive control (Fig. 1a).

In contrast, QPP2 exhibits a distinct developmental trajectory: while DMN-DAN’s anti-correlation strengthens with age, DMN-FPN coupling progressively shifts toward positive synchrony. This divergent pattern suggests that the FPN may serve as a transitional hub that flexibly couples with both DMN and DAN depending on task demands, a capacity that emerges during early development (Fig. 1b).

Notably, we observed a marked transition around 24 months of age: QPP1 and QPP2 undergo a swap in their dominant spatiotemporal features (see Fig. 1 arrows). Prior to this transition, DMN-TPN anti-correlation in QPP1 increases significantly (Fig. 2a), paralleled by growing squared differences between these networks (Fig. 2b). This reorganization around 24 months may represent a critical developmental milestone in large-scale network dynamics, where dominant (or driving force) of the toddler’s brain spontaneous activity and co-activity may turn to previously immature TPN network, potentially reflecting a fundamental shift in the brain's functional architecture in urgent need for adaptive attention control during early childhood.

Conclusion

Using quasi-periodic patterns from 0-36 month resting-state fMRI data, we identified two critical developmental neural markers. First, DMN-TPN anti-correlation progressively strengthens from sensorimotor to higher-order systems, while DMN-FPN coupling shifts toward synergy. Second, a marked reorganization occurs around 24 months with a swap in dominant QPP configurations. These findings demonstrate that spatiotemporal dynamics are fundamental to understanding early brain maturation and provide a novel neuroimaging biomarker reflecting this previously undetected milestone.
