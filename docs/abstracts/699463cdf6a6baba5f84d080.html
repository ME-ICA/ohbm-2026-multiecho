Introduction

Focal demyelinating lesions are the pathological hallmark of multiple sclerosis (MS). In the white matter, these lesions are most sensitively detected on T2-weighted MR images. However, the total number and volume of T2 lesions correlate only moderately with clinical disability, a well-known phenomenon called the clinico-radiological paradox. This discrepancy is thought to arise because T2 images do not capture the severity of tissue damage within lesions. T1-weighted images can provide more insight into this, with the most severely damaged lesions appearing as hypointense black holes (BHs). BHs are traditionally defined on 2D T1-weighted spin-echo (SE) images as white matter lesions that appear iso- or hypointense relative to cortical grey matter (cGM)1. 2D T1-defined BHs have a well-established histopathological correlate of  severe demyelination and axonal loss, and show a stronger association with clinical disability than T2 lesion load, hence their positioning as a marker of more aggressive disease2.

However, modern imaging protocols have moved towards 3D T1 gradient-echo (GRE) acquisitions rather than 2D T1 SE. The different acquisition increases sensitivity to T1 variation, this results in a greater number of lesions appearing hypointense compared to 2D T1 images. In addition, manual BH segmentation is time-consuming and impractical within an already busy clinical workflow. As such, there is a clear need for the development of automated BH segmentation methods on 3D T1 images that account for the altered tissue contrast.

The aim of this study was to develop and validate a semi-automated method that segments BHs on 3D T1-weighted images, using manual and automated3 2D T1 BH segmentations as reference, in people with MS (pwMS). This approach preserves the established radiological definition and histopathological specificity of 2D T1 BHs while enabling reproducible, quantitative assessment of irreversible white matter damage on modern 3D T1-weighted images.

Methods

88 pwMS were retrospectively included from the Amsterdam MS cohort based on the availability of pre-contrast 2D T1 SE, 3D T1 GRE and 3D fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) MRI scans. 20 pwMS spanning a wide range of BH and FLAIR lesion volumes comprised the training set; 68 pwMS comprised the test set.

FLAIR lesions were defined as hyperintense regions (>5 voxels) relative to surrounding tissue and automatically segmented using nicMSlesions.

Figure 1 outlines the study overview, summarized:

Manual segmentation (gold-standard reference). BHs were manually segmented within FLAIR lesions on 2D T1 images from the training set, following the original radiological definition1, and served as the gold-standard reference. 

Optimization of the validated semi-automated 2D T1 method (silver-standard reference). Manual BH segmentation is time-consuming and was therefore limited to the training set. A validated semi-automated 2D T1 BH segmentation method3 was used instead as silver-standard reference in the final validation step. This method classifies individual lesional voxels as BH voxels if their T1 intensity falls below a cGM-derived threshold. To accommodate the use of pre- rather than post-contrast 2D T1 images, this upper BH threshold was re-optimized. For each subject, the mean/median cGM intensity was scaled by factor α (0.9-1.1; step size 0.05). The α yielding BH segmentations with the highest volumetric and spatial agreement with the gold-standard reference in the training set was then used to generate silver-standard reference segmentations for the final validation step. 

Development and optimization of the semi-automated 3D T1 method. The semi-automated 3D T1 BH segmentation method was developed and optimized on the training set against the gold-standard reference. The 2D T1 manual segmentations were transformed to 3D space using the inverse transformation matrix from the 2D-to-3D T1 registration, applying trilinear interpolation (0.5 threshold). A high-specificity cGM mask was derived from the SynthSeg posterior probability map with threshold 1. Two strategies were evaluated to derive the upper BH intensity threshold, the T1-weighted intensity below which lesional voxels are classified as BH. The threshold was computed by scaling the mean/median cGM intensity using a factor α3 or by adding the product of the standard deviation (SD) or median absolute deviation (MAD) of cGM and a factor β4, as shown in the equations below.

BH threshold equations:

    T1 intensity threshold=mean(cGM)*α

    T1 intensity threshold=median(cGM)*α

    T1 intensity threshold=mean(cGM)+β*SD(cGM)

    T1 intensity threshold=median(cGM)+β*MAD(cGM)

Factor ranges, step size

•    α = [0.8, 1.2, 0.05]

•    β = [-0.5, 0.5, 0.1]

The threshold strategy yielding the highest volumetric and spatial agreement with the gold-standard in the training set was selected for the final 3D T1 BH segmentation method. Clusters < 3 voxels were removed.

Validation of the automated 3D T1 BH segmentation method. The final goal was to evaluate how well the optimized 3D T1 method reproduced BH segmentations as compared to the silver-standard reference in the independent test set. BH segmentations from the 2D silver-standard were transformed to 3D space and volumetric and spatial agreement with 3D T1 segmentations were evaluated. 

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed in RStudio (v2024.12.0). Spatial agreement was evaluated using the Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC), 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (HD95), Average Symmetric Surface Distance (ASSD) in Python (v2024.01). Volumetric agreement was evaluated using the two-way random effects intra-class correlation coefficient (ICC[3,1]). The optimal BH T1 intensity strategy was chosen based on the highest total weighted ranking of the metrics (0.4*DSC + 0.3*((HD95+ASSD)/2) + 0.3*ICC). Association of lesion volumes with clinical disability was assessed using (multiple) linear regression, uncorrected and corrected for age and sex. 

Results

Demographics. The final sample included 88 pwMS, 68% were female, the mean (± SD) age was 45.8 (± 8.5) years, the mean disease duration was 12.5 (±2.5) years and median (IQR) EDSS score was 3.0 (2.0 – 3.6). 20 pwMS comprised the training set, and 68 pwMS comprised the test set. 65% of the training set was female, with a mean (± SD) age of 50.5 (± 7.8) years, disease duration of 12.3 (± 3.2) years and median (IQR) EDSS score of 3.0 (2.6 – 3.4). 69% of the test set was female, with a mean (± SD) age of 44.5 (± 8.2) years, disease duration of 12.6 (± 2.3) years and median (IQR) EDSS score of 2.5 (1.7 – 3.3). 

Manual segmentation and fine-tuning of the semi-automated 2D T1 BH segmentation method. On 2D T1 images in the training set, the previously validated semi-automated 2D T1 BH method performed best relative to the gold-standard when using a T1 intensity threshold defined as mean(cGM) × α, with α = 0.95 (mean ICC = 0.97 with 95% CI  [0.93, 0.99]; mean ± SD DSC = 0.74 ± 0.07; HD95 = 2.30mm ± 1.10, ASSD = 0.49mm ± 0.23). The corresponding median (IQR) BH volumes were 3.09mL (1.65 – 7.61) for the manual segmentations and 3.12mL (1.65 – 7.99) for the optimized automated 2D T1 method. 

Development and optimization of the semi-automated 3D T1 BH segmentation method. On the 3D T1 images in the training set, the highest agreement with the gold-standard was achieved using a T1 intensity threshold defined as median(cGM) × α, with α = 0.95 (ICC = 0.91 with 95% CI [0.79 – 0.97]; mean ± SD DSC = 0.57 ± 0.11; HD95 = 4.66mm ± 3.55; ASSD = 1.15mm ± 0.54). The resulting median (IQR) automated 3D T1 BH volume was 2.64mL (1.20 – 6.23). 

Validation of the semi-automated 3D T1 BH segmentation method. In the test set, the optimized 3D T1 method showed good agreement with the silver-standard reference (ICC = 0.90 with 95% CI [0.84, 0.94], mean ± SD DSC = 0.58 ± 0.10, HD95 = 5.77mm ± 7.87, ASSD = 1.30mm ± 1.31). The median (IQR) BH volumes were 1.12mL (0.65 – 2.41) for the 2D T1 method and 1.55mL (0.78 – 2.55) for the 3D T1 method. Examples of manual and automated BH segmentations are provided in Figure 2. The BH-to-FLAIR lesion volume ratio in the test set was 26% ± 9. 

Association between BH volumes and clinical disability. In the test set, automated 3D T1 BH volume was positively associated with EDSS score (β = 0.34, p < 0.005), whereas FLAIR lesion volume showed a weaker association (β = 0.25, p = 0.04). Including both lesion types in the same model showed that BH volume was independently associated with EDSS score (β = 0.99, p = 0.006), whereas FLAIR volume only showed a trend (β = -0.69, p = 0.06). Results remained consistent when accounting for sex and age. 

Conclusion

This study developed and validated a semi-automated approach for segmenting black holes on 3D T1-weighted images, initially trained on manual 2D T1 segmentations. Following optimization against the manual ground truth in the training set (N = 20), the final 3D T1 method showed good volumetric (ICC = 0.90) and moderate spatial (DSC = 0.58) agreement with the validated semi-automated 2D T1 reference method in the independent test set (N = 68). Black hole (T1) lesion volumes were more strongly related to clinical disability than total (T2-FLAIR) lesion volumes. 

The observed moderate DSC values (0.40 – 0.59) are consistent with those reported in other single-centre lesion segmentation studies (~0.50-0.70)5. Despite modality differences, the optimal 3D T1 intensity threshold for classifying BH voxels (median(cGM) × α = 0.95) closely matched the fine-tuned 2D T1 intensity threshold (mean(cGM) × α = 0.95). Although this suggests some continuity in the relative T1 intensity of BHs to cGM across sequences, differences in segmentation tools between the 2D and 3D pipelines likely affected the absolute mean/median cGM value used to compute the threshold, and therefore the α value. The resulting BH/FLAIR ratio of  ~26% aligns well with previously published BH/T2 ratios, which typically range between 15-40%. 

Limitations include the single-centre, single-scanner design and the use of EDSS as the sole clinical score. An additional limitation is the use of semi-automated 2D T1 BH segmentations as the silver-standard in the final validation step. However, the 3D T1 method was initially developed and optimized against gold-standard manual segmentations in the training set.

An important next step will be translating the voxel-wise BH segmentations into T2 lesion-level classifications consistent with radiological convention (currently in progress). In addition, future work should validate the method across centres and scanners, confirm its histopathological correlate, and integrate BH metrics with other markers of tissue damage (e.g., diffusion weighted imaging) and chronic inflammation (e.g., slowly expanding lesions/paramagnetic rim lesions). 

By enabling segmentation of BHs on modern 3D T1-weighted images, this work provides a bridge between the historical definition of 2D T1-defined BHs and current MRI practice. This approach supports the preservation of the radiological and histopathological interpretability of BHs within modern protocols, and facilitates their use in large-scale studies of MS-related tissue damage.
