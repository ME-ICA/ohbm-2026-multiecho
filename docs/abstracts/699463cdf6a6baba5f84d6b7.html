Introduction

Physical exercise (PE) improves cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) by increasing peak oxygen uptake (VO2peak)1. Growing evidence suggests that PE is also a potent lifestyle behavior for enhancing brain health2. Imaging studies in elderly have shown that regular PE influences intrinsic resting-state networks (RSNs)3.  So far, there is no evidence from randomized controlled trials that PE reshapes intrinsic RSNs in sedentary young adults. This study examines whether and when a structured PE intervention program over 6 months alters resting-state functional connectivity (rs-FC) in RSN and, in addition, whether changes in rs-FC are associated with changes in CRF.

Methods

Young sedentary adults were randomly assigned to either an intervention group (IG: aerobic exercise training, 3x/week; N = 25, age = 24.8 ± 4.5, and sex (M:F) = 7:18) or an active control group (CG: stretching and toning, 3x/week; N = 25, age = 25.2 ± 4.5, and sex (M:F) = 7:18) over a period of six months, with VO2peak and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) measured at three time points (baseline (T0), after 3 months (T3), and after 6 months (T6): Figure 1 for study design). 

The rs-fMRI data were acquired using a Philips 3T Elition scanner employing a multiband EPI sequence (585 volumes, TR=1020 ms, TE=30 ms, Flip angle=52°, SENSE=2, MB=3, voxel size 2.17*2.17*2.5 mm, 51 slices). The rs-fMRI data were preprocessed to remove motion and susceptibility artifacts using fmriprep (https://fmriprep.org/en/23.2.3/). Next, acompcor methods were used to extract the 5 main noise components from each the cerebrospinal fluid and white matter, as well as 12 motion parameters and 8 cosine function regressors using XCP-D (https://xcp-d.readthedocs.io/en/0.7.1/) for subsequent denoising. Data were smoothed (6 mm) and bandpass filtered (0.01-0.08Hz). A group-level independent component analysis (ICA) was performed on the denoised rs-fMRI data to obtain data-driven RSNs. We selected 6 RSNs (default mode network (DMN), somatomotor network (SMN), dorsal attention network (DAN), cingulate-opercular network (CON), right frontoparietal network (RFPN), and left FPN (LFPN)) that reliably matched the atlas-based RSNs using the Network Correspondence Toolbox 4. Finally, dual regression was performed to obtain spatial maps for these 6 RSN’s in each individual. These were entered into a 2 (group) x 3 (time points) repeated measures ANOVA. A complementary 2x3 repeated measures ANOVA was conducted to analyze changes in VO2peak. Finally, we performed Spearman correlation analyses to examine the relationship between rs-FC changes in RSNs clusters showing significant changes and VO2peak changes. 

Results

A significant group-time interaction effect was observed for VO2peak (p < 0.001), with post-hoc comparisons indicating significant increases VO2peak in the IG only, at both T3 and T6 relative to T0 (Figure 2a). 

No significant group-time interaction was found for the RSN analysis, however, 5 RSNs showed significant changes over time (p < 0.005 (voxel-level), cluster-level p<.05 family-wise error corrected (FWEc). Exploratory post hoc comparisons suggest that these time effects were mainly driven by a significant increase (p < 0.001 (voxel-level), p<.05 FWEc) of rs-FC within SMN, DAN, CON, RFPN, and LFPN at T3 and T6 compared to T0 in the IG only (see Figures 2b, c, d, e, and f) while no complementary changes were observed in the CG. Moreover, VO2peak changes showed significant positive associations with increased FC within the RFPN at T6.

Conclusion

This is the first randomized controlled trial in young sedentary adults finding preliminary evidence for a significant increase in rsFC within cognitive and motor networks after regular physical exercise, pointing towards a role of PE in improving functional brain health5. Furthermore, direct relationships between increases in rs-FC in the cognitive and motor networks and VO2peak suggest that cardiac fitness improvements may contribute to brain health by improving connectivity within attention, executive control, and motor networks.
