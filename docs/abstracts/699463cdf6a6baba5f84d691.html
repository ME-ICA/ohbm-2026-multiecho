Introduction

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is a critical non-invasive diagnostic tool for evaluating complex fetal brain and other physiological developments, offering superior soft tissue detail compared to ultrasound. To enhance the uniformity of imaging language and maximize the value of relevant imaging reports for scientific data analyses, radiology societies increasingly advocate for standardized and structured reporting1. Recently, large language models (LLMs) have shown great potential in extracting structured information from free-text reports2-4. However, these methods still rely on static, pre-defined templates based on expert consensus. Creating and maintaining such templates is not only laborious but also often too rigid to capture the fine-grained linguistic diversity and evolving clinical descriptions inherent in massive historical free-text report5. To address the lack of automated template induction tools, this study proposes the Fetal brain MRI Automated Induction of Standardized reporting Templates (FAIST) framework, which utilizes LLMs to automatically mine unified reporting structures from large-scale fetal MRI reports, offering a novel solution to reconcile the conflict between diverse clinical descriptions and the rigidity of standardization.

Methods

Data Collection: Between October 2015 and December 2024, a women’s and children’s hospital generated 2,248 fetal brain MRI reports for pregnant women scanned on one 3.0 T system (Siemens MAGNETOM Skyra) and two 1.5 T systems (Philips Achieva and United Imaging uMR 570). All reports were fully de-identified to protect patient privacy. For FAIST development, we randomly sampled subsets of varying sizes from the corpus: subsets ranging from 32, 64, 128, 512, 1024, to 2048 cases were selected for template construction. For the test set used to evaluate template results, we selected a subset of 200 cases that was disjoint from the template construction subsets.

FAIST Framework (Figure 1): FAIST is a three-stage LLM-based framework. (1) Stage I: Extraction and Refine Slots Library. Leveraging the semantic extraction capabilities of LLMs, spans for all medical concepts and attributes are extracted from selected reports. These spans are then subjected to semantic field clustering and normalization to generate a unique medical concept-attribute slots library for subsequent use. (2) Stage II: Construct and Evolve Template Tree. An initial template tree is constructed using sampled reports; this structure is then evolved using the slots library, a process which simultaneously updates the mounting paths of slots within the template tree. (3) Stage III: Refine Schema Tree. In the final stage, FAIST performs semantic reorganization and normalization of enumerated values between and within nodes to yield a consistent and concise final template tree.

Template Quality Assessment: The evaluation phase employs two distinct assessment methods. First, the ROUGE-L score is utilized to measure information retention by comparing original and restored reports. Specifically, original reports from the test set are processed by an advanced LLM (Qwen3-Max) to populate the generated template. To minimize hallucinations, value selection for each attribute is strictly constrained during this process. The same model then attempts to reconstruct the original text relying solely on the structured output. ROUGE scores calculated between the source and restored texts serve as a comprehensive metric of information completeness. Second, the fill rate for each template field is calculated across structured reports; high fill rates distinguish core fields, while low rates characterize long-tail or infrequently used attributes.

Results

Figure 2a quantitatively demonstrates the stabilization behavior of FAIST-induced templates under increasing corpus sizes. While the median ROUGE-L score remains relatively stable across all settings (approximately 0.65–0.70), a pronounced reduction is observed in the low-score tail of the distribution. Specifically, the proportion of test reports with ROUGE-L < 0.5 decreases from 9.8–10.5% when using 32–64 reports for templating to 3.6% when using 1024 or more reports, after which the tail ratio converges. This indicates that additional reports primarily improve coverage of structurally atypical or linguistically rare cases rather than boosting average reconstruction quality, highlighting a reduction in long-tail failure modes during template induction.

Figure 2b further explains this phenomenon from a structural perspective. With limited data, a large fraction of template fields fall into the long-tail category (fill rate < 0.01), reaching 39.7% at 32 reports, while core fields (fill rate ≥ 0.3) account for only 22.3%. As the corpus size increases, the proportion of core fields rises steadily to 48.2%, whereas long-tail fields decrease to 13.7% at 2048 reports. This indicates that FAIST not only captures dominant reporting concepts but also progressively reorganizes rare yet valuable concepts into coherent mounting paths, reducing redundancy and improving practical usability of the template.

Figure 2c illustrates a representative segment of the induced template in a clinically intuitive tree-to-form layout. The schema is organized into major anatomical sections (e.g., Fetal Overview, Head, Heart & Thorax), and further decomposes the head module into substructures such as the ventricular system, midline/posterior fossa, and parenchyma/cortex. Each leaf slot is mapped to a concise field with constrained value types, covering both categorical descriptors (e.g., normal/abnormal, visible/not visible) and quantitative measurements (e.g., ventricular width (mm)).

Conclusion

In conclusion, the FAIST framework automates the induction of high-quality reporting templates, effectively resolving the conflict between clinical diversity and standardization. By eliminating the need for laborious expert consensus, our approach promotes the popularization of structured reporting and significantly enhances the utility of imaging data for scientific research.
