Introduction

Precise neuromodulation requires accurate identification of the specific functional circuits underlying brain disorders. However, these functional circuits exhibit substantial individual variability and cannot be reliably localized based on anatomical features alone. Resting-state functional MRI (rsfMRI)-based personalized functional circuit mapping has emerged as a promising solution, enabling individualized target localization for techniques such as transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), focused ultrasound stimulation (FUS), deep brain stimulation (DBS), and intracranial electrical stimulation (iES). Despite its promise, the broader clinical translation of this approach is hindered by slow and failure-prone preprocessing pipelines, especially in patients with large lesions, and the absence of a unified, automated, and neuromodulation modality-general framework for target localization.

Methods

We present UNITE (Unified platform for Neuromodulation with Individualized Target Estimation),1 a generic, rapid, and robust platform for personalized functional circuit–guided neuromodulation targeting (Fig. 1). UNITE incorporates DeepPrep,2 our recently developed end-to-end fMRI preprocessing pipeline that achieves a over 10-fold acceleration compared with conventional pipelines while substantially improving robustness in clinical populations with severe anatomical distortions. Using DeepPrep outputs, UNITE performs personalized functional circuit mapping through iterative precision functional mapping, with three default cortical atlas options: Yeo’s 18-network atlas, Gordon’s 18-network atlas, and a fine-grained 213-region atlas derived through a coarse-to-fine approach. These individualized parcellations define subject-specific search spaces for neuromodulation targeting. UNITE then executes fully automated, circuit-guided target planning for TMS, FUS, and DBS. The platform includes three TMS targeting algorithms for post-stroke functional rehabilitation3,4 and one FUS targeting algorithm for Parkinson’s disease (PD),5 combining personalized parcellation, resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC), and anatomical constraints (e.g., gyral crowns for TMS, thalamic boundaries for FUS). Finally, the platform outputs target coordinates in native/MNI spaces, neuronavigation-ready ROIs, comprehensive quality control, and visual verification reports. A graphical interface enables one-click execution and supports user-defined targeting algorithms.

Results

This protocol has been applied to more than 1,500 patients, including over 150 stroke patients and 48 individuals with PD in published TMS and FUS studies, demonstrating robust clinical effectiveness. UNITE maintains high processing speed and stability, completing the full individualized workflow—including preprocessing, functional parcellation, and target localization—within approximately 30 minutes per patient on a GPU-equipped workstation. Over 30% of stroke patients that could not be processed using fMRIPrep due to their extensive brain lesions were successfully processed by DeepPrep, enabling them to receive interventions. 

Representative applications demonstrate personalized targeting for post-stroke cognitive impairment, aphasia, and upper-limb motor deficits, each showing substantial clinical improvements following TMS therapy (Fig. 2 a-d). 

In a retrospective PD cohort, UNITE identified individualized thalamic FUS targets by locating the dominant functional cluster maximally connected to each patient's symptom-related cortical region. The Euclidean distance between UNITE-derived and clinician-selected targets significantly predicted motor improvement (Spearman’s ρ = –0.68, Fig. 2 e-f), indicating strong potential for personalized FUS planning in PD.

Conclusion

UNITE provides a fast, automated, and scalable solution for individualized neuromodulation targeting across diverse disorders and modalities, including TMS, FUS, DBS, and iES. By combining deep-learning–accelerated preprocessing with personalized functional parcellation and RSFC-based circuit mapping, UNITE delivers precise cortical and subcortical targets tailored to each patient’s pathological network architecture. Its standardized inputs, reproducible computational environment, and comprehensive quality-control procedures make it well suited for multi-center clinical trials, large-scale research studies, and mechanistic investigations of neuromodulation-induced network reorganization.
