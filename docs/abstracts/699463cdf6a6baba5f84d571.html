Introduction

The Papez circuit is neural loop in the limbic brain connecting different structures in the brain including anteroventral (AV) thalamic nuclei, mammillary bodies (MB), anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), parahippocampal gyrus (PHG), and the hippocampus (Hc) and is critical for consolidation of episodic memory. It is made up of four major white matter tracts- fornix (connecting Hc to MB), mammillothalamic tract (MTT, connecting MB to AV), cingulum (connecting the cingulate cortex to PHG), anterior thalamic radiation (ATR, connecting AV to the cingulate cortex). Malfunction of this circuit has been associated with episodic and other memory impairments. Structural integrity of the Papez circuit may serve as a biomarker for several pathologies including Alzheimer’s disease1. Effective reconstruction of all four white-matter tracts that comprise the Papez circuit is highly dependent on the appropriate selection of seed regions and tractography parameters.  Very few studies have been reported including one study using 7T MRI1 and one using 3T MRI2 and they involve a lot of manual tweaking. TractSeg is a deep learning-based tool that segments white matter fiber tracts with the aid of fiber orientation distribution function peaks3. We investigated TractSeg for automatic reconstruction of the full Papez circuit. TractSeg generates masks for ATR, cingulum and the fornix while omitting MTT. So, we investigated using ROIs generated from popular subcortical and cortical segmentation tools in conjunction with TractSeg ROIs to generate all 4 segments of the Papez circuit at 3T.

Methods

Data: 

Diffusion weighted imaging (DWI) data from 12 normal subjects were considered in this study to generate Papez circuit in the left hemisphere. Multi-shell DWI data were acquired using Philips 3T MR scanner with a spin-echo EPI sequence (voxel size: 1.7 mm isotropic; b-values: 500,1K, 2K, 3K s/mm²; 45 directions per shell).  

Data Analysis: 

DWI data was first upsampled to 1.25mm x 1.25mm x 1.25mm and next preprocessed using QSIPrep4. Then white matter fiber orientation distribution functions were generated using QSIRecon. Different sets of ROIs were extracted using Freesurfer, Sclimbic and THOMAS segmentation techniques as well as using fiber bundles masks obtained using TractSeg. Papez circuit reconstruction was finally attempted using the above extracted ROIs and with the aid of MRtrix. 

Tractogram analysis:

The tractograms fornix, mammillothalamic tract (MTT), cingulum and anterior thalamic radiation (ATR) were generated using 4 sets of ROIs namely, TractSeg based bundle segmentation ROIs, TractSeg based endings ROIs, ROIs from Freesurfer (FS) and THOMAS alone and a hybrid mechanism which uses ROIs from FS, THOMAS5 and TractSeg3. Further the tractograms were compared qualitatively and quantitatively. For quantitative evaluation fractional anisotropy (FA) values were extracted within the respective tracts using FA volume. 

Results

All 4 parts of the Papez circuit were generated successfully for all 12 subjects using the hybrid approach. Figure 1 represents the Papez circuit generated using the four different approaches for four different control subjects. Panel A shows the Papez circuit generated using the TractSeg based bundle segmentations, it could be noticed that the white matter streamlines generated for the cingulum and ATR regions are over exaggerated. Panel B depicts the Papez circuit generated using the beginning and endings of the respective ROIs. In this case ATR and cingulum seem to be slightly better than panel A.  However, spurious streamlines exist and fornix is observed to be underrepresented across all four subjects. Panel C represents the Papez circuit generated using the Freesurfer and THOMAS ROIs, and it could be noticed that the ATR and cingulum tracts are better than the previous two approaches, however fornix tract is severely affected in this approach. The Papez circuit represented in panel D generated using the hybrid approach of ROIs. An improved fornix tract is generated in this approach. The quantitative FA (mean ± SD) values derived using the respective tracts found to be higher in cingulum (0.48 ± 0.03) and lower in MTT (0.31 ± 0.02) with hybrid ROIs. While fornix and ATR tracts shared similar range of FA values (0.41 ± 0.03).

Conclusion

Papez circuit generated using the two variants of TractSeg ROIs appears to be suffering with exaggerated generation of streamlines which might be due to slightly over segmented ROIs from TractSeg especially ATR and Cingulum, with endings variant faring moderately better. The explicit utilization of the beginning ROI as a seed and ending ROI as waypoint regions in this approach might have influenced the slightly better streamline generation. However, fornix tract is clearly underrepresented, and the tract generation has failed to generate formidable streamlines consistently across the four subjects. This might be because the fornix ROIs from the TractSeg based beginning, and endings are insufficient for this tract. Papez circuit generated using the third approach shown appears to be better except for the Fornix tract. This might be due to the proximity of the hippocampus and mammillary bodies regions leading to ineffective determination of the white matter tract belonging to fornix region. A robust Papez circuit is generated using the hybrid approach which overcomes the third approach by utilizing the Fornix region from TractSeg as a waypoint. Higher FA values in the cingulum in the hybrid approach might be suggesting large presence of myelinated white matter tracts. Reduced FA values in the case of MTT might be attributed to slightly lower presence of the intact and oriented white matter tracts.  Hence, FA values derived using the respective tracts provides a quantitative means to assess the integrity of white matter tracts. Hence, this study demonstrates the need for a hybrid approach of seed regions selection and tract specific generation parameters for robust and consistent Papez circuit generation which might be aiding in the early diagnosis of the neurological pathologies.
