Introduction

Introduction:

The pulvinar nucleus constitutes approximately 30% of the total thalamic volume and is the largest thalamic nucleus. Cytoarchitectonically, it is divided into anterior (PuA), inferior (PuI), medial (PuM), and lateral (PuL) subregions¹. As a higher-order thalamic nucleus, the pulvinar receives extensive cortical inputs and sends reciprocal projections back to the cortex. Its widespread connectivity with frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, and cingulate cortices suggests a critical role in higher cognitive, affective, and somatosensory functions2. Accordingly, pulvinar pathology has been implicated in various neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disorders3. Previous studies have reported pulvinar grey matter volume reductions in patients with Parkinson’s disease (PD)4, which have also been linked to cognitive impairment. However, to date, no study has systematically investigated both the resting-state functional connectivity and grey matter volume of the pulvinar subnuclei.

The present study aimed to investigate resting-state functional connectivity and grey matter volume alterations in the PuA, PuI, PuM, and PuL subregions in elderly individuals and patients with Parkinson’s disease dementia, using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) as a clinical cognitive measure. Given the lack of established clinical markers linking pulvinar subregions to neurodegenerative disorders, this study sought to identify potential functional and structural alterations in these pulvinar subregions in PD.

Methods

Methods: Forty participants (20 healthy controls and 20 Parkinson’s disease patients with dementia; aged 50–80 years) were selected from the Istanbul Medipol University Functional and Neuroimaging Lab. Groups were age-matched, and all participants had high-quality fMRI and T1 images with no history of neurological or psychiatric disorders, head trauma, or stroke. ROIs were generated using FreeSurfer v7.1.1 with the recon-all pipeline in MNI 2-mm space, followed by thalamic parcellation to extract bilateral PuA, PuI, PuM, and PuL regions5.

Image preprocessing and functional connectivity analyses were conducted using the CONN toolbox v21a in MATLAB R2020b. Motion-related outliers were identified using ART-based scrubbing (97th percentile; motion > 0.9 mm, global signal z > 5) and included as covariates during denoising. Images were normalized to MNI space, resampled to 2-mm isotropic resolution, and smoothed with a 4-mm FWHM Gaussian kernel. Data were band-pass filtered (0.008–0.09 Hz), and noise was reduced by regressing motion parameters, their derivatives, white matter, and CSF signals (aCompCor), and resting-state confounds. ROI-to-voxel correlation coefficients were Fisher z-transformed and compared between groups using second-level ANOVA, with thresholds of p-unc < 0.001 (voxel level) and FDR-corrected p-FDR < 0.05 (cluster level).

ROI-based grey matter volume analysis was performed using FreeSurfer recon-all pipeline for T1-weighted anatomical images. We obtained the absolute volumes of each thalamic nuclei from these automated methods, based on Bayesian interference probabilistic atlases derived from ex vivo MRI and histological data. In addition, gender, age, education years, and total intracranial volume (TIV) were added into the analysis as covariates.

Demographic and clinical variables were analyzed using Jamovi v2.3.19.0, with normality assessed by the Shapiro–Wilk test. Group differences were examined using independent t-tests or Mann–Whitney U tests, and chi-square tests for gender (p < 0.05). Group comparisons of pulvinar subregional grey matter volumes and functional connectivity beta values were performed using ANCOVA, controlling for age, education, gender, and total intracranial volume. Partial correlations and binomial logistic regression were used to assess relationships between clinical scores, imaging measures, and diagnosis, with significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Results: It was observed that the right PuA showed decreased connectivity with the left middle temporal gyrus and left temporal pole (Cluster 1: p=0.000919, FDR-corrected), as well as the right temporal pole, right inferior temporal gyrus, and right middle temporal gyrus (Cluster 2: p=0.038627, FDR-corrected) in the PD group compared to HCs (Figure 2-A1, A3).

The ANCOVA analysis revealed a significant difference in the volume of the right PuA between HCs and patients with PD. The PD group had a significantly lesser volume in the right PuA relative to the controls (p=0.044). However, there were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of other subnuclei (the left PuA, the left PuI, the left PuL, the left PuM, the right PuI, the right PuL, and the right PuM; p=0.769, p=0.715, p=0.506, p=0.520, p=0.892, p=0.872, p=0.585, respectively). (Figure 2B).

Our correlation analysis revealed that the right PuA clusters showing significant functional connectivity differences between the two groups showed significant positive correlation with grey matter volume of the right PuA in both group (cluster 1 and cluster 2, r=0.387 p=0.016, and r=0.350 p=0.031, respectively). We found a negative correlation between UPDRS scores and functional connectivity in the PD group (cluster 1 r=-0.641 p=0.006). All correlations were controlled by age and education years; moreover, the volume parameter was also controlled by TIV, besides age and education years (Figure 2C). 

Conclusion

Conclusions: The findings of this study showed that the right PuA sub-region is functionally and structurally affected by PD. More specifically, the right PuA region exhibited less functional connectivity in bilateral temporal regions of the brain, suggesting that decreased connectivity between those regions and the right PuA may result in cognitive and motor symptoms in PD, which was confirmed by our correlation analysis between UPDRS and the right-PuA connectivity. To conclude, our findings show that altered PuA connectivity with temporal cortices plays a critical role in cognitive impairment and provides a unique and particular network pathology in PD manifested with cognitive and motor impairment.
