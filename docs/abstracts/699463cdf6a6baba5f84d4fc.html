Introduction

I'm the second year PhD student in neurosciences in the University of Geneva. My PhD project mainly focus on the temporal dynamics and brain networks of reappraisal. 

Methods

Fifty-five university students with healthy mental states were recruited to perform an emotion regulation task, in which they need to watch or reappraise the emotion that are triggered by the pictures (40 neutral and 120 negative, from the Nencki Affective Picture System) according to the instructions. There are three types of instructions: watch naturally, more positive reappraisal (MPR), and less negative reappraisal (LNR). Different regulation strategies were explained to the participants before the experiment and a training session was conducted to make sure theymastered the reappraisal strategies.  A total of 160 trials were equally divided into 8 blocks (Watch-Neutral, Watch-Negative, MPR-Negative, and LNR-Negative). At the beginning of each block, the full instruction was presented to the participants. When the participants finished reading the instruction and remembered what they have to do, they pressed any key to continue. First, a white cross appeared in the center for 3 seconds. Then, a picture was presented for 6 s, during which they need to react exactly as the instruction indicated. Finally, they rated the arousal, positive emotion and negative emotion scales by moving the arrows to left or right using left and right arrows, and using down arrow to register the decision. For the positive (or negative) emotion scale, left represents not at all emotional and right very much emotional (positive or negative); Likewise, for the arousal scale, left represents very relaxed and right represents highly aroused. After each block, there was a 1 min break. The ratings were transformed into a number from 1 to 9. At the same time, brain oscillations were measured using a 64-channel electroencephalography. To balance the strategy order effect, 33 participants performed the LNR blocks after MPR blocks, and the remaining 22 participants in reverse. 

Results

Behavioral results indicated that both MPR and LNR successfully reduced arousal, negative emotion and increased positive emotion. In both order groups, the strategy which was implemented in the second order induced more decrease in arousal and negative emotion. Besides, the heart rate variability, which is measured by root mean square of successive differences (RMSSD), was always significantly higher in the second reappraisal strategy condition than the watch neutral condition, indicating more stronger emotion regulation ability. Event-related potential anaylses indicated that the second reappraisal strategy in each group induced a larger late positive potential in frontal areas, which may suggest higher cognitive effort.

Conclusion

Our study indicated that the both reappraisal strategies, which are targeted at regulation processes themselves rather than classcial way of indicating regulation goal, can successfully reduce the negative experience and improve the positive emotion. More importantly, it's always the regulation strategy which was implemented latter mainfested a stronger emotion regualtion ability and cognitive effort, suggesting an accumulated regulation effect and the cognitive effort is coupled with the regulation ability. This study firstly provides the insight into the coupling between cognitive effort and regulation ability by combing the neural and vegal responses. To put it in a nutshell: no pain, no gain in reappraisal.
