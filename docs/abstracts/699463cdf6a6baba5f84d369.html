Introduction

Echo-planar imaging (EPI) is a common magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) technique used to acquire functional and structural measures such as blood oxygen level dependent (BOLD) and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) scans. It is well known that EPI scans are susceptible to spatial distortions due to inhomogeneities in the static magnetic field along the phase encoding direction. These distortions can be corrected by acquiring additional sequences, e.g., a pair of gradient echo images or coupling an EPI acquisition with a second acquisition where the phase-encoding direction is reversed. However, sometimes such data are unavailable for individual participants within a dataset or even entire datasets. A recent 3D U-Net model, SynBOLD-DisCo (Yu et al., 2023), has been developed to generate synthetic undistorted BOLD EPI. Here we present two novel strategies: 1) FmapSynth, an alternative 3D U-Net model architecture and 2) FmapSynth-Pop, a strategy that draws on fieldmap data available from other participants in the same dataset, for estimating a synthetic fieldmap that can be applied to generate distortion-corrected BOLD EPI acquisitions. We evaluate their performance on spatial and temporal metrics against reference distortion-corrected BOLD data derived from acquired fieldmap scans.

Methods

Model training was performed on data from 190 participants across four OpenNeuro datasets (ds005038, ds002422, ds003745, ds004044). All training data were acquired on Siemens 3T scanners except ds002422, which was acquired on a Siemens 1.5T.

Testing of estimated models was evaluated on data from 90 participants across eight independent OpenNeuro datasets (ds003835, ds005165, ds000224, ds001454, ds004182, ds005263 ds002898, ds004073). All testing data were acquired on a Siemens 3T scanner except of ds004073, which was acquired on a 3T Philips scanner.

All datasets included: 1) a high-resolution T1-weighted structural scan, 2) a BOLD EPI time series, and 3) a gradient-echo fieldmap acquisition including a phase-difference image and two magnitude images. Prior to model training, motion correction was applied to BOLD EPI data with FSL MCFLIRT and skull-stripping with BET in FSL (Jenkinson et al., 2012). Reference (“gold-standard”) distortion-corrected BOLD EPI data were generated by 1) estimating a fieldmap based on the acquired magnitude/phase-difference scans using fsl_prepare_fieldmap, then 2) unwarping the BOLD EPI data with the estimated fieldmap using FUGUE.

Our FmapSynth model implements a 3D U-Net architecture with input and output tensors structured as [n,c,x,y,z] where n is training batch size (n = 8), c is number of channels; x, y, and z are the dimensions of the brain imaging data. The input consisted of c = 2 channels, i.e., the T1-weighted structural scan and a single BOLD EPI volume, whereas the output consisted of c = 1 channel, i.e., the model-estimated fieldmap. The BOLD EPI data were then unwarped using FUGUE based on the model-estimated fieldmap. Model encoding (downsampling) consisted of two 3D convolution operations: instance normalization, and a rectified linear unit activation function per block; 3D max-pooling was applied after each of these blocks. Decoding (upsampling) applied trilinear interpolation to match encoding dimensions until producing a fieldmap image with x,y,z dimensions matching the input. Ten time points from each of the 190 training datasets were used for training, i.e., 1900 BOLD EPI volumes. Samples were batched to ensure the same dimensionality for all images within a batch. Model training was performed on an NVIDIA Quadro P6000 GPU. Training used a learning rate of 10-4, adam optimizer betas of B = {0.9, 0.99} and a weight decay l2 = 10-5. Early stopping was applied with a Δmin = 10 and patience = 5.

Our FmapSynth-Pop approach simply warped estimated fieldmaps from other participants in a dataset to a target participant. That is, for each of the 90 test participants, the reference fieldmap was generated with fsl_prepare_fieldmap for each of the n-1 participants from the respective dataset. These n-1 fieldmaps were then spatially warped to the test dataset based on the high-resolution T1-weighted scans. The mean of these n-1 warped fieldmaps was calculated and used as a surrogate fieldmap for target participant using FUGUE.

Model performance for FmapSynth and FmapSynth-Pop on the 90 test participants was assessed via structural and temporal fidelity. Temporal fidelity was measured by computing the voxelwise Pearson correlation coefficient (ρ) between the time series of BOLD EPI signal intensity values from the model-generated and reference distortion-corrected BOLD EPI data. Structural fidelity was measured as the mean-squared error (MSE) of BOLD signal intensity values between the model-generated BOLD EPI and the reference distortion-corrected BOLD EPI.

Results

As a benchmark for model performance, we observed that across all 90 participants in the test datasets, the temporal and structural fidelity of the distorted BOLD EPI data vis-à-vis the reference distortion-corrected BOLD EPI data was ρ = 0.862 and MSE = 0.00431. FmapSynth improved both the temporal (ρ = 0.925) and structural (MSE = 0.00279) fidelity. FmapSynth-Pop also improved both temporal (ρ = 0.939) and structural (MSE = 0.00223) fidelity, out-performing FmapSynth. Both strategies outperformed SynBOLD-DisCo (ρ = 0.497, MSE = 0.020). Visual inspection of voxelwise temporal correlations showed that temporal fidelity was increased by FmapSynth and FmapSynth-Pop within areas mostly strongly affected by associated distortions, i.e., the ventromedial prefrontal cortex, the temporal poles, and anterior portions of the cerebellum. Code is available on https://github.com/Cyrkryta/fmdc-ai-thesis including a Docker image https://hub.docker.com/r/maglindchr/fmapsynth.

Conclusion

Overall, we observed that our FmapSynth 3D U-net model and FmapSynth-Pop strategy effectively estimated fieldmaps improved both the temporal and structural fidelity of BOLD EPI data vis-à-vis reference distortion-correction BOLD EPI data derived with a fieldmap sequence. Notably, FmapSynth and FmapSynth-Pop demonstrated superior performance in both temporal and structural measures compared to a recently described tool (SynBOLD-DisCo), indicating FmapSynth and FmapSynth-Pop may be beneficial strategies for deriving distortion-corrected BOLD EPI data in the absence of an available fieldmap acquisition. Notably, although the FmapSynth model was trained exclusively on Siemens acquired data, it improved temporal and structural fidelity of Philips test data acquired with an opposite phase encoding direction, indicating FmapSynth may be robust across scanner platforms.

Interestingly, our FmapSynth-Pop strategy, which simply warped available fieldmaps from other participants into the space of a target participant, out-performed our 3D U-net model (FmapSynth). FmapSynth-Pop warped fieldmaps estimated in N = 9 or 19 participants. This suggests that a very good surrogate fieldmap can be estimated for individual participant data if an acquired fieldmap is available in even only a few other participants within the same dataset. Integration of FmapSynth and FmapSynth-Pop for estimating distortion-corrected BOLD EPI data with existing preprocessing pipelines can be an effective strategy for more effectively leveraging individual participant data where associated fieldmap acquisitions are unavailable.
