Introduction

Neuroimaging meta-analyses can reveal convergent brain regions that show abnormal activity and help infer shared neural mechanism across psychiatric disorders. Among clinical features, emotional dysfunction is one of prominent symptoms cross-diagnostically. Investigating emotional processing transdiagnostically may thus provide a comprehensive understanding of how affective symptoms relate to underlying neural alterations. However, recent cross-diagnostic meta-analyses have typically focused on a limited set of psychiatric disorders and/or heterogeneous domains (e.g., social, cognitive and emotional), often restricted to the most frequently studied conditions in the literature [1, 2]. Such domain heterogeneity complicates the results interpretation, while focusing solely on the most prevalent diseases also constraints the results generalization to a broader set of disorders. Additionally, despite the shared nature of emotional impairment at the clinical level, it is still an open question, if aberrant transdiagnostic neural patterns are shared by all disorder categories or specific to certain subgroups. This uncertainty mainly arise from the disorder-specific variations in how emotional dysfunction manifests, both in form and severity. Therefore, using the Activation Likelihood Estimation (ALE) algorithm, the present study aims to investigate the alterations in neural activity during emotional processing across several psychiatric disorders and to further characterize results by disease category.

Methods

To identify eligible fMRI studies a systematic search of publication in recent 5 years was conducted in PubMed, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, with AI-assisted screening using AsReview. We included task-fMRI studies investigating group differences between a patient with diagnoses listed in DSM-5 and healthy controls in brain activity across the whole brain during emotional processing (i.e., emotional perception, recognition, experiencing, regulation and anticipation) were considered. The flowchart presents the screening steps according to guidance [3], along with the exclusion criteria. An overall ALE meta-analysis across all psychiatric disorders was performed. For each resulting cluster derived from the cross-diagnostic analysis, we then extracted the experiments contributing to the respective cluster and calculated for each DSM-5 disease category: i) the contribution proportion (value from 0-100%) of the respective category relative to the overall number of contributions for that cluster and ii) the representation ratio for each category indicating over- or underrepresentation of the respective category (value > 1 or value < 1) among the contributing experiments compared to the baseline rate (value = 1). Robustness was further tested using leave-one-(diagnosis)-out (LOO) analyses (i.e., Coordinates of one diagnosis were left out at a time, and another meta-analysis were run on the remaining data for each cross-diagnostic region). All meta-analyses were cluster-level Family-Wise Error corrected (p < 0.05), with a cluster-forming threshold of p < 0.001 [4].

Results

In total 305 studies that reported in 313 experiments were included [Anxiety-related(42 experiment were included, ‘exp’ as abbreviation), Bipolar(30 exp), Eating(24 exp), Depressive(65 exp), Neurocognitive(3 exp), Neurodevelopmental(21 exp), Obsessive compulsive (OCD) (21 exp), Personality(19 exp), Post-traumatic stress (PTSD)(16 exp), Schizophrenia(37 exp), Substance Use and Addiction(25 exp), Sleep related(4 exp), Somatic related(2 exp), Mix of different disorders(4 exp)]. Across all diagnostic groups consistent alteration in activation in emotional processing were found in left amygdala (50 exp contributed) and right amygdala (71 exp contributed), left anterior cingulate cortex (ACC, 60 exp contributed), left posterior cingulate (PCC, 29 exp contributed) and left precentral gyrus (PreCG, 38 exp contributed) (Figure.2.A).  Contributions revealed that multiple disease categories contributed to the convergence of the five regions (B). LOO results showed that bilateral amygdala and ACC were robust across all diseases, while PreCG was driven by the depression and PCC by multiple psychiatric diseases (C). However, while some disorders strongly contributed to all regions, other categories provided no or only few experiments that reported aberrant activation or only contributed only to specific clusters. More specifically, anxiety-related, OCD, PTSD and schizophrenia contributed well to almost all clusters, while eating and bipolar disorders as well as categories with only a few experiments in our dataset, contributed less. Interestingly, for depression, quite some experiments report aberrant activation in bilateral amygdala, ACC, and PreCG. But only in the PreCG, its contribution was higher than what would be expected given their prevalence in the dataset.

Conclusion

The current results indicate shared aberrant brain activation during emotion processing across several psychiatric disorders in bilateral amygdala, precentral gyrus as well as midline structures (PCC, ACC) partially replicating previous findings of cross-diagnostic meta-analyses [1, 2, 5]. Anxiety-related disorders, PTSD, OCD and schizophrenia emerged as strong drivers of the observed shared neural patterns, whereas eating and bipolar disorders as well as disorders with less prominent emotional symptoms (e.g., neurocognitive disorders) contributed less. This pattern suggests that stress-related symptoms may underlie the shared neural deficits during emotional processing in the amygdala and midline structures across different psychiatric disorders. Changes in brain activation in PreCG, in turn, may be more associated with depression, possibly reflecting the influence of experimental characteristics rather than symptomatology. In summary, our results highlight the need to examine findings from the perspective of sample characteristics, to allow more specific interpretations of cross-diagnostic findings.
