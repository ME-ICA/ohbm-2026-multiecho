Introduction

Multiple neurotransmitter systems are affected by the pathology of Lewy-body disease (LBD). According to the Dual Syndrome Hypothesis¹, deficits in executive function, attention, and working memory arise from fronto-striatal dopaminergic dysfunction. In contrast, under the “body-first” hypothesis of the α-Synuclein Origin and Connectome (SOC) model², the locus coeruleus (LC)–noradrenaline system is thought to be affected earlier than the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc) in prodromal LBD, particularly in patients exhibiting REM sleep behavior disorder, visual hallucinations, or attentional fluctuations. In the present study, we examined how early LC and SNpc pathology influences longitudinal decline in executive functioning — a cognitive domain modulated by both nuclei in healthy individuals and in LBD.

Methods

Our sample consisted of patients with core clinical symptoms of prodromal dementia with Lewy bodies (24 with and 34 without mild cognitive impairment), each with two longitudinal assessments. Linear mixed models were used to predict changes in four executive-function tests — Digit Symbol Substitution, Letter–Number Sequencing, Lexical Fluency, and Picture Arrangement — from baseline integrity of the right caudal LC (operationalized as neuromelanin signal intensity) and the SNpc (free-water imaging). The right caudal LC was selected based on prior evidence demonstrating reduced integrity in prodromal LBD compared with healthy controls³.

Results

Lower baseline integrity of the right caudal LC significantly predicted longitudinal decline in several executive-function measures, including Digit Symbol Substitution (β = 4.05, p = .021) and Lexical Fluency (β = 4.89, p = .055). Both the right caudal LC (β = –6.56, p = .066) and SNpc (β = –6.72, p = .070) integrity showed a trend-level effect on Picture Arrangement. All these significant effects were observed from baseline to the second follow-up (that is, third timepoint) and remained robust after adjusting for age, sex, and varying intervals between baseline and follow-up. These results highlighted the predominant contribution of LC pathology to executive dysfunction in prodromal LBD.

Conclusion

Clinically, these findings suggest that individuals with lower right caudal LC integrity are more vulnerable to cognitive deterioration, underscoring the potential of neuromelanin-sensitive LC imaging as a monitoring biomarker for cognitive decline in prodromal LBD. More broadly, the results provide empirical support for the SOC model.
