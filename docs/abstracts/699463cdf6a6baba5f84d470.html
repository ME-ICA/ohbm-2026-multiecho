Introduction

Glymphatic dysfunction has been implicated in the pathophysiology of schizophrenia, yet its specific role in Treatment-Resistant Schizophrenia (TRS) remains elusive. While enlarged perivascular spaces (PVS) are typically associated with aging and neuroinflammation, it is hypothesized that TRS, characterized by distinct neurodevelopmental and inflammatory profiles, may exhibit a unique perivascular phenotype. This study investigated MRI-visible PVS alterations in TRS compared to First-Episode Psychosis (FEP) and Healthy Controls (HC), utilizing normative modeling to dissect heterogeneity and neurodevelopmental deviations.

Methods

We analyzed T1-weighted MRI data from 127 participants (30 TRS, 50 FEP, 47 HC), balanced for age using Coarsened Exact Matching (CEM). PVS volumes in the basal ganglia (BG) and white matter (WM) were quantified using an automated Frangi-filtering based segmentation with manual refinement. Group differences were analyzed using ANCOVA, adjusting for age, tissue volume, and chlorpromazine-equivalent doses. To disentangle illness chronicity from trait effects, we employed a within–between interaction model. Furthermore, Bayesian ridge regression-based normative modeling was applied to healthy controls to predict age-related PVS trajectories, allowing for the quantification of individual deviations (z-scores) in patients.

Results

Patients with TRS exhibited significantly reduced BG-PVS volume compared to both FEP and HC ($p < 0.015$), with no significant differences observed in WM-PVS. This reduction remained robust after controlling for potential confounders including antipsychotic exposure. The within–between decomposition analysis revealed that the association between smaller PVS and illness variables was driven by group status (TRS vs. FEP) rather than within-group progression, suggesting a trait-like abnormality. Crucially, normative modeling demonstrated that the TRS group followed a downward-shifted trajectory of BG-PVS volume relative to the normative range across the age span. A deviation threshold of $|z| > 2$ distinguished TRS from HCs with a high specificity of 97.8%.

Conclusion

This study is the first to report a region-specific reduction of basal ganglia PVS volume in TRS, contrasting with the PVS enlargement typically seen in neurodegenerative disorders. The normative modeling results suggest that this reduction represents an intrinsic, early-neurodevelopmental perturbation in the formation of the glymphatic scaffold, rather than a secondary consequence of chronic illness or medication. These findings highlight reduced BG-PVS as a potential structural biomarker for identifying the distinct biological biotype of treatment resistance in schizophrenia.
