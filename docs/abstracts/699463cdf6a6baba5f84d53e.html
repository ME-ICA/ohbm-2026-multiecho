Introduction

Epilepsy is one of the most common chronic neurological conditions, affecting approximately 1% of the population worldwide and frequently beginning in childhood. Early-onset focal epilepsies can severely impair cognitive development, behavior, and academic performance. After failure of two adequately chosen antiseizure medications, the probability of achieving seizure freedom with further pharmacological treatment is very low (<5%). In such cases, epilepsy surgery represents the most effective therapeutic option.

However, especially in extratemporal epilepsies — and particularly in pediatric patients—the epileptogenic zone is often subtle, spatially complex, and difficult to delineate using a single diagnostic modality. Multimodal neuroimaging, integrating structural, metabolic, and electrophysiological data, is therefore essential for precise localization and safe surgical planning. Advances in co-registration techniques and patient-specific 3D modeling increasingly allow the synthesis of heterogeneous data into a unified spatial framework, facilitating both presurgical decision-making and intraoperative navigation.

Here, we present a pediatric case of drug-resistant focal epilepsy in which convergent multimodal neuroimaging and invasive electrophysiology were integrated into an individualized 3D model of the epileptogenic zone, enabling successful extratemporal epilepsy surgery with excellent clinical and neurodevelopmental outcome.

Methods

We report the case of a 7-year-old boy with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Since the age of two years, he experienced olfactory auras and automotor seizures. From age seven onward, additional focal tonic seizures involving the left arm and leg occurred. After an initially normal psychomotor development, a marked increase in seizure frequency (up to five seizures per day) was associated with cognitive decline and behavioral abnormalities. The epilepsy proved resistant to multiple antiseizure medications, including oxcarbazepine, valproate, zonisamide, levetiracetam, lamotrigine, carbamazepine, eslicarbazepine, and phenytoin.

Structural MRI revealed a subtle blurring of the gray–white matter junction in the right frontoorbital cortex, suspicious for focal cortical dysplasia. Interictal FDG-PET demonstrated a concordant focal hypometabolism in the same region and postictal ECD-SPECT showed a concordant focal hypoperfusion. 

Non-invasive video-EEG monitoring with scalp electrodes recorded 44 seizures of varying semiology, including olfactory auras, complex motor seizures, bilateral tonic seizures, dialeptic, and subclinical seizures. All seizures consistently originated from the right frontal region with propagation to the right temporal lobe.

Based on the congruence of clinical, imaging, and electrophysiological findings, invasive video-EEG monitoring was performed. Twelve stereotactically implanted depth electrodes were placed in the right frontal, insular, and temporal regions. Over several days, 97 seizures were recorded, including additional hypnopompic and left-sided tonic seizures. Seizure onset was consistently highly focal in a limited number of right frontoorbital contacts, with early propagation to the anterior insula. 

Extraoperative electrical stimulation mapping did not identify eloquent cortex within the seizure onset zone.

Structural MRI, FDG-PET, invasive EEG data, and MR angiography were co-registered and integrated into a patient-specific 3D model of the epileptogenic zone, which was used for resection planning and intraoperative neuronavigation.

Results

A tailored resection of the right frontoorbital cortex with partial resection of the anterior insular cortex was performed according to the preoperative plan derived from the multimodal 3D model. Surgery was completed without intraoperative complications.

Histopathological examination revealed focal cortical dysplasia type IIA, as well as numerous heterotopic neurons within the adjacent white matter. Postoperatively, the patient showed no new neurological deficits.

At one-year follow-up, the patient remains completely seizure-free (Engel class I) on valproate monotherapy. In parallel with seizure control, a marked improvement in cognitive performance and school achievement was observed, and behavioral abnormalities resolved.

This case demonstrates a high degree of spatial concordance between subtle structural MRI abnormalities, metabolic PET and SPECT findings, and invasive electrophysiological seizure onset patterns. The integration of these modalities into a unified 3D representation enabled precise delineation of the epileptogenic zone and facilitated a functionally safe surgical approach. 

Conclusion

This case highlights the value of multimodal neuroimaging integration in the presurgical evaluation of pediatric extratemporal epilepsy. Even when structural abnormalities are subtle, the combination of high-resolution MRI, metabolic imaging, and invasive electrophysiology—co-registered into a patient-specific 3D model—can provide robust localization of the epileptogenic zone and support successful surgical intervention.

For children with drug-resistant focal epilepsy, early consideration of epilepsy surgery is crucial, as ongoing seizures may lead to progressive cognitive and behavioral impairment. From a neuroimaging perspective, this case illustrates how advanced data integration and visualization strategies can translate complex multimodal information into actionable surgical guidance. Beyond seizure freedom, timely and precise intervention may allow recovery of neurodevelopmental trajectories and long-term functional outcomes.
