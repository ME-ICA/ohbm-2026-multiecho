Introduction

Difficulty with regulating emotions is an established risk factor for both the development of a range of psychiatric disorders and the maintenance of psychiatric distress despite engagement in therapeutic interventions1-2. To our knowledge, no meta-analyses thus far have quantified the main effect of neural activity across transdiagnostic clinical populations undergoing emotion regulation functional imaging task paradigms. Characterizing these brain activation patterns could lead to optimization and novel development of broadly effective neuroscience-based interventions. Therefore, we completed a coordinate-based meta-analysis to identify functional brain activation changes during emotion regulation across psychiatric disorders as a primary goal. We expected to observe increases in emotion regulation brain regions associated with cognitive control, such as lateral prefrontal cortex, as well as decreases in emotion generation regions, such as the insula and amygdala. We were also interested in studying whether different regulatory goals are supported by different neural processes. Therefore, the secondary aim of the study was to explore whether neural circuitry supporting emotion regulation differs depending on whether affect is downregulated versus upregulated. 

Methods

A systematic review of the literature was conducted in adherence with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses statement. We extracted 10,466 articles across four primary databases (Embase, APA PsycINFO, PubMed, and Web of Science) using a comprehensive search term to identify studies of emotion regulation fMRI paradigms. After removing 3,192 duplicates across databases and identifying 191 additional articles from relevant meta-analyses, 7,465 unique articles were screened. Of these, 7,240 records were excluded based on irrelevance of title and abstract. The full text of the remaining 224 articles was assessed for eligibility. Inclusion criteria were English language publication in a peer-reviewed scientific journal, publication after 1991 (development of fMRI), original scientific reports of a paradigm conducted in an MRI scanner during which participants regulated affect, analysis of an adult participant group with a current DSM diagnosis at the time of the study, and whole-brain task-fMRI BOLD results reported in MNI or Talairach coordinates. Meta-analyses were conducted via activation likelihood estimation using GingerALE Version 3.0.23. Difference in activation between the “regulation” and “baseline” conditions, the “downregulate” and “baseline” conditions, and the “upregulate” and “baseline” conditions were examined using a familywise error rate significance level of α = .05 (1000 permutations, p = .001) for correction of multiple clusters.

Results

18 studies (n = 356 participants) met eligibility criteria for inclusion. Diagnoses represented across the studies were avoidant personality disorder, bipolar I disorder, borderline personality disorder, gambling disorder, major depressive disorder, obsessive-compulsive disorder, schizophrenia, seasonal affective disorder, and one transdiagnostic sample. During general regulation of affect, we found significantly increased activation in the left inferior frontal gyrus (ventrolateral prefrontal cortex [vlPFC]; z = 4.66, p = .0000015456, the left superior frontal gyrus (dorsomedial prefrontal cortex [dmPFC]; z = 5.55, p = .0000000139), and the left superior temporal gyrus [STG] (z = 4.51, p = .0000031773). During downregulation of negative affect, we also found significantly increased activation in the left vlPFC (z = 4.75, p = .0000010016) and left dmPFC (z = 5.64, p = .0000000087), in addition to greater activation in the left putamen (z = 4.52, p = .0000030378). No significant changes in activation across studies emerged when upregulating affect compared to baseline conditions.

Conclusion

Consistent with our hypothesis, increased left vlPFC activation emerged as a transdiagnostic index of emotion regulation in adults with psychopathology. We also found significant increases in the left dmPFC and left STG. Based on the appraisal model of PFC function4, which proposes that PFC subregions contribute to emotion regulation by appraising different types of emotional content, results may indicate alterations in the vlPFC’s appraisal of emotional state and regulatory strategies based on current goals and context. The dmPFC’s appraisal of the mental states, intentions, and traits of others may also be affected in clinical populations. Increased STG activation during regulation supports changes in social cognitive processing and perspective-taking during regulation in transdiagnostic clinical populations. Finally, increased putamen activation specifically during downregulation of affect suggests that downregulation strategies recruit brain emotional reactivity brain regions during regulation in adults with psychopathology. One limitation of the current study is homogeneity in emotion regulation paradigm stimuli; 94% of studies used emotional faces from the International Affective Picture System database, limiting generalizability of results to other emotional induction procedures. Additionally, all studies used cognitive regulation strategies, limiting generalizability of neural findings to other regulation techniques such as mindfulness or relaxation. This review may inform the development of neuroscience-based interventions for psychiatric disorders, as well as increase the specificity of region-of-interest selection in future affective clinical neuroimaging studies.
