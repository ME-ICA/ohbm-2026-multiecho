Introduction

Empirical studies have firmly established the ventral occipitotemporal cortex (vOTC) as a critical hub for reading, centering on the visual word form area1. While early models posited a single specialized module, recent evidence reveals a subregions along the posterior-to-anterior axis: posterior regions (pOTS or VWFA-1) process perceptual features, whereas anterior regions (mOTS or VWFA-2) support lexical integration2 (Fig. 1A). However, the heterogeneity of existing functional localizers and experimental protocols complicates the precise segregation of these subregions at the individual-subject level. This variability hinders reproducible mapping of the reading circuitry. In this project, we aim to develop a single-subject analysis pipeline for the automatic and robust delineation of vOTC reading subregions leveraging a dense-sampling dataset acquired with multimodal MRI imaging across multiple sessions per participant. First, we established a ground truth parcellation for each subject. Then, a stability analysis to quantify the factors contributing to the univocal identification of these regions was performed. Finally, evidence-based recommendations for the minimum acquisition requirements necessary to reliably localize these functional regions are provided within a specified confidence interval.

Methods

We utilized a dense-sampling dataset collected from 5 healthy adults (mean age 33.6 ± 9.21) across 26 total sessions using four different 3T Siemens scanners (2 different Verio systems at NIPS, Japan and two Prisma Fit in Tamagawa University, Japan & BCBL, Spain). Participants performed a block-design one-back task using functional localizers adapted from2,3. The paradigm consisted of six runs per session, with five experimental conditions (two contrast-controlled categories each): Words, Checkers, Phase Scrambled Words, Consonants, False Fonts, Faces, Bodies and Limbs (Fig. 1C). Stimuli were presented for 400ms (100ms inter stimulus interval) in 6-second blocks. Structural images were acquired at 1mm³ isotropic resolution using MPRAGE (TR/TE = 2300/2.97 ms, FA = 9 °, FOV = 256 mm, voxel size = 1 mm3) and MP2RAGE sequences (TR/TE = 5000/2.98 ms, FA = 4 ° & 5 °, FOV = 258 mm, voxel size = 1 mm3). High-resolution functional MRI data were acquired at 1.5mm³ isotropic resolution (TR/TE = 2000/36 ms). Data were BIDS-curated (RRID:SCR_016124) using HeuDiconv (RRID:SCR_017427). To optimize signal quality, functional acquisitions underwent NORDIC denoising4. All functional data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep (RRID:SCR_016216), including motion correction and spatial normalization, and subsequently registered to individual cortical surfaces (fsnative) and common space (fsaverage) using FreeSurfer’s boundary-based registration (RRID:SCR_001847)). 

Our automated region-of-interest (ROI) delineation tool was divided into two stages. The purpose of stage 1 was to initialize a base for Word-Form Area (WFA) by determining candidate ROIs in a vOTC search space (Fig. 1B), using a high-powered functional contrast: Word vs. PER, where PER is defined as a combination of two perceptual stimuli, checkerboards and scrambled words (adapted from Lerma-Usabiaga et al., 2018; Fig. 1C). We systematically normalized the T-values across sessions and identified the T-max of the WFA. We used an iterative round-robin strategy where each session's cluster served as a spatial anchor to seed corresponding ROIs in the remaining sessions: for each session, an initial ROI was calculated using a custom vertex-wise clustering algorithm (Python adaptation of Freesurfer’s mri_surfcluster) around the Tmax. ROIs in subsequent sessions were calculated around the same Tmax region. The highest-scoring ROIs, determined by the Dice similarity coefficient across sessions, were selected. Their vertices were removed from subsequent calculations, and the procedure was iteratively continued until all above-threshold vertices were assigned to an ROI. This established a set of initialized ROIs.

On stage 2, we evaluated the correlation of non-target functional contrasts (e.g., face vs. rest, bodies vs rest) and structural data (e.g., FreeSurfer’s parcellation, sulcal depth, and curvature) with the initialized ROIs across sessions. Variables showing significant cross-session correlation were then used as supplementary features to confirm vertex assignment reliability and refine the final ROI boundaries. This two-stage process was optimized to yield a minimum of three consistent and reliable ROIs per subject: pOTS (VWFA-1), mOTS (VWFA-2), and aOTS (VWFA-3), with additional consistently appearing ROIs retained.

Finally, following ROI selection, we perform an exhaustive leave-k-out session-resampling procedure that computes mean pairwise Dice coefficients across all combinations with progressively fewer sessions to determine the minimum number of sessions required for stable ROI recovery. This analysis quantified the minimum number of acquisitions (functional and structural) required to reliably identify these same regions within a specified confidence interval. Furthermore, subjects whose data did not yield sufficient statistical power in the initial analysis were flagged, providing a crucial mechanism for identifying and excluding poor-quality data from the overall study population.

Results

We first validated the quality of the dense-sampling dataset by computing average T-values for the primary contrast (Words vs. Checkerboard and Scrambled stimuli) across all five subjects, confirming robust activation patterns within the vOTC search space (Fig. 2A). Consistent with established literature on the visual word form area (VWFA), we observed high-powered activity within the occipitotemporal sulcus (OTS), and more in the posterior part. Subsequent systematic normalization of T-values effectively harmonized signal intensity across all the sessions of the same subject, yielding consistent functional maps despite the use of different scanners (Fig. 2B). As a design choice, the specific per-session values were not the focus of Stage 1; rather, this initial stage aimed to have as homogeneous as possible contributions from all sessions.

In Stage 1 of the automated pipeline, the iterative clustering algorithm successfully initialized a broad set of candidate ROIs around local T-maxima. While the precise number and extent of these initial regions varied depending on the selected clustering parameters, the method consistently captured the broad reading circuitry, identifying several candidate ROIs (Fig. 2C delineated on top of the T maps, and in 2D separated in colors).

Proceeding to Stage 2, the tool applied cross-session stability metrics to filter these candidates, successfully isolating the three distinct target subregions, pOTS (VWFA-1), mOTS (VWFA-2), and aOTS (VWFA-3). The final selection and precise delineation of these ROIs relied on integrating both functional information (derived from non-target contrasts like Faces vs. Rest and Bodies vs. Rest) and structural information (such as sulcal depth, curvature, and FreeSurfer parcellations), ensuring maximum cross-session reliability and topographical consistency. Conclusion

The highly specialized and compact nature of the ventral occipitotemporal cortex (vOTC), combined with significant inter-subject variability, renders standard group-averaged maps insufficient for precise localization of the reading circuitry. This limitation has historically hindered the consistent segregation of functional subregions, such as the pOTS (VWFA-1) and mOTS (VWFA-2), at the individual level. Our work directly addresses these challenges by validating a fully automated, single-subject analysis pipeline. Leveraging a high-density dataset acquired across multiple scanners, we demonstrate that robust delineation is achievable through a two-stage process. First, regions are anchored using a high-powered functional contrast (Words vs. PER). Crucially, the second stage refines boundaries and confirms vertex assignment reliability by integrating stable structural and non-target functional features. This multi-feature approach ensures that the resulting parcellation reflects the posterior-to-anterior regions described in recent literature.

We specifically show that the consistent delineation of the reading subregions (pOTS, mOTS, aOTS) is aided by their relative localization to nearby non-target functional regions (e.g., face vs. rest, bodies vs. rest) and by key structural anchors. For instance, by assessing the correlation of features like sulcal depth, curvature, and the precise boundaries of the Occipitotemporal sulcus (OTS) with the candidate ROI boundaries, we ensure that the final selected parcellation is not merely a statistical cluster, but a functional and anatomical entity that is stable relative to the surrounding cortical landscape. This establishes that our automated parcellation respects the established topographical organization of the vOTC, a necessary step for high-fidelity mapping.

Furthermore, beyond the automated tool, this study provides empirically derived guidelines for experimental design. Through an exhaustive leave-k-out session-resampling analysis, we established the minimum acquisition requirements, quantifying the specific amount of functional and structural data necessary to recover these ROIs within a specified confidence interval. This advances the field from arbitrary scan durations toward evidence-based acquisition protocols, ensuring future studies are statistically powered to reliably detect these specific reading subregions. Consequently, this resource serves as a fundamental prerequisite for high-fidelity longitudinal tracking and accurate cross-study comparisons, allowing for the standardization of vOTC parcellation across diverse populations and scanners.
