Introduction

Schizophrenia spectrum disorders (SSD) present marked neuroanatomical abnormalities, including widespread cortical thinning, subcortical volume reductions, and ventricular enlargement, although some regions such as the pallidum and caudate may show increased volume. Several of these alterations relate to poorer clinical outcomes and greater symptom severity. Since direct comparison of brain characteristics across individuals can be confounded by age and sex related variation, normative models provide a common reference framework that enables individualized assessment of deviations from expected values. Within this framework, deviations in cortical thickness (CT), particularly in temporal and parietal regions, have been linked to symptom severity and functional outcomes and showed improved predictive value over raw volumetric measures.

Microstructural abnormalities also contribute to SSD. Diffusion-based indices such as fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) reflect white-matter integrity, while cortical MD (cMD) captures microstructural disruption within the cortical ribbon even without detectable atrophy. Elevated cMD has been associated with greater symptom severity and poorer functioning, suggesting its potential as a sensitive but underused biomarker.

This study examines individual-level deviations in cMD, CT, subcortical volumes, and FA-derived tracts obtained through normative models in minimally medicated first-episode SSD patients. We also test whether these deviations relate to positive and negative symptom severity. We hypothesize that patients will show significant structural abnormalities and that the magnitude of these deviations will be associated with clinical presentation.

Methods

Participant recruitment was conducted through the Program for Attention to the Initial Phases of Psychoses (PAFIP), established at Marqués de Valdecilla University Hospital, Santander, Spain. Individuals in the clinical group met DSM-IV criteria for SSD. At intake MRI, SSD individuals had no prior antipsychotic medication or were medicated for less than 6 weeks . Sample comprised 112 neurotypical controls (males = 68; mean age = 29.73 ± 7.42 years) and 113 SSD individuals (males = 74; mean age = 29.82 ± 8.69 years) with the following distribution of DSM-IV diagnoses: schizophrenia (n=50), schizophreniform disorder (n=33), brief psychotic disorder (n=23), and psychosis not otherwise specified (n=7). Diagnoses were confirmed after a six month follow-up. All procedures involving human subjects were approved by the CEIC-Cantabria (clinical trial numbers NCT0235832 and NCT02534363) and the Ethic committee of the University of Seville (2024-2534)

Structural MRI data (3D T1-weighted) were acquired using a 3T Philips Medical Systems MRI scanner. Diffusion Tensor Images (DTI) were collected during the same scanning session. DTI images underwent ENIGMA-DTI visual quality assurance (Q/A) and T1w images underwent quality assurance (Q/A) protocol. Regional brain volumes and CT were extracted using the 68 Desikan–Killiany atlas and volumes for eight subcortical structures (thalamus, pallidum, accumbens, hippocampus, caudate, amygdala, third and fourth ventricle) with FreeSurfer v7.4.1. 

TBSS processing was performed following standardized protocols provided by the ENIGMA-DTI consortium. Regional FA measures were extracted from 21 bilateral or mid-sagittal regions of interest (ROIs) defined by the JHU ICBM-DTI-81 WM atlas1. Cortical ribbon MD was projected across the cortical depth, and its values were mapped to each subject’s native surface. These values were parcellated according to the Desikan-Killiany atlas. 

CT and subcortical volumes were referenced against normative data derived from Dorfschmidt et al. (2025)2 . Similarly, centile values for FA of WM tracts (referred here as tract centiles) were computed using the normative method proposed by Zhu et al. (2025)3 implemented in 'eHarmonize' Python package.

Clinical assessments were obtained  from  participants diagnosed with SSD and included:  CGI , DAS, BPRS,SAPS and SANS. In addition, average cognitive functioning was computed across seven domains.

To examine the association between SSD diagnosis and morphometric or microstructural features, multivariate regressions were run for each brain region or tract:

structural feature ~ 1+diagnosis+sex+age+etiv +Euler         (1) 

Four structural features  were considered: CT centiles, subcortical volumes centiles, tract centiles, and cMD.  Diagnosis was coded as control vs. SSD. Euler index was included to correct for motion-induced artefacts. 

Finally, we assessed whether the mean structural measures were associated with clinical variables as CGI, DAS, average cognitive functioning. Symptom severity assessed with BPRS, SAPS, and SANS. 

clinical measure  ~ 1+ sex +age + etiv+Euler + structural feature        (2)

Structural features studied were CT, subcortical and tract centiles and cMD.

Results

Global mean CT centiles were reduced in individuals with SSD (T=-2.52, p=0.01). At the regional level, widespread significant reductions of CT centiles were found at parietal (T<-2.90, p<0.04) and cingulate (T<-2.94, p=0.04) cortices (Fig. 1A). Subcortical volumetric centiles  revealed that SSD had significantly larger third (T=4.13, p<10-3) and fourth (T=3.66, p<10-2) ventricles (Fig. 1B). Additionally, reduced centiles were observed in several subcortical GM structures, including the left accumbens (T=-2.90, p=0.01), bilateral thalamus (right T=-2.48, p=0.03; left T=-2.52, p=0.03), right amygdala (T=-2.59, p=0.03) and bilateral hippocampus (right: T=-2.87, p=0.01; left: T=-2.99, p=0.01). No significant differences in tracts centiles survived FDR correction (Fig. 1C) . Nonetheless, the anterior corona radiata (T=-2.70, p=0.09) and genu of the corpus callosum (T=-3.06, p=0.06) showed trends toward significance. Individuals with SSD showed widespread cMD increased values in frontal (T>2.64, p<0.02), cingulate (T>2.52, p<0.02), occipital (T>2.31, p<0.03), parietal (T>2.20, p<0.04), and temporal (T>2.15, p<0.04) regions (Fig. 1D)

Mean cMD value was significantly and positively associated with hospitalization (T=2.7, p= 0.02), CGI (T=3.51, p<0.01) and SAPS scores (T=3.71, p< 0.01). In contrast, mean CT centile showed a significant negative correlation with days of hospitalization (T=–2.63, p=0.04) and with CGI (T=-2.99, p=0.03). Finally, the mean centiles of the third and fourth ventricles were significantly and positively correlated with SAPS (T=3.14, p=0.02). No other associations remained significant after the FDR correction.

In the regional analyses, correlations between BPRS scores and both GM morphometric features and cMD did not survive FDR correction. BPRS was significantly correlated with the FA centile of two WM tracts: sagittal stratum (T=-3.00, p=0.04) and the posterior thalamic radiation (T=-3.20, p=0.04). The association between CT centiles and SAPS (Fig. 1E) survived the FDR corrected threshold for the right superior temporal gyrus (T=-3.54, p=0.03), supramarginal gyrus (T=-3.28, p=0.03), and frontal pole of the right (T=-3.33, p=0.03). A widespread positive correlation between SAPS and cMD (Fig. 1F)  was found (cingulate T>2.72, p<0.02; frontal T>2.31, p<0.04; insula T>3.49, p<0.01; occipital T>2.8, p<0.015; parietal T>2.48, p<0.03; temporal T>3.32, p<0.01). None of the negative associations between SAPS and regional tract centiles remained significant after correction. Correlations between SAPS and subcortical centiles (Fig. 1G) did not survive multiple corrections, except for the third ventricle, which exhibited a positive association (T=3.59, p=0.01). SANS did not show significant associations with any of the features examined.

Conclusion

Our findings provide converging evidence that early-stage psychosis is characterized by a distinct pattern of structural alterations involving cortical, subcortical, and WM alterations. cMD showed the highest sensitivity to positive symptoms and was associated with key clinical features at the first episode, including hospitalization and overall illness severity. While reductions in CT, ventricular enlargement, and increased cortical diffusivity were prominent, weaker effect sizes were found in DWM. The differential correlations between symptom dimensions and structural features support the presence of distinct neurobiological mechanisms underlying positive, negative, and global psychopathology. These results emphasize the need for multidimensional clinical assessment and suggest that early deviations from normative brain structure and cMD may serve as valuable markers for disease onset and progression in SSD.
