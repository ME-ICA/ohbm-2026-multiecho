Introduction

The apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 genotype is a genetic risk factor for late-onset Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Resting state functional connectivity measured with MRI (rsfMRI) is disrupted in AD, with consistent reports of hypoconnectivity in the default mode network (DMN), particularly in the parietal lobes and posterior cingulate cortex (PCC).1 Results in cognitively intact APOE ε4 carriers (e4+) have been inconsistent, with reports of both stronger and weaker connectivity strength when compared to non-carriers.2 

Here, we present a longitudinal analysis of global functional connectivity (GFC) in older, cognitively intact e4+ carriers and non-carriers (e4-). Rather than evaluating the strength of connectivity itself, GFC assigns each voxel a value indicating the number of other voxels in the brain with which it shares a strong connection,3 thereby identifying regions of abnormal or changing connectivity without reliance on network-based measures. We use a data-driven, functionally relevant parcellation approach,4 hypothesizing that e4+ participants will show weaker GFC in the PCC than e4- participants and that GFC will decline over time in the e4+ group.

Methods

This analysis included ninety-six cognitively intact, healthy participants enrolled in an IRB-approved protocol. Fifty-four were APOE e4+ (mean age 69.3 ± 3.6; 30 males) and forty-two were APOE e4- (mean age 71.2 ± 4.1; 21 males). Participants were scanned on a 3T Siemens Prisma at two visits separated by a mean of 1.94 ± 0.22 years. Scans included a whole-brain anatomical MPRAGE (voxel size = 1mm3) and rsfMRI scan with the following parameters: 132 repetitions, axial slices acquired with TE/TR=29ms/2800ms, voxel size=2x2x4mm². Participants were instructed to keep their eyes closed during scans and a bite-bar was used to reduce head motion.

RsfMRI scans were pre-processed with measured physiologic signal removal and motion correction. 

GFC calculation: GFC was calculated using a previously presented method.3 The following was performed in native space for each participant, for each grey matter voxel: 1) Low-pass temporal filtering (<0.1Hz). 2) Pearson cross-correlation to every other voxel in the grey matter. 2) The cross-correlation was converted to a Student's t. 3) A gaussian fit to full-width at half-maximum of frequency distribution of the Student's t. 4) The difference in area between the fit distribution and the observed distribution above a threshold (> |2.5|) was taken as positive and negative GFC for that voxel (Figure 1).

Functional parcellation: Prior to the parcellation, rsfMRI data was warped to MNI space, detrended, and filtered at 0.01–0.1 Hz. A group analysis using a cohesive parcellation method4 resulted in 360 grey matter parcels. The parcellation was warped to native space for each participant. At the subject level, ROIs with less than 10 voxels were not included in the analysis. At the group level, ROIs that were missing in 20% or more of participants were not included in the analysis. This resulted in 180 cortical grey matter parcels remaining in the analysis.

Statistical analysis: Positive and negative median GFC were calculated for each grey matter region. Linear mixed effect modeling was used to compare GFC by group (e4+, e4-) and visit (V1, V2) with terms for age, sex, education, and a group-by-visit interaction. The Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied to the p-values for the full model across all tested regions. For those models surviving FDR correction, a region was considered significant if the effect of group (APOE variant) or group-by-visit reached the p < 0.05 level. Posthoc analysis was performed using unpaired t-tests.

Results

Groups did not differ on sex (p < 0.293), education (p < 0.196), or time between visits (p < 0.662); however the e4+ group was significantly younger than the e4- group (p < 0.015). For negative GFC, 86/180 regions remained significant after correction for multiple comparisons. These differences were primarily driven by sex; no regions were significant for the effect of group or the group-by-visit interaction. For positive GFC, 13/180 regions were significant after FDR correction (p < 0.01). Eight regions were significant for the effect of group (p < 0.05, Figure 2), with the e4+ group showing lower positive GFC in all regions. No regions were significant for visit or the group-by-visit interaction. Paired t-tests showed a significant effect of group at visit 1, although this difference did not persist at visit 2.

Conclusion

Our findings are consistent with reports of weakened connectivity in cognitively intact e4+ older adults and of diffuse disruption throughout brain networks.5 Notably, group differences were only significant at visit 1. This could be due to temporal changes in within- and between-network connectivity, which the GFC measure does not differentiate. Consistently lower connectivity at visit 1 in the e4+ group suggests that additional network-based analysis is warranted. Consistent, early differences in rsfMRI in APOE ε4 carriers could be mechanistically relevant to cognitive decline due to AD.
