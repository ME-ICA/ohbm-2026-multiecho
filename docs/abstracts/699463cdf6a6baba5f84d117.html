Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is marked by progressive tau pathology, with substantial inter-individual variability reflecting clinical heterogeneity. The mechanisms driving tau accumulation, however, remain poorly understood. The connectome hypothesis proposes that neural connections serve as conduits for tau spread, with neural activity promoting its release and propagation^1. Here, we test this hypothesis by quantifying the alignment between tau accumulation and brain connectivity using connectome harmonic decomposition, including structural, functional, and partial functional connectivity (SC, FC, pFC). This approach represents tau signals in terms of their spatial-frequency components along the brain network^2, enabling us to assess how closely tau patterns follow the intrinsic organization of the connectome and to derive multimodal tau-connectivity features predictive of clinical progression.

Methods

We investigated n=313 subjects (71.6±8.2y, 54%w) assessed at Geneva University Hospitals Memory Clinic, Switzerland with T1w MRI, [18F]flortaucipir tau-PET, amyloid status (A+/A- from amyloid-PET or CSF), Mini Mental State Examination (baseline and follow-up MMSE), and clinical classification into cognitively unimpaired (CU), mild cognitive impairment (MCI), or dementia (DEM) (‘GENCORE’ cohort). Analyses focused only on subjects within the Alzheimer’s continuum (A+; n=154, 73.1±8.0y, 55%w) and healthy ageing (HA; A-T-CU; n=54, 68.2±8.3y, 65%w) as reference group. Decliners were defined as subjects having MMSE decline > 1.5 points/year (n=92 A+ subjects with available longitudinal MMSE data, of whom 54 (59%) were decliners). Braak staging was determined by a clinician from tau-PET visual inspection. T1w and tau-PET data were processed to obtain tau Standardized Uptake Value Ratio (SUVR) values for N=400 cortical regions (Schaefer parcellation) and an AD-related meta-region. A tau-covariance matrix was computed as Fisher z-transformed Spearman’s rank correlation between region pairs’ SUVR across A+ subjects (Fig.1a). Template brain connectivity matrices (400x400) were obtained from DWI and rs-fMRI data of 100 Human Connectome Project healthy subjects using state-of-the-art pipelines. SC was quantified as the number of connecting streamlines per unit surface of region pairs; FC as Fisher z-transformed Pearson’s correlation between regional time series; pFC as normalized precision matrix estimated as Tikhonov regularized inverse of the time series covariance matrix^3; DIST as Euclidean distance between region centroids in MNI space (Fig.1a).

Symmetrically normalized Laplacians of tau covariance and connectivity matrices were decomposed into eigenmodes (Fig.1b). These represent the main axes of tau accumulation and connectivity variations across the cortex and can be used as orthonormal bases for the (spatial) harmonic analysis of signals defined over cortical parcels^2. 

At the group-level, the degree of alignment between tau and connectivity was estimated by progressively considering the first k eigenmodes of each dimension (k=2 to N) and computing the Procrustes distance between the two sets of eigenmodes. The area under the curve (AUC) of the resulting distance curves effectively quantifies tau-connectivity alignment across the different connectivity dimensions (confidence interval: 100 stratified bootstraps of A+ CU/MCI/DEM subjects for tau-covariance estimation).

At the individual level, tau-connectivity alignment was quantified by decomposing each participant’s tau-SUVR map into connectivity eigenmodes, thereby obtaining its harmonic spectrum, conceptually similar to decomposing a temporal signal into its constituent frequency components. Individual tau spectra were characterized using: (i) a diffusion index (exponential decay fitting of the power spectrum); (ii) energy measures, computed both globally and within integrative, degenerate, and segregative harmonic regimes^4 (integral of the energy spectrum within predefined frequency bins), yielding 5 harmonic measures across 4 connectivity dimensions (Fig.1c). These metrics constitute multimodal tau-connectivity features that describe how tau accumulation relates to the underlying connectivity substrates. For comparison, the average tau SUVR within an AD meta-region was included as a reference measure.

Linear and 10-fold cross-validated logistic regression models were used to assess the predictive power of tau feature (one model per feature) for baseline MMSE and for cognitive decline (decliner status), while accounting for age, sex, and education. Baseline MMSE was additionally included in the models predicting cognitive decline. 

Results

Descriptive statistics of the cohort are reported in Fig.2a.

The first three tau-covariance eigenmodes spanned the temporo-occipital/frontal, temporo-limbic/neocortical, and unimodal/heteromodal axes, in line with recently reported tau-covariance gradients and possibly reflecting distinct spatiotemporal tau accumulation patterns across individuals^5 (Fig.1b). The first brain-connectivity eigenmodes, corresponding to lower spatial frequencies, spanned unimodal/heteromodal (FC, pFC), left/right, and occipital/frontal axes (SC), confirming the architecture of major brain gradients across connectivity dimensions (Fig.1b). All DIST eigenmodes were highly spatially localized, reflecting the predominance of spatial proximity rather than long-range connectivity (Fig.1b).

At the group level, we found that tau covariance architecture aligns with both structural and functional connectivity, while it is not explained by geometric constraints (DIST). Among connectivity dimensions, pFC most closely reflected tau accumulation (AUC 170.86 [166.84, 174.44]) compared to FC (AUC 191.71 [187.84 195.82]) and SC (AUC 201.94 [197.73 206.45]) (Fig.2b). Consistently, individual tau pFC-spectra followed an exponential decay with power concentration in the lower frequencies, indicating tau SUVR spatial smoothness with respect to the underlying pFC network (Fig.2c).

Multimodal tau-connectivity features, embedding tau SUVR and brain connectivity substrate, were computed for each subject, and considering SC, FC, pFC, and DIST harmonic spaces. All features were positively correlated and progressively increased across Braak stages (Fig.2d), suggesting clinico-biological relevance.

Among the diffusion features, only pFC- and SC-based diffusion indices showed meaningful associations with MMSE and predictive power for cognitive decline after adjusting for age, sex, education, and baseline MMSE. pFC-based diffusion had the strongest association with MMSE (β=–0.96, p=.009, partial R2=5%, adjusted R2=18%); SC diffusion showed a weaker effect (β=–0.72, p=.049, partial R2=3%, adjusted R2=16%); FC and DIST diffusion were not associated with MMSE (p>.05). pFC-based diffusion index was also the strongest predictor for cognitive decline, with higher diffusion index nearly doubling the odds of being a decliner (OR=1.93 [1.14, 3.28], cross-validated AUC=0.67±0.11); SC and DIST diffusion showed weaker associations; FC did not predict decline.

All energy-based features showed consistent associations with MMSE and significant predictive power for cognitive decline, outperforming the classical meta-region tau SUVR measure (Fig.2e). PFC- and SC-based tau energy components in the degenerate regime, corresponding to intermediate harmonic frequencies, had the largest explanatory power for MMSE (pFC-based: β=–1.97, p=<1e-6, partial R2=17%, adjusted R2=28%; SC-based: β=–2.10, p=<1e-6, partial R2=18%, adjusted R2=29%; meta-region: β=–1.72, p=<1e-5, partial R2=14%, adjusted R2=26%). Energy metrics were associated with future decline (OR ranging from 4 to 8, cross-validated AUCs from 0.76 to 0.84), capturing more predictive variance than the classical meta-region (OR=3.52 [1.82, 6.81], cross-validated AUC=0.71±0.15).

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that tau accumulation in the Alzheimer’s continuum aligns with the brain’s structural and functional connectivity—most strongly with partial functional connectivity—and that multimodal tau-connectivity features have high predictive value for cognitive decline. As a proxy for long-distance monosynaptic communication, partial functional connectivity may provide an optimal substrate for tau diffusion across the brain, consistent with the connectome hypothesis, which posits that both axonal and functional links contribute to tau spread. Tau energy features capture the extent of tau involvement across multiscale networks (unlike regional meta-region uptake), and their strong predictive performance suggests they may index network-level neural disruption that ultimately drives cognitive deterioration. Taken together, these results highlight connectome harmonics as a promising framework for quantifying network-dependent tau propagation and deriving biologically meaningful, predictive biomarkers. Future work should extend this approach to longitudinal imaging data and subject-specific connectivity to support individualized prognosis.
