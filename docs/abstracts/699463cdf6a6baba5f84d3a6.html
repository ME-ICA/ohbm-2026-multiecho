Introduction

    Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) has emerged as a promising non-invasive intervention for mild cognitive impairment (MCI), with evidence suggesting improvements in cognitive function through modulation of neural networks1. The therapeutic mechanisms of rTMS are fundamentally governed by neuroplasticity principles, particularly Hebbian-like synaptic plasticity, where neural circuits activated during stimulation are preferentially modulated2. Further, recent evidence suggests that combining brain stimulation such as rTMS with specific state beyond rest (e.g. cognitive tasks or naturalistic stimuli) may enhance therapeutic efficacy through state-dependent plasticity mechanisms3. Naturalistic stimuli such as movie-watching have been shown to engage ecologically valid cognitive processes and activate memory-related networks4. However, the potential effects of rTMS combined with recall movie-watching (rTMS+movie) stimulation on functional connectivity of MCI patients are still unclear. Therefore, in this work, using resting-state EEG data, effects of rTMS combined with recall movie-watching on MCI patients were investigated.

Methods

    The resting-state EEG data (64-channel) were collected from MCI patients at baseline and post-intervention. The study was approved by the local ethics board, and all participants provided written informed consent. Patients were randomly assigned to two intervention groups (Fig.1a): conventional rTMS group (n=12) received 10 Hz rTMS to left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex during rest, while rTMS+movie group (n=11) received identical rTMS during recall movie-watching. Both groups completed 20 intervention sessions over 4 weeks (single-session protocol in Fig.1b).All EEG data were preprocessed using the WeBrain EEG pipelines5. The preprocessing steps included: bandpass filtering (1-40 Hz), artifact removal using Independent Component Analysis (ICA), interpolating bad channels using reference electrode standardization interpolation technique (RESIT) and re-referencing to REST. Phase synchronization index (PSI) was calculated to construct functional connectivity networks across multiple frequency bands (delta: 1-4 Hz, theta: 4-8 Hz, alpha: 8-12.5 Hz, beta: 12.5-30 Hz, gamma: 30-40 Hz) at the cortical source level using the AAL 82-region atlas. Then, for each frequency band, a mixed-design two-way ANOVA (grouptime) was performed to assess interaction effects, followed by post-hoc independent t-tests comparing connectivity changes (post-pre) between groups, with FDR correction (P<0.05). Next, Pearson correlation analyses examined the relationship between connectivity changes and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score improvements within each group separately.

Results

 Results of ANOVA and the post-hoc t-test showed significant grouptime interaction effects for theta-band functional connectivity, especially in 160 connections (p<0.05, FDR corrected). Post-hoc analysis revealed lower connectivity enhancement in the rTMS group (blue edges in Fig.2a), predominantly involved in the frontoparietal and temporal networks, including middle frontal gyrus, superior frontal gyrus, temporal pole, inferior parietal lobule, supramarginal gyrus, insula and anterior cingulate cortex regions. And, higher connectivity enhancement in the conventional rTMS group (red edges in Fig.2a), primarily located in the sensorimotor, auditory and limbic regions. Correlation analysis revealed that changes of these connections were positive correlated with changes of MoCA values between baseline and post-intervention (r=0.412, p=0.05, Fig.2b).

Conclusion

   In a summary, this study reveals theta-band functional connectivity reorganization patterns, mainly located in the frontoparietal and temporal regions, induced by rTMS combined with recall movie-watching in MCI patients. These findings support the concept of state-dependent neuroplasticity and provide a new perspective for understanding the neural mechanisms of state-dependent rTMS intervention strategies in MCI.
