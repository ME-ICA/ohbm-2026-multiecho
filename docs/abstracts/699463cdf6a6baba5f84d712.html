Introduction

Isolated REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) is recognized as a prodromal synucleinopathy, with multiple neurotransmitter systems implicated in its pathophysiology. This study aimed to investigate baseline gray matter atrophy in isolated rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder (iRBD) patients compared to the controls and to evaluate associated neurotransmitter changes. Moreover, we evaluated gray matter atrophy and its associated neurotransmitter associated with phenoconversion in IRBD patients.

Methods

We analyzed 109 iRBD patients who completed at least one follow-up evaluation and 24 age- and sex-matched healthy controls who underwent structural MRI. Voxel-based morphometry was performed to assess GMV changes. The JuSpace toolbox was utilized to evaluate the spatial correlations between the gray matter atrophy maps and specific neurotransmitter receptor/transporter distributions derived from PET/SPECT atlases. 

Results

Median age of IRBD patients was 69 years old and 51.4% were male which was similar to the controls. At baseline, iRBD patients showed reduced GMV in the right cerebellum and occipital fusiform gyrus compared to controls. This spatial pattern of cerebellar atrophy was significantly associated with Dopamine (D1, D2), Serotonin (5-HT1a), and mGluR5 receptors. Longitudinally, iRBD phenoconverters exhibited reduced GMV in the right medial orbital gyrus compared to non-converters. Notably, atrophy in the left putamen and pallidum was spatially associated with Dopaminergic (D1) and Noradrenergic (NAT) transporters (FDR-corrected p < 0.05).

Conclusion

iRBD patients exhibit regional gray matter atrophy that spatially aligns with specific neurotransmitter systems. In particular, gray matter atrophy patterns associated with dopaminergic systems may represent a potential biomarker of neurodegenerative progression in iRBD.
