Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD), a chronic inflammatory bowel disorder, is increasingly recognized for its systemic effects, including significant impacts on the central nervous system via the gut-brain axis. Systemic inflammation, driven by proinflammatory cytokines and immune dysregulation, is thought to induce neuroplastic changes that disrupt brain structure and function, contributing to cognitive and affective symptoms even during clinical remission.

Neuroimaging studies have revealed structural and functional alterations in CD patients (CDs), including gray matter thinning in prefrontal and cingulate regions, white matter integrity loss, and aberrant functional connectivity1,2. However, most prior research has relied on volume-based fMRI approaches, which are limited by spatial inaccuracies during normalization, insensitivity to cortical folding, and potential smoothing-related artifacts. Surface-based analysis offers a more precise alternative by modeling functional data directly onto the cortical surface, improving inter-subject alignment and cortical specificity.

Additionally, traditional resting-state functional connectivity often depends on predefined regions of interest, overlooking localized spontaneous brain activity. Metrics such as the amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF), fractional ALFF (fALFF), regional homogeneity (ReHo), and degree centrality (DC) provide complementary, ROI-free insights into intrinsic brain function at the local level.

Therefore, the present study employs a surface-based resting-state fMRI (RS-fMRI) approach combined with ALFF, fALFF, ReHo, and DC to investigate localized functional alterations in CDs during remission. We aim to identify disease-related changes in sensory and prefrontal regions and explore their relationship with cognitive performance, thereby advancing a more refined understanding of the neural correlates of CD.

Methods

Twelve CDs in remission (mean age = 26.08±4.9 years, 5 female) and twelve age- and gender-matched healthy controls (HCs; mean age = 24.5±3.6 years, 4 female) were included. All participants underwent RS-fMRI and T1-weighted structural imaging on a 3T GE scanner. The parameters for RS-fMRI data: TR = 2600 ms, TE = 22 ms, flip angle = 60°, slice thickness = 3.5 mm. The parameters for T1-weighted data: TR = 8160 ms, TE = 3.18 ms, flip angle = 12°, in-plane resolution = 1 × 1 mm2, and slice thickness = 1 mm. Data preprocessing was performed using DPABISurf, a surface-based pipeline incorporating tools from Freesurfer and SPM12. Steps included motion correction, spatial normalization to the fsaverage5 surface, band-pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz for ReHo and DC), and regression of nuisance signals.

Four local brain activity metrics – ALFF, fALFF, ReHo, and DC – were computed: ALFF and fALFF reflect the magnitude of spontaneous low-frequency oscillations; ReHo measures local temporal synchronization; and DC quantifies nodal connectivity within whole-brain networks. Group differences between CDs and HCs were assessed using vertex-wise two-sample t-tests, with age, gender, and head motion as covariates. Multiple comparisons were corrected via TFCE with 5,000 permutations (p < 0.05, cluster size > 100 vertices). Correlation analyses examined relationships between RS-fMRI metrics and verbal fluency (VF) scores, as well as anxiety measures (BAS and BIS) in CDs. 

Results

    Significant differences in local brain activity were observed between CDs and HCs. CDs exhibited decreased ALFF in visual and sensory regions, including the bilateral lingual gyrus, right cuneus, fusiform, and inferior occipital gyri. ReHo was increased in prefrontal regions (left inferior orbitofrontal and middle frontal gyri) but decreased in the left lingual and right postcentral gyri. DC was elevated in the left angular, middle orbitofrontal, middle frontal, and right middle occipital gyri, while it was reduced in the left middle cingulate cortex, left superior temporal, and bilateral postcentral gyri. No significant group differences were found in fALFF (see Figure 1).

Correlation analyses revealed that in CDs, VF scores were negatively correlated with ALFF in the right putamen (r = -0.846). In HCs, VF scores correlated negatively with ALFF in the right lingual and left inferior orbitofrontal gyri, and positively with ALFF in the left postcentral gyrus. Among CDs, higher BAS-Reward Responsiveness scores were associated with lower ReHo in the left lingual gyrus (r = -0.830), while higher BIS scores correlated with lower ReHo in the left inferior orbitofrontal gyrus (r = -0.777) and higher ALFF in the right lingual gyrus (r = 0.917).

Conclusion

       The present study demonstrates that patients with Crohn’s disease in remission exhibit distinct alterations in local brain function, particularly in sensory and prefrontal regions, as captured by surface-based RS-fMRI metrics. Decreased ALFF and ReHo in visual and somatosensory areas suggest impaired sensory processing, while increased ReHo and DC in prefrontal regions may reflect compensatory mechanisms for cognitive control3,4,5. The correlations between neural activity and verbal fluency performance highlight the functional relevance of these changes, indicating that CD-related brain alterations may contribute to cognitive symptoms. Furthermore, associations with anxiety-related traits underscore the interplay between emotional regulation and brain function in CD.

These findings support the involvement of the gut-brain axis in CD and emphasize the value of surface-based fMRI and local activity metrics in detecting subtle neural changes. Limitations include the small sample size and cross-sectional design. Future studies with larger cohorts and longitudinal assessments are needed to clarify the progression of these brain changes and their relationship with disease activity, treatment, and behavioral outcomes.
