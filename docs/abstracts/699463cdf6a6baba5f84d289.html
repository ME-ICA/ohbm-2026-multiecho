Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is an increasingly prevalent psychiatric illness 1 that disrupts large-scale brain connectivity and oscillatory coupling, as determined by electroencephalography (EEG) 2. Deep learning techniques for EEG diagnosis have poor interpretability and rely heavily on huge datasets. Functional connectivity 3, particularly wavelet-coherence-based measurements, offers a physiologically meaningful and data-efficient approach to characterizing frequency-specific dysconnectivity in MDD. Our study examines the effectiveness of band-specific wavelet coherence features derived from resting-state EEG in distinguishing persons with MDD from healthy controls. We also compare these features against statistical baselines and a deep learning algorithm.

Methods

We evaluated a publicly accessible resting-state EEG dataset 4 of 64 participants (34 MDD, 30 healthy controls), recorded using a 64-channel montage at 256 Hz. Signals were band-pass filtered, notch-filtered, evaluated for artifacts, corrected using ICA, downsampled to 100 Hz, and segmented into epochs with 50% overlap, resulting in 570 MDD and 532 healthy segments. Nineteen standard electrodes were kept to maintain consistency between participants. We estimated wavelet coherence between channel pairs for each epoch and averaged values within frequency bands (δ, θ, α, β). We used Welch’s t-tests with false discovery rate correction to detect significant sensor-level edge features. The classification was conducted using a support vector machine (SVM) with 5-fold subject-wise cross-validation over 50 random seeds. We compared performance to two baselines:
(i) statistical EEG characteristics, and (ii) EEGNet 5, a compact convolutional neural network trained on the same segments. Fig. 1 illustrates the entire processing pipeline, from raw EEG to connection estimation, feature selection, and classification.

Results

Under subject-wise 5-fold cross-validation across 50 random seeds, wavelet coherence connectivity characteristics in the δ band demonstrated the highest group classification accuracy (82.7%), sensitivity (78.4%), and specificity (88.2%). The results were significantly better than those of statistical EEG features (70.1% accuracy) and EEGNet 5 (74.5% accuracy; all comparisons, p < 0.01). After false discovery rate correction, the δ band showed the most significant changes in sensor-level connection between MDD and controls, while θ, α, and β bands had less significant effects. The most discriminative edges were found between fronto-temporal and fronto-parietal pairs. The feature importance analysis revealed that δ-band coherence had a significant role in classification evaluations, aligning with group-level connectivity patterns. Fig. 2 displays a representative
significance matrices for each frequency band, illustrating the distinct δ-band dysconnectivity characteristic in MDD.

Conclusion

Wavelet-coherence connectivity, especially in the δ band, is a reliable biomarker for distinguishing MDD patients from healthy controls. Connectivity-derived features outperformed statistical EEG characteristics and a compact deep learning model in terms of classification performance and physiological interpretation. The observed δ-band dysconnectivity at the sensor level suggests
that frequency-specific network changes in MDD can be detected. Future research will combine graph-theoretic measures with directed or phase-based metrics to better understand the network-level dynamics driving depressive disorders.
