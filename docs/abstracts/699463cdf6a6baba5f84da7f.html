Introduction

Resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) is widely used to characterize large-scale brain networks at the individual level, with important applications in neuroscience research and clinical setting1. However, reliable estimation of functional connectivity remains challenging, as network measures are sensitive to signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), spatial resolution, and the number of rs-fMRI runs included in analysis2. Although 3T MRI remains the clinical standard, ultra-high field 7T scanners offer substantially enhanced imaging capabilities. The main advantage of 7T lies in its superior SNR and Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR), which scales supra-linearly with the magnetic field strength. Furthermore, it has been suggested that 7T has the potential to improve precision and functional specificity of neural measurements rather than simply increasing signal amplitude by acquiring data with higher spatial resolution3. Therefore, ultra-high-field rs-fMRI has potential to enable more accurate and stable network estimation within shorter acquisition times. 

In the present study, we directly compare functional connectivity estimates of the motor network from the extensive sample of fMRI data acquired at 3T and 7T, in the same subject. The main objective was to determine to what degree 7T data differs from 3T data “intrinsically”, meaning that the differences are not due to not collecting “enough” data. For each magnetic field strength, we acquired eight rs-fMRI runs, allowing connectivity to be estimated using either half of the data (four runs) or the full dataset (eight runs). This design enables two complementary questions to be addressed: 1) whether motor network connectivity estimated at 7T is more robust and spatially specific than at 3T; 2) whether a reduced number of runs at 7T yields network estimates comparable to, or better than those obtained with longer acquisitions times at 3T. 

Methods

One healthy right-handed female participant was recruited to undergo MRI scanning at both 3T (Siemens Skyra) and 7T (Siemens Terra.X). Written informed consent was obtained in accordance with a protocol approved by our institution’s Institutional Review Board. Each scanning session consisted of a high-resolution anatomical T1-w MEMPRAGE [BAI1] scan acquired with 1.0 mm and 0.75 mm isotropic resolution for 3T and 7T, respectively. Eight runs of resting-state fMRI data were acquired using gradient-echo (GE) 2D EPI sequence with a multiband factor of 5, in-plane acceleration of 2 and anterior-to-posterior (AP) phase encoding, for both 3T and 7T. At 3T, images were acquired with 2.5 mm isotropic voxels, TR = 1000 ms, TE = 30 ms, flip angle = 64°, a matrix size of 90 × 90 × 67 (in-plane FOV = 221 × 221 mm²). At 7T, images were acquired with 1.6 mm isotropic voxels, TR = 921 ms, TE = 23 ms, flip angle = 55°, a matrix size of 130 × 130 × 80 (in-plane FOV = 208 × 208 mm²). Each resting-state run lasted around 7 minutes and consisted of 422 and 450 volumes for 3T and 7T, respectively. Additionally, at each session a pair of spin-echo (SE) 2D EPI runs matched to GE-EPI protocols, was acquired, with opposite polarities of the phase encoding gradient.

Data acquired at both 3T and 7T were processed using similar analysis pipelines. 7T anatomical images were first corrected for intensity inhomogeneity using Statistical Parametric Mapping (SPM) toolbox. FreeSurfer was used to generate subject-specific brain masks, and cortical and subcortical segmentations. The motor network seed was defined by extracting the precentral gyrus from the FreeSurfer segmentation. A refined region of interest was then manually defined around the hand knob region and constrained to the cortex by masking with the FreeSurfer brain segmentation to prevent inclusion of cerebrospinal fluid4. This approach for defining the motor cortex seed was kept identical for both the 3T and 7T datasets but the final seed size differed slightly between field strengths due to differences in spatial resolution of the data. Preprocessing of fMRI data included rigid-body motion correction followed by EPI distortion correction using SE-EPI data and reversed gradient method (topup, FSL).

Functional connectivity analyses were performed using the CONN toolbox5. Slice-timing correction was performed to correct for temporal offsets between slice groups acquired simultaneously within each repetition time. Artifact detection and scrubbing were used to reduce the influence of motion- and signal-related outliers. Functional images were also co-registered to the anatomical data and spatial smoothing with a 4-mm 3D Gaussian kernel was then used to enhance SNR while maintaining spatial specificity. Subsequently, a temporal denoising step was performed to restrict the signal to low-frequency fluctuations (0.008–0.09 Hz) characteristic of resting-state connectivity. Seed-based connectivity maps were computed voxel-wise by correlating BOLD time series from the predefined motor cortex seed with BOLD signals across the brain, followed by Fisher z-transformation of correlation coefficients for statistical analysis. To evaluate the impact of the amount of data on network estimation, the above preprocessing steps were conducted independently using four-run subsets and the full eight-run datasets for both 3T and 7T.

Results

Figure 1 summarizes the effects of magnetic field strength and the amount of data used in analysis on signal stability and motor network connectivity. Figure 1A presents whole-brain temporal SNR (tSNR) maps for the 3T and 7T datasets, shown for a representative rs-fMRI run. Both 7T and 3T tSNR had similar spatial distribution, with 7T appearing slightly more heterogenous, consistent with higher spatial resolution, consistent with reduced partial-volume effects and improved anatomical fidelity at higher spatial resolution. Figure 1B summarizes the mean tSNR in white and gray matter, averaged across all runs, for each magnetic field strength. Mean tSNR values were higher at 3T (gray matter: 53.19 ± 0.18; white matter: 51.92 ± 0.11) than at 7T (gray matter: 31.04 ± 0.19; white matter: 34.14 ± 0.06), likely reflecting the larger voxel size and higher per-voxel signal amplitude at 3T. The spatial resolution of the 7T/3T data was 1.6/2.5 mm isotropic, while TR was approximately matched at 1s. Together, these results confirm that ultra-high-field fMRI offers an advantage in terms of the spatiotemporal sampling of BOLD-fMRI signals.

Figure 1C presents seed-based motor cortex connectivity maps for the 3T and 7T datasets under both the four-run and eight-run analysis conditions. At 3T, motor network connectivity estimated from four resting-state runs was noticeably weaker, more spatially diffuse, and less well confined to canonical motor regions compared with estimates derived from eight runs, which showed greater coherence and anatomical consistency. In contrast, connectivity maps derived from 7T data revealed a robust and spatially specific motor network even in the four-run condition, closely resembling the connectivity patterns obtained using eight runs. Across both four- and eight-run results, 7T connectivity was more focal and consistently localized to canonical motor regions, including the precentral and postcentral gyri as well as homologous contralateral areas, with reduced spatial spread into adjacent non-motor cortex. Collectively, these results indicate that ultra-high-field fMRI not only strengthens motor network connectivity estimates but also enhances their spatial specificity, enabling accurate and precise network delineation with fewer resting-state acquisitions.

Conclusion

This work examined the combined effects of MRI field strength and the amount of functional data available on resting-state motor network connectivity estimation using a within-subject comparison of 3T and 7T fMRI. We found that motor network connectivity derived from 7T data was more robust, spatially specific, and anatomically constrained than connectivity estimated at 3T. By varying the number of resting-state runs while keeping the same acquisition and analysis procedures, ultra-high-field 7T fMRI demonstrated clear advantages in both signal stability and functional connectivity estimation, over 3T. Specifically, connectivity maps obtained from four runs at 7T closely resembled those derived from eight runs, whereas 3T connectivity estimates showed a stronger dependence on the number of rs-fMRI runs. These findings highlight the role of field strength in precision and stability quality of the estimated network.

The improved performance of 7T fMRI reflects its higher spatial resolution and greater sensitivity to gray-matter–localized BOLD signals, which together improve the precision and specificity of functional connectivity estimates. The ability to obtain reliable motor network connectivity with fewer runs has important practical implications. Shorter scan durations reduce subject fatigue and motion-related artifacts and improve tolerability factors that are particularly relevant for patient populations. Reduced acquisition time also lowers scanning costs and increases scanner availability, which improves the efficiency of high-resolution functional imaging protocols.

Future work will evaluate whether the reduced acquisition times and enhanced spatial specificity achieved at 7T for motor network connectivity generalize to other large-scale functional systems, including the default mode and frontoparietal networks. In addition, connectivity analyses will expand beyond seed-to-voxel approaches to include targeted ROI-to-ROI interactions, such as prefrontal and motor circuits that are commonly implicated in cognitive control and neuromodulation applications. Additional efforts will continue to refine acquisition and analysis strategies to fully exploit high-resolution 7T data, ensuring that gains in signal quality translate into meaningful improvements in network precision rather than redundant increases in acquisition duration.
