Introduction

Pooling structural MRI data across acquisition sites increases statistical power but also introduces scanner-specific biases that obscure biological variability and compromise cross-site analyses.1 Classical statistical approaches such as ComBat mitigate some of these effects at the feature level but are limited when full image consistency is required.2 Image-level harmonization methods based on U-Nets or GANs have therefore emerged, yet these architectures often struggle to preserve fine anatomy under domain shifts and may introduce artifacts or over-corrections.3 

Diffusion models have recently transformed generative modeling due to their training stability and high-fidelity synthesis, making them attractive for MRI harmonization. In particular, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)4 provide a principled framework for gradually removing noise to reconstruct data with strong structural realism. However, most 3D MRI applications rely on latent-space diffusion for efficiency, and the associated compression-decoding step can blur subtle neuroanatomical details that are critical for clinical downstream analyses. Recent advances in classifier-free guidance during the diffusion model inference have further improved controllable generation, with manifold-constrained variants such as CFG++ enabling targeted style manipulation while preserving underlying content.5 

Motivated by these limitations, we develop a full-resolution, patch-based 3D diffusion framework that disentangles anatomical structure from site-dependent style. Our method uses an explicit anatomical prior and site-conditioned denoising to achieve harmonization without sacrificing subject-specific detail, offering a principled alternative to latent diffusion and GAN-based domain translation. 

Methods

Our method harmonizes T1-weighted MRI by conditioning a 3D diffusion model on explicit anatomical priors and learned site embeddings. The full workflow is illustrated in Figure 1. 

Anatomical representation. To isolate structure from scanner-dependent contrast, each training volume is first perturbed with a global gamma transform. From this perturbed image, we compute a gradient-magnitude map capturing macrostructural geometry and a high-frequency residual obtained by subtracting a Gaussian-smoothed version of the image. Their weighted sum yields an anatomical map A. A lower-resolution context map Acoarse  is obtained via trilinear downsampling. These maps provide fine and global structural constraints and details that remain stable across acquisition sites. 
Patch construction. All scans undergo skull stripping, N4 bias correction, affine registration to 1 mm MNI space, cropping to 160×192×160 voxels, and median-based intensity normalization. From each volume, we extract paired patches of size 80×96×80, ensuring ≥50% overlap during inference. Each patch is concatenated as an input for the model with the corresponding anatomical patch A and context map Acoarse.  
Diffusion formulation. We adopt the DDPM framework, where the forward process progressively corrupts a clean patch x0 with variance schedule βt, and the reverse process estimates the noise ϵ from a noisy patch xt . The denoiser is a 3D U-Net with four resolution levels, residual GroupNorm–SiLU blocks, and multi-head attention in the two middle scales. Input channels are concatenated as [xt ,A, Acoarse ]. Timestep embeddings are encoded with a sinusoidal representation fed to a 2-layer MLP. The model predicts ϵθ(xt, t, c), where c includes anatomical and site-conditioning signals. Training minimizes the noise-prediction MSE with Adam (learning rate 10e−4, batch size 5). 
Site-style conditioning. Each acquisition site is assigned a learnable 512-dimensional embedding injected into cross-attention layers, enabling the network to modify contrast without altering structure. To preprare the use of CFG++ during inference, classifier-free dropout replaces the site-embedding with a null token in 15% of iterations. At inference, harmonization is performed by substituting the target site embedding. 
Guidance using CFG++. To control the strength of site adaptation, we use manifold-constrained classifier-free guidance (CFG++). We compute both unconditional and style-conditioned noise predictions using identical anatomical inputs. The final prediction is 

ϵ = ϵuncond +λ(ϵcond −ϵuncond),  

with λ∈[0,1] the guidance strength. Because both branches share the same anatomical maps, the guidance affects only style, preventing structural drift. 
Sampling and reconstruction. We use DDIM sampling with 50 steps for efficient deterministic generation. Each synthesized patch is multiplied by a separable 3D Hann window, and the final harmonized volume is reconstructed by weighted voxel-wise averaging across all overlapping patches. This eliminates seam artifacts and ensures smooth spatial continuity. 
Training data and augmentation. The model is trained on 1,000 T1-weighted scans from seven public cohorts, representing a total of 10 sites. To improve robustness across anatomical and scanner variability, we apply random affine perturbations, elastic deformations, and left–right flipping. Anatomical maps are recomputed after each augmentation to preserve consistency between the structural prior and input patch. 
Evaluation dataset. Quantitative evaluation uses the SRPBS traveling-subject dataset, in which the same 10 participants are scanned at 10 sites. This design enables site-effect isolation and a controlled assessment of the proposed harmonization method. 

Results

We evaluate the proposed diffusion-based harmonization method on the SRPBS traveling-subject dataset, which provides repeated T1-weighted scans of the same 10 individuals across 10 MRI sites. This configuration allows precise quantification of residual site effects and assessment of anatomical preservation. 

Similarity metrics across guidance strengths. We first analyze the behavior of classical classifier-free guidance and its manifold-constrained variant (CFG++). Across all guidance values, CFG++ yields higher and more stable SSIM than classical CFG (0.874±0.058 vs 0.871±0.059 respectively), confirming that constraining the guidance direction to the style subspace effectively prevents anatomical distortion. We therefore adopt CFG++ with λ=0.6 for all subsequent experiments. 
Tissue ratio comparisons. We assess the reduction of scanner-induced variability using SPM segmented gray matter (GM) and white matter (WM) tissue ratios. For each non-reference site, we compute paired t-tests against the KPM reference site and this for 3 states of the art methods (STGAN, HACA3 and IGUANe), the results are summarized in Figure 2. For GM, our diffusion method decreases the number of sites showing significant differences relative to the preprocessed baseline, whereas HACA3 and IGUANe introduce additional discrepancies. STGAN reduces GM differences most strongly but at the cost of lower overall subject-level preservation (see LMM analysis below). For WM, our method removes all statistically significant inter-site differences, matching HACA3 and outperforming STGAN, which introduces a new significant deviation at KUT. These results demonstrate that the diffusion model reduces scanner-driven bias without collapsing biologically meaningful variation. 
Linear mixed-effects models. To quantify residual site variance while accounting for repeated measures, we fit linear mixed models with site as a fixed effect and subject as a random effect. 
For GM, the diffusion method lowers marginal R2 from 0.729 (preprocessed) to 0.507, indicating reduced site-driven variability, while achieving a high conditional R2 (0.909), demonstrating preserved subject-specific variance. STGAN achieves a lower marginal R2 (0.359) but also shows a substantial drop in conditional R2 (0.771), suggesting loss of anatomical individuality. For WM, our method similarly reduces marginal R2 from 0.599 to 0.214 and retains a high conditional R2 (0.942). HACA3 achieves the lowest marginal value (0.127) but at the cost of less stable GM behavior. IGUANe offers moderate improvements but does not consistently outperform preprocessing. 

Conclusion

The present work introduces a patch-based, anatomy-guided 3D diffusion framework for multi-site T1-weighted MRI harmonization, designed to address two limitations of existing approaches: the difficulty of preserving fine neuroanatomical structure and the instability of generative models when exposed to heterogeneous multi-site data. By conditioning the denoising process on explicit anatomical priors and learnable site embeddings, and by constraining style manipulation through CFG++ guidance, our method enables accurate contrast harmonization while maintaining subject-specific morphology.  
The combined gradient and high-frequency anatomical representation proved critical for stabilizing the diffusion process. Unlike GAN-based models that sometimes alter cortical boundaries or suppress subtle tissue transitions, our conditioning strategy ensures that denoising operates within a structurally grounded space. This is reflected in the high conditional R2 values and the preservation of individual features across sites. The patch-wise processing scheme, together with smooth weighted fusion, further prevents the decoder-related blurring that characterizes latent diffusion approaches. 
Across similarity metrics, statistical tests, and mixed-effects modeling, our method consistently decreases scanner-driven variability. WM ratios show complete suppression of significant site differences, and GM ratios are reduced without inducing new artifacts. Although some baselines achieve stronger suppression for specific tissues (e.g., HACA3 for WM), they often trade off anatomical variability or introduce inconsistencies. Our diffusion model provides a more stable and globally balanced outcome. 
The comparison between classical classifier-free guidance and CFG++ demonstrates the advantage of restricting guidance to the site-style manifold. Whereas classical guidance can distort anatomical structures when guidance weights increase, CFG++ maintains high SSIM across all strengths and avoids over-sharpening or contrast drift. This property is essential for clinical or neuroimaging analyses, where small structural deviations can bias downstream measurements. 
Despite randomized gamma perturbations, some residual contrast cues may remain in the anatomical prior and mildly influence harmonization. Future work could explore contrast-invariant descriptors learned from self-supervised models. Moreover, while patch-based diffusion preserves resolution, it increases inference time. Acceleration strategies, e.g., fewer sampling steps, model distillation, or hybrid latent-patch variants, would facilitate deployment in large cohorts. Finally, our evaluation focused on anatomical consistency and tissue ratios; validating harmonization effects on downstream tasks such as morphometric prediction or connectivity analyses remains important. 
Our findings indicate that patch-level 3D diffusion with explicit anatomical priors and CFG++ guidance provides an effective and anatomically faithful solution for multi-site MRI harmonization. The method robustly reduces scanner-related variance while preserving inter-subject biological differences, and it avoids the structural degradation common in latent diffusion and GAN-based methods. These properties make the approach a promising tool for large-scale neuroimaging studies requiring reliable cross-site consistency without sacrificing structural fidelity.
