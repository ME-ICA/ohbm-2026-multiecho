Introduction

Tractography facilitates the reconstruction of the white matter pathways from diffusion MRI. Subsequent comparison of diffusion metrics requires anatomically consistent coordinates to compare bundles section-wise across subjects and hemispheres.[3,2,1] Classical manifold-based approaches can produce high-fidelity, locally euclidean coordinates but are computationally heavy, sensitive to registration errors, and must be recomputed for every subject and bundle. We introduce DeepBundleMAP, a transformer-based model that directly regresses joint-manifold coordinates from native-space tractography, collapsing the cost of establishing correspondence across subjects and hemispheres into a single forward pass.

Methods

Our dataset comprised of pre-parcellated bundles from 100 HCP subjects spanning 42 major white matter bundles.[5,4] Ground truth coordinates were derived once using the CoBundleMAP pipeline. [2,3] We applied label-consistent geometric augmentations in world space, including streamline flips with probability 0.5, rigid rotations (15-45 degrees about cardinal axes), global axis-aligned crops (35-75% keep range) with mask updates, and small isotropic coordinate noise. Each subject's bundle is centered and scaled into an [-1, 1] unit cube and partitioned into subtractograms of size 2000. Streamlines were downsampled to 15 vertices. Streamlines were then flattened, serving as input tokens to transformer model with 8 attention heads, 4 layers. Streamline tokens within each subtractogram serve as context for each other for the parametrization task. Training minimized a Huber loss between the predicted streamline-vertex parametrizations and the CoBundleMAP ground truth. Optimization was performed using AdamW with a cosine learning-rate scheduler.

Results

Despite occasional breaches of parametrization-monotinicity in our training labels, DeepBundleMAP learned smooth, consistent parametrizations for 42 major white matter bundles. Across tracts, the predicted coordinates showed substantially fewer monotonicity breaches (typically 40-60% reduction) and a right-shifted ECDF of monotonic streamline fractions toward 1.0. Median vertex-wise coordinate MAE was < 0.02 across tested rotations (0°-45°), indicating stability under pose changes. Predictions were stable under streamline flips and resilient to global crops that simulated incomplete field-of-view or lesion-truncated streamlines. DeepBundleMAP reduced per-bundle runtime by approx. 120 ×, compared to CoBundleMAP.

Conclusion

DeepBundleMAP reduces a multi-stage geometric correspondence pipeline into a single learned mapping, delivering anatomically plausible, monotonic along-the-tract coordinates with gains both in runtime and robustness. The model provides a practical foundation for scalable tractometry. Future work will validate across diverse cohorts such as clinical populations with large lesions.
