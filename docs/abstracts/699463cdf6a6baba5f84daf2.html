Introduction

Advanced biomarker studies increasingly require the integration of spatially resolved neuroimaging with high-dimensional fluid proteomics. Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) provides quantitative iron metrics with established histological validity [3], while NULISA enables attomolar-sensitivity detection of CNS inflammatory proteins [4]. However, no standardized workflow currently exists to align NULISA proteomic signatures with region-specific susceptibility estimates. We present a reproducible, fully automated pipeline designed for large-scale multimodal integration in studies of Huntington’s disease (HD) and related disorders.

Methods

Paired CSF and multi-echo GRE MRI were obtained from PREVENT-HD gene-expanded individuals. NULISAseq CNS and NIF panels were processed using the NULISA cloud-based analytical suite [4]. QSM reconstruction was performed using the QSMxT pipeline [1], comparing a deep-learning approach (NeXtQSM) and a classical Total Generalized Variation (TGV) solver.


We developed a Python-based bridge module that:
(1) performs automated subcortical segmentation using FastSurfer,

(2) resamples segmentations to susceptibility space with topology-preserving interpolation,

(3) applies one-voxel morphological erosion to mitigate partial-volume contamination, and

(4) extracts mean susceptibility and converts to iron concentration (µg/g) using validated calibration curves [3].

Automated quality-control (QC) heuristics detect reconstruction failures—defined by contrast-flattening, noise floor elevation, or physiologically implausible iron estimates—and dynamically reassign subjects to TGV reconstruction.

Results

The pipeline successfully automated the extraction of iron values and linked them to proteomic targets, see Figure 1 for a complete example. A key methodological insight was the observation that while deep-learning reconstruction (NeXtQSM) offers rapid processing, it produced non-physiological ("flat") contrast maps in a subset of cases. This finding underscores the necessity of the automated Quality Control (QC) steps implemented in our pipeline, identifying where classical reconstruction (TGV) is required to recover accurate iron susceptibility values. In subjects with valid reconstruction, we demonstrated proof-of-concept correlations between striatal iron load and inflammatory fluid biomarkers, validating the pipeline’s utility.

Conclusion

We introduce a scalable OHBM-aligned workflow for integrating ultrasensitive CSF proteomics with quantitative iron mapping. The modular QC-aware architecture ensures reconstruction reliability and provides a standardized method for multimodal biomarker discovery. This framework enables future studies to systematically evaluate mechanistic relationships between molecular inflammatory signatures and spatially resolved iron deposition.
