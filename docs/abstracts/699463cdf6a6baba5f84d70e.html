Introduction

Maternal adaptations during pregnancy may contribute to changes in a woman's brain, body and brain-body interactions. These maternal adaptations are suggested to be driven by physiological, hormonal, and structural adaptations to support maternal well-being and fetal development 1. These changes may also impact the arousal network in the brain, including brainstem nuclei critical to consciousness, which regulate wakefulness, attention, and sleep. Earlier research suggests that up to 76.3% of pregnant women experience sleep disturbances, including increased nighttime awakenings, reduced slow-wave sleep, and fragmented REM sleep 2 . The changes in maternal adaptations may alter the arousal system, as pregnancy-related neurobiological adaptations can alter the balance between wake-promoting and sleep-promoting mechanisms. However, the structural impact within the brainstem arousal nuclei during pregnancy remains largely unexplored. Therefore, this study uses the openly available longitudinal imaging dataset to investigate the volumetric changes in the brainstem arousal nuclei 1. 

Methods

Method:

Data: 

This study leverages publicly available longitudinal imaging datasets from a 38-year-old healthy first-time pregnant woman 1. The dataset includes 26 imaging sessions covering the pre-pregnancy phase, all three trimesters of pregnancy, and the postpartum period 1. 

Brainstem arousal nuclei segmentation and statistical analysis: 

Using the available structural MRI datasets, we segmented the brain stem arousal nuclei using the newly developed brainstem tool within the FreeSurfer 3. We used the volume of each arousal-related brainstem nucleus across sessions for the statistical analysis to investigate the volumetric changes. The generalised additive models examined the statistical comparison of volumetric changes in each brainstem arousal nuclei over the gestational timeline 1,4. Furthermore, we calculated the associations between volume measures and mood and lifestyle scores across pregnancy.

Results

The statistical analysis of the volumes of brainstem arousal nuclei revealed significant, non-linear (effective degrees of freedom = 4); specifically, the right midbrain reticular formation (mRt) volume initially increases before declining. In contrast, the pedunculotegmental nucleus (PTg), ventral tegmental area (VTA), and periaqueductal gray (PAG) show bilateral reductions.  (Figure 1). 

Behavioural association: 

A significant association was observed in the volume of brainstem nuclei involved in arousal to gestational hormone levels, depressive and fatigue symptoms, overall mood, and sleep quality. This may indicate an adaptive mechanism that supports maternal physiological and behavioural adjustments throughout pregnancy and postpartum.

Conclusion

These results show significant volume changes in mRT, PTg, PAG and VTA brainstem arousal nuclei during pregnancy and their associations with sleep quality, mood, depressive symptoms, fatigue, and hormonal levels. The findings suggest that these brainstem arousal nuclei may support the adaptive physiological, emotional, and brain-body communication changes essential for pregnancy, among other material adaptation processes.
