Introduction

Delirium is an acute neuropsychiatric syndrome characterized by fluctuating disturbances in attention, awareness, and cognition. Postoperative delirium (POD) is common in older patients after major surgery and is linked to prolonged hospitalization, increased morbidity, and long-term cognitive decline1,2. While white matter pathology, characterized by abnormalities in microstructural integrity or white matter hyperintensities (WMH), has been shown to predict POD, previous research primarily focused on global disconnectivity, which may obscure region-specific disruptions crucial for cognitive functions1,3. Here, we examined how WMH-related tract disconnection and microstructural integrity relate to POD to clarify vulnerabilities in specific white matter tracts. 

Methods

We used preoperative Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) and Fluid-Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR) data of 66 cognitively healthy older participants (mean ± SD age: 71 ± 7 years) enrolled in a prospective clinical trial undergoing elective spine surgery2. POD occurred in 16 patients (24 %). 

WMH were segmented from FLAIR images using FSL BIANCA4. Probabilistic tractography was performed on DTI data using FSL PROBTRACKX2. To estimate the WMH-related tract disconnection, individual WMH masks derived from FSL BIANCA were incorporated into tractography as regions of avoidance, such that streamlines were constrained to bypass lesioned tissue5. Tract disconnection was quantified as the proportion of streamlines lost due to WMH-related avoidance (0 = no disruption, 1 = complete disruption). Mean diffusivity (MD) and fractional anisotropy (FA) were calculated within the resulting WMH-constrained tracts. 

Analyses focused on major association and projection pathways that were robustly reconstructed using probabilistic tractography and represent large-scale white matter networks. Analyses were restricted to tracts affected by WMH in the study population, based on group-level lesion mapping. For interpretability, tracts were grouped according to their predominant cognitive and motor domain which they have been described to mediate. Frontal executive tracts included the frontal aslant tract, superior longitudinal fasciculus, forceps minor, and cingulum bundle, which support executive control and attentional processes. Language-related tracts comprised the arcuate fasciculus, inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, and uncinate fasciculus. Sensorimotor tracts included the corticospinal tract, forceps major, and inferior longitudinal fasciculus. 

Linear mixed models were used to assess the relationships between tract-specific white matter measures (tract disconnection, MD, FA) and POD. Each model included the interaction of tract region and POD to investigate region-specific associations. Each model was adjusted for potential cofounders such as age, sex, total WMH and intracranial volume. Subject-specific random intercepts were included to account for repeated measurements across tracts within individuals. Effects are reported with 95 % confidence intervals in square brackets. For further exploration, the predictive value of WMH-related tract disconnection for POD was assessed for each tract using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses, quantified by the area under the curve (AUC) with 95% confidence intervals derived from bootstrapping.

Results

Linear mixed models revealed that POD was associated with increased WMH-related tract disconnection. This association was tract-specific. The strongest effects were observed in executive function-related tracts (β = 0.6 [0.1, 0.9], p = 0.01), indicating larger disconnection in patients who develop POD (0.4 [0.3, 0.6]) compared to those who do not (0.2 [0.1, 0.2]). In contrast, tracts supporting language functions (β = 0.2 [-0.2, 0.6], p = 0.3) and sensorimotor functions (β = 0.3 [-0.1, 0.7], p = 0.2) showed no significant difference in disconnection between participants who developed POD (language: 0.3 [0.2, 0.4], sensorimotor: 0.5 [0.4, 0.6]) and those who did not POD (language: 0.2 [0.1, 0.2], sensorimotor: 0.3 [0.2, 0.3]). These group differences in tract-specific disconnection are visualized in Figure A, with tract-specific AUC values for POD prediction shown in Figure B. In contrast, microstructural measures (MD, FA) were not associated with POD. No significant POD*tract group interactions were observed for executive (MD: β = -0.5 [-1.1, 0.2], p = 0.2; FA: β = -0.1 [-0.5, 0.3], p = 0.6), language (MD: β = 0.3 [-0.4, 1.0], p = 0.4; FA: β = 0.1 [-0.2, 0.5], p = 0.5) or sensorimotor tracts (MD: β = 0.3 [-0.4, 1.0], p = 0.4; FA β = 0.1 [-0.3, 0.5], p = 0.6).

Conclusion

We used a newly described multimodal combination of probabilistic tractography that incorporates individual white matter lesions. Preoperative lesional disconnection, rather than tract-specific microstructure, was associated with the vulnerability to develop postoperative delirium, highlighting the importance of macrostructural network integrity in POD pathophysiology. Our results suggest that POD may be characterized by selective white matter disruption of higher-order association networks rather than global white matter microstructural abnormalities. This specific disconnection pattern defines a distinct network target for precision prevention and neuromodulation, supporting brain-health initiatives for safer surgery and better neurocognitive outcomes in older adults.
