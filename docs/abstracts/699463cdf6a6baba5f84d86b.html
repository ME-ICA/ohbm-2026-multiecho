Introduction

Current efforts to evaluate the viability of the cingulum bundle (CB) as a standalone neuromodulation target overlook the importance of inter-individual variability in local sulcal morphology. The paracingulate sulcus (PCS) is a common tertiary sulcus coursing dorsal and parallel to the cingulate sulcus and is linked to reorganization of local cytoarchitecture. Recent work has highlighted the importance of ipsilateral PCS morphology in shaping asymmetric electrophysiological response robustness following CB stimulation1. However, sample size precluded an in-depth evaluation of the role of interhemispheric distribution of PCS pattern as well as the regional specificity of directed connections. Here, we leverage stereo-electroencephalography and single-pulse electrical stimulation to examine the impact of bilateral PCS morphology in modulating response amplitude. 

Methods

Evoked potentials following SPES delivered to the white matter of the anterior cingulate from 137 patients with treatment-refractory epilepsy were evaluated from data collected as part of the multi-centric F-TRACT project2. Using a linear mixed-effects modeling approach, we included data from the dominant phase-charge window and modeled the log response amplitude for left and right hemispheres separately. We assembled per-ROI x hemisphere subsets and restricted modeling to subsets with consistent region-of-interest (ROI) coverage across PCS morphology levels. Predictors included PCS morphology (leftward asymmetry as reference) and age group. Structural information was integrated from the HCP-MMP atlas.

Results

We examined 12 left hemisphere and 9 right hemispheres ROIs for analysis (Median per-subset n was 269/234 stimulation-recording pairs and 18/16 patients). Across joint tests of PCS morphology, left hemisphere stimulation demonstrated multiple significant ROIs following FDR correction: mid-cingulate, insula, and anterior cingulate. The mid-cingulate demonstrated significantly reduced evoked response amplitude in the bilateral presence of PCS morphology relative to the leftward asymmetric PCS pattern (p = 0.0265). Following right hemisphere stimulation, the inferior frontal cortex demonstrated a significant omnibus effect of PCS morphology on response amplitude (p < 0.0001).

Conclusion

PCS morphology was found to relate to evoked response amplitude in select ROIs, with a hemisphere-specific pattern. These findings support that bilateral PCS morphology relates to modulated evoked responses in a region-specific manner. Our results further confirm the importance of stratifying across left and right hemispheres. Implications of these findings are relevant for characterizing a hemispheric specialization of the CB and for considering optimal CB electrode placement in stimulation-based interventions.
