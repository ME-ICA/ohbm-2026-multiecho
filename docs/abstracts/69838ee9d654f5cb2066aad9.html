Introduction

Human infancy is characterized by rapid and profound brain development. Understanding early brain development is crucial for revealing the foundations of cognitive and neurological function as well as investigating the onset of many neurodevelopmental disorders. However, progress in infant neuroimaging has been hindered by the lack of age-appropriate, high-fidelity templates and atlases. To address this gap and facilitate better usage of large-scale infant datasets, we developed a comprehensive age-specific, multi-contrast infant brain atlas resource. This resource includes volumetric templates at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months across 7 structural and diffusion contrasts, cortical surfaces aligned to each template, and detailed cerebral and cerebellar atlases of gray and white matter regions. Preliminary evaluations demonstrate markedly improved registration accuracy using age-specific, multimodal templates. The full resource will be distributed in a Docker environment for broad accessibility and reproducible analysis.

Methods

Data acquisition and processing: 

A total of 348 typically developing infants aged 0 to 24 months were recruited in this study, and 146 infants underwent brain MRI scans at a 3.0T Siemens Prisma MRI system. To construct age-specific templates, scans from 21 infants (2.5–4.5 months), 23 (5.5–7.5 months), 15 (9–15 months), and 17 toddlers (17–25 months) were used for the 3-, 6-, 12-, and 24-month templates, respectively. T1-weighted images (MPRAGE, TR/TE = 2400/2.24 ms, 0.8 mm isotropic) and T2-weighted images (SPACE, TR/TE = 3200/564 ms, 0.8 mm isotropic) were acquired. Diffusion MRI (dMRI) were collected using multi-band EPI (TR/TE = 4750/113 ms, b-values = 0, 1000, 2500 s/mm², 48 directions per shell, 1.2 mm isotropic). All images were preprocessed using the lab internal pipeline. 

Establishment of population-averaged and single-subject templates:

A seven-step procedure was used to create age-specific multi-contrast templates, generating both population-averaged and single-subject templates per age. Structural images were AC-PC aligned, co-registered with dMRI, and iteratively improved through affine transformations to produce the population-averaged-linear template. A representative single-subject template was then aligned to this linear template, followed by nonlinear (LDDMM) transformations to create the population-averaged nonlinear template. Transformations applied to T1w images were extended to dMRI fields for cross-modality consistency. All templates were rigidly registered to MNI-152 space, preserving the original brain size. 

Establishment of comprehensive cerebral and cerebellum atlases and cortical surfaces: 

This study provides a comprehensive brain analysis resource, including age-specific cerebral and cerebellar atlases and corresponding cortical surfaces for each developmental timepoint. The whole-brain atlases contain 124 regions, and the cerebellar atlases include 36 regions, with finely delineated gray and white matter structures that were meticulously annotated and refined by expert neuroanatomists to ensure anatomical accuracy. Cortical surfaces aligned to the volumetric templates were reconstructed using iBEAT and FastSurfer to generate inner, middle, outer, inflated, and spherical surfaces across four timepoints. 

Evaluation of the age-specific templates and atlases for automated labeling:

To evaluate inter-subject registration and automated labeling accuracy, 10 infants aged 1–6 months were randomly selected. For each subject, representative brain structures were manually delineated to provide ground-truth references. Registration was performed using single-channel (structural only) or dual-channel (structural + diffusion) approaches, combined with either age-specific or non-age-specific templates. Automated segmentation was achieved by inverse transformation of template atlases into native space using affine and LDDMM algorithms. The transformed labels were compared with manual delineations to assess anatomical correspondence. Segmentation performance was quantified using Dice ratio for reliability and L1 error for misclassification.

Results

We generated high-resolution, age-specific infant brain templates at 3, 6, 12, and 24 months that capture rapid structural maturation across early development. For each age, multi-contrast volumetric templates were constructed and paired with comprehensive cerebral and cerebellar atlases, including finely delineated gray matter, white matter, and deep nuclear subregions. These atlases provide detailed anatomical representations of brain structures, enabling precise characterization of early brain development. Corresponding cortical surface models were robustly derived from the volumetric templates and consistently aligned across ages, supporting downstream surface-based analyses of cortical organization and maturation. 

Quantitative evaluation demonstrated that age-specific, dual-channel registration substantially improved anatomical alignment compared with non-age-specific or single-channel approaches. Multi-modal templates, which utilize gray and white matter information jointly, resulted in significantly higher Dice similarity coefficients and lower L1 errors for major white matter and cortical structures, including the corpus callosum (cc) and cingulate gyrus (CG), in both hemispheres. These improvements were consistent across contrasts and registration stages, indicating enhanced robustness and anatomical fidelity when age-matched information and multi-tissue constraints were incorporated.

The age-specific atlases enabled coherent and anatomically consistent labeling of cerebellar structures across major developmental stages. Cerebellar gray matte, deep white matter, and vermal subregions were clearly delineated, with smooth boundaries and minimal discontinuities at four time points. Side-by-side visualization in sagittal, coronal, and axial planes revealed pronounced developmental changes of cerebellum during the first two years of life. Three-dimensional reconstructions further demonstrated consistent anatomical topology and well-defined regional boundaries across ages.

To assess practical utility, registration accuracy was evaluated in independently selected 1-month-old infants from the Baby Connectome Project dataset. Inverse transformation of the age-matched 3-month template and atlas produced automated cerebellar segmentations that closely followed native anatomy, capturing individual morphological variability. In contrast, use of an age-mismatched 12-month template resulted in less accurate regional correspondence. Quantitatively, volume estimates derived from the 3-month atlas were significantly more consistent with manual delineations than those obtained using the 12-month atlas, both for whole cerebellum and for individual lobular subdivisions. These results demonstrate that age-specific atlases substantially improve anatomical accuracy and quantitative reliability in infant brain analyses.

Conclusion

This work establishes a comprehensive, age-specific infant brain MRI resource designed to address the unique challenges of quantifying rapid neurodevelopment during the first two years of life. By integrating high-resolution, multimodal volumetric templates with co-registered cortical surfaces and finely labeled cerebral and cerebellar atlases, this resource provides a unified anatomical reference that substantially improves both spatial alignment and anatomical interpretability in infant neuroimaging studies.

A central contribution of this work is the demonstration that age-specific and dual-channel registration strategies markedly enhance anatomical correspondence relative to non-age-matched or single-channel approaches. Incorporating both gray and white matter information leverages complementary tissue contrasts, yielding more accurate alignment of major structures such as the corpus callosum and cingulate gyrus. These improvements are particularly important in infancy, where rapid changes in tissue contrast, morphology, and relative proportions often undermine the performance of conventional adult-derived or age-agnostic templates.

The cerebellum represents a major strength and novelty of this resource. Despite its critical role in motor, cognitive, and affective development, the infant cerebellum has remained underrepresented in existing neuroimaging atlases due to its small size, complex folding, and rapid postnatal maturation. The age-specific cerebellar atlases presented here provide detailed delineation of gray matter, deep white matter, and vermal subregions across multiple developmental stages. These atlases capture pronounced age-related morphological changes and enable consistent labeling for both qualitative interpretation and quantitative analysis.

In conclusion, this work provides a foundational, age-specific infant brain MRI resource that advances methodological rigor and anatomical precision in early-life neuroimaging. By enabling accurate volumetric, surface-based, and region-specific analyses of both cerebral and cerebellar development, this resource offers a robust platform for studying normative brain maturation, individual variability, and early markers of neurodevelopmental disorders. As large-scale infant datasets continue to expand, the availability of developmentally appropriate templates and atlases will be essential for translating neuroimaging data into biologically meaningful and clinically relevant insights. We anticipate that this publicly accessible resource will facilitate broader and more effective use of large-scale infant datasets, ultimately supporting the neuroscience community in improving our understanding of typical and atypical neurodevelopment.
