Introduction

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and autism are frequently comorbid, and both have been linked to subcortical and cerebellar volume differences. However, the extent to which these neuroanatomical associations are shared or distinct remains unclear. Here, we examined whether ADHD symptoms and autism-related traits, measured via the Short Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS; Constantino, 2002), show differential associations with regional brain volumes after accounting for shared variance between traits.

Methods

We utilized data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD; Volkow et al., 2018) Study, including regional brain volumes derived from T1-weighted MRI data (ASEG segmentation), ADHD symptom severity from the Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL; Achenbach & Rescorla, 2001), and parent-reported SRS scores, along with demographic data including age and sex. In total, we analyzed data from 11,267 subjects with high-quality T1 images. Left and right hemisphere regions were combined to form bilateral volumes. For each region, we fit linear mixed-effects models with regional volume as the dependent variable: volume ~ age + sex + ADHD + SRS + (1 | site/family) using lme4 package in R (Bates et al., 2015). ADHD and SRS scores were modeled as continuous predictors and standardized to enable comparison of effect sizes. To directly compare the magnitude of ADHD and SRS coefficients within each model, we used the linearHypothesis function from the car package in R (Fox et al., 2007), which performs a Wald chi-square test of the null hypothesis that the two coefficients are equal. False discovery rate correction was applied to account for multiple comparisons.

Results

Several structures showed significantly different associations with ADHD versus SRS symptoms after FDR correction (Table 1), including the hippocampus, ventral diencephalon, cerebral white matter, pallidum, caudate, thalamus, putamen, and amygdala. In all cases, the significant coefficient differences were driven by ADHD: ADHD symptoms were significantly associated with smaller volumes across all regions (all p < 0.001), whereas SRS associations were non-significant in every region (all p > 0.05). Findings remained consistent after additionally controlling for Euler number, a summary metric of cortical surface reconstruction quality that serves as a proxy for motion and image quality.

Conclusion

These findings are consistent with ENIGMA consortium meta-analyses (Hoogman et al, 2017) showing reduced subcortical gray matter volume in ADHD, and extend this pattern to dimensional ADHD traits in a large developmental sample. Importantly, these effects appear to be disorder-specific: autism-related traits (SRS) showed negligible associations with subcortical and cerebellar volumes after accounting for shared variance with ADHD.

Limitations
MRI and CBCL data were collected at baseline visits, whereas SRS scores were first collected at the Year 1 follow-up. This temporal mismatch may influence the interpretability of cross-sectional associations. We plan to extend these analyses to model longitudinal changes in brain structure and neurodevelopmental traits across ABCD waves
