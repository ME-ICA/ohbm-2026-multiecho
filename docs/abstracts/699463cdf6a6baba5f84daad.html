Introduction

The convergence of neuroscience and artificial intelligence has accelerated dramatically in recent years, creating unprecedented opportunities for mutual advancement. As these fields become increasingly intertwined, coordinated community efforts have become essential to navigate methodological challenges, establish best practices, and facilitate meaningful collaboration. Multiple NeuroAI initiatives have emerged globally, each bringing different emphases and expertise to this interdisciplinary space. The Organization for Human Brain Mapping (OHBM), as the leading international organization dedicated to neuroimaging research, occupies a unique position in this landscape.

The objective of this work is threefold: first, to systematically map the existing NeuroAI SIG landscape and understand the focus areas of established groups; second, to identify gaps and opportunities in the current ecosystem; and third, to propose a distinctive positioning for the OHBM NeuroAI SIG that complements existing initiatives while leveraging OHBM's unique strengths in neuroimaging methodology and open science.

Methods

Comparative Analysis of Existing NeuroAI SIGs

NORA NeuroAI SIG (Norway)

The Norwegian Artificial Intelligence Research Consortium (NORA) has established a NeuroAI Special Interest Group with a primary focus on brain-inspired AI development. This group operates at the intersection of brain sciences and AI, with a core mission of leveraging insights from neuroscience to drive the development of more energy-efficient and flexible AI systems. The NORA SIG takes a distinctly technology and engineering-driven orientation, emphasizing how understanding biological neural systems can inform the design of artificial neural architectures.

Key activities within the NORA NeuroAI SIG include the development of bio-inspired algorithms and exploration of neuromorphic computing approaches. The group's particular strength lies in fostering strong collaborations between engineering and neuroscience disciplines, creating a pipeline from fundamental neuroscience discoveries to practical AI implementations. This approach positions NORA as primarily concerned with the direction of knowledge transfer from neuroscience to AI, asking how the brain's computational principles can make artificial systems more efficient and capable.

Alan Turing Institute - NeuroAI Supra-Interest Group (UK)

The Alan Turing Institute hosts a Clinical AI Interest Group that includes a dedicated NeuroAI supra-interest group with a distinctly different emphasis. This group's primary focus centers on clinical AI applications in neuroscience, with a stated mission to "celebrate, connect and catalyse innovations in artificial intelligence applied to neuroscience." The orientation here is clearly toward clinical translation and practical application in healthcare settings.

The Turing NeuroAI group spans multiple domains including data science, clinical neurosciences, neurotechnology, regulation, and industry partnerships. Key activities involve developing AI tools for clinical neuroscience applications, navigating regulatory frameworks for medical AI, and facilitating industry-research collaborations. The group's particular strength lies in bridging the clinical-industry-research triangle, ensuring that AI innovations in neuroscience can successfully transition from research settings to clinical practice. This represents a primarily application-focused approach where AI techniques are applied to solve pressing problems in clinical neuroscience.

Comparative Summary

Comparing these two established NeuroAI SIGs reveals complementary but distinct approaches to the neuroscience-AI intersection. NORA emphasizes the engineering and computing dimensions, focusing on how neuroscience can inspire better AI systems with a primary knowledge flow from neuroscience to AI. The Turing group emphasizes clinical and applied dimensions, focusing on how AI can address challenges in clinical neurosciences with a primary knowledge flow from AI to clinical practice. NORA's core expertise lies in brain-inspired computing and algorithm development, while Turing's strength is in clinical neuroscience and healthcare implementation. These different orientations suggest that the NeuroAI space is large enough to support multiple complementary initiatives, each serving distinct but important roles in advancing the field.

Results

Proposed Positioning: OHBM NeuroAI SIG

The OHBM NeuroAI SIG is positioned as the methodological nexus for neuroimaging and artificial intelligence, focusing on advancing measurement science, interpretability, and bidirectional innovation. This strategy is built on four core pillars. First, Neuroimaging-AI Methods Development requires establishing methodological standards and best practices for applying advanced AI (like deep learning) to neuroimaging data. Key areas include AI-driven quality control, multimodal integration, and creating validation frameworks to ensure AI captures genuine brain signals. Second, Interpretability and Validation is critical and involves developing systematic approaches for AI model interpretability and establishing reproducibility standards. This pillar emphasizes biological plausibility, rigorous benchmarking against ground-truth data, and aligns perfectly with OHBM's tradition of prioritizing measurement validity. Third, Bidirectional Translation means facilitating mutual knowledge transfer: translating brain mapping insights into more capable AI architecture design principles (brain-inspired computing), and applying cutting-edge AI for novel neuroimaging analyses, explicitly bridging brain function and artificial systems. Finally, Open Science and Standards leverages OHBM's commitment to transparency by developing robust data sharing protocols, establishing widely adoptable methodological best practices, and creating accessible educational resources for the broader community.

Distinctive Value Proposition

The OHBM NeuroAI SIG fills a clear gap in the current ecosystem by focusing primarily on the methodological foundations of neuroimaging-AI integration, complementing initiatives that focus on engineering (NORA) or clinical applications (Turing Institute). Our key contributions stem from three unique strengths. First, our emphasis on Methodological Rigor ensures AI approaches meet high standards of validity and reliability through measurement science, preventing the capture of spurious patterns. Second, the Deep Neuroimaging Expertise of OHBM members provides unique insights into the specific complexities and biological constraints of neuroimaging data, ensuring AI applications are scientifically meaningful. Third, our Global Reach and Open Culture—including an emphasis on transparency and reproducibility from inception—promotes the generalizability of methodological advances. This complementary approach creates natural opportunities for collaboration, where OHBM sets the methodological standards that underpin and support both engineering innovations and clinical applications within the NeuroAI ecosystem.

Implementation Framework

The framework leverages OHBM infrastructure through several key activity streams. We will build community capacity via Educational Workshops and Tutorials that disseminate best practices through hands-on learning and accessible resources. We will foster robust science through Collaborative Projects, conducting multi-site validation studies and establishing shared datasets to benchmark methods and confirm reliability across diverse contexts. We will create forums via Annual Symposia and Working Groups to debate challenges, present advances, and produce concrete outputs (e.g., position papers, guidelines) on focused topics like foundation models and reproducibility. Finally, Publication Initiatives will disseminate these standards and method guidelines to raise the bar for rigor in the field. Success will be evaluated based on quantifiable metrics, including member engagement, the number of methodological publications, utilization of educational resources, and the establishment of productive collaborations for method validation.

Conclusion

The proposed OHBM NeuroAI SIG addresses a critical gap in the current NeuroAI ecosystem by focusing on methodological innovation and validation at the intersection of neuroimaging and artificial intelligence. While NORA emphasizes brain-inspired computing and the Turing Institute focuses on clinical applications, OHBM can make unique contributions through its neuroimaging measurement expertise, commitment to open science, and emphasis on rigorous, reproducible, and interpretable NeuroAI research. Rather than competing with existing initiatives, this positioning creates synergistic opportunities for collaboration and mutual advancement.

The convergence of neuroimaging and AI presents both tremendous opportunities and significant methodological challenges. Ensuring that this integration rests on sound methodological foundations is essential for realizing the potential of NeuroAI while avoiding pitfalls of overfitting, lack of generalizability, and misinterpretation. The OHBM community, with its deep expertise in measurement science and commitment to reproducibility, is ideally positioned to provide this foundation. We invite OHBM members to join in shaping this initiative, contributing their expertise to establish standards and best practices that will guide NeuroAI research for years to come.
