Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is characterized by a long-term progressive neurodegenerative process. Despite the fact that more than 80% of patients with PD are reported to develop dementia within 20 years of diagnosis, effective therapies for cognitive impairment remain scarce. In PD, the development of dementia is one of key determinants of mortality, underscoring the growing importance of interventions targeting cognitive function. Consequently, there is a critical need to develop prognostic biomarkers that can predict early cognitive decline, and recent integrative reviews have highlighted the clinical relevance of various biomarkers. 1

Two complementary biomarker modalities have recently attracted attention: blood-based markers—neurofilament light chain (NfL) and glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP)—and microstructural diffusion MRI indices, including free water (FW) and the intracellular volume fraction (ICVF). Based on previous reports, these biomarkers may be conceptualized along two complementary biological axes: a neuroinflammatory axis represented by GFAP and FW, and an axonal degenerative axis represented by NfL and ICVF. In blood, NfL—a marker of neuroaxonal injury—and GFAP—a marker of reactive astrogliosis—are reliably quantifiable using ultrasensitive assays and each relates to cognitive outcomes in PD. 2 In neuroimaging, diffusion MRI provides microstructural indices linked to inflammation and degeneration, with FW reflecting extracellular/interstitial water and ICVF indexing axonal integrity, and abnormalities in these measures being associated with cognitive outcomes in PD. 3,4

Although FW/GFAP and NfL/ICVF may capture complementary processes, their longitudinal and spatiotemporal coupling across cognitive stages in PD remains unclear. 5 To address these gaps, we investigated the spatiotemporal dynamics of multimodal plasma biomarkers and diffusion MRI metrics, and their relevance for early prediction of cognitive decline.

Methods

Participants (n = 99) who met the Movement Disorder Society (MDS) clinical diagnostic criteria for clinically established/probable PD were retrospectively identified from the single-center Juntendo PD Registry (Tokyo, Japan). The registry includes patients evaluated between January 2020 and April 2024. Participants were classified as Parkinson’s disease with cognitive impairment (PD-CI) if their Mini‐Mental State Examination (MMSE) scores were ≤ 26 at baseline or at the final evaluation, whereas those who maintained scores ≥ 27 at both points were classified as Parkinson’s disease with normal cognition (PD-NC). Clinical evaluations, diffusion MRI scans, and blood sampling were obtained at baseline and at one or more follow-up visits at 1, 2, or 3 years. To define longitudinal cohorts, participants who completed these assessments at baseline and at 1-year follow-up were included in the “1-year cohort,” and those who completed all three assessments at baseline and 2-year follow-up were included in the “2-year cohort.” Among the 99 participants at baseline, 84 met criteria for the 1-year cohort and 40 for the 2-year cohort.

A 3-T scanner (MAGNETOM Prisma; Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) with a 64‐channel head coil was used for all imaging. Multi-shell DWI was performed using two b values (700 and 2,000 s/mm2) along 40 and 80 isotropic diffusion gradients for each shell. Free water (FW) and intracellular volume fraction (ICVF) were extracted from the anterior limb of internal capsule (ALIC), corpus callosum (CC), and superior longitudinal fasciculus (SLF). Associations between baseline plasma biomarkers and changes in neuroimaging biomarkers, obtained from tract-wise analyses, were assessed using adjusted general linear models (GLMs) including age, sex, disease duration, LEDD, and intracranial volume as covariates. All continuous predictors and outcomes were standardized (centered and scaled by their standard deviation) prior to analysis to permit cross-model comparisons.

Voxel-wise whole-brain analyses of longitudinal diffusion MRI changes were performed using tract-based spatial statistics (TBSS), and associations with baseline plasma biomarkers were assessed using models adjusted for age, sex, disease duration, LEDD, and intracranial volume.

A machine-learning model based on gradient boosting was applied to predict 1-year cognitive decline (MMSE ≤ 26) using baseline plasma biomarkers, diffusion MRI metrics, and clinical scores including H&Y Scale and MMSE. An ablation study was performed using cross-validation to determine the contribution of each biomarker modality to prediction performance.

Results

In the 1-year cohort, baseline GFAP levels were positively correlated with changes in FW levels (ΔFW) within ALIC (All: β = 0.398, q = 0.016, PD-NC:  β = 0.450, q = 0.019), and within the SLF in PD-NC (β = 0.371, q = 0.032). No significant correlations were observed between baseline GFAP levels and ΔFW levels in CC. Baseline NfL levels in PD-CI were negatively correlated with ΔICVF in ALIC (β = −0.573, q = 0.029), CC (β = −0.615, q = 0.040), and SLF (β = −0.633, q = 0.029). No significant associations were observed between baseline NfL levels and ΔFW, or between baseline GFAP levels and ΔICVF. In the 2-year cohort, higher baseline NfL levels were significantly associated with ΔICVF decreases within the CC (All; β = −0.370, q = 0.048) and SLF (All; β = −0.389, q = 0.048). Baseline GFAP levels were associated with a ΔICVF decrease only in the SLF within PD-CI (β = −1.014, q = 0.039). Consistent with the findings in the 1-year cohort, there were no significant associations between baseline GFAP levels and ΔFW or between baseline NfL levels and ΔFW. Voxel-wise TBSS analyses confirmed patterns consistent with the tract-wise findings. (Figure)

 In the machine-learning analysis, feature importance was evaluated based on the magnitude of the decrease in the AUC of the ROC curve when each feature was excluded. For single-feature exclusion, removing FW caused the largest decline in AUC (AUC = 0.47); for paired exclusions, removing FW and GFAP produced the largest drop (AUC = 0.39).

Conclusion

Our multimodal longitudinal analysis demonstrates that, as cognitive function declined, the significant associations between plasma and imaging biomarkers shifted from neuroinflammatory to neurodegenerative markers in PD. This pattern was supported by tract-wise and voxel-wise analyses. Machine-learning analyses further identified FW and GFAP as the strongest predictors of 1-year cognitive decline. Taken together, these multimodal longitudinal findings provide a foundation for an integrated biomarker panel to identify individuals at high risk of dementia and to enable early therapeutic intervention in PD.
