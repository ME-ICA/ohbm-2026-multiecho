Introduction

The naturalistic paradigm offers high ecological validity for studying socio-emotional processing in children and adolescents. However, significant inter-subject variability and age-related differences in functional topology within large developmental cohorts complicate voxel-wise functional correspondence. This limitation hinders our ability to quantify how brain "state trajectories" evolve during dynamic narratives. Furthermore, existing linear methods are often limited in capturing the complex, non-linear state-space structures (e.g., loops, branches) inherent in natural viewing. To address these challenges, we propose an integrated analysis framework: "Functional Alignment—Manifold Representation—Dynamics Quantification." We first utilize the Shared Response Model (SRM) to learn a group-shared subspace, achieving functional alignment to mitigate individual heterogeneity. Subsequently, we apply T-PHATE non-linear manifold learning to construct low-dimensional neural trajectories. This framework aims to reveal individual differences and developmental principles of neural dynamics in children aged 6–18 years, providing an interpretable interface for multi-level stimulus encoding.

Methods

This study used a large pediatric naturalistic fMRI cohort (retention N=1,625; ages 6–18) after strict quality control. Participants viewed the silent animated film Bao (~430 s). Data were preprocessed (fMRIPrep) and denoised without global signal regression. To ensure cross-age comparability, we constructed a voxel-wise group mask based on tSNR > 30, yielding 370 qualifying parcels (Schaefer-400). Functional alignment was performed using the Robust Shared Response Model (RSRM) in a parcel-wise manner (k=20), projecting individual data into a shared functional space to improve the comparability of group trajectories. Low-dimensional manifolds were then learned from these SRM-aligned representations using T-PHATE (d=10, kNN=15, t=19) to preserve temporal structure. We analyzed: (i) whole-brain group trajectories aligned with event boundaries; (ii) subject-level trajectories to quantify developmental effects on individual dynamics; and (iii) network-level trajectories compared using ANOVA. Dynamic descriptors (e.g., speed, curvature, participation ratio) were extracted and tested against age using FDR-corrected linear models.

Results

1. Manifold Dynamics Validation: The T-PHATE 2D projection of the SRM-aligned group representation revealed a clear, non-linear state trajectory (Figure 1A). The instantaneous speed profile co-occurred with multiple narrative transitions, confirming that manifold dynamics are sensitive to state shifts in naturalistic narratives. 2. Developmental Trajectories: At the individual level, linear models revealed significant age-related evolution in neural dynamics (Figure 1B). Core metrics related to trajectory complexity and agility (mean speed, speed SD, mean curvature) increased with age, whereas the "trajectory participation ratio" decreased. This indicates that throughout development, neural state trajectories become faster, more curved, and structurally more efficient, reflecting enhanced flexibility in state transitions. This developmental shift suggests a neural mechanism for the increasingly rapid and nuanced processing of complex socio-emotional information. 3. Network Specificity: In the parcel-wise analysis, distinct manifold geometries emerged across regions (Figure 1C). Aggregating by networks revealed that somatomotor and attention-related networks exhibited significantly higher transition speeds compared to Default (DMN) or Control networks (e.g., SalVentAttn > Default, p < .01), highlighting heterogeneous dynamic timescales across functional systems.

Conclusion

We proposed and validated a naturalistic analysis framework for large developmental cohorts: tSNR-driven screening → parcel-wise RSRM alignment → T-PHATE non-linear manifold modeling → Trajectory dynamics quantification. Our results demonstrate that:

  1. Group-level manifold dynamics align temporally with narrative events, validating the physiological relevance of the method.
  2. Neural dynamics exhibit robust developmental trends: The age-related increase in trajectory speed and curvature, coupled with a decrease in participation ratio, reveals that brain state organization becomes increasingly complex and efficient during childhood and adolescence.
  3. Manifold dynamics capture systematic network-level differences, distinguishing rapid sensory-attentional processing from slower high-order integration. Overall, this framework provides quantifiable candidates for "dynamic biomarkers" to characterize the development of neural state organization, laying a methodological foundation for linking dynamic encoding with individual differences in future research.
