Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a severe psychiatric illness characterized by disruptions in brain functional networks detectable through resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI).1  Recent advances in large-scale rs-fMRI databases focusing on MDD,2 the dynamic graph-learning architecture, such as the Dynamically Adaptive Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional Network (DAST-GCN), have recently demonstrated superior performance in classifying MDD patients from controls by effectively capturing both temporal and spatial features.3,4  However, the utility of dynamic architectures for regression, specifically predicting disease severity tasks, remains largely unexplored.  This study investigated whether incorporating temporal dynamics into architecture improves depression severity prediction, with a particular focus on the impact of time series length.5

Methods

We used rs-fMRI data of 835 patients from the Rest-meta-MDD consortium to predict depressive severity measured by Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD) scores.  Data parcellated by the Dosenbach atlas were stratified by age into training (80%), validation (10%), and testing (10%) subsets.  The DAST-GCN architecture, initially designed for classification, was modified for HAMD score regression.  As shown in Figure 1, the architecture comprises three AST-GCN blocks that simultaneously learn both temporal weights and dynamic inter-node correlations, followed by global mean pooling to aggregate spatiotemporal features for prediction.  We evaluated models with varying temporal length (90 and 170 time points) and compared them against a static baseline that utilized correlation features but disabled temporal learning.  Models were trained by the Adam optimizer (learning rate = 2e-3, weight decay = 1e-5) for 100 epochs, with a batch size of 16 and a dropout rate of 0.5.  Performance was evaluated using RMSE and Pearson correlation (r) across 30 independent resamples, with statistical significance determined via repeated-measure ANOVA and post-hoc comparisons at a significance level of 0.05.

Results

The dynamic models yielded RMSEs of 7.24 ± 0.57 (mean ± std) and 7.26 ± 0.61 for 90 and 170 time points, respectively, representing minimal and non-significant improvements over the baseline RMSE of 7.28 ± 0.58.  Notably, models incorporating temporal dynamics resulted in a significant deterioration in prediction-observation correlations.   While the baseline models maintained a positive correlation (r = 0.17 ± 0.14), the dynamic models exhibited significantly lower correlations of -0.03 ± 0.23 and -0.03 ± 0.25 for 90 and 170 time points, respectively (t = 4.24, p < 0.001; t = 3.79, p = 0.001).

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that integrating temporal dynamics into GNNs marginally reduced RMSEs; however, it significantly deteriorated correlation with observed HAMD scores compared to static connectivity models.  These findings suggest that static functional connectivity provides a more robust representation for predicting depression severity, highlighting the importance of task-specific model selection in neuroimaging research.
