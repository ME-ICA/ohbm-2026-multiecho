Introduction

Ultra-high-field (7T) functional MRI offers unprecedented spatial resolution for studying human visual processing, but current standard head coils, such as the NOVA coil, present limitations for vision research. Traditional 7T fMRI setups often employ a cylindrical quadrature transmit (Tx) coil with a whole-head phased-array receiver. While this provides strong transmit efficiency at the centre of the head, the Tx field becomes substantially weaker toward the periphery, especially in occipital and inferotemporal cortex, which are critical regions for visual neuroscience. Another limitation is that cylindrical transmit coils positioned close to the head restrict space, making task presentation (mirrors, projection, goggles) difficult, particularly when high-performance head gradients further reduce bore diameter (≈36 cm). On the receive side, previous work has shown that dense arrays of small, closely fitting surface coils provide high SNR for superficial cortex without sacrificing deeper sensitivity 1.

To address these limitations, we introduce a new open-head 7T coil that provides a substantially larger visual field and improved signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in the early visual cortex, which are advantageous for enabling large-FOV visual stimulation and high-quality task presentation, both essential for ultra-high-resolution V1/V2/V3 mapping. Compared to the NOVA coil, the vision coil increases the effective reception field, enabling presentation of wide-field stimuli essential for retinotopic mapping, naturalistic visual paradigms, and perception of large-scale spatial layouts. Preliminary results demonstrate enhanced SNR and BOLD sensitivity in V1 - V3, highlighting its advantages for high-precision studies of cortical organisation and visual computations. This coil design provides valuable advancements for researchers requiring wide-field stimulation and high-quality 7T measurements in the visual system.

Methods

One 29-year-old male with corrected-to-normal vision participated in three fMRI experiments: two 7T sessions using Vision and Nova coils and one 3T session (School of Psychology and Neuroscience, University of Glasgow), to compare retinotopic mapping performance and functional signal quality across RF coil configurations. The 7T Vision coil consisted of an 8-channel transceiver array and a 32-channel receive array 2. The transceiver array comprised eight loop elements arranged in two rows on a semi-circular former, with geometric overlap and counter-wound inductors used for inter-element decoupling and incorporated a folded-end local RF shield to reduce radiation loss and improve power efficiency. At the 7T scanner, the Vision head coil provided a large field of view (~100 degrees) with 32 receive channels, whereas the Nova head coil offered a more limited field of view (~ 20 degrees) with the same number of channels (Figure 1a).

The retinotopic mapping stimulus consisted of a rotating wedge spanning 45° of visual angle that completed one full cycle every 60 s (Figure 1b). Four cycles were presented per run, resulting in a total scan duration of 4 minutes.

Each scanning session included a 4-minute retinotopic mapping run using a polar-angle stimulus and a 10-minute anatomical run. 7T functional images were acquired using identical EPI sequence parameters across sessions (TR = 2000 ms, TE = 26.3 ms, the field of view (FOV): 200 mm, flip angle: 77 °, 22 slices, spatial resolution: 0.8 x 0.8 x 0.8 mm). Spatial resolution was 0.8 mm isotropic for the two 7 T sessions. 3T EPI sequence parameters involve [echo time (TE): 30ms, repetition time (TR): 1500ms, the field of view (FOV): 200 mm, flip angle: 77 °, 22 slices, spatial resolution: 2 x 2 x 2 mm]. Imaging slabs were positioned to cover the full extent of the early visual cortex (V1 - V3) (Figure 1a).

Functional data were preprocessed and analysed using BrainVoyager. Retinotopic borders of visual areas V1, V2, and V3 were defined separately for each coil based on the recorded activity maps, to test mapping accuracy. To compare functional signal strength across coils, we selected a region of interest corresponding to the lower-right visual field representation (polar-angle sector 6, Figure 1b). A general linear model (GLM) contrast was computed for each 7T session to extract activation maps within this region. These maps were then compared across coil configurations to evaluate differences in sensitivity and activation amplitude. To further quantify signal quality, we used FSL to compute the temporal signal-to-noise ratio (tSNR) for each coil and compared tSNR values across sessions. To examine condition-specific signal changes, we further defined ROIs in V1 in both the central visual field and the far periphery, with similar sizes in each 7T session. From these ROIs, we extracted event-related averages over a 20-time-point window to present the BOLD response associated with the polar-6 stimulus to test the performance between coil types.

Results

Retinotopic mapping revealed marked differences between coil configurations. The Vision coil produced a larger and more clearly defined representation of the early visual cortex compared to the Nova coil. Notably, the delineation of the V1 border extended further toward the peripheral visual field with the Vision coil, whereas the Nova coil yielded a more restricted cortical coverage (Figure 2b).

To test activation strength obtained with the Vision and Nova coils, we performed a GLM contrast comparing the polar-6 condition against all other polar sections (both p = 0.049) in both coils’ data (Figure 2c). This contrast revealed that functional activation exhibited greater spatial extent and stronger responses when data were acquired with the Vision coil. In contrast, activation within early visual areas was reduced for the Nova coil, whereas the Vision coil showed broader and more robust activation, consistent with higher sensitivity and improved signal quality. Additionally, the tSNR map demonstrated a clear improvement in the occipital lobe when using the Vision coil (Figure 2a).

The event-related responses showed that the Vision coil produced higher BOLD activity than the Nova coil in both regions: fovea and far-periphery. In the central foveal ROI, the Vision coil reached a peak signal of 4.65 (SE = 0.50), compared with 1.71 (SE = 0.31) for the Nova coil. Similarly, in the far-periphery ROI, the Vision coil showed a higher peak response (4.57, SE = 0.06) than the Nova coil (3.35, SE = 0.56). The Vision coil indicated consistently stronger and more reliable BOLD responses than the Nova coil across both central and peripheral visual field regions.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the improvements in retinotopic mapping performance and functional signal quality in the early visual cortex when using the new 7T Vision coil. Compared to the Nova coil, the Vision coil’s larger field of view enabled more complete coverage of early visual areas with more precise and extensive delineation of early visual cortex boundaries, particularly toward the peripheral visual field. In addition, the Vision coil showed stronger, more spatially extensive functional activation, reflecting increased sensitivity in the early visual cortex. These benefits were further supported by higher tSNR values, indicating enhanced signal stability and overall data quality. Collectively, these findings highlight the advantages of the Vision coil’s broader coverage and improved sensitivity for high-resolution retinotopic mapping and functional imaging of the early visual cortex at ultra-high field strengths.
