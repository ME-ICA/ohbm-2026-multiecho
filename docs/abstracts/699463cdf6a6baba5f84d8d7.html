Introduction

Our overall goal is to implement image-guided transcranial ultrasound stimulation (TUS) technology as a method for causal manipulation in the human brain. TUS has high spatial resolution (up to ~1 mm) that can be shaped to the target structure, can modulate neural activity through its impact on mechanosensitive ion channels and thermal effects, and can target biopharmaceuticals into the brain. Here I describe our initial studies of noninvasive EEG- and MR-based FUS biomarkers. 

Methods

We developed a novel paradigm to test FUS parameters using the visual system as a testbed. FUS was targeted to the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN), a small, deeply seated thalamic nucleus that relays visual information from the retina to the primary visual cortex. The BrainBox 4-element CTX-500 transducer was used in a neuronavigated setup, with BabelBrain and ZTE pseudo-CT scans used for simulations.   We measured ssVEPs and contrast increment psychophysical thresholds in the contralateral (sonicated) and ipsilateral (non-sonicated) visual hemifields. We stimulated at different pulse repetition frequencies (PRFs), which differentially modulate mechanosensitive ion channels and may have differing effects on neural activity, and at two different sites: LGN and a control non-visual site. To measure FUS focality and targeting in situ with MR-ARFI, a single-shot spiral-out MRI sequence with correction for cardiorespiratory motion artifacts was implemented. 

Results

We hypothesized that LGN TUS would have a stronger effect than control site TUS, and that stronger effects would be observed in the sonicated (right) hemifield compared to the non-sonicated (left hemifield). A group analysis of VEP measurements in 14 participants showed that LGN-FUS at 5 Hz PRF reduced responses compared to control-site FUS on the sonicated hemifield more than the contralateral hemifield (p=0.02, Wilcoxon test), while 500 Hz PRF FUS in the same participants showed the opposite effect of increased VEP amplitude on the sonicated side (p=0.008). Perceptual sensitivity was reduced in the non-sonicated side in the LGN and control site TUS, suggestive of a non-specific effect. Follow-up perceptual and ssVEP studies with an auditory mask failed to show the expected pattern of effects: TUS effects were not restricted to LGN TUS site and contralateral hemifield. In addition, our optimized ARFI method generated ARFI maps that were largely immune to bulk and pulsatile brain motion with reduced scan time (80 sec per acquisition), using low intensity FUS that does not produce significant tissue heating. 

Conclusion

These experiments tentatively set an upper bound on the neuromodulation effects possible with neuronavigated focused ultrasound. Explanations for the lack of specific effects that we plan to evaluate include targeting accuracy, online vs. offline effects (our study purely evaluated online effects), and the specific circuit targeted (LGN contrast encoding may be robust to TUS compared to other circuit functions).
