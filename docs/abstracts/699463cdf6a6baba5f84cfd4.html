Introduction

Event-related potentials (ERPs) measured with scalp EEG provide high temporal resolution indices of cognitive processing, but their spatial organization is still typically defined in terms of individual electrodes or data-driven components.^1 Canonical components such as N170, N2pc, N400, P3, LRP, and ERN are often summarized as mean amplitudes at a handful of channels (for example, Pz, PO7), which simplifies analysis but treats topographies as secondary descriptors and hinders anatomical interpretation and multimodal integration. Spherical harmonics offer a smooth, multiscale basis on the sensor surface but are not tied to cortical geometry. In parallel, recent work in fMRI and MEG has shown that Laplace-Beltrami (LB) eigenmodes of the cortical surface provide geometry-aligned “harmonics” that capture large-scale activity patterns.^2,3

Here we ask whether forward-projected cortical LB eigenmodes can serve as a geometry-aligned sensor-space basis for ERPs. Using the ERP CORE dataset, which includes standardized paradigms for seven canonical ERP components, we evaluate how efficiently LB modes represent trial-averaged time-frequency (TF) patterns, how evoked TF energy is distributed across spatial scales, and how reliable and anatomically interpretable LB mode coefficients are, in comparison to spherical harmonics (SPH) and data-driven group PCA/ICA bases. 

Methods

We analyzed the publicly available ERP CORE dataset (39 participants), which comprises six paradigms designed to elicit seven canonical ERP components (MMN, N170, N2pc, N400, P3, LRP, ERN) with a 30-channel scalp montage and standardized acquisition and preprocessing.^4 EEG data were preprocessed using the published MNE–BIDS pipeline: 0.1–30 Hz band-pass finite impulse response filtering, 128 Hz downsampling, linked-mastoid or average referencing, ICA-based ocular artifact removal, and automatic trial rejection.^5 Epochs were defined using the ERP CORE event codes and conventional component windows, and TF power was computed per trial using Morlet wavelets (25 logarithmically spaced frequencies, 2–30 Hz) with baseline correction and conversion to decibel units.

Cortical LB eigenmodes were computed on the FreeSurfer fsaverage pial surface using a cotangent finite-element discretization of the Laplace-Beltrami operator and ordered by increasing eigenvalue (spatial scale). Using an MNE-derived three-layer BEM head model (fsaverage anatomy, standard conductivities, fixed-orientation dipoles), we forward-projected LB modes to the 30-channel group montage to obtain a sensor-space LB dictionary B(LB) (channels × modes). For comparison, we constructed (i) an SPH dictionary B(SPH) by evaluating real spherical harmonics at the same sensor positions (omitting the DC term so that all modes are non-constant), and (ii) group PCA and ICA bases B(PCA) and B(ICA) trained on trial-averaged TF topographies concatenated across subjects, conditions, and tasks, followed by orthonormalization (PCA) or mixing-matrix extraction (ICA).

For each subject, condition, and component window, trial-averaged TF maps were reshaped to Y (time × frequency samples × channels) and z-scored across channels at each time-frequency point to emphasize spatial pattern rather than absolute power. Span efficiency was quantified as mean R2(K): the average row-wise explained variance obtained by least-squares projection of Y onto the first K modes of each basis. Mode-wise TF energy spectra were computed by treating LB/SPH modes as spatial filters, summing squared coefficients over the component time-frequency region, normalizing within subject and basis, and examining cumulative energy and Top-m fractions (Top-3/5/10/15). Reliability was assessed via split-half ICC(3,1) of mode coefficients computed on half-averaged TF maps. Finally, we reconstructed group-level canonical ERP contrast topographies in the span of the first 10–15 modes and quantified correlations with the original contrast maps.

Results

Across paradigms, trial-averaged TF patterns were well represented by low-dimensional subspaces. In span tests, PCA provided the highest R²(K), as expected for a variance-maximizing basis, but LB consistently matched or slightly outperformed SPH for trial-averaged TF topographies, especially at small and moderate K (for example, at K = 10 LB explained approximately 0.55–0.70 of TF variance across N170, N2pc, P3, and ERN, compared with 0.51–0.69 for SPH, while PCA reached 0.73–0.84). ICA was substantially less efficient for the same K.

Mode-wise TF energy spectra revealed a much stronger difference between LB and SPH. For N170, N2pc, P3, ERN, and the supplementary components (N400, LRP, MMN), the first three LB modes captured roughly 35–45% of normalized TF energy, the first five modes about 50–55%, and the first ten modes approximately 65–70%. In contrast, SPH Top-3 values were typically 5–15%, Top-5 values 15–35%, and Top-10 values 30–55%, with Top-15 energy markedly lower for SPH (~0.53–0.74) than LB (~0.82–0.84). Cumulative energy indices showed that LB required on average about 11–12 modes to reach 70% of TF energy and about 22–23 modes to reach 90%, whereas SPH typically required 3–6 additional modes. Peak-latency contrast spectra showed nearly identical patterns, indicating that ERP condition differences are also dominated by a small set of low-order LB modes.

These low-order LB modes are anatomically interpretable because they are defined on the cortex and encode intrinsic cortical geometry. Mode 1 expresses a posterior–anterior gradient (occipital visual cortex to prefrontal cortex), mode 2 a dorsal–ventral gradient (superior motor/parietal to inferior temporal cortex), mode 3 emphasizes the central “body” of the hemisphere (somatomotor and adjacent parietal cortex) relative to frontal and occipital poles, and mode 5 highlights DMN-like posterior territories (precuneus, angular gyrus, lateral temporal cortex) opposite the sensorimotor strip and medial prefrontal cortex. Mode × frequency maps at peak-latency contrasts showed that these same low-order modes repeatedly carry component-specific TF power: for example, mode 1 is prominent across paradigms, with lower-frequency concentration for ERN and N400 (approximately 3–12 Hz), mid-range bands for P3 and MMN/N170, and higher-frequency concentration for N2pc; higher, still interpretable modes (such as mode 10, which partitions frontal and parietal cortex across the central sulcus) contribute weaker but task-dependent bands.

Split-half ICC analyses indicated that low- and mid-order LB modes had moderate to high cross-trial reliability (often ICC ≥ 0.7 up to mode index ~15), frequently exceeding corresponding SPH modes, particularly at higher indices and for components with strong spatial structure (for example, N2pc, LRP, ERN). Reconstructions of canonical ERP contrast topographies in the span of the first 15 modes yielded high correlations for all bases (ρ ≈ 0.95–1.0), with LB reconstructions preserving recognizable posterior N170, centro-parietal N400/P3, and fronto-central ERN patterns while remaining in a geometry-aligned basis. Compared with SPH, which provides a smooth but purely sensor-defined expansion, LB thus shows that ERP TF contrasts are well described by a small number of cortex-defined spatial axes (posterior–anterior, dorsal–ventral, center vs poles, DMN-like, fronto–parietal) rather than arbitrary electrode combinations. 

Conclusion

Forward-projected cortical LB eigenmodes provide a compact, geometry-aligned sensor-space basis for ERPs. Across seven ERP CORE components, most evoked TF power and condition differences are carried by a small set of low-order LB modes that also show good cross-trial reliability and reconstruct canonical ERP contrast topographies with high fidelity. Compared with SPH, LB achieves similar or better span efficiency while concentrating evoked energy into fewer modes that have direct cortical interpretations as posterior-anterior, dorsal-ventral, center-vs-poles, DMN-like, and fronto-parietal gradients for EEG-resolvable low-order modes; PCA, while most efficient, lacks such anatomical anchoring and a dataset-independent ordering.

In practical terms, LB coefficients offer a low-dimensional set of spatial features that summarize ERPs in terms of cortical gradients and networks rather than electrode labels or dataset-specific components. This representation is directly comparable across subjects, paradigms, and datasets, and can be used upstream of, or alongside, inverse models. Because LB modes already serve as a natural basis for fMRI and MEG, the present results provide a sensor-space foundation for geometry-aligned EEG–fMRI integration and for future work on frequency-resolved connectivity and longitudinal modeling in an LB coordinate system.
