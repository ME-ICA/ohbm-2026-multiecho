Introduction

Mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI) and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) are highly prevalent types of neurotrauma in military personnel and often present with overlapping symptoms, complicating clinical diagnosis. Conventional neuroimaging frequently fails to detect chronic or subtle injury patterns, particularly in veterans with repetitive exposure to neurotrauma. Magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) is a noninvasive, in vivo imaging technique that enables measurement of metabolite levels and longitudinal alterations in brain chemistry. Traditional one-dimensional (1D) MRS is limited by spectral overlap that hinders reliable quantification of key metabolites such as N-acetylaspartate (NAA) and N-acetylaspartylglutamate (NAAG) individually. Two-dimensional localized correlated spectroscopy (2D COSY) overcomes these limitations by resolving J-coupled resonances into distinct cross-peaks, enabling clearer measurement of neuronal, neuropeptide, and energetic markers. In this study, we compare metabolite profiles in military veterans with chronic neurotrauma including mTBI, PTSD, or combined neurotrauma to military subjects without neurotrauma. We hypothesized that 2D COSY would be more sensitive to metabolite alterations associated with neurotrauma due to greater confidence in the metabolite quantification compared to conventional 1D MRS.

Methods

Participants included 21 military veterans with neurotrauma (31.2 ± 9.9 years, all male) including mTBI, PTSD, or combined mTBI and PTSD, age-matched within 5 years to 21 healthy veterans without neurotrauma (26.8 ± 5.9 years, all male) from a previously reported cohort1. All procedures were approved by the Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant. 

MRI and MRS Protocol: All data were acquired on a Siemens Skyra 3T MRI system (Siemens AG, Erlangen, Germany) running VD13, equipped with a 32-channel head coil. This platform supports advanced spectroscopy protocols, including 2D COSY.  Routine structural MRI was first obtained for voxel placement, consisting of axial 3D-MPRAGE with reconstruction in sagittal and coronal planes at 2 mm slice resolution. A 3×3×3 cm³ voxel was positioned in the posterior cingulate gyrus (PCG) for both 2D and 1D spectroscopy acquisitions. The PCG was selected for its sensitivity to mTBI, based on established MRS studies reporting neurometabolic changes in the PCG following repetitive neurotrauma even when conventional MRI presents normally2.

2D COSY: Following established methods3, MR spectra were acquired with a repetition time of 1500 ms using 64 t₁ increments and 8 averages each, yielding a total acquisition time of 12.8 minutes. Data were collected with a vector size of 1024 points and an acquisition time of 512 ms, with spectral widths of 2000 Hz in F₂ and 1250 Hz in F₁, corresponding to a t₁ increment of 0.8ms. Localized shimming was performed using the vendor’s automated B0 field mapping procedure, followed by manual zero-order adjustments to achieve a water linewidth of 14 Hz or less. Water suppression enhanced through T1 effects (WET) was integrated into the COSY pulse sequence and applied after frequency correction. Spectra were quantified using the ProFit prior-knowledge fitting algorithm4. Figure 1A shows a sample spectrum acquired using 2D COSY. ProFit incorporated a GAMMA-simulated basis set including metabolite-specific chemical shifts, J-couplings, and linewidth constraints. Combined linear and nonlinear optimization was used to fit metabolite cross-peaks. For each participant, the t₁ alignment parameter was manually adjusted to ensure accurate indirect-dimension placement. Quality control procedures ensured that measurement uncertainty, as quantified by Cramér–Rao lower bounds, was lower than 20%. 

1D MRS: Single-voxel proton (1H) MRS data were acquired in the same voxel location as the COSY using conventional point-resolved spectroscopy (PRESS), with TE=30ms, TR=2s, bandwidth=1.2 kHz, 1024 complex data points, water saturation, and 128 averaged acquisitions. Unsuppressed water spectra with the same parameters and 16 averages were also acquired for scaling reference, resulting in a total scan time of 4.8 minutes. PRESS data were processed and analyzed using the Osprey toolbox5, which performs coil combination, frequency and phase correction, eddy-current correction, and linear-combination modeling with a vendor-specific basis set, as well as partial volume correction of grey and white matter and cerebrospinal fluid. Figure 1B shows a sample spectrum acquired using 1D MRS after processing and quantification using linear-combination modeling.

Statistical Analysis: Metabolite values from 2D COSY and 1D MRS were aggregated by diagnostic group. Cohort comparisons between military veterans with and without neurotrauma were performed using Welch’s tests (two-sample t-tests assuming unequal variance), with significance defined as p < 0.05. Within the neurotrauma cohort, individuals with mTBI only were statistically compared to individuals with PTSD or combined mTBI and PTSD. Cohen’s d was computed to measure effect size.

Results

A summary of study participant characteristics by cohort is provided in Table 1. 2D COSY enabled separate quantification of NAA and NAAG, revealing significantly elevated NAA in military veterans with neurotrauma compared with healthy veterans with a large effect size (t=-2.67, p = 0.01, Cohen’s d =-0.82). Furthermore, veterans with PTSD or mTBI and PTSD demonstrated significantly elevated NAA compared to veterans with mTBI only with a large effect size (t=-2.09, p=0.05, Cohen’s d=0.92). While NAAG and total NAA (combined signal of NAA+NAAG) were both elevated in veterans with neurotrauma on average, this difference was not statistically significant.

In contrast, 1D MRS could not reliably quantify NAA and NAAG separately, and total NAA showed no significant group differences. There were no significant differences in other metabolites detected by either 2D COSY or 1D MRS.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that 2D COSY provides enhanced sensitivity for detecting neurochemical and neurometabolic alterations associated with chronic neurotrauma in military veterans compared with conventional 1D MRS. By resolving J-coupled resonances, COSY enabled the separate quantification of NAA and NAAG, revealing significant elevations in NAA among veterans with neurotrauma that were not detectable using 1D MRS. These findings contrast with the decreases in NAA commonly reported in studies of acute mild traumatic brain injury in athletes and military populations and may instead reflect neurochemical alterations associated with chronic or repetitive neurotrauma, consistent with prior reports of elevated NAA in sub-concussive impacts in retired soccer players where the increase in NAA is thought to reflect increased neuroplasticity 2. Our results underscore the importance of improved spectral resolution for identifying metabolite changes that may reflect long-term neuronal and axonal adaptations following mTBI and PTSD.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these results. First, spectroscopy was performed in a single voxel located in the PCG, a region known to be sensitive to neurotrauma but not necessarily representative of the spatial heterogeneity of injury-related metabolic changes across the brain. Neurotrauma-related alterations likely involve distributed networks, and restricting analysis to one region may underestimate broader patterns of dysfunction. Second, the study cohort consisted exclusively of male participants, reflecting the demographics of the sampled population but limiting generalizability. Future studies incorporating female participants and more diverse samples are essential to characterize sex-specific and population-level variability in responses to mTBI and PTSD.

Despite these limitations, the present findings motivate several important directions for future work. Extending 2D COSY to multivoxel spectroscopic imaging would enable the generation of spatially resolved metabolic maps in whole brain, allowing investigation of regionally specific metabolite patterns and network-level alterations. In addition, larger cohorts will allow more robust differentiation between mTBI, PTSD, and combined injury groups, facilitating identification of condition-specific and shared neurometabolic signatures.

By improving confidence in metabolite quantification and enabling future spatial and computational extensions, 2D COSY represents a promising tool for advancing biomarker discovery and precision characterization of neurotrauma in military populations.
