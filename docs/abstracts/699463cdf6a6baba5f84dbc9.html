Introduction


Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is characterized by progressive cognitive decline associated with amyloid-beta (Aβ) deposition, tau neurofibrillary tangles, and neurodegeneration. While tau is strongly linked to neuronal loss and symptom severity, accumulating evidence indicates that Aβ may alter white matter (WM) microstructure even before tau becomes abnormal. Studying amyloid burden in tau-negative individuals provides a unique opportunity to characterize early, pre-tau WM vulnerability and to understand how centiloid-based Aβ accumulation relates to white matter pathway integrity before substantial tau pathology is present. Diffusion MRI (dMRI) tractometry offers high-resolution, along-tract quantification of WM microstructure and may reveal subtle abnormalities that precede symptomatic decline. In this study, we applied BUAN (Bundle Analytics) tractometry [1] to a large, multi-cohort dataset to evaluate how continuous amyloid centiloids relate to tract-specific microstructural patterns in tau-negative participants. Our goal was to test whether Aβ burden alone, absent tau positivity, exerts localized effects on WM pathways and to assess whether multi-cohort harmonized tractometry enhances sensitivity to early pathological signatures.

Methods

We analyzed dMRI data from 3,938 participants drawn from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative Phase 3 (ADNI3; n = 730) and the Health and Aging Brain Study – Healthy Diversity (HABS-HD; n = 3,208). We identified 1,334 tau-negative (Tau-) individuals, who formed the final study sample for examining amyloid-related white matter alterations.

In ADNI3, theTau- subset included 401 participants, comprising 293 Cognitively Normal (CN) participants, 95 diagnosed with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), and 13 diagnosed with Dementia, with 192M and 209F. In HABS-HD, the respective subset included 933 participants, consisting of 652 CN, 246 MCI, and 33 Dementia participants, with 368M and 565F. ADNI3 dMRI data were acquired using seven distinct protocols (GE36, GE54, P33, P36, S127, S31, S55) across GE, Siemens, and Philips scanners, whereas HABS-HD data were collected using a single protocol (SE204) on Siemens Skyra and Vida systems. 

Tau positivity (Tau+) was defined separately for ADNI3 and HABS-HD using each cohort’s validated tracer-specific criteria. In ADNI3, tau burden was assessed with 18F-flortaucipir (FTP), where tau+ corresponded to a medial temporal SUVR>1.23, computed relative to inferior cerebellar gray matter uptake [2]. In HABS-HD, tau was measured using 18F-PI-2620. Tau+ was defined using medial temporal composite SUVR>1.311, calculated as two standard deviations above the mean SUVR among amyloid-negative CN individuals [3]. 

Global amyloid burden was expressed in Centiloids for both datasets. In HABS-HD, florbetaben SUVR values (whole cerebellum reference) were converted to Centiloids. In ADNI3, global cortical uptake from florbetapir or florbetaben PET was similarly expressed in Centiloids following the standardized ADNI PET Core pipeline [4]. The Centiloid scale normalizes amyloid PET to a 0–100 reference range, enabling direct comparison across tracers and cohorts.

dMRI data preprocessing involved several steps: denoising raw dMRI data using local principal component analysis (PCA) for GE data, and Marchenko-Pastur PCA for Siemens and Philips data; Gibbs artifacts correction, and skull stripping. Eddy currents and motion were corrected with additional corrections for slice-to-volume and outlier detection, and bias field inhomogeneities correction. The diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) model was used to extract 4 microstructural measures: fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), axial diffusivity (AxD), and radial diffusivity (RD). 

Whole-brain local probabilistic tractography was performed using constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD or MSMT-CSD) based on single and multi-shell data. BUAN tractometry was applied to extract thirty-six major WM bundles from each subject using auto-calibrated RecoBundles. BUAN tractometry divides each WM bundle into 100 segments along the tract for statistical comparisons. Each point on every streamline is assigned to a segment based on the minimum Euclidean distance to the corresponding point on the model bundle centroid, ensuring consistent segment correspondence across subjects and cohorts in standard space. Microstructural metrics are then projected onto the subject’s native-space. For this analysis, we used a weighted-segment profiling approach, where points near the tract core were assigned higher weights than peripheral points, creating a weighted mean bundle profile per subject.

To address scanner and protocol variability, the resulting subject-level bundle profiles were harmonized using ComBat [5]. Linear Mixed Models (LMMs) were used on harmonized bundle profiles to examine the association between continuous amyloid centiloids and microstructural metrics in tau-negative individuals only. In each segment, LMM was modeled by including amyloid centiloids as the main predictor, with diagnostic status, age, and sex as fixed effects and scanner_protocol as a random term. Tract-specific false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied across 100 segments per bundle.

Results

In Tau- individuals, amyloid burden showed significant, spatially localized associations with WM microstructure, particularly in diffusivity-based measures (MD, RD, AxD). These effects suggest that subtle WM disruptions emerge before the onset of measurable tau pathology, indicating that amyloid burden alone may confer early vulnerability to microstructural alterations. This finding is consistent with emerging evidence that amyloid-related neuroinflammatory or myelin-related processes may precede overt tau deposition and clinical decline.

Across the tracts showing significant associations, the direction of microstructural change was highly tract- and metric-specific, reflecting the diverse biological processes captured by different diffusion metrics. FA was increased in the left inferior longitudinal fasciculus (ILF_L), potentially indicating compensatory reorganization or selective sparing of coherent fiber populations during early pathological change. AxD) showed a mixed pattern: decreases in the left middle longitudinal fasciculus (MdLF_L), forceps minor, and left arcuate fasciculus (AF_L) are suggestive of early axonal compromise, whereas increases in the left fornix (F_L) and right spinothalamic tract (STT_R) may reflect axonal swelling or altered axonal integrity. Radial diffusivity (RD) demonstrated predominantly increases, particularly in the bilateral spinothalamic tracts (STT_R, STT_L) and the right inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus (IFOF_R), patterns typically associated with myelin disruption. Conversely, RD decreases in ILF_L mirror the FA increases observed in the same tract, highlighting a unique microstructural signature that may reflect early, regionally specific processes or compensatory mechanisms. Mean diffusivity (MD), a general marker of extracellular water content and tissue degeneration, was elevated in multiple pathways, including the left optic radiation (OPT_L), right medial longitudinal fasciculus (MLF_R), right fornix (F_R), and left STT, while showing decreases in ILF_L and forceps minor. Note that in OPT_L and IFOF_R, significant associations appeared near the cortical endpoints, which may indicate early gray–white matter interface vulnerability to amyloid deposition. At the same time, these terminal regions are more susceptible to tractography noise and partial-volume effects, so biological and methodological factors may jointly contribute.

Taken together, these findings reveal heterogeneous yet biologically coherent patterns of early amyloid-related microstructural alteration across association, projection, and commissural pathways. The presence of these effects in tau-negative individuals underscores the possibility that amyloid burden contributes to subtle white matter vulnerability before the emergence of downstream tau pathology, offering insight into the earliest phases of ADprogression.

QQ-plot analyses demonstrated that combining ADNI3 and HABS-HD improved detection sensitivity compared with either cohort alone (Fig. 2). This suggests that harmonized multi-cohort tractometry enhances the statistical power needed to identify early, subtle microstructural abnormalities linked to amyloid accumulation.

Conclusion

Our findings show that amyloid burden is associated with tract-specific microstructural alterations in Tau- individuals, suggesting that Aβ may influence early loss of WM coherence well before the emergence of detectable tau pathology. These results support a model in which Aβ alone exerts subtle, spatially constrained effects on axons and myelin, potentially through neuroinflammatory, metabolic, or glial mechanisms that precede classical tau-driven neurodegeneration. BUAN tractometry allowed us to identify fine-grained, spatially localized effects that would likely be obscured in voxel-based or global summary approaches, highlighting the added sensitivity of along-tract analyses for detecting early-stage pathology.

Importantly, the distinct patterns across FA, MD, RD, and AxD demonstrate that amyloid-related changes are not uniform but instead vary by tract and diffusion metric, reflecting the complex biological processes that underlie early tissue vulnerability. These early WM signatures offer potential as sensitive preclinical biomarkers and may capture the earliest diverging trajectories between individuals at elevated risk for AD. 

Moreover, by harmonizing multi-site tractometry data across a broad range of acquisition protocols, we show that large-scale, multi-cohort dMRI datasets can be effectively integrated to improve detection power and generalizability. This approach supports a scalable framework for studying early disease progression in heterogeneous populations and strengthens the feasibility of using multisite dMRI harmonization to uncover subtle neurobiological signals. 

Future directions include examining tau-specific tract degeneration to characterize the transition from amyloid-driven to tau-driven structural decline, integrating longitudinal cognitive trajectories to link early WM disruptions with downstream clinical change, and incorporating APOE genotype to understand genetic modulation of white matter vulnerability. Additionally, future work will assess the robustness and generalizability of these findings by stratifying analyses by race, ethnicity, and socioeconomic factors (including education and income), where sample sizes permit, particularly within the HABS-HD cohort, which includes participants from diverse racial, ethnic, and socioeconomic backgrounds. Together, these efforts will help map biomarker-specific pathways and advance early detection strategies in AD.
