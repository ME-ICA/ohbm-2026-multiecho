Introduction

 Recent advances in machine learning-based EEG analysis provide tools for interpreting epilepsy patients' brain dynamics. However, developing frameworks that flexibly process diverse EEG recording types while maintaining analytical consistency remains challenging. This study introduces a generalizable deep learning framework for comprehensive EEG analysis in epilepsy, demonstrated through two tasks: predicting vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) therapeutic efficacy in drug-resistant patients (good, moderate, bad) and classifying genetic mutation types (SCN1A, SCN2A, STXBP1, SCN8A).

 The framework employs multi-stage feature extraction mapping EEG signals to probabilistic latent spaces. A VAE-based encoder generates low-dimensional latent embeddings that summarize biological variability and capture time-evolving neural structures. Parallel to VAE, a masked autoencoder implements self-supervised learning on connectivity features to learn stable functional network representations. Routine EEGs are state-segmented to quantify genotype-specific activation differences, providing a methodological foundation extensible to epilepsy research beyond demonstrated applications.

Methods

 The framework comprises four components: multimodal EEG preprocessing and feature extraction, latent space modeling, supervised/self-supervised predictive modeling, and state-based interpretive analysis. Analysis utilized two clinical EEG datasets from Severance Children's Epilepsy Center: long-term EEG (17–40 hours) from 91 VNS-treated drug-resistant epilepsy patients and 30-minute routine EEG from 75 pediatric patients classified by four genetic mutations. All data underwent standardized preprocessing (bandpass filtering, artifact rejection, ICA) and 10-second window segmentation.

 Functional connectivity was quantified across standard frequency bands using cross-spectral density (CSD). Dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) and discrete wavelet transform (DWT) are extracted for capturing spatiotemporal and time-frequency dynamics. These features were input to a VAE-based encoder yielding 64-dimensional latent embeddings. For VNS outcome prediction, VAE-derived latent representations informed random forest classification, progressing from high-divergence segment selection to embedding-sequence modeling. For genetic mutation classification, VAE encoder and masked autoencoder models were trained separately. The masked autoencoder used self-supervised reconstruction to learn stable functional representations, subsequently applied to supervised classification. Performance was evaluated across connectivity (CSD) and autoregressive (MVAR) input representations.

 Routine EEGs were segmented by annotation into eyes-closed, photic stimulation, hyperventilation, and post-activation rest states. State-specific spectral power and connectivity features underwent statistical analysis with multiple comparison correction. Independent VAEs identified potential mutation patterns in particular physiological states.

Results

 The framework showed promising VNS outcome prediction performance, achieving 81–85% subject-level classification accuracy (vs. 38.6% chance level) with random forest classifiers. Embedding-sequence modeling enhanced prediction stability and identified potentially relevant time intervals, suggesting capture of patient-specific pathological features from long-term clinical EEG. VAE and masked autoencoder models for genetic mutation classification achieved ~78% and 81.0% average accuracy, respectively (vs. 50.8% and 58.4% chance levels). Post-activation rest EEG yielded 79.7% VAE accuracy, robust across cross-validation. Masked autoencoder performance persisted across CSD and MVAR inputs, indicating generalizability in capturing latent network structures.

 Spectral power and connectivity analyses suggested genotype/stimulation state differences. During photic stimulation, SCN1A patients tended to show higher frontal/central power compared to SCN2A patients, consistent with extended hyperexcitability reports. State-specific VAE training indicated potential mutation patterns in particular brain states, hinting at possible genotype-EEG network associations. 

Conclusion

 These findings demonstrate framework versatility: VNS prediction suggests long-term EEG utility for therapeutic decision support, while genetic classification indicates routine EEG potential for precision diagnostics. The dual-pathway design provides complementary representation strategies. Clinical implications include optimized VNS screening and genotype-phenotype mapping. Cross-task consistency establishes a foundation for multi-center validation and future multimodal neuroimaging integration.
