Introduction

Neurodegeneration is an understudied area of research in Pakistan, especially limited by the cost of neuroimaging. Ultra-low-field (ULF) MRI (0.064T) shows promise in combination with image super-resolution methods as a low-cost tool for research and diagnostics for neurodegenerative disorders in LMICs. We assessed the feasibility of ULF MRI in patients with Parkinson's disease (PD), Parkinson's disease (PDD), Alzheimer's disease (AD), and healthy older adults (HC) as controls.

Methods

We scanned a total of 16 participants, including PD, PDD, AD, and HC, using axial T1 and FLAIR MRI sequences. Consenting and eligible patients were recruited through Neurology clinics from January to April 2025 at a tertiary care hospital in Pakistan. Images were assessed qualitatively by a neuroradiologist for image quality and Fazekas scoring for White Matter Hyperintensities (WMH). The MRI images were super-resolved using the publicly available Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based Freesurfer tool "SynthSR" to produce 3D images for the evaluation of medial temporal lobe atrophy (MTA). Images were also preprocessed using Freesurfer for segmentation and calculation of cortical thickness and subcortical gray matter using the recon-all-clinical pipeline and white matter hyperintensities (WMH) using WMH-Synthseg.

Results

We included 6 PD (mean age 59.3 ± 10.9 years), 2 PDD (mean age 69± 8.5 years), 2 AD (mean age 79 ± 4.24 years), and 6 HC (mean age 66.8 ± 7.27 years). We obtained a reasonable quality of images from the scanner. We were able to demonstrate disease-specific changes in dementia, such as hippocampal atrophy, widening of sulci, and thinning of gyri in the lobes, as well as the presence of deep white matter hyperintensities (WMH). Fazekas scores were calculated using axial FLAIR MRI. Further super-resolution to 1mm MPRAGE images helped calculate MTA scores from T1-weighted images. We calculated cortical thickness and subcortical gray matter, and WMH for all the images using automated software; however, group-wise comparisons were not possible due to a limited sample size. We found the imaging feasible for this age group, and the participants were able to complete the imaging procedure comfortably. There was a low response during patient recruitment, mainly due to advanced disease and unwillingness to participate in a research project voluntarily.

Conclusion

This study is the first to demonstrate the feasibility of ULF MRI in patients with neurodegenerative disorders and healthy older adults in a lower-middle-income country (LMIC). This is a pilot study; further high-powered studies are needed for group-wise comparisons.
