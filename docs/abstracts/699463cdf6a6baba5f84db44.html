Introduction

The medial temporal lobe (MTL) circuitry is crucial for episodic memory, however, it is vulnerable to aging and Alzheimer’s disease (AD). While structural 7 Tesla (7T) MRI imaging hints towards higher vulnerability of deeper cortical layers to aging1, particularly superficial layers of the entorhinal cortex, followed by the hippocampal subregions CA1 and subiculum, are susceptible to early tau pathology2. Facilitated by amyloid (A⁠β)-related hyperactivity and connectivity, MTL tau propagates along functional pathways to the neocortex, with the retrosplenial cortex (RSC) among the earliest affected regions3. Aging and AD might elicit distinct early differences in functional connectivity (FC), with aging being rather related to hypoconnectivity, particularly in posteromedial regions, and early AD pathology to hyperconnectivity, particularly of the perforant path4. While animal and human post mortem studies have provided insight into the vulnerability of cortical layers, ultra-high field 7T MTL imaging allows investigation of the human MTL circuitry at the mesoscale in vivo. It is, however, still unclear whether histology- and structure-based layer-specific vulnerabilities are reflected in functional alterations in humans. 

We hypothesized that i) lower resting‐state FC strength between MTL regions is associated with older age in individuals without AD pathology (A‐T‐), but higher FC strength involving CA3 might be observed, ii) higher resting‐state FC strength between MTL regions and the RSC is associated with higher RSC tau pathology burden in individuals with available tau-PET scans, and iii) superficial layers might show a spatial dissociation, with age-related differences in functional connectivity emerging rather in posteromedial regions and tau-related differences emerging in entorhinal connections and along the perforant path of the MTL circuitry.

Methods

We preregistered our study (https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/YSR2H) before analyzing cross-sectional 7T resting‐state BOLD fMRI (EPI, 0.9 mm isotropic), PET, and blood data from 76 cognitively unimpaired and physically healthy older adults. For details regarding the cohort, see Behrenbruch et al5. An overview of the data and timepoints of measurements is provided in Figure 1. We used SPM, CONN, and custom Matlab and Python scripts to process data. MTL ROIs were derived from T2 (TSE, 0.4*0.4*1 mm) ASHS segmentation (Figure 2a), RSC ROIs from T1 (MP2RAGE, 0.5 mm isotropic) FastSurfer segmentation (Figure 2b). Segmented ROIs received rims defining the inner and outer border. Using Laynii, we derived 20 equidistant layer compartments, with layers 2 - 9 binned to a superficial layer, and layers 12 - 19 binned to a deep layer (Figure 2c). Layers were derived in the hippocampal body for Cornu Ammonis (CA) 1, CA3, subiculum, as well as for Brodman area (BA) 35, entorhinal cortex (EC) and RSC, where layers passed visual inspection. ROI-to-ROI FC was assessed in CONN using the Network Based Statistics (NBS) approach. Results were thresholded using a connection-level threshold of p < 0.001 and an FDR-corrected network-level threshold of p < 0.05. Regarding quality control, standardized visual checks of motion, coregistration, alignment of ROI masks, and layer profiles were implemented. Further, participants were excluded if more than 20% of the fMRI volumes were detected as outliers using Artifact Detection Tools (ART). Voxels with mean intensity over time < 2 SD of the mean intensity (across all voxels) per ROI were removed. PET scanning was performed in a full dynamic setting (0–60 min post-injection on average 3 months after the 7T MRI scan). To generate parametric images expressed as distribution volume ratio (DVR), the Multilinear Reference Tissue Model 2 in Matlab was used. EDTA–blood plasma was taken to derive p-tau217, Aβ1-40, Aβ1-42, and glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) levels on average 6 months after 7T MRI.

We assessed FC in i) plasma A⁠β1-42/A⁠β1-40- and p‐tau217‐negative older adults (A‐T‐, N=60, 70±6 years, 26 female, 9 APOE4) to investigate associations with age and ii) all older adults with available [18F]PI-2620 tau PET (N=54, 71±6 years, 23 female, 13 APOE4) to investigate associations with RSC tau pathology. First, we analyzed associations of FC and age controlling for sex and education. Second, we analyzed associations of FC and RSC tau PET burden controlling for age, sex and education. Exploratively, we investigated interaction effects of age and RSC tau burden, respectively, with the genetic AD risk factor APOE4 and the inflammation-related blood-based biomarker GFAP as moderator in both models. 

Results

A-T- older adults had mean GFAP levels of 53.15 (SD=18.52) pg/ml. Lower FC within the MTL circuitry was associated with older age, specifically between the left deep EC layer and the right superficial BA35 layer as well as between the left deep EC layer and the right deep layer of the subiculum (network-level p = 0.046). There was no interaction effect of age or APOE4 status (all network-level p > 0.05). However, higher FC between the right PHC and the right superficial layer of the CA3 was associated with older age under higher GFAP levels (interaction effect; network-level p = 0.025).

Participants with available tau PET scans had a mean RSC DVR of 0.84 (SD=0.07), meaning that on average there was no increased signal, and mean GFAP levels of 57.12 (SD=22.52) pg/ml. Higher FC between the left superficial EC layer and the left superficial RSC layer, as well as between the right hippocampal tail and the left RSC (deep and superficial layer) was associated with higher RSC tau burden (network-level p = 0.006). There were no interaction effects with APOE4 status or GFAP levels (all network-level p > 0.05).

Conclusion

Our findings in A-T- older adults indicate that age primarily weakens functional neuronal pathways involving deeper layers of the MTL, specifically for regions involved in processing output from the hippocampus to the MTL. We did, however, not find involvement of superficial layers of posteromedial regions, as BA35 is part of the anterior MTL. Further, inflammation-related processes might elicit localized hyperconnectivity with age. More specifically, reactive astrogliosis assessed via GFAP might increasingly influence hippocampal networks involving CA3 with age, even in the absence of A⁠β and tau pathology. Future work should investigate the relation to cognition to evaluate potential detrimental or beneficial effects of aberrant FC with age.

Our findings in older adults with tau-PET scans indicate that higher FC is visible particularly for regions and layers affected very early by tau pathology, specifically for superficial layers of the EC. We did, however, not find strong evidence for particular involvement of the perforant path, which processes mainly input to the hippocampus. The observed higher FC between the hippocampal tail, which is dominated by CA1, and the RSC supports the idea that regions accumulating early tau show higher functional coupling. With CA1 being the main MTL region that is structurally and functionally connected to the RSC, our finding moreover aligns with the theory of more tau spread when there is higher FC strength between regions. Yet, longitudinal data is needed to strengthen this claim. 

Taken together, our work provides mesoscale insights from laminar fMRI that complement existing histological and structural evidence on MTL organization and its vulnerability to aging and early Alzheimer’s pathology. Our results using a previously unprecedented resolution in humans open up pathways for future research to advance our understanding of aging and AD.
