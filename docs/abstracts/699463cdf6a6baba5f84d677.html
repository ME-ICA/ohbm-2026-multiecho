Introduction

Generalized epilepsies are characterized by bilaterally synchronous seizure activity, commonly expressed as spike-and-wave discharges (SWDs) on EEG. While intrinsic bursting within thalamic relay and reticular neurons—arising from the interplay between T-type Ca²⁺ channels, GABAergic inhibition, and Na⁺-dependent excitability—is considered central to seizure generation, the network pathways through which these local dynamics give rise to large-scale cortical synchronization remain unclear. Competing models emphasize intrinsic thalamic resonance, direct thalamo-cortical drive, or cortico-cortical propagation. Disentangling these mechanisms requires a modeling framework that integrates biophysically grounded neural dynamics with network-level inference. Here, we apply thalamo-cortical Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM) to ictal EEG recorded during generalized seizures to infer the effective connectivity and channel-level mechanisms underlying generalized spike-and-wave activity.

Methods

We analyzed 19-channel EEG recordings from pediatric patients with generalized epilepsy exhibiting ictal spike-and-wave discharges. EEG data during seizures were epoched at the peaks of 3 Hz SWDs and treated as event-related potentials (ERPs), enabling precise temporal alignment of seizure-related activity. Spike-locked ERPs were averaged and characterized by their spatiotemporal propagation patterns. K-means clustering of ERP features identified distinct seizure propagation phenotypes, from which a representative cluster with spatially constrained dynamics was selected. Source reconstruction defined two cortical regions of interest (frontal and occipital). Effective connectivity was estimated using Dynamic Causal Modeling for ERPs with a thalamo-cortical neural mass model incorporating cortical populations, thalamic relay (RL), and thalamic reticular (TRN) populations. T-type Ca²⁺ and Na⁺ channel dynamics were explicitly modeled in thalamic populations. Eight competing network architectures were compared using Bayesian Model Comparison, followed by group-level Parametric Empirical Bayes analysis.

Results

Bayesian Model Comparison identified a model incorporating both direct thalamo-cortical pathways and intrinsic thalamo-thalamic (relay–reticular) connectivity as the winning architecture explaining ictal ERPs. Models lacking either thalamo-cortical or thalamo-thalamic pathways showed substantially lower evidence. Group-level Parametric Empirical Bayes analysis revealed seizure-related increases in thalamic relay excitability and weakened inhibitory control within the thalamic reticular nucleus, consistent with enhanced T-type Ca²⁺ channel–mediated bursting. Direct thalamo-cortical coupling significantly contributed to the transmission of thalamic oscillatory activity to cortical regions, supporting a causal role of the thalamus in driving large-scale synchronization. ERP-based clustering further revealed heterogeneity in seizure propagation patterns, indicating variability in the relative contribution of thalamo-cortical pathways across patients.

Conclusion

Our findings support a thalamus-centered network mechanism for generalized epilepsy in which intrinsic thalamo-thalamic resonance initiates pathological oscillations, and direct thalamo-cortical pathways actively drive their cortical expression. This framework integrates cellular-level channel dynamics with large-scale network propagation and reconciles intrinsic thalamic and thalamo-cortical models of generalized seizures. Thalamo-cortical DCM provides a principled approach for identifying the causal pathways underlying generalized spike-and-wave activity and offers a foundation for patient-specific mechanistic modeling of epilepsy.
