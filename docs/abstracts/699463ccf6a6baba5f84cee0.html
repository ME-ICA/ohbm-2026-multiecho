Introduction

Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) has proven its efficacy to treat resistant depression, typically targeting the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) but still with highly heterogeneous clinical response rates (≈35-50%). Individualized targeting strategies informed by functional connectivity (FC) between DLPFC and subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC) have increasingly been associated with higher antidepressant efficacy1. However, given the substantial impact of several parameters of fMRI processing on acquisition parameters, preprocessing, denoising choices and intra-individual variability sources on fMRI signal stability2, establishing the reliability of this promising biomarker is a critical prerequisite to develop robust and valid individualized TMS therapies. This study aims to assess the impact of various fMRI processing parameters on this FC pattern to identify those associated with an optimal reliability.  To address this question, we studied the reliability of sgACC-DLPFC FC under various conditions to identify factors that maximize it. 

Methods

We leveraged two test-retest resting-state fMRI cohorts : a subset of 132 participants (mean age 31.0 ± 21.6 years, 51% female) from the Nathan Kline Institute-Rockland Sample (NKI-RS) and 30 healthy adults (mean age 24.4 ± 2.4 years, 50% female) from the Hangzhou Normal University (HNU).3,4 NKI-RS is a large dataset of the general population built to capture brain-behavior relationship complexity across the lifespan. For this dataset, we focused on participants with complete test-retest data across two visits (baseline) and follow-up. Resting-state fMRI data were acquired on a Siemens Tim Trio 3T scanner with multiband sequences (TR=645 ms, 10min, voxel size = 3 × 3 × 3 mm³, matrix = 74 × 74 × 40, 30 slices; TR=1400 ms, 10min, voxel size = 2 × 2 × 2 mm³, matrix = 112 × 112 × 64, 30 slices). For both visits and TR modality, we restricted our analysis to the available preprocessed and quality-controlled derivatives released by the Reproducible Brain Chart (RBC) initiative team, which resulted in a final sample of 132 subjects participants.3 The HNU dataset included 30 healthy participants who completed ten resting-state fMRI sessions of 10 min each, with one scan every three days over one month. Data were acquired on a GE Discovery MR750 3T scanner using a standard single-band EPI sequence (TR=2000 ms, 10min, voxel size = 3.4 × 3.4 × 3.4 mm³, matrix = 64 × 64, 30 slices, flip angle = 90°).4 Preprocessing for the NKI-RS dataset was performed by the RBC team using C-PAC including motion, slice-time correction, normalization to MNI152 template and two nuisance regression strategies : the 36 parameter model (reg-36) with global signal regression (GSR) and aCompCor (reg-aCompCor).3 For HNU data, we used fMRIPrep 25.0.0 to apply matching preprocessing steps and implemented four denoising pipelines : reg-36, reg-36-FD, reg-aCompCor, reg-aCompCor-FD, including band-pass filtering (0.01-0.1 Hz). The reg-36 strategy included CSF, white matter, global signal, motion parameters, and their temporal derivatives and quadratic terms; its variant “reg-36-FD” additionally included framewise displacement.  The aCompCor models used six aCompCor components with the same derivative and quadratic expansions, with FD added in the variant reg-aCompCor-FD. To assess the impact of scan duration, we extracted all available volumes as well as the first half of the time series, yielding scans of 10minutes and 5minutes, respectively. Time series were extracted from DLPFC parcellated with Schaefer’s 1000 parcellation atlas (93 subregions) and sgACC defined with twelve methods. These methods included a predefined anatomical mask and spherical ROIs centered on different MNI coordinates spanning right, neutral and left sgACC selected for relevance in the literature on antidepressant effects of TMS. For seeds defined with a sphere, we tested different sizes increased by 2mm from 6 to 12mm. We tested different smoothing kernel sizes ranging from FWHM=0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 to 12mm. We computed the FC as a Pearson's correlation (Fisher’s z transformed) between sgACC mean time series and each DLPFC parcel mean time series. We quantified the reliability with the univariate intra-class correlation coefficient metric (ICC agreement type) estimated with a two way random linear mixed model (LMM) implemented in the ReX toolbox, controlling for age and sex effect and multivariate identification-based metrics including distance-based ICC (dbICC), image ICC (I2C2), discriminability and fingerprinting.5 Normality distribution of ICCs was assessed with the Shapiro-wilk test. For pairwise comparisons of univariate ICCs across multiple conditions, we applied paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and Bonferroni correction.  FDR-corrected permutation testing (x5000) was applied to compare metrics while preserving repeated-measures structure.  We sequentially evaluated the influence of TRs, scan durations, preprocessing/denoising choices, seed features and smoothing kernel sizes.

Results

Across datasets (HNU and NKI), DLPFC-sgACC FC reliability estimates ranged from poor to fair (ICC < 0.5). In the NKI dataset, univariate reliability was higher for TR=645 ms (mean ICC=0.44) than with a TR=1400ms (mean ICC=0.32; t = 8.72, p_corr = 1.7 × 10⁻¹²). Short-TR scans also showed higher multivariate reliability relative to longer TR scans (dbICC=0.48, discriminability=0.88, fingerprinting=0.33) and lower within-individual variation, with permutation testing confirming significant improvements for mean ICC (Δ = +0.114, q < 0.001), dbICC  (Δ = +0.130, q = 0.0013), discriminability (Δ = +0.097, q = 0.0005), and fingerprinting (all q < 0.01), while I2C2 remained unchanged. Longer scan duration showed improvement of reliability in both datasets, with NKI showing mean ICC=0.3 for 5min versus 0.44 for 10min scans (Wilcoxon signed-rank = 271, p_corr = 2.2 × 10⁻¹³) and HNU showing mean ICC = 0.19 for 5min versus 0.24 for 10min scans (Wilcoxon signed-rank = 243, p_corr = 6.3 × 10⁻¹⁴). Regarding the seed definition, in NKI dataset, the highest mean ICC was 0.44 obtained with an anatomical mask of sgACC while in HNU the highest mean ICC value was 0.28 obtained when defining neutral sgACC with an 8mm sphere around Jing et al.’s MNI coordinates [0,16,-10]1. In this dataset, reliability was significantly higher when using a 8mm sphere compared to other sphere sizes. Additionally, in the NKI dataset, we observed that a 12mm FWHM smoothing kernel consistently outperforms all smaller kernels (4-10 mm; all p_corr < 1e-6), whereas differences between intermediate sizes (4-10 mm) were not significant (p_corr > 0.7). No significant difference in the HNU dataset. Permutation testing for other conditions are still under analysis. These preliminary results indicate that TR, scan duration, seed definition and smoothing all impact univariate reliability of sgACC-DLPFC FC with TR and scan duration also influencing multivariate metrics.

Conclusion

In this study, we identified several key methodological factors that influence the sgACC-DLPFC FC reliability. In both datasets NKI and HNU, acquisitions with a shorter TR, longer scan durations and a higher FWHM size may significantly optimize reliability. Seed definition also affects reliability with anatomically defined sgACC performing best in NKI dataset and a 8mm spherical seed centered on previously published MNI coordinates. This work provides a quantification of sgACC-DLPFC FC reliability, supporting further investigations towards its use as a promising biomarker for TMS targeting in precision psychiatry.
