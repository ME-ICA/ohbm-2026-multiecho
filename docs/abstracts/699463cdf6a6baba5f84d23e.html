Introduction

Psychedelics are often grouped into classical agents—such as lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), and psilocybin—and non-classical agents including ketamine and nitrous oxide (N₂O). N₂O is an odorless, colorless gas widely used in medical and dental settings for its rapid-onset anesthetic, analgesic, and anxiolytic effects. At subanesthetic concentrations, N₂O primarily acts via NMDA receptor antagonism and produces phenomenological and large-scale neural effects that partially overlap with those of classical psychedelics acting through serotonin 5-HT2A receptor agonism.

Resting-state fMRI and EEG studies demonstrate that subanesthetic N₂O induces widespread reorganization of brain networks, including reduced within-network connectivity, increased between-network integration, and shifts toward less organized oscillatory states, particularly within sensory systems1,2. Some network changes persist beyond the acute phase3, suggesting durable modulation of sensory processing. Together, these findings indicate that N₂O produces a brain state distinct from natural wakefulness or general anesthesia by disrupting the balance between network segregation and integration, commonly quantified using modularity.

Visual perception is among the most consistently altered sensory modalities under psychedelics, including N₂O, yet most mechanistic insights derive from resting-state paradigms, limiting understanding of how altered network organization impacts stimulus processing.

To address this gap, we employed a structured visual task during fMRI to examine how subanesthetic N₂O modulates visual processing and associated brain connectivity in healthy individuals. We combined region-of-interest analyses of right anterior insula (rAI) connectivity, a key hub for salience detection and sensory gating4,5, with whole-brain modularity metrics and hierarchical clustering of task-dependent functional connectivity patterns to characterize condition-specific network reorganization.

Methods

Participants and Ethics

This study was approved by the University of Michigan Institutional Review Board (IRB# HUM00096321) and registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03435055). All participants provided written informed consent. Nineteen healthy adults were enrolled in a randomized cross-over design. Participants were excluded for excessive head motion (n=2) or incomplete imaging data (n=4), yielding a final sample of 13 participants (7 female; mean age ± SD: 24.9 ± 4.0 years).

Experimental Design and Data Acquisition

Participants completed two scanning sessions under placebo (medical air) and subanesthetic nitrous oxide (35% N₂O in oxygen), administered via MRI-compatible anesthesia equipment under anesthesiologist supervision. Physiological monitoring was continuous throughout scanning. Imaging was performed on a 3 T Philips Achieva scanner using T2*-weighted EPI (TR = 2000 ms, TE = 30 ms, 48 slices, 3 mm isotropic). Each functional run lasted 6 minutes. High-resolution anatomical images were acquired for registration.

The scanning protocol included resting-state, visual stimulation, pain, and combined tasks. The present analyses focus exclusively on the visual task, which consisted of alternating blocks of a flashing blue–yellow checkerboard (8 Hz) and fixation ( Figure 1). After each run, participants rated stimulus intensity and unpleasantness (0–10 scale).

fMRI Preprocessing and Quality Control

Structural and functional data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep (v20.2.7) in a BIDS-compliant workflow, including motion correction, normalization to MNI space, and confound estimation. Data were subsequently denoised in CONN using aCompCor, motion regressors, outlier detection, and linear detrending, with a high-pass filter of 0.008 Hz. Participants were excluded if motion parameters exceeded three interquartile ranges above the group median. 

Functional Connectivity Analysis

Task-dependent functional connectivity was estimated using generalized psychophysiological interaction (gPPI) models. Analyses were performed using 32 atlas-defined ROIs spanning eight canonical networks (DMN, salience, dorsal attention, sensorimotor, visual, frontoparietal, language, cerebellar). For each seed–target pair, gPPI models included the seed BOLD time series, task regressors, and their interaction. Resulting ROI-to-ROI connectivity matrices were Fisher z-transformed.

At the group level, seed-to-voxel analyses focused on the right anterior insula (MNI: 47, 14, 0), selected a priori for its role in salience detection and sensory gating. Voxel-wise results were thresholded at p < 0.001 (uncorrected), with cluster-level FWE correction at p < 0.05. ROI-to-ROI effects were assessed using CONN’s cluster-based FWE correction. Subjective ratings were included as covariates.

Network Modularity and Hierarchical Clustering

Systems-level organization was assessed by computing modularity from gPPI-derived ROI-to-ROI matrices using the Louvain algorithm with the negative_asym formulation, accommodating weighted networks with mixed positive and negative edges. Lower modularity values indicate reduced segregation and greater cross-network integration.

To characterize mesoscale organization, we applied agglomerative hierarchical clustering (average linkage) to subject-level connectivity matrices. Individual partitions (k = 4 clusters) were aggregated into condition-specific co-association matrices reflecting the proportion of subjects in which ROI pairs clustered together. Consensus clustering was performed on these matrices to derive condition-level community structure. Similarity between placebo and N₂O conditions was quantified by correlating the upper-triangular elements of the co-association matrices, providing a model-free measure of hierarchical reorganization.

Results

Subjective Ratings of the Visual Task

Visual stimulus-evoked intensity and unpleasantness ratings were obtained following each run under placebo and N₂O. Change scores (post–pre) showed no significant group-level effects for intensity (mean Δ = 0.54, SD = 2.47) or unpleasantness (mean Δ = –0.58, SD = 1.78; Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, p > 0.05 for both), consistent with non-normal distributions. Despite the absence of group effects, substantial inter-individual variability was observed, with participants reporting both increases and decreases in perceived intensity and unpleasantness under N₂O. 

gPPI Seed-to-Voxel Connectivity of the Right Anterior Insula

Using the rAI as an a priori seed, we examined N₂O-related changes in whole-brain task-modulated connectivity. No significant main effect of N₂O on rAI connectivity was observed. However, when subjective change scores were included as covariates, unpleasantness—but not intensity—was significantly associated with altered rAI connectivity (voxel threshold p < 0.001 uncorrected; cluster-level FWE p < 0.05). Two clusters showed significant negative associations (figure 2): one encompassing the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and bilateral paracingulate gyrus (peak MNI: 0, 30, 24; cluster size = 101 voxels; pFWE = 0.0048), and another in the superior division of the right lateral  occipital cortex (peak MNI: 50, –64, 26; cluster size = 79 voxels; pFWE = 0.0194).

gPPI ROI-to-ROI Connectivity and Network-Level Inference

At the ROI level, significant task-dependent connectivity changes were observed for the left lateral sensorimotor cortex (ROI-level mass = 95.43, pFWE = 0.024), involving distributed connections with regions in the frontoparietal, language, and salience networks, as well as the contralateral sensorimotor cortex.

At the network level, a significant cluster of modulated connections was identified (network-level mass = 832.57, pFWE = 0.001), spanning multiple large-scale networks including frontoparietal, sensorimotor, dorsal attention, default mode, language, and salience networks. Inference was restricted to the network-level cluster; only one individual edge—linking superior sensorimotor cortex and right posterior parietal cortex—survived edge-level correction (Figure 4).

Network Modularity

Network modularity (Q*) derived from gPPI connectivity matrices was significantly reduced following N₂O administration (mean ΔQ* = –0.15, SD = 0.14; t(12) = –3.87, p = 0.002), indicating decreased functional segregation and increased network integration during visual processing. This reduction was consistent across nearly all participants. Changes in modularity were not significantly correlated with changes in intensity or unpleasantness ratings.

Hierarchical Clustering and Mesoscale Organization

Hierarchical clustering of task-modulated connectivity revealed marked condition-dependent differences in mesoscale network organization. Under placebo, co-association matrices exhibited a structured, block-like organization with strong within-module consistency and well-defined boundaries between functional communities. In contrast, the N₂O condition showed a diffuse co-association pattern with reduced clustering stability and weakened module boundaries, indicating disruption of mesoscale community structure.

Consensus dendrograms further illustrated these effects: placebo networks displayed a stable hierarchical structure with four well-differentiated modules, whereas N₂O networks showed compressed branch lengths and earlier cluster merging, reflecting collapse of higher-order modular organization.

Quantitatively, similarity between placebo and N₂O co-association matrices was extremely low (Pearson r ≈ 0.037), confirming substantial reorganization of mesoscale community structure under N₂O.

Conclusion

In this study, we investigated how subanesthetic nitrous oxide (N₂O) alters visual processing by integrating regional, network-level, and mesoscale analyses of task-modulated functional connectivity.

 Although N₂O did not produce significant group-level changes in perceived visual intensity or unpleasantness, pronounced interindividual variability was observed. At the neural level, subjective unpleasantness—but not intensity—was associated with altered connectivity of therAI. Specifically, individuals reporting increased unpleasantness under N₂O showed reduced task-related coupling between the rAI and regions in the anterior cingulate/paracingulate cortex and right lateral occipital cortex (LOC). The rAI and ACC are central hubs of the salience network, supporting detection of behaviorally relevant stimuli and coordination between sensory and higher-order systems. Reduced rAI–ACC coupling may therefore reflect impaired salience integration, leading to altered affective appraisal of visual input. Reduced rAI–LOC coupling further suggests attenuated integration of salience signals with higher-order visual representations, potentially contributing to altered perceptual salience or unpleasantness.

 Beyond localized effects, N₂O induced widespread reorganization of task-related connectivity. ROI-to-ROI and network-level analyses revealed a redistribution of connectivity away from modality-specific sensorimotor coupling toward frontoparietal, salience, and language networks. Notably, early visual networks were not prominently involved, despite the visual nature of the task, suggesting that N₂O primarily affects higher-order integrative systems rather than early sensory encoding. This pattern is consistent with altered contextualization and affective appraisal of sensory input rather than changes in sensory encoding per se.

 Graph-theoretical analysis further demonstrated a significant reduction in modularity under N₂O, reflecting a shift toward greater global integration and reduced functional segregation. Lower modularity indicates less constrained communication between functional modules and is consistent with prior EEG evidence showing that N₂O disrupts network topology. Such integrated network states have been linked to altered perceptual and conscious experiences under psychedelic and dissociative compounds.

 Hierarchical clustering analyses provided convergent evidence at the mesoscale level. Under placebo, functional connectivity exhibited stable and well-defined community structure, with strong within-module consistency across participants. In contrast, N₂O produced a diffuse co-association pattern with weaker module boundaries and compressed hierarchical organization, indicating reduced stability of functional communities. The near-zero similarity between placebo and N₂O co-association matrices confirms substantial reorganization of mesoscale network structure. Together with reduced modularity, these findings suggest that N₂O weakens the cooperative relationships that sustain hierarchical brain organization, promoting a more fluid and less differentiated network configuration.

 Taken together, these multi-scale findings—from rAI-centered connectivity changes to reduced modularity and disrupted hierarchical structure—demonstrate that N₂O induces a hierarchical reconfiguration of brain connectivity during visual processing. This reorganization provides a mechanistic account of altered salience attribution and sensory experience under N₂O and supports the broader view that psychedelic compounds act by redistributing connectivity from segregated, unimodal systems toward transmodal, integrative networks.
