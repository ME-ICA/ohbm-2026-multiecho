Introduction

Spatial dynamics have recently become an important area of investigation for resting-state fMRI studies of human brain, recognizing that networks expand, shrink, and change their structure over time. Our previous work has identified significant disruptions in schizophrenia (SZ) in dynamic network features, such as reduced volumetric variability and altered complexity of networks [1,2,3]. In this study, we investigate spatially dynamic network interactions and their joint density distributions using dynamic ICA combined with 2D Histograms. We quantify the magnitude of transient deviation of network interactions from the average functional state using Root-Mean-Square Error (RMSE) and Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD). We hypothesize that SZ patients will exhibit altered spatial dynamic network interactions. 

Methods

rsfMRI data from a combined cohort of 508 subjects (315 CN, 193 SZ) were preprocessed and analyzed using a dynamic spatial pipeline (Figure 1). First, group spatial ICA identified 14 networks - VIS1 (visual primary), SUB (subcortical), MTR−1 (somatomotor primary), CER (cerebellar), ATN (attention - dorsal), FRNT (frontal), MTR−2 (somatomotor secondary), FPN−R (frontoparietal right), VIS2 (visual secondary), pDMN (default mode posterior), FPN−L (frontoparietal left), SN (salience), PARA (temporal), and aDMN (default mode - anterior) networks. Second, the spatial patterns of these networks were tracked across sliding windows (30 TRs) using multi-objective optimization ICA with reference (MOO-ICAR). Third, for each of the 91 network pairs, a 2D histogram of z-scored voxel-level activity was computed at each window. The overall group mean 2D histogram was subtracted from each window’s 2D histogram to create a 2D Difference Histogram. The scalar metrics RMSE and MAD were computed for each 2D Difference Histogram. Finally, subject-level mean RMSE and mean MAD across all windows were calculated and compared between CN and SZ groups using two-sample t-tests, corrected for multiple comparisons across network pairs using a 5% False Discovery Rate (FDR).

Results

The two-sample t-tests revealed significant group differences in both mean RMSE and mean MAD across numerous network pairs (Figure 2). The network pairs (VIS1-aDMN), (SUB-FPN-L), (CER-pDMN), (FRNT-VIS2), (MTR2-VIS2), (ATN-FPN-R), (MTR1-MTR2), (CER-ATN) show significant CN vs. SZ group differences for both mean RMSE and mean MAD, while several other network pairs show significance for mean RMSE. For all significant network pairs, the results were consistently positive (red), indicating that the CN group exhibited significantly higher mean RMSE and mean MAD values than the SZ group. This finding suggests that, across multiple inter-network interactions, the dynamic joint density distributions of SZ patients show a reduced magnitude of transient deviation from the overall average functional state compared to healthy controls. 

Conclusion

The reduced mean RMSE and mean MAD metrics observed in SZ patients imply a constrained dynamic repertoire and a lower spatial dynamic variability, where network interactions are less likely to express large magnitude deviations from the mean state. This finding aligns with reduced dynamical flexibility and the concept of maladaptive complexity previously reported in SZ spatial dynamics, suggesting the brain is "stuck" in a less adaptable, more rigid state. Our dynamic ICA approach combined with RMSE/MAD analysis of 2D histograms provides a global, voxel-agnostic measure that confirms diminished dynamic deviation as a fundamental feature of Schizophrenia psychopathology.
