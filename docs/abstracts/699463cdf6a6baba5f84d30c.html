Introduction

Insomnia disorder (ID) is often associated with cognitive impairment, with glymphatic system (GS) dysfunction proposed as a key link. The choroid plexus (CP), a central GS structure, presents a practical neuroimaging biomarker through its volume (CPV). However, the specific role of CPV in insomnia-related cognitive impairment has not been established. This study investigated CPV alterations in ID patients with and without cognitive impairment and its potential mediation between insomnia and cognition.

Methods

This prospective study included 111 ID patients—divided by MoCA scores into 44 with normal cognition (ID-NC) and 67 with impaired cognition (ID-IC)—and 63 healthy controls (HCs). All participants underwent MRI. CPV was manually segmented and normalized to intracranial volume (CPV/ICV). Group differences in CPV/ICV were analyzed using analysis of covariance, controlling for age, sex, and education. Partial correlation analyzes examined relationships between CPV/ICV and clinical measures within patient groups. Mediation analysis using Hayes' PROCESS macro investigated whether CPV/ICV mediated the relationship between insomnia severity and cognitive performance.

Results

Both ID-NC and ID-IC groups had significantly larger CPV/ICV than HCs. However, CPV/ICV correlated negatively with insomnia severity and delayed recall only in the ID-IC group. Critically, mediation analysis confirmed a significant indirect effect of insomnia severity on delayed recall through CPV/ICV in this group.

Conclusion

CPV enlargement is a common feature of ID, but its critical role as a mediator between severe insomnia and memory impairment is specific to ID-IC. This suggests a distinct "insomnia-glymphatic-cognition" pathway, identifying the CP as a potential intervention target.
