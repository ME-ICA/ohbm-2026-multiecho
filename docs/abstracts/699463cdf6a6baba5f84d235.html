Introduction

Patient motion during MRI introduces artefacts that degrade image quality, increase diagnostic uncertainty, and may require rescan or even exam rescheduling. 2D multi-slice Turbo/Fast Spin-Echo1 (TSE), a high-resolution clinical workhorse sequence, is particularly motion-sensitive, and no direct retrospective motion-correction method is routinely available. Motion-robust alternatives such as radial-type acquisitions are not widely adopted due to longer acquisition time and reduced image quality.2

We propose a novel deep learning-based approach able to estimate motion in the raw multi-coil k-space of motion-corrupted TSE brain scans. A motion-corrected image can then be reconstructed using non-uniform Fourier transform, leveraging the estimated head motion trajectory. By decoupling motion estimation from correction and implementing the latter with a non-deep learning approach, the risk of deep hallucinations is eliminated by design. Working in k-space confers several important advantages. It provides access to the temporal order of motion events – which  is not possible in image space, where motion is distributed everywhere. Not relying on image-domain features, reduces dependence on specific contrasts or image content seen during training and circumvents image-domain ambiguities where artefacts can be confounded with anatomical structures. 

Our deep learning model was trained on more than 200k 2D rigid-body motion simulations consistent with TSE physics, generated from 750 motion-free slices (25 patients, approximately 30 slices each). A validation set of 10k simulations was generated from three acquisitions unseen during training. Generalization was assessed on an in-vivo test set with volunteers performing prescribed head-motion choreographies following our published Choreography Controlled motion (ChoCo) protocol.3 

Preliminary results demonstrate good generalization capabilities of the approach in-vivo with significant reduction of blurring and ghosting artefacts.

Methods

Our deep learning model is constructed as a combination of convolutional layers capable of learning a robust and compressed representation followed by a series of transformer modules leveraging self-attention to extract motion information globally across the entire motion-corrupted k-space. Our model was trained in a supervised manner by providing a corrupted multi-coil k-space (magnitude and phase) as input and the corresponding simulated motion trajectory as the desired output. Three motion parameters (in-plane rotation angle and two translations) are learned for each phase encoding line and the model is trained slice-wise (each slice is treated independently).  The network learns from raw k-space data but we provide a selection of 4 complex channels in order to reduce the input dimensionality. The coil selection algorithm selects channels with high SNR and low inter-channel correlation to maximize spatial coverage. Leveraging the estimated motion trajectory, we apply a non-uniform fast Fourier transform to reconstruct a motion-corrected version of a given slice, after removing phase ramps in k-space to correct for translational motion. This approach avoids, by design, the risk of deep-learning hallucinations: if motion estimation fails, the worst case is amplification of motion-like artefacts rather than the creation of plausible but spurious anatomical structures. In-silico generalization is assessed on a validation set comprising simulated motion applied to three acquisitions not used for training. In-vivo generalization was evaluated on one volunteer not included in the training data, scanned while performing simple prescribed motion events inside the MRI scanner.

Results

On the in-silico validation set, preliminary results achieve an aggregated mean squared error (MSE) of 0.51 and mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.25 over the in-plane rotation (θ) and translational parameters (t_x and t_y). Individually, the MSE is 0.47 (θ), 0.49 (t_x), and 0.59 (t_y), with MAE of 0.25 (θ), 0.24 (t_x), and 0.28 (t_y). Moreover, we computed the average (across minibatches) Pearson correlation coefficient between the the predicted motion trajectory and ground truth and obtained: 0.92 (θ), 0.90 (t_x), and 0.89 (t_y).

On the in-vivo test set, we are observing qualitatively an important improvement in the image quality (reduction of blur and ghosting) as well as a quantitative improvement of ~2% in information gain (reduction of entropy from 1599 to 1570)  as shown in the Fig. 2 for a single motion event performed by a volunteer in the middle of the acquisition. The volunteer’s exact motion amplitude is unknown, but the model’s predictions are consistent with the prescribed motion. 

Conclusion

A novel deep learning approach is proposed to tackle the problem of motion estimation and correction directly in raw k-space of fast/turbo spin-echo acquisitions, leveraging the transformer architecture to analyse the input globally. The approach is free from deep hallucinations by design since image reconstruction is performed using classical non-deep learning algorithms. Good results were obtained both in-silico and in-vivo, demonstrating the generalization capability of the approach and its potential towards deployment in a clinical setting.
