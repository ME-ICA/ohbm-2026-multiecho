Introduction

Stroke is one of the most common cerebrovascular diseases in the world. Language and cognitive impairment after stroke constitute a common outcome, and its impact extends broadly to patients’ quality of life1,2. The symptom and severity are heterogeneous by individual. Thus, it is crucial to understand the underpinning core neural correlates before setting a rehabilitation plan that is based on the individual’s symptom. In this study, we identified the core cognitive-language factor from multidimensional assessments using a principal components and the neural correlates of language and cognitive function using voxel-based lesion symptom mapping (VLSM) analysis.

Methods

A total of one hundred and two patients with unilateral stroke enrolled in this study. All patients completed the Korean version of the Western Aphasia Battery (K-WAB) and a comprehensive cognitive function test, the computerized neuropsychological test (CNT). A principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted to summarize the multidimensional behavioral variables, behavioral scores of language and cognitive assessments. And a lesion-symptom mapping analyses of language function and cognitive factors affecting language impairment were conducted using the principal component loadings.

Every participant underwent MRI scanning. All MR images were acquired on a 3T Siemens Prisma scanner (Siemens, Erlangen, Germany). Lesion maps were identified by manual lesion segmentation. The lesion-symptom mapping was conducted using a Statistical NonParametric Mapping (SnPM; version 13.1.08, http://nisox.org/Software/SnPM13/) toolbox implemented in the MATLAB to run the non-parametric analysis.

Results

A PCA produced five independent principal components: language, cognitive control, visuospatial-executive function, attention, and verbal-semantic memory, respectively. Factor 4 showed significant correlations with auditory word recognition and sequential commands, indicating close relationship between attention in language comprehension. There was a significant correlation between factor 5 and word fluency score.

VLSM results showed significant neural correlates of three factors out of five. The results showed that a significant relationship with language factor was found in the left superior and middle temporal gyrus. The second factor related to cognitive control significantly related to the damage to the left caudate nucleus. Visuospatial executive functional deficits showed a significant association with right temporal and fusiform gyrus. 

Conclusion

To address the core cognitive and language component that affected by stroke and identify the neural correlates, we conducted a PCA using multidimensional behavioral scores and mapped the components to lesions. A PCA produced five independent factors related to cognitive and language function after stroke. We also found the close relationship between language and cognitive functions, especially attention and semantic/verbal memory. The neural correlates showed that core regions related to three language and cognitive factors that could affect language deficits.
