Introduction

Introduction: Whole-brain MR spectroscopic imaging (MRSI) enables the in vivo quantification of multiple neurochemicals, offering a direct window onto cerebral biochemistry beyond what is accessible with structural or functional MRI. Here, we extend our metabolic similarity matrix (MetSiMs), networks that quantify how similar pairs of brain regions are based on their multimetabolite profiles and capture large-scale biochemical organization, to test cross-cohort robustness, exploit higher-field metabolite sensitivity, and derive pathway-like representations of biochemical organization.

Methods

We analyzed two independent 3T cohorts acquired with the same whole-brain MRSI sequence at 5 mm isotropic resolution and constructed regional MetSiMs (^1) from multimetabolite profiles using NAA+NAAG, Cr+PCr (tCr), Cho, Ins, and Glx. We further recruited a third independent cohort of healthy participants aged 14–35 years scanned at 7T with a matched sequence at 3 mm isotropic resolution, enabling quantification of nine metabolites: Glu, Gln, NAA, NAAG, tCr, Cho, GSH, GABA, and Ins. We replicated the MetSiM pipeline in 7T data and assessed alignment with the 3T-derived organization when restricting 7T to the overlapping metabolite combination. We additionally constructed a GABA/Glu MetSiM and a glutamate-cycle MetSiM (Glu, Gln, GSH, NAAG). From these MetSiMs, we derived a principal metabolic path in each dataset from a PCA-to-tSNE dimensional reduction of the full MetSiM matrix, yielding a pathway-like trajectory capturing maximal metabolic variability. We re-derived this path in 7T using both the 3T metabolite subset and the full nine-metabolite set, with both submitted to topological analysis. Publicly available PET datasets were used for qualitative and quantitative comparisons to highlight convergent and complementary neurochemical information.

Results

 The 7T MetSiM recapitulated the major 3T biochemical organization. Alignment was strongest when the 7T data were restricted to the same metabolite combination used at 3T, supporting cross-field reproducibility of the MetSiM construct. The higher metabolite dimensionality at 7T enabled the derivation of a more finely resolved principal metabolic path, while re-derivation with the 3T subset produced a path consistent with 3T topology. Notably, the principal metabolic paths derived at both 3T and 7T followed a similar rostro–caudal trajectory, consistent with a brain-wide tendency to maximize spatially proximal metabolic similarity while preserving overall metabolic diversity. The GABA/Glu and glutamate-cycle MetSiMs revealed distinct but biologically interpretable similarity patterns. Comparisons with PET showed complementary sensitivity profiles, underscoring the added value of MRSI-derived markers, particularly for glutamatergic biology, which remains only partially addressed by current PET tracer landscapes.

Conclusion

Across independent datasets and field strengths, MetSiM reveals a robust and interpretable macro-organization of human brain biochemistry that replicates across cohorts and acquisition settings. The 7T cohort not only validates the 3T-derived topology when analyses are restricted to the same metabolite set, but also shows that expanded metabolite coverage and higher spatial resolution enable finer-grained delineation of pathway-like principal metabolic paths while preserving the core rostro–caudal axis of biochemical organization. Metabolite-specific MetSiMs (GABA/Glu and glutamate-cycle) further disentangle distinct, biologically meaningful similarity motifs linked to excitation–inhibition balance and glutamatergic metabolism, offering candidate network-level markers for probing alterations in cognition, ageing, and psychiatric disorders. Finally, comparisons with publicly available PET maps highlight convergent regional signatures as well as complementary sensitivity, underscoring MRSI’s value as a multi-target, radiation-free approach that can both benchmark against and extend beyond current PET tracer landscapes for in vivo mapping of neurochemical systems.
