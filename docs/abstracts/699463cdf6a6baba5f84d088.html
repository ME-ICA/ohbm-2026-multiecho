Introduction

Growing evidence suggests that neuroinflammation plays a pivotal role in the etiology of schizophrenia. Activated glial cells may contribute to dysregulated inflammatory cytokines,1 which could disrupt white matter microstructure, manifesting as reduced neurite density and increased extracellular free water.2 Identifying neuroinflammation and peripheral inflammatory abnormalities in the clinical high-risk population (CHR) before the onset of psychosis could provide critical insights into the pathological pathways. Although aberrant peripheral inflammatory markers and white matter alterations have been reported in CHR,3,4 their interplay remains unclear, particularly whether these associations differ between those who later develop psychosis (converters) and those who do not (non-converters). We investigated the relationship between inflammatory cytokines and white matter microstructure in CHR, aiming to elucidate the pathological mechanisms underlying the onset of psychosis.

Methods

Participants were recruited from Shanghai Mental Health Center, comprising first-time help-seeking outpatients meeting criteria for CHR as determined by the Structured Interview for Prodromal Syndromes (SIPS).5 Healthy controls (HCs) were matched through community/school recruitment via online advertisements and underwent identical baseline assessments. Both groups completed blood collection and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scanning. CHR participants were prospectively followed for one year to assess conversion to psychosis, defined by onset of full-threshold psychotic symptoms (operationalized as ≥6 on SIPS positive symptom items). Serum concentrations of eight cytokines (IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, IL-12, TNF-α, and IFN-γ) were assayed in duplicate using high-sensitivity ELISA kits. Values below the detection limit of the standard curve were excluded. Cytokine levels were log-transformed to normalize distributions. Multi-shell diffusion data were processed using the Neurite Orientation Dispersion and Density Imaging (NODDI) model, deriving neurite density index (NDI), free-water fraction (FWF), and orientation dispersion index (ODI). Region-of-interest (ROI) analyses were performed on 36 ICBM-DTI-81 white matter tracts, with mean values extracted for each tract. Associations between white matter metrics and cytokine levels were tested via multiple linear regression, adjusting for age, sex, and the age × sex interaction. Significance thresholds were set at FDR-corrected p<0.05.

Results

The study cohort comprised 295 CHR individuals and 79 demographically matched HCs. Longitudinal follow-up revealed that 55 CHR participants (18.6%) converted to psychosis within one year. No significant demographic differences were observed between CHR and HCs or between converters and non-converters (all p>0.05). Distinct correlation patterns emerged between inflammatory markers and white matter microstructure in CHR converters versus CHR non-converters. CHR converters demonstrated a significant positive association between global ODI and IL-8 levels (t=2.798, FDR-corrected p=0.037; Figure 1A, D). CHR non-converters demonstrated a significant negative association between global NDI and IL-1β levels (t=−2.606, FDR-corrected p=0.049; Figure 1B, D), and a trend-level association between global ODI and IL-8 levels (t=2.207, FDR-corrected p=0.142; Figure 1B, E). No significant cytokine-white matter relationships were observed in HCs (Figure 1C). ROI analyses further suggested that mean NDI in the left inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus (IFOF) and right uncinate fasciculus was negatively associated with IL-1β levels in CHR non-converters (Figure 1F), whereas mean ODI in the left superior fronto-occipital fasciculus, fornix, left anterior corona radiata, and genu of corpus callosum were associated with IL-8 levels in CHR converters (Figure 1G). The left IFOF emerged as a potentially critical white matter tract, with mediation analysis revealing that the IL-1β-mediated association between NDI reduction and transition outcome (indirect effect =−2.9175, 95% CI [−7.0049, −0.0319]).

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate a divergence in the interaction between inflammation and white matter microstructure between CHR converters and non-converters. Specifically, we identified that CHR converters exhibited IL-8–associated increase in ODI, suggesting that pro-inflammatory processes may drive microstructural disorganization during psychotic transition, whereas CHR non-converters showed IL-1β–related reduction in NDI, potentially representing a protective neuroadaptive response and impeding the onset of psychosis.
