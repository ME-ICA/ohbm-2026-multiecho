Introduction

Early childhood (0–3 years) is a critical period marked by rapid reorganization of large-scale brain functional networks. Prior findings, derived mainly from Western populations, show that brain topology transitions from local segregation toward global integration, converging on a "small-world" archetecture that balances local processing with long-range communication efficiency to support adaptive behavior. Such maturation during this sensitive period may be shaped by both genetic programming and experiential plasticity. ^1

However, these normative findings come almost exclusively from Western populations, limiting their generalizability to non-Western populations. It is therefore essential to identify both universal and population-specific patterns in early functional brain network development, as well as to investigate how population-specific factors interact with basic demographic variables such as sex—a known risk factor for many neurodevelopmental disorders. To address these gaps, we directly compared infant brain functional network topology between the Chinese Baby Connectome Project (CBCP) and the U.S. Baby Connectome Project (BCP). ^2, 3 We examined whether developmental trajectories of functional network integration, local specialization, and communication efficiency differ by population and sex, providing the first characterization of population-specific versus universal principles governing early brain network maturation.

Methods

This study included 402 longitudinal resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) scans from 239 infants and toddlers (BCP: n = 135, 77 males, mean age = 13.52 ± 19.19 months old; CBCP: n = 104, 65 males, mean age = 14.00 ± 10.90 months old). Data were preprocessed using infant-specific pipelines in native spaces,^4 including motion correction, distortion correction, co-registration to anatomical MRI, ICA-based denoising, and volume censoring (framewise displacement (FD) > 0.5 mm). ^5 Functional connectivity matrices were constructed based on the Schaefer-100 parcellation projected to native space. Small-worldness was computed across network densities from 10–80% in 1% increments. Densities from 10–55% were retained for subsequent analyses because networks in this range met the small-world criterion (e.g., σ>1, γ>1, and λ≈1). Four graph-theoretical metrics were calculated: Global Efficiency (Eg; long-range integration), Clustering Coefficient (Cp; local segregation), Characteristic Path Length (Lp; communication cost), and Local Efficiency (Eloc; regional processing). Area-under-curve (AUC) values across densities 10–55% were derived for each metric for developmental trajectory modeling and comparisons.

To reduce confounding effects due to age and sex differences across cohorts, we applied an age-stratified matching procedure prior to longitudinal modeling. Ages were divided into seven developmentally meaningful bins (1–3, 4–6, 7–9, 10–12, 13–18, 19–24, and 25–36 months). Within each bin, CBCP and BCP infants were strictly matched on age (all p > 0.5) and sex (all p > 0.5) to ensure comparability between groups. Welch’s t-test for age and chi-square tests for sex were conducted for each age bin. 

To test population differences in developmental trajectories, we fitted two GAMMs for each network topological metric. The first model included a cohort×age interaction, allowing cohort-specific trajectory fitting. The second model excluded this interaction but retained the age-match constraint across cohorts. Both models included smoothed age terms (k = 3), cohort, sex, site, and mean FD as fixed effects, with subject random intercepts to account for repeated measurements. ANOVA compared the two models via likelihood ratio tests to determine whether cohort-specific trajectories significantly improved model fit. Sex differences within each cohort were assessed similarly using a sex × age interaction. False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction (Benjamini-Hochberg, p < 0.05) was applied across all comparisons.

Results

All network metrics showed significant age-related changes. Consistent with prior work, Eg increased significantly with age (F = 12.92, p < 0.001), accompanied by a significant decrease in Lp. Metrics reflecting local network segregation also showed significant age effects, with Cp (F = 13.60, p < 0.001) and Eloc (F = 4.89, p = 0.011) both decreased significantly (F = 13.04, p < 0.001). These results indicate robust age-dependent changes in both global and local network properties during early development. Despite these age-dependent patterns, the developmental trajectories of network metrics differed significantly between cohorts for all metrics, as confirmed by likelihood ratio tests comparing models with and without age-by-group interactions (Eg: L.Ratio = 9.415, p = 0.016; Eloc: L.Ratio = 11.489, p = 0.013; Cp: L.Ratio = 8.844, p = 0.016; Lp: L.Ratio = 7.165, p = 0.028) (Fig.1a).  

For Eg, both cohorts exhibited age-related increases; however, CBCP showed a markedly steeper rise compared to the more gradual increase observed in BCP. In contrast, Eloc and Cp showed divergent patterns: BCP displayed higher values in early infancy followed by a gradual decline, whereas CBCP started at lower values but demonstrated a more rapid decrease over time. Lp exhibited an opposite pattern to Eg, further highlighting cohort-specific differences in the timing and pace of network reorganization.

Significant age-by-sex interactions were also observed for all metrics in both cohorts (all p < 0.05) (Fig.1b). For Eg, BCP showed a significant age-by-sex interaction (p = 0.012), with males demonstrating a gradual increase over time, while females showed a decreasing trend. In CBCP (p = 0.012), females exhibited a rapid increase from early infancy, plateauing at approximately 20 months, whereas males displayed a trajectory similar to BCP males. For Eloc and Cp, sex-related effects showed similar overall directions across cohorts, although differences in timing and shape were observed. In BCP, males started at higher values followed by a rapid decline, while females showed a more gradual increase (Eloc: p = 0.028; Cp: p = 0.046). In CBCP, males also started at higher values followed by a largely linear decline, whereas females showed a linear decrease in Eloc and a pronounced early decline in Cp that stabilized at around 20 months of age (Eloc: p = 0.012; Cp: p = 0.019). For Lp, significant age-by-sex interactions were observed in both cohorts (BCP: p = 0.019; CBCP: p = 0.019). In BCP, males started with higher Lp values and showed a more rapid decline than females. In CBCP, females started at higher values, declined rapidly, and plateaued at around 20 months of age, while males again followed a trajectory similar to BCP males. Collectively, these findings show that although age-related changes in global and local network metrics are observed across populations, the developmental trajectories of both global integration– and local organization–related properties differ between cohorts and are further shaped by sex.

Conclusion

Leveraging large Chinese (CBCP) and U.S. (BCP) infant cohorts, we identified both universal and population-specific patterns in early functional network maturation. While both populations showed age-related shifts toward efficient small-world topology, significant cohort×age interactions across all metrics revealed divergent developmental trajectories. Population-specific sex effects further demonstrated that network maturation timing and magnitude are not universally conserved. Together, these findings suggest that early functional network maturation reflects a dynamic balance between conserved developmental processes and population-specific influences, manifested as differences in the timing and pace of network integration and segregation.
