Introduction

Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) targeting the prefrontal cortex is a safe and scalable intervention for major depressive disorder (MDD), though recent multicenter randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have yielded mixed results regarding its antidepressant efficacy.1 Identifying neurobiological predictors of treatment response could enable more targeted applications. In a previous RCTcomparing tDCS, escitalopram, and placebo in MDD, we found that larger left dorsolateral prefrontal gray matter volume was associated with improved tDCS outcomes.2 However, this finding has not been replicated, and comprehensive multimodal imaging studies examining structural, functional, and connectivity markers in depressed patients receiving tDCS remain scarce.

The present study aimed to replicate our prior volumetric findings in an independent sample and to characterize tDCS-associated changes in resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) and white matter microstructure. We hypothesized that larger baseline prefrontal volumes would predict greater clinical improvement specifically in participants receiving active tDCS.

Methods

This study was a secondary analysis of the DepressionDC trial (NCT02530164), a triple-blind, Multicenter RCT investigating tDCS as additional treatment to selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors in MDD.1 Participants aged 18-65 years with MDD (HDRS-21 ≥15), with inadequate response to 1-4 antidepressant trials, were randomized to receive active tDCS (bifrontal montage with anode at F3, cathode at F4; 2 mA; 30 minutes) or sham tDCS for 24 sessions over 6 weeks. Participants at the Munich site underwent optional multimodal MRI at baseline, week 6, and week 30. MRI data were acquired on a 3T Siemens Skyra scanner, including T1-weighted MPRAGE, resting-state fMRI, and diffusion-weighted imaging. Preprocessing used FSL, AFNI, and custom pipelines with quality control via MRIQC. Structural images underwent brain extraction and tissue segmentation; gray matter volumes were corrected for intracranial volume. Functional data preprocessing included motion correction, spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM), bandpass filtering (0.005-0.1 Hz), nuisance regression, and spike regression for motion outliers. Diffusion tensor imaging provided fractional anisotropy maps for white matter analysis. Individual electric field modeling was performed using SimNIBS.

Regions of interest included prefrontal areas (Brodmann areas 8, 9, 10, 46) based on the Sallet atlas, and the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC). Linear mixed-effects models tested three-way interactions between baseline imaging metrics, treatment group, and time, with Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) total score as primary outcome.

Results

Of 74 participants recruited at the Munich site, 57 had MRI data (25 active tDCS, 32 sham). Groups did not differ in baseline demographics or clinical characteristics (mean age 36.3 years, 49% male, mean baseline MADRS 21.8). 

Replicating our prior findings, analysis revealed significant volume × time × group interactions for both left prefrontal cortex (lPFC: d=0.28, p=0.046, d=0.28) and right prefrontal cortex (rPFC: d=0.32 p=0.019, d=0.32). 

In the active tDCS group, larger baseline prefrontal volumes were associated with greater MADRS improvement from baseline to week 6. Effects remained significant after controlling for age, sex, and episode duration. 

Baseline rsFC from prefrontal ROIs and sgACC did not significantly predict clinical improvement in either group. However, longitudinal analysis revealed a significant group × time interaction for sgACC rsFC (d=0.16, p=0.017, d=0.16). Exploratory seed to whole brain analysis showed decreased sgACC connectivity to bilateral angular gyrus (p_FDR=0.019 and 0.03) and increased connectivity to superior frontal regions (p_FDR=0.001) compared to sham tDCS.

Integrated multimodal analysis showed that larger baseline prefrontal volumes were associated with both greater MADRS improvement and more pronounced sgACC rsFC reductions in the active tDCS group (lPFC: p=0.007, d=-0.38; rPFC: p=0.022, d=-0.33). Baseline lPFC volume correlated with sgACC rsFC change in the tDCS group (r=-0.68, p=0.01) but not sham. DTI analyses revealed no significant white matter changes.

Conclusion

This study independently replicates prefrontal gray matter volume as a predictor of tDCS antidepressant response based on  what is, to our knowledge, the largest tDCS neuroimaging dataset from a RCT in depression. Despite the absence of group-level clinical efficacy in the parent trial, we identified distinct neurobiological markers associated with tDCS effects. Active tDCS modulated sgACC connectivity patterns consistent with default mode network changes, and multimodal analysis linked baseline anatomy to both clinical and connectivity outcomes.

Limitations include modest effect sizes (d=0.28-0.32), suggesting prefrontal volume alone provides insufficient predictive power for clinical stratification as well as sample attrition reducing power for longitudinal analyses, and the ROI-based approach potentially missing predictive signals outside predefined regions. Our findings suggest that individual differences in prefrontal neuroanatomy may moderate tDCS therapeutic effects. The convergence of structural predictors with stimulation-induced connectivity changes provides a foundation for developing biomarker-guided tDCS protocols.
