Introduction

Cerebral amyloid-related angiopathy (CAA) is a prominent feature in Alzheimer's disease (AD)/related dementias (ADRD). CAA is characterized by the accumulation of amyloid ß (Aß) deposits in the walls of cortical and leptomeningeal vessels [1]. AD immunotherapies targeting Aß have marked significant milestones, including FDA approval for early AD however, their clinical benefits remain limited and are often associated with CAA-linked complications, such as amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) [1].

Squirrel monkeys (SQMs) are a unique non-human primate (NHP) model for studying AD due to their propensity of naturally developing age dependent CAA [2]. Alongside this, they have been shown to demonstrate spontaneous ARIA [3,4]. Their sporadic occurrence of ARIA manifests as vasogenic edema (ARIA-E) and cerebral microhemorrhage (ARIA-H).

            Previously, we demonstrated the effectiveness of multi-gradient echo (MGE) in generating T2*-w anatomical brain maps with enhanced precision via combined echo-averaging, used in tandem with quantitative R2* mapping to discern age-related regional differences in SQM brains [5]. R2* proved to be an effective neuroimaging biomarker sensitive to iron-related neuropathology. Building upon our prior analysis, here we utilized a larger dataset spanning a broader age range of SQMs. The present study evaluated brain tissue integrity in vivo, with particular emphasis on WM microstructural changes, through integrated multi-parametric MRI analyses. These included high-resolution 3D T2*-w brain datasets serving as detailed anatomical maps, 2D T2-w TSE and FLAIR imaging, and 2D multi-shell diffusion, complemented by immunohistochemistry.

Methods

In vivo MRI of SQMs with ages ranging from 6-23 was performed on a 7-Tesla Bruker 7030 Biospec scanner interfaced to an Avance-III console operated under Paravision 6.1 and TopSpin 3.1. 

In-vivo scan protocols included the following sequences: Multigradient echo sequence (MGE, 8 Echoes, TE/ES=2.96/4.0ms TR=41ms, FA=13°, FOV=38.4 × 25.6 × 25.6mm, Matrix=384 × 256 × 256; Acq. Time=45min, Total scan=45min), 2D T2-w Turbo-spin-echo (TSE, 1TFac=10, TE=36ms, TR=3200ms), 2D FLAIR (TFac=10, TE=35ms, TR=3200ms, TI=1557ms; and Diffusion MRI using 2D Monopolar PGSE EPI (TE/TR=34/5500ms, Spatial resolution=0.8 x 0.8mm, slice thickness=0.8mm 5 non-DWI, b-values=250, 500, 1000, 2500, 5000s/mm2, with 60 directions each). 

Diffusion metrics including mean diffusivity (MD), fractional anisotropy (FA), and mean kurtosis (MK) were calculated. Immunohistochemistry was subsequently performed to characterize the underlying neuropathology of observed MRI abnormalities.

Results

R2* analysis revealed differing trends in aging between cortex and WM. The whole brain and GM trend in R2* displayed a significant linear increase with age (Fig. 1; p < 0.0001), reflecting iron accumulation across lifespan, similar to what has been described in humans [8]. Conversely, the WM displayed a reversal when comparing early to late geriatric animals, modeled by a polynomial quadratic function (Fig. 1; p < 0.01). This R2* decrease in the later lifespan was likely attributable to edema/influx of fluid, visible as ARIA-E like hyperintensities. This was further exacerbated by a loss of iron in regions exhibiting demyelination as seen histologically (Fig. 2).

 The GM continuous R2* increase with age likely corresponded to the accumulation of iron-related pathologies including: a general rise in free iron within the parenchyma, accumulation of iron within insoluble cores of vascular amyloid plaques, localized hypoxia, and potential microhemorrhages, 

            We examined the occurrence of ARIA-E like hyperintense regions on T2-w TSE and FLAIR images, previously shown to be linked to the presence of parenchymal vasogenic edema. Multiple subjects from our late geriatric SQMs presented with ARIA-E asymmetric signal hyperintensities. 

One notable case presented with a lesion in the fronto-parietal region that extended several mm along the rostro-caudal axis. Diffusion MRI maps derived from 2D PGSE-EPI showed evidence of an increased sensitivity in the detection of conventional MRI-visible lesions. Diffusion metrics, MD, FA, and MK were visibly altered in the defined lesion area (Fig. 2, A-E).

Histopathological features corresponding to the T2-w signal hyperintensity included: IBA1 positive microglia exhibiting dystrophic morphology, pronounced GFAP reactive astrocytes, plasma extravasation evidenced by fibrinogen deposits, indicative of blood brain barrier (BBB) dysfunction. Furthermore, axonal abnormalities and severe myelin loss was also detected in the MRI defined ARIA-E hyperintense WM (Fig. 2, F-Q).

Conclusion

The present study promotes the combination of advanced MRI to study neuroimaging biomarkers, potentially linked to age-associated cerebrovascular dysfunction, evolving from gradual vascular Aß accumulation to associated hypoxia, leading to a loss of vascular integrity, resulting in BBB leakage and ultimately microhemorrhage. These findings promote the SQM model as an opportune environment to study AD, CAA, and ARIA, establishing a critical bridge between preclinical and clinical applications.
