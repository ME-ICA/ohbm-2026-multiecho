Introduction

Autistic traits are continuously distributed in the general population and share phenotypic and neurobiological features with Autism Spectrum Disorder. While cross-sectional neuroimaging studies have reported structural correlates of autistic traits, little is known about their longitudinal neurodevelopmental trajectories. Diffusion MRI offers insights into white-matter microstructural maturation during adolescence and early adulthood, a period marked by substantial changes in connectivity relevant to social behavior. Understanding how white-matter development relates to individual variability in autistic traits, and how these trajectories may differ by biological sex, remains a key challenge for developmental neuroscience. Here, we investigated whether trajectories of white-matter microstructure from adolescence to young adulthood are associated with autistic traits in a large community-based cohort.

Methods

Participants were drawn from the IMAGEN study, a longitudinal European cohort. A total of 574 individuals completed diffusion MRI at three time points: ages 14, 18 and 23. Autistic traits were assessed using the Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS) at age 16, including subscores (Social Motivation, Social Communication, Social Cognition, Social Perception, Social Communication and Interaction [SCI]). Diffusion data were preprocessed using MRtrix3, complemented by tools from FSL and ANTs, within an unbiased longitudinal preprocessing framework. Fractional anisotropy (FA) values were extracted from 41[HL1]  major white-matter tracts defined by the JHU atlas. Site-related variability was harmonized using ComBat-GAM. Longitudinal trajectories were modelled using generalized additive mixed models including age, biological sex, and SRS scores, with participant-specific random intercepts. We tested main effects of autistic traits on FA and age-by-trait and sex-by-trait interactions. Multiple comparisons were controlled using false discovery rate correction.

Results

Fractional anisotropy in the right posterior limb of the internal capsule (rPLIC) showed a significant nonlinear interaction between age and Social Motivation scores, such that higher Social Motivation was associated with steeper age-related decreases in FA from 14 to 23 years, whereas lower Social Motivation was linked to flatter trajectories (rPLIC; Age × Social Motivation = 4.91, EDF = 1.96, pFDR = 0.00567).

When examined separately by sex, distinct and opposing developmental patterns emerged (rPLIC; Age × Social Motivation × Sex: F = 12.68, EDF = 1.00, pFDR = 0.00038). In females, lower Social Motivation scores were associated with steeper age-related decreases in rPLIC FA compared with higher scores. In contrast, males showed an inverse pattern, with higher Social Motivation scores associated with more pronounced, non-linear age-related FA changes, while lower Social Motivation was linked to flatter trajectories.

Conclusion

White-matter pathways involved in sensorimotor and subcortical–cortical communication show developmental differences associated with autistic traits in adolescents. The observed sex-specific trajectories highlight the importance of modelling biological sex in neurodevelopmental research. These findings support a dimensional and continuous view of autistic traits, suggesting that variation in early-maturing projection fibers contributes to individual differences in social motivation.
