Introduction

In the computational modeling of the neural correlates of consciousness, many efforts have focused on the whole-brain scale. Although this powerful approach successfully captures global dynamics, it may overlook potentially insightful details at the population and microcircuit levels. A complementary strategy is to concentrate on a limited subset of nodes considered most essential and represent them with greater biological specificity, with the potential for later embedding into a whole-brain network within a multiscale modeling framework.1 Moreover, growing evidence points to the contribution of single-cell computations, namely in layer 5 cortical pyramidal neurons, to the maintenance of arousal and awareness.2 Because these dynamics can be integrated into mesoscale and even macroscale models,3 their incorporation represents a natural and important next step in computational approaches to the study of consciousness. Here, we present a biophysically grounded cortico-subcortical spiking network model that aims to capture processes across cellular, microcircuit, and brain network levels, underlying physiological and pathological states of consciousness.

Methods

The model includes selected nuclei of thalamus and basal ganglia, along with a subset of cortical regions chosen for their theoretical significance, clinical relevance to the disorders of consciousness (DoC), and availability of experimental recordings. Cortical regions primarily comprise nodes of the default mode (DMN), fronto-parietal, salience, and dorsal attention networks. This selection substantially overlaps with the global neuronal workspace. Each cortical region of interest is represented as a column, organized into excitatory and inhibitory populations in superficial, middle, and deep layers. Pyramidal cells in layers 2/3 and 5 are implemented as morphological cells, enabling more accurate forward modeling of local field potentials (LFP). The remaining cell types are modeled as single-compartment neurons.

Thalamus comprises the intralaminar and higher-order nuclei, and thalamic reticular nucleus. The inclusion of basal ganglia (striatum and globus pallidus internal) further enables the model to test predictions of the anterior forebrain mesocircuit (AFM) hypothesis - the fronto-striato-thalamic circuit model for loss and recovery of consciousness in DoC.4

Interareal projections are constrained by anatomical structural connectivity. The layer-specific patterns of cortico-cortical projections are informed by each region’s hierarchical position within the feedforward and feedback streams of information flow, which is thought to be essential for supporting awareness. Laminar targeting of subcortico-cortical projections is also dictated by anatomical data and differs across thalamic nuclei. This laminar resolution also provides an appropriate architecture for modeling how single layer 5 pyramidal neurons couple inputs arriving at different cortical layers.3

Although this work focuses on modeling pathological impairments of consciousness, we used data from medically induced loss and recovery of consciousness as an entry point for model development. Partly comparable to DoC, anesthesia provides a controlled perturbation of consciousness with well-defined state transitions, allowing causal manipulations and access to reach laminar-resolved recordings. In particular, we employed multiareal laminar intracranial recordings (LFP and firing rates) from awake and anesthetized macaques to validate and constrain the thalamocortical modules. Network-level coordination was further adjusted to match fMRI-based functional connectivity in awake versus anesthetized animals, using LFP signal correlation as a proxy for functional connectivity. The model was implemented in NetPyNE,5 a tool for modeling large neural circuits with the NEURON simulator.

Results

In this preliminary work, we identified an effective cortical-column architecture that accounts for experimentally observed intra-areal and inter-areal activity while preserving computational tractability. As part of this, we developed a minimally viable layer-5 pyramidal cell model that reproduces backpropagation-activated calcium (BAC) firing while remaining suitable for large-scale network simulations. This is achieved using a morphologically and physiologically reduced L5 pyramidal neuron (4–5 dendritic compartments) equipped with dendritic Ca-HVA and SKCa currents in addition to standard Na⁺ and K⁺ conductances. 

We also performed preliminary analyses of how alterations in the integrity of long-range pathways affect functional connectivity among the model’s regions of interest, with emphasis on interactions within and between the DMN and AFM. These perturbations were ranked according to whether they produced unconscious-like dynamics. We plan to identify connectivity and parameter regimes that approximate known empirical patterns of structural impairment in DoC, thereby providing mechanistic insight into the emergence of pathological states of consciousness.

Conclusion

The proposed framework aims to provide a unified mechanistic platform for capturing signatures of conscious states, with particular relevance to pathological conditions. Its biophysical plausibility enables testing hypotheses about pharmacological, invasive, and non-invasive interventions. The model offers a means to assess metrics such as fronto-parietal communication breakdown, frequency-band alterations, changes in structure-function correlation, neural complexity, and integration and segregation of information. Ultimately, this work seeks to establish a generative platform that bridges theoretical, experimental, and clinical perspectives on the mechanisms of consciousness and its disorders.
