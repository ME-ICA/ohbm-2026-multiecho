Introduction

The frontoparietal network (FPN) is a large-scale resting-state network supporting flexible, goal-directed behavior. While often treated as a unitary system, recent evidence suggests it consists of two distinct subnetworks,1 each showing preferential coupling to different large-scale systems such as the default mode network (FPNA → DMN) or dorsal attention network (FPNB  → DAN). These patterns have been interpreted as evidence for distinct functional roles, yet the nature of this differentiation remains poorly understood and has so far been inferred mainly from meta-analytic studies1 or narrow task paradigms.2 

Importantly, because the FPN exhibits the greatest inter-subject variability in topological organization,3 group-level approaches are poorly suited to investigate the functional differentiation of its subnetworks. A deeper understanding of FPNs subnetwork specialization therefore requires approaches that (i) respect individual variability in FPN topology and (ii) characterize subnetwork function across a broad and representative cognitive space at the individual level as it is difficult to predict a priori how these functionally similar subnetworks will differentiate under a limited set of cognitive tasks. To overcome this limitation, we leverage the Individual Brain Charting (IBC) dataset,4 which provides densely-sampled multi-task (up to 80 tasks per subject, yielding ~ 550 contrasts) and resting-state fMRI in 13 individuals at high spatial resolution (1.5mm isotropic, 3T, TR=760ms for resting state), enabling unprecedented precision in mapping FPN subnetworks and systematically investigating their functional specialization at the individual level.

Methods

We included participants with >30 minutes of usable resting-state data after motion scrubbing (average of ~41 min) from the IBC dataset, yielding a final sample of 8 participants with 55-63 distinct tasks per subject (ARCHI, HCP batteries, and additional cognitive tasks), comprising ~550 task contrasts per subject.

Preprocessing was performed with fMRIPrep, which included motion correction, susceptibility distortion correction using additional reverse phase-encoded  EPI scans, co-registration to anatomical images, and spatial normalization. Outputs were generated in Human Connectome Project’s fsLR surface space. We performed additional denoising including confound regression (6 motion parameters plus their derivatives, squares, and squared derivatives) and removal of white matter and CSF signals. Resting-state data further underwent motion scrubbing (framewise displacement > 0.2mm). 

Infomap, a state-of-the-art community detection approach for precision functional mapping, was then applied to identify the FPN and 19 additional large-scale networks for each subject.5 FPN subnetworks were derived using two approaches to establish convergent evidence: (1) k-means clustering (k=2) of Infomap communities based on their whole-brain connectivity profiles to all other networks, and (2) k-means clustering (k=2) applied directly to within-FPN time series. To probe subnetwork functional specialization, we assessed  activation selectivity, comparing mean z-scores from a GLM analysis of all task contrasts between subnetworks. In order to better interpret associations between contrasts and subnetworks, we assigned cognitive domain labels (https://www.cognitiveatlas.org/) to each contrast, thereby grouping related cognitive processes.

Results

Both clustering methods successfully identified two FPN subnetworks in all eight participants with spatial distribution across lateral prefrontal, parietal, and temporal cortex. Subnetwork components showed widespread correspondence across methods (Fig. 1), both identifying a spatially interleaved subnetwork architecture, consistent with earlier findings.1 For subsequent activation analyses we focused on the Infomap community clustering solutions due to greater stability and better network interpretability. Resting-state connectivity analyses confirmed that the two subnetworks exhibit differentiated coupling to other large-scale networks: Cross-network integration analyses further indicated that FPNA coupled more strongly with the DMN, while the FPNB preferentially integrated with the DAN across subjects (Fig. 1).

Activation selectivity analysis on the task data revealed a robust functional dissociation (Fig. 2): Across subjects FPNA was preferentially activated in 17 contrasts (p < 0.05 uncorrected) spanning self-related cognition, language and symbolic processing, biological-motion interpretation, working memory updating, and reward evaluation. FPNB activated preferentially for 21 contrasts dominated by low- to mid-level perceptual processing, including visual motion discrimination, color perception, visual attention, and letter or pattern working memory maintenance, with an additional contribution from basic linguistic decoding. This pattern revealed a clear functional dissociation: FPNA showed a preference for abstract, symbolic, or internally oriented cognition, while FPNB was preferentially engaged during perceptually demanding, stimulus-driven, and action-oriented processing, providing converging evidence for their distinct functional roles in internally versus externally oriented control.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that the frontoparietal network is not a unitary system but comprises distinct subnetworks with separable functional specializations. Activation selectivity analyses suggest the following dissociation: FPNA is preferentially involved in abstract, symbolic, and internally oriented processing, while FPNB is stronger engaged during perceptually demanding, stimulus-driven tasks. These results align with their large-scale network affiliations: FPNA‘s coupling with the default mode network situates it within systems supporting internally oriented cognition, while FPNB‘s integration with the dorsal attention network links it to externally oriented control.

This  dissociation, supported by converging evidence from activation profiles and resting-state coupling, provides the first individual-level, task-based confirmation of FPN heterogeneity and extends prior group-level observations to the precision mapping scale. Critically, despite substantial inter-subject variability in subnetwork topography, functional specialization remained consistent across individuals. 

Together, these results establish frontoparietal subnetwork architecture as a robust, individually reproducible substrate for flexible cognitive control.
