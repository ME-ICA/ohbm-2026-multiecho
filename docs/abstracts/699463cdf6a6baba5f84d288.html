Introduction

Focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) is a structural abnormality affecting the cortex and a leading cause of drug-resistant focal epilepsy. Surgery may offer a path to seizure freedom, with improved post-surgical outcome when FCD lesions are detected on MRI scans1. However, FCDs can be very subtle and 30% of them are missed by radiologists on visual inspection of MRI scans (i.e. MRI-negative). International initiatives, such as the Multi-centre Epilepsy Lesion Detection (MELD) project, have developed automated AI tools capable of detecting those subtle lesions and tailored for clinical evaluation. Our latest tool, MELD Graph2 uses a graph CNN model trained on surface-based cortical features from a large multicentre cohort of patients with FCD (n=278) and healthy controls (n=180). More recently, a pilot study from the University of Bonn demonstrated improved performance using a voxel-based nnU-Net3 model trained directly on 3D MRI scans from a single centre cohort of patients4 (n=118 patients). In this study, we leverage a larger multicentre cohort of FCD patients (n>600) to train a nnU-Net model to detect FCD lesions. We compare this model to MELD Graph on an independent test site, in the aim to assess the benefit of using a voxel-based approach compared to a surface-based approach for detection of subtle epilepsy lesion from MRI scans.  

Methods

The nnU-Net model was trained on T1w MRI scans using manual lesion masks drawn by expert radiologists as the ground truth. The training dataset comprised 622 patients with radiological or histological diagnosis of FCD and 1191 healthy controls from 31 hospitals worldwide. Training was performed using five-fold cross validation for 2000 epochs with the ‘3d_fullres’ configuration and a PolyLR learning rate. For independent evaluation, the model was applied on T1w scans from an open-source dataset of patients with FCD and healthy controls5. For comparison, MELD Graph was applied on the same independent dataset using T1w and FLAIR scans. Figure 1 illustrates the inference pipelines applied for MELD Graph and MELD nnU-Net for the independent evaluation. Both models were evaluated using the following metrics: 1) their sensitivity in detecting lesions, defined as one-voxel-overlap between the prediction and the manual lesion mask, 2) their specificity in controls, defined as predicting no putative lesion, 3) the number of false-positive clustered predictions they generated, 4) time taken to run per subject. The McNemar test was used to assess the significance of the difference in sensitivity and specificity between both models. Significance was set at 0.05. 

Results

MELD Graph and MELD nn-UNet were applied on T1w scans from 85 patients and 84 controls from the independent test set. Two patients and two controls failed the MELD Graph preprocessing pipeline. MELD nn-UNet detected 52 out of the 85 FCD lesions (61.2% sensitivity) with a median of 0 false-positive in patients (Table 1). The model accurately predicted no putative lesions in 59 out of the 84 controls (70.2% specificity) and produced a median of 0 false-positive in controls. In comparison, MELD Graph achieved 63.5% sensitivity, 39.3% specificity, with a median of 0 false-positive in patients but 1 false-positive in control. The McNemar test showed that the difference in sensitivity between the two models was not significant (10.0, p=0.83), whereas the increased specificity for MELD nnU-Net was significant (10.0, <0.01). Among the five patients that were reported MRI-negative, both models detected one lesion (20% detection rate). Running the MELD nnU-Net inference pipeline took approximately 10 seconds per subject (on a GPU), whereas MELD Graph pipeline took 6 hours, primarily due to the FreeSurfer-dependent feature extraction step (Figure 1A and 1B). Both models output a prediction mask on the T1w native scan. In addition, MELD Graph also provide an interpretable report with a confidence score and saliency information, providing insight into which features (e.g. blurring of the grey-white matter, cortical thickness, FLAIR intensities) were salient to the model. An example prediction mask from both models on a FCD patient is given in Figure 1C.   

Conclusion

In this study, we demonstrate that an nnU-Net model trained on a large multicentre dataset achieves close to comparable performance to an established FCD detection tool, MELD Graph. The nnU-Net model had a similar sensitivity to MELD Graph (61% vs 63%), while achieving a higher specificity (70% vs 39%).  Future work will focus on incorporating additional modalities, such as FLAIR, to improve the sensitivity of the nnU-Net model. Finally, MELD nnU-Net also offers practical advantages for clinical integration, as it is substantially faster and more robust to preprocessing failures. However, this must be weighed against the benefits of feature-based models like MELD Graph, which can provide interpretable outputs detailing which features drive classifier predictions.
