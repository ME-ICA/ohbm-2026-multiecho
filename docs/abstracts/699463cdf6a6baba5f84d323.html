Introduction

Humans can encode the structure of their social networks to navigate complex interpersonal environments.1 Previous studies showed that, similar to spatial navigation, the representation of social networks relies on distinct reference frames.2, 4 One is an egocentric frame centered on the self, and the other is an allocentric frame describing relations between others.2, 3 These frames are supported by distinct neural processing mechanisms.2-4 Previous research on social networks has predominantly focused on the egocentric or allocentric representations, respectively.1, 4 However, it is still unclear the difference of the neural substrates between these two frameworks in self-involved social networks. Thus, we collected the functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data when subjects performing a social relationship judgment task and directly compared the neural activity associated with egocentric versus allocentric perspectives.

Methods

Subjects: We initially enrolled 32 healthy adult subjects from South China Normal University (SCNU). Two subjects' datasets were excluded due to their excessive head motion (translation > 2.0 mm or rotation > 2.0°), resulting in a final sample of 30 subjects (14M/16F, aged 20.80 ± 2.41 years old). The study was approved by the Institutional Research Board (IRB) of SCNU. All the subjects provided written informed consent prior to the study and obtained rewards for attending the experiment.

Data acquisition and preprocessing. MRI data were acquired on a 3.0 T Siemens Prisma-Fit MRI scanner with a 64-channel phased array head coil. Functional (BOLD-fMRI), T1-weighted structural, and field map data were collected. The fMRI data were acquired using a single-shot SMS/multi-band GE-EPI sequence with the following parameters: TR = 1,520 ms, TE = 31 ms, flip angle = 70°, voxel size = (2.4 mm)³, and 63 transversal slices. High-resolution brain structural images were acquired using a T1-weighted 3D MP-RAGE sequence (TR = 1,800 ms, TE = 2.0 ms, TI = 900 ms, flip angle = 9°, voxel size = 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 mm³). Additionally, Field maps utilized a double-echo gradient-echo sequence (TR = 621 ms, TE1/TE2 = 4.92/7.38 ms, flip angle = 60°, and voxel size = 2.4 × 2.4 × 2.4 mm³). Preprocessing was performed using fMRIPrep 20.2.7.5 Functional images underwent head motion correction and distortion correction, followed by normalization to MNI space (2 mm isotropic). Finally, spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM) and high-pass filtering (1/100 Hz) were performed, with subsequent GLM analysis utilizing tools from FSL/FEAT.

Experimental design and procedures. The experiment was conducted over two consecutive days. On Day 1, the subjects learned a novel social network derived from the TV drama Stand by Me Through Campus Days. First, the subjects were requested to memorize the faces and names of 9 major characters (5 males and 4 females) and completed a study-test procedure until they reached 100% accuracy, ensuring reliable identification of each character. Next, they watched Episodes 11–12, which were selected because they clearly depict the relationships among these characters. After viewing, the subjects completed a quiz on the plot and character relationships. Only those subjects who achieved an accuracy ≥ 85% were allowed to proceed to the fMRI session.

On Day 2, the subjects underwent fMRI scanning. Figure 1. shows the procedure of social relationship judgment task. Subjects assessing social distance in allocentric (between two characters) or egocentric (self vs. character) conditions. The task comprised 144 trials in total, with 72 trials per condition, randomly presented and administered in two runs of 72 trials each (two 36-trial blocks per run). On each trial, character names were presented on the screen, and subjects viewed them and rated relationship closeness by pressing the corresponding keys (0–6) on an fMRI-compatible response box. The task employed an event-related design with a pseudorandomized presentation to balance perspective transitions.

Whole-brain GLM analysis. A general linear model (GLM) was applied to analyze the task-fMRI data using FSL/FEAT. In the calculation, we included two main regressors ("allocentric" and "egocentric" condition). Additionally, six head motion parameters were included as regressors of no interest. To identify the distinct neural correlates associated with each of the reference frames, we computed brain activations for the bidirectional contrasts: "egocentric > allocentric" and "allocentric > egocentric". Group-level differences in activation were assessed using a one-sample t-tests. Statistical significance was determined using a cluster-based inference in FSL, with cluster-wise correction based on Gaussian random field (GRF) theory. The cluster-forming threshold was set at Z > 3.1 (voxel-wise, uncorrected), and only clusters surviving a cluster-wise corrected p < 0.05 were considered significant.

Results

Figure 2a. shows the locations of significant activation clusters for the contrast of egocentric > allocentric. We identified three spatially distinct clusters, which were located in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC; peak: -2, 36, 14; Z = 5.78), the posterior cingulate cortex (PCC; peak: 0, -24, 44; Z = 4.93), and the bilateral angular gyrus within the inferior parietal lobule (IPL; left: -62, -54, 36; Z = 5.75; right: 64, -50, 34; Z = 5.45). Figure 2b. shows significant activation for the contrast of allocentric > egocentric. We detected four significant activation clusters, which were located in the occipital pole (peak: -22, -4, -92; Z = 6.19), bilateral insula (left: -28, 26, 10; Z = 5.23; right: 30, 24, 8; Z = 4.97), the superior medial frontal gyrus (-8, 12, 52; Z = 4.97), and cerebellum (4, -54, -40; Z = 4.44). Conclusion

We found that the default mode network (DMN, specifically the angular gyrus and cingulate cortex) is involved in egocentric representations, whereas the sensory-salience regions (specifically the occipital pole and insula) are involved in allocentric representations. These results suggest that distinct neural systems are involved in processing different social reference frames. Specifically, egocentric representation primarily engages self-referential processing mediated by the DMN, while allocentric social mapping involves significant components of visual scene reconstruction and salience processing. These findings provide key evidence for the neural mechanisms of distinct social reference frames and enrich our understanding of the neural substrates of social network representation.
