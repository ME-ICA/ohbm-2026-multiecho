Introduction

Introduction

Current clinical diagnosis of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) relies largely on behavioral observation scales, and objective biomarkers remain limited. The first year of life (0–12 months) represents a critical window for brain development, during which neural systems are highly sensitive to environmental influences—particularly maternal mental health—which can shape long-term neurodevelopmental outcomes [1]. Although prior studies have examined associations between neonatal dynamic functional connectivity (FC) and later autistic traits [2], it remains unclear whether these associations are influenced by the spatial distance between functionally connected brain regions.

Postpartum depression is a prevalent maternal mental health condition linked to multiple adverse psychiatric and developmental outcomes in offspring [3]. However, its moderating role in the relationship between neonatal FC and later ASD risk has not been fully explored. This study aimed to identify novel neuroimaging markers for early ASD risk prediction based on distance-dependent FC characteristics and to assess their relationship with postpartum depression.

Methods

Methods

Data Acquisition: We used publicly available neonatal data from the dHCP, which includes maternal Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS) scores and offspring ASD risk scores assessed at 18 months using the Quantitative Checklist for Autism in Toddlers (Q-CHAT). During MRI acquisition, T2-weighted images were obtained at 0.8-mm isotropic resolution. Resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) was collected using a multi-slice gradient-echo EPI sequence (field of view: 145 × 145 × 108 mm³; TR = 12,000 ms; TE = 156 ms). After screening subjects for postmenstrual age and excessive head motion, 244 full-term infants were retained for analysis.

Data Processing: rs-fMRI data were preprocessed using the dHCP neonatal-optimized pipeline. Signals from white matter, cerebrospinal fluid, and six head-motion parameters were regressed out. Data were subsequently spatially normalized and smoothed. For each subject, we generated three types of functional connectivity density (FCD) maps: global FCD, short-range FCD, and long-range FCD. Long-range and short-range connectivity were defined using a Euclidean distance threshold of 12 mm. Global FCD was computed as the mean absolute Pearson correlation between each voxel's time series and all other voxels in the brain; short- and long-range FCD used only voxels within or beyond the 12-mm distance threshold, respectively. 

Statistical Analysis: Pearson correlations were calculated between prenatal/postpartum EPDS scores and offspring Q-CHAT scores at 18 months, with 1,000 permutation tests performed to ensure result robustness. We next conducted region-wise linear regression analyses between neonatal FCD and Q-CHAT scores, controlling for age, sex, and head motion, using the Automated Anatomical Labeling atlas. To assess the potential influence of postpartum depression, neonates were stratified into high- and low-EPDS subgroups based on the median maternal score, and regression analyses were repeated within each subgroup. All p-values were adjusted using the FDR method (p < 0.05).

Results

Results

We observed a significant positive correlation between postpartum depression scores and offspring ASD risk scores at 18 months (r = 0.19, p =7.35×10-6). In contrast, prenatal depression scores were not significantly associated with later ASD risk. When controlling for postmenstrual age, gFCD was negatively associated with total Q-CHAT scores in the left precentral gyrus. The sFCD was positively associated with social subscale scores in left subcortical regions, including the caudate nucleus, putamen, globus pallidus, and thalamus. When controlling for gestational age, gFCD showed a positive association with motor subscale scores in the right precuneus, cuneus, and cingulate gyrus. The sFCD was positively associated with social scores in the right inferior frontal gyrus and negatively associated with total scores in the left hippocampus and bilateral thalamus. When controlling for postnatal age, gFCD was negatively associated with social scores in the left rolandic operculum and positively associated with motor scores. Except for the social subscale, lFCD showed patterns consistent with gFCD (Figure 1). Finally, subgroup analyses based on maternal depression severity revealed that the regression slope was steeper in the high-depression group than in the low-depression group (Figure 2), with the most pronounced differences observed for sFCD. These findings suggest that higher levels of postpartum depression may amplify the relationship between neonatal FC patterns and later ASD risk.

Conclusion

Discussion and Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate a significant association between maternal postpartum depression and increased ASD risk in offspring at 18 months, whereas prenatal depression showed no such relationship. These results are consistent with a prior research indicating a stronger postnatal rather than prenatal influence of maternal depressive symptoms on early neurodevelopment [4]. We further identified robust associations between neonatal distance-dependent FCD and later ASD-related behaviors, particularly within language networks, motor networks, and subcortical structures. These regions are frequently implicated in ASD and are known to contribute to core symptoms such as social communication difficulties, stereotyped behaviors, and restricted interests [5]. The spatially specific FCD patterns observed in our study highlight their potential utility as early neuroimaging biomarkers for ASD risk prediction. Importantly, we found that the relationship between neonatal FCD and later ASD traits was modulated by the severity of maternal postpartum depression. Stronger associations in infants of mothers with higher EPDS scores suggest that postpartum depression may alter early brain functional organization, thereby shaping neurodevelopmental trajectories linked to ASD risk. This moderating effect underscores the importance of considering maternal mental health when interpreting early functional connectivity markers. Together, these findings support neonatal FCD as a promising early indicator of ASD risk at 18 months and provide preliminary evidence for a multi-level mechanistic framework linking maternal postpartum depression, infant brain functional connectivity, and later ASD-related outcomes. This work highlights the value of integrating maternal mental health assessments with neonatal neuroimaging to improve early identification and intervention strategies for ASD.
