Introduction

The pulsatility of the BOLD fMRI signal is related to neurovascular health with documented changes in the amplitude of the pulse with age.1 Harmonics of this pulsatile signal are related to vascular changes in disease, such as Alzheimer’s disease,2 and other aspects of cardiovascular fitness. The harmonics may also have sex-related differences which could be relevant for risk of vascular disease or dementia. The pulsatility and harmonics would ideally be measured using a fast BOLD fMRI sequence (TR = 100ms),2 but they can also be observed after retrospectively realigning the BOLD fMRI signal to a pulse plethysmograph (PPG). For longer TR (~1-2 s) fMRI data, which is more commonly acquired, retrospective realignment to the PPG signal results in one cardiac cycle averaged throughout the scan. Here we use human connectome project (HCP), young adult dataset, with BOLD fMRI TR = 720 ms, which does not sample the cardiac harmonics within Nyquist limits, and show that retrospectively aligned cardiac waveforms can be used to robustly estimate harmonic features of the pulse along with characterizing sex differences in pulsatility in young adults. 

Methods

We used a commonly available fMRI dataset from HCP to test our processing and to characterize pulsatility. We used a young adult group to demonstrate sensitivity to sex-related differences here, complementing previous age-related pulsatility differences in the literature. 

Data: The HCP young adult dataset was used. It has TR = 720 ms, 1200 time points, and 2 mm isotropic resolution. Three files were downloaded for 190 subjects: the unprocessed BOLD fMRI for LR phase encode, the simultaneously acquired physiological data (PPG), and the T1w anatomical data.

Preprocessing: Motion correction was performed in ANTs with rigid registration. 12 parameter motion regression (the 6 motion parameters and their derivatives) was performed. Framewise displacement (FD) was used to identify high-motion subjects. Registration from fMRI to MNI was also performed with ANTs, using a ‘Rigid’ transformation to the anatomical data and a ‘Syn’ transformation to the MNI template.

Pulse reliability: Reliability of the pulse signal was determined for each subject voxel-wise after retrospective realignment. The time series were randomly split in half, time point-by-point, grouped into 40 uniform bins of the cardiac phase, then the coefficient of determination, R2, from computed between the two halves of data. The process was repeated 5 times and the median R2 was the voxel’s pulse reliability. The R2 maps were registered to MNI and averaged across subjects using the Fisher transformation. The group R2 mask was transformed into native fMRI space for each subject to create a mask where group R2 > 0.1. Figure 1A shows the group pulse reliability.

Cardiac Harmonics: After normalizing each voxel to its DC component, the single cardiac waveform resulting from retrospective alignment was projected onto the harmonic frequencies (complex exponentials) after binning the cardiac phase into 40 uniform bins. Harmonic maps for each subject were created by taking the magnitude of the result for the first 6 harmonics (H1-H6).

Region of interest: The intersection of the group R2 mask and an MNI vessel probability > 0.05 mask3 were applied in fMRI space prior to calculating each subject’s harmonic magnitudes by averaging within the mask. Normalization to the first harmonic and the harmonic distortion (HD) calculations were performed after averaging. HD is a measure of waveform complexity,4 and relative harmonic power measured in this way has been shown to display differences between control and Alzheimer’s subjects.2 HD was computed as the mean square of harmonics 2 to 6 over the square of the first harmonic.

Subject-level filtering: Subjects were removed if 20% of their FD measures were greater than 0.5 mm or if their mean FD was greater than 0.3 mm. Outlier removal removed subjects if their harmonic measure was outside of the third quartile plus 3 times the IQR or the 1st quartile minus 3 times the IQR. Outlier removal was applied to the 6 harmonic metrics in ascending order, first to H1/DC and ending with H6/H1.

Statistics: The ordinary least squares method was used to test for sex differences, controlling for age and heart rate (measured by PPG). False positive correction was performed with fdrcorrection from statsmodels.stats.multitest in python.

Results

Subject-level filtering: Motion exclusion removed 10 subjects. Then, 20 additional subjects were removed due to harmonic outlier removal. One subject was removed due to missing age data. Subject-level filtering reduced the number of subjects from 190 to 159. 

Figure 2 shows the sex-differences for the harmonic metrics. Significant differences were found for all metrics tested except the first harmonic (H1/DC) and the second harmonic (H2/H1).

Conclusion

Typical TR BOLD fMRI data can be used to examine pulsatility in assessment of vascular health, even when not using a specialized custom pulse sequence. Harmonic magnitude can be reliably measured with retrospectively aligned BOLD fMRI data. The magnitude of the harmonics can be extracted from a single cardiac waveform after retrospective realignment of the BOLD fMRI data to a simultaneously acquired PPG waveform. The largest effect size in Figure 2 was for the third harmonic, and this result reproduces that found previously with a customized, fast fMRI method with TR = 65 ms, which measures cardiac harmonics without retrospective realignment.5 Further we show harmonic distortion (HD) differs between males and females, which could have implications in vascular pathology related to AD.2
