Introduction

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is characterized by persistent executive dysfunction and dysregulated emotional processing. While functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) studies have consistently identified hypoactivation in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) during the verbal fluency task (VFT) in acute MDD, the etiological nature of this deficit remains debated: given the genetic basis, does it represent a pre-existing genetic vulnerability shared by at-risk individuals, or is it a neurobiological “scar” resulting from the cumulative burden of the disease? Furthermore, prior research has largely focused on “cold” executive tasks, potentially overlooking subtle deficits in “hot” cognition—the interface between executive function and emotional regulation. To address these gaps, this study adopted a comprehensive three-group design comparing remitted MDD (rMDD) patients, high-risk (HR) individuals, and healthy controls (HC). By employing an emotional VFT to impose an affective regulatory load, we aimed to: (1) determine whether DLPFC hypoactivation persists in the remitted state; (2) assess whether unaffected high-risk individuals exhibit similar trait deficits or intact function; and (3) disentangle the neurobiological correlates of genetic risk from disease chronicity.

Methods

We recruited 87 participants: 35 patients with rMDD (remission≥2 months), 15 unaffected HR individuals (first-degree family history of MDD, no personal psychiatric history), and 37 HCs. Participants performed a block-design emotional VFT comprising semantic (Animals, Supermarket; neutral condition) and emotional (Positive, Negative) blocks. Active blocks (60s) alternated with a 60s control blocks (repeating numbers “1, 2, 3, 4…”) to correct for articulation artifacts. Hemodynamic responses were recorded using a 52-channel continuous-wave fNIRS system (ETG-4000, Hitachi Medical Co.) covering the bilateral prefrontal cortex. Signal processing was performed using HOMER 2. The primary outcome was the concentration change of oxyhemoglobin (∆HbO) in the bilateral DLPFC. Resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC) was also computed; network topology (global/local efficiency) was analyzed using graph theory across sparsity thresholds (0.1–0.5). Behavioral performance and ∆HbO were analyzed using a 3×3 repeated-measures ANCOVA (group × condition) and partial Pearson correlations, controlling for age, gender, and education.

Results

ANCOVA revealed a significant main effect of group on bilateral DLPFC activation [F(2, 81) = 3.696, p = 0.029, ηp2 = 0.084], in the absence of significant interaction effect or main effect of condition. Post-hoc Bonferroni-corrected comparisons indicated rMDD group exhibited significantly attenuated ∆HbO increases in the bilateral DLPFC compared to HCs (t(81) = -2.705, p = 0.024), confirming that neural hypoactivation persists as a stable dysfunction despite symptomatic remission. In contrast, the HR group showed no significant difference in activation compared to HCs (p = 0.700) or the rMDD group (p = 1.000). This places the HR group in an intermediate position, suggesting that genetic risk alone is insufficient to produce the robust prefrontal deficits observed in the disease state. Behavioral data revealed a significant main effect of emotional condition on VFT accuracy [F(1.77, 143.59) = 8.462, p = 0.001, ηp2 = 0.095], and a significant group × condition interaction [F(3.55, 143.59) = 4.013, p = 0.006, ηp2 = 0.090], with no main effect of group (p = 0.554), suggesting preserved behavioral capacity in rMDD and HR groups despite altered neural engagement. The interaction indicated a consistent performance gradient across all cohorts (neutral > negative > positive; all p < 0.01). No significant brain-behavior correlations were found. Resting-state functional networks revealed no significant group differences in global efficiency (p = 0.860) or local efficiency (p = 0.417), indicating that while dynamic task-based recruitment is compromised in rMDD, the underlying resting-state network topology remains structurally intact.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that blunted prefrontal activation during executive tasks acts primarily as a neurobiological scar or a residual marker of MDD, rather than a fully penetrant genetic endophenotype. While rMDD patients exhibited clear hypoactivation, high-risk individuals displayed an intermediate profile lacking the statistically significant deficits seen in the disease group. This implies that genetic vulnerability may create a subtle susceptibility, but the full manifestation of prefrontal dysfunction is likely acquired through disease chronicity. The dissociation between the preserved resting-state network architecture and the altered task-based activation in rMDD further indicates that while the underlying functional structural potential remains intact, the recruitment of neural resources during cognitive demand is compromised. Consequently, fNIRS-measured activation holds promise as an objective tool for monitoring biological recovery, though its utility for early screening of at-risk populations requires further investigation.
