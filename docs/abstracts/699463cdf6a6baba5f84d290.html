Introduction

Typical brain maturation involves major reorganization of intrinsic functional architecture, yet the multiscale pathways linking local, regional, and large-scale network changes remain poorly defined. Clarifying these processes is key for building normative benchmarks that inform models of cognitive development and enable early detection of neuropsychiatric risk. Although resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) is central for studying neurodevelopment, most studies analyze isolated metrics or single network levels, limiting understanding of how voxel-wise, seed-based, and systems-level connectivity jointly characterize typical maturation^1,2. Moreover, little is known about how data-driven centrality measures relate to targeted connectivity patterns or how these interactions shape large-scale functional architecture across the transition from childhood to adolescence^3. Degree centrality (DC) provides an unbiased index of whole-brain connectivity density and can reveal developmentally sensitive hubs. However, its integration with DC-derived seed connectivity and network-level analyses is rarely applied in normative pediatric cohorts and has not been combined into a unified multiscale framework. Such integration is needed to map how developmental effects cascade from local nodes to cross-network interactions^4. Using a large multisite cohort of typically developing youth, we apply a fully data-driven framework combining voxel-wise DC, DC-informed seed connectivity, and network-level edge-based analyses to characterize functional maturation from ages 7–15. Our goal is to delineate age-related architecture and identify the multiscale pathways through which childhood sensory-anchored connectivity evolves into more integrated subcortical–attentional–frontoparietal systems in adolescence.

Methods

We analyzed resting-state fMRI and structural MRI data from 322 typically developing participants (7–15 years) from ABIDE I/II. Inclusion criteria required: (1) age 7–15 years, (2) IQ >80, (3) eyes-open resting-state acquisition, (4) ≥20 TD participants per site. Ten sites met all criteria. A two-step quality control procedure combined visual inspection of coverage, alignment, and motion artifacts with quantitative framewise displacement (FD) thresholds (group mean + 2 SD). No participants were excluded, yielding 169 children (7–10.99 years) and 153 adolescents (11–14.99 years). Preprocessing was performed using DPABI/DPARSF (SPM12). The pipeline included discarding the first 10 volumes, slice-timing correction, motion estimation, nuisance regression (WM, CSF, linear trends, 24-Friston parameters), DARTEL normalization to MNI space, and resampling to 3 mm isotropic voxels. Functional data were not smoothed before DC estimation; smoothing (4 mm FWHM) was applied afterward to Fisher-z-transformed DC maps. Degree centrality was computed voxel-wise by correlating each voxel with all others in the brain, applying a threshold of r > 0.25 to reduce spurious correlations. Weighted DC values were derived by summing suprathreshold correlations and normalized using Fisher r-to-z transformation. To minimize non-biological multisite variance, harmonization was applied using ComBat/CovBat in DPABI with a 90% PCA covariance threshold, following recommended parameters for developmental multisite rs-fMRI data. Between-group comparisons (children vs. adolescents) were performed using voxel-wise t-tests with Gaussian Random Field correction (voxel p < 0.001, cluster p < 0.05). To ensure interpretability and reproducibility, only clusters showing r ≥ 0.20 (converted from peak t values) were retained for seed definition. For seed-based FC, each suprathreshold DC cluster served as a 6-mm spherical seed. Voxel-wise FC maps were computed for each seed using Fisher-z-transformed correlations. Group differences were tested using PALM (TFCE, 5,000 permutations, cluster p < 0.05). Again, only clusters with r ≥ 0.20 were retained. Network-level analyses used 28 ROIs (4 DC-derived + 24 seed-derived), from which mean BOLD signals were extracted to construct individual 28×28 ROI-to-ROI matrices. Correlation coefficients were Fisher-z-transformed. Group-level comparisons employed PALM with 5,000 permutations and FDR correction (p < 0.05), including site as a covariate. Suprathreshold edges were labeled using the Yeo 7-network parcellation to characterize developmental reorganization across Visual (VN), Somatomotor (SMN), Dorsal Attention (DAN), Ventral Attention (VAN), Limbic, Frontoparietal (FPN), and Default Mode (DMN) networks.

Results

Voxel-wise DC analyses revealed four clusters exhibiting age-related differences. Children showed higher DC in the right angular gyrus and right superior occipital gyrus, which correspond to posterior sensory-perceptual hubs. Adolescents showed higher DC in the right thalamus and middle cingulate cortex, reflecting subcortical and attentional-control hubs. These clusters were used as data-driven seeds for FC analyses. Seed-based connectivity revealed widespread strengthening of connectivity in adolescents, particularly within subcortical, attentional, and frontoparietal systems. The thalamic seed showed greater adolescent FC with the basal ganglia (pallidum, putamen, caudate), inferior temporal cortex, and precentral gyrus. The superior occipital seed showed increased adolescent FC with precuneus and hippocampus, while children displayed stronger FC with thalamus. The middle cingulate seed showed enhanced adolescent FC with frontal, temporal, and posterior midline regions, reflecting maturation of attentional and integrative hubs.

At the network level, adolescents exhibited systematic increases in ROI-to-ROI connectivity across multiple large-scale systems. The Ventral Attention Network emerged as a central integrative system, and most VAN nodes, including middle cingulate, middle frontal gyrus, putamen, and pallidum, showed stronger FC with SMN, VN, DAN, and FPN. These increases included both intra-network strengthening and extensive cross-network integration. Subcortical structures showed widespread adolescent increases in FC, particularly involving the basal ganglia and thalamus with VAN and FPN nodes. The FPN exhibited selective increases, with adolescents showing greater FC between frontoparietal regions and subcortical and VAN nodes, indicating an age-related strengthening of systems supporting cognitive control and flexible goal-directed behavior. The SMN displayed heightened adolescent integration with subcortical and salience-related nodes, suggesting increased coordination between sensorimotor and motivational systems. The DAN exhibited targeted strengthening centered on the precuneus and inferior temporal gyrus, indicating maturation of distributed attentional regulation. Together, these multilevel findings reveal a coherent developmental transition in which children rely more on posterior sensory-anchored hubs, whereas adolescents increasingly recruit subcortical, attentional, and frontoparietal systems that support flexible control, internal monitoring, and integrative behavior.

Conclusion

This multiscale rs-fMRI study provides a comprehensive characterization of normative functional maturation from childhood to adolescence. Across voxel-wise, regional, and network levels, results converge on a developmental shift from sensory-anchored posterior connectivity toward increasingly integrated subcortical–attentional–frontoparietal systems^1,5. Enhanced adolescent DC and FC within the thalamus, basal ganglia, middle cingulate cortex, and frontoparietal hubs underscore the emergence of higher-order integrative architectures that support cognitive control, attentional flexibility, and goal-directed behavior^4.

The VAN emerged as a central node of developmental reorganization, displaying both intra-network strengthening and broad cross-network coupling with SMN, DAN, FPN, and subcortical nuclei^2. This pattern suggests that adolescence is marked by refinement of salience and attentional-control systems that coordinate internal states with behaviorally relevant information. By integrating voxel-wise DC, DC-derived seed analyses, and edge-based network construction, this study uniquely maps how developmental effects propagate across scales^3. The consistency of findings across analytic levels demonstrates that typical maturation reflects coordinated restructuring rather than isolated regional changes. 

These results establish a normative reference framework valuable for interpreting individual variability and detecting early deviations associated with neurodevelopmental risk. The use of large multisite datasets, harmonization procedures, and conservative effect-size thresholds strengthens reproducibility and provides a transparent analytic pathway for future developmental neuroimaging. Overall, this work highlights the importance of integrative, multilevel approaches for capturing the architecture of typical brain maturation and for informing future research on both normative and atypical developmental trajectories.
