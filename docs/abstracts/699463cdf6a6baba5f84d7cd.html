Introduction

Motivational tendencies are anchored in adaptive mechanisms that ensure survival. It has been proposed the existence of a behavioral immune system (BIS) whose function is to detect cues signaling potential pathogen threats and to motivate behaviors that minimize exposure to infection1. Activation of the BIS may occur in response to external contextual markers or internal bias, depending on the individual’s perceived vulnerability to infection2. 

To ensure its protective role, this system may be hyperreactive and signal threat to benign cues1,2, influencing both perceptions and motor actions3 in order to facilitate avoidance. In the contamination subtype of obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), BIS may portray an even more aberrant response due to the hypervigilance to any clue of dirt. Furthermore, this enhanced attentional bias towards symptom-related triggers may contribute to the development of a memory bias in this disorder4. 

Consistent with the evolutionary purpose of this system, memory studies have demonstrated the relevance of disease cues, even in healthy populations. Indeed, the association of previous neutral stimuli with infection cues, seems to contribute to better memory for those, a phenomenon characterized as contamination effect5. This makes it particularly relevant to study this effect on clinical populations like OCD, to understand the extent it impacts individual and social behaviors, as the BIS can also lead to social withdraw or even discrimination against people portraying unhealthy or uncommon attributes2.

The present study aims to investigate the behavioral and neural correlates of the BIS in OCD compared to healthy controls by examining memory performance and associated brain activity during a functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) task.

Methods

Twenty-four adults with a primary diagnosis of OCD and 21 healthy controls participated in the study. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hospital of Braga (CEHB 24_2021). All participants completed the following self-reported questionnaires: the Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory (OCI-R); the Perception of Vulnerability to Disease (PVD); and the Disgust Propensity and Sensitivity Scale (DPSS). 

MRI data collection was done in a Siemens Verio 3T (26 participants) and a Siemens Skyra Fit 3T (19 participants) due to a scanner update, both using a 32-channel antenna. The protocol included a 20-minute task functional EPI sequence and an MPRAGE structural sequence. 

fMRI memory task

The fMRI task comprised three main phases: encoding, distractor, and recognition, to evaluate memory associated with contamination vs healthy cues. 

During the encoding phase, each block consisted of three pictures of objects presented during 5s and associated with a phrase that could add a contamination or healthy meaning to the picture. Participants were instructed to pay attention to the pictures because after each block, they would have to decide which cue (i.e., healthy or contamination) was associated with each object, but without telling them that it was a memory task. Participants were also told that they would have other tasks to perform, but that it would be easy to understand the instructions presented to them on the MRI screen. The distractor task comprised the discrimination of even or odd numbers. The task ended with the recognition phase, in which the 24 images of objects previously seen by the participants in the encoding phase were mixed with 24 new pictures to assess the retention of the learned material. The use of any mnesic strategies during the task was assessed on the end of the recognition to control for the accidental nature of the experiment.  After the task, debriefing was offered to the participants to reduce the feeling of distrust, and to explain the rationale of not explaining the nature of the memory task due to the effect it would have on the performance.

MRI preprocessing and analysis

Preprocessing was done using fMRIPrep 25. Images were smoothed with a 4 mm kernel, and first-level analyses were performed using the statistical parametric mapping software (SPM12). Five contrasts of interest were defined: Encoding Contamination > Encoding Healthy, Decision Contamination > Decision Healthy, Recognition Contamination > Recognition Healthy, Recognition Old > Recognition New, Contamination > Healthy, to compare activations of contamination vs healthy cues in the different phases of the task, as well as compare activation in the recognition phase for pictures viewed during encoding and new pictures. Conditions were modeled for each participant according to the timings that each phase was presented on the screen and did not include instruction periods. The BOLD response at each voxel was convolved with the SPM12 canonical hemodynamic response function using a 128 s high-pass filter. The mean corticospinal fluid (CSF) and white-matter (WM) signals were used as covariates, as well as variables related to movement computed during fMRIprep preprocessing (the first 6 aCompCor components, framewise displacement and DVARS). Two-sample t-tests were used at the second-level to compare the groups in our contrasts of interest. Data were analyzed at the whole-brain level, and the SPM12 cluster thresholding correction was used, requiring an uncorrected p voxel of 0.001, and a family-wise error (FWE) corrected p cluster of 0.05.

Statistical analysis

Sociodemographic and psychological data was compared between groups using independent-sample t-tests or non-parametric equivalents. The scores on the psychological questionnaires were correlated between each other to understand the relationship between disease and disgust-related questionnaires and OCD symptoms. 

Results

No group differences were found for age, sex, and education years. Regarding the association between psychological measures for the whole sample, we found that DPSS was positively associated with OCI-R washing (rs= 0.424, p = .004), obsessing (rs= 0.581, p<.001), neutralizing (rs= 0.323, p = .031) and total scores (rs= 0.492, p<.001). PVD correlated positively with OCI-R washing (rs= 0.444, p = .002) and DPSS (r= 0.382, p =.010). For the patient group, DPSS correlated significantly with OCI-R washing (rs = 0.455, p = .025), OCI-R ordering (r = 0.422, p = .030), OCI-R obsessing (rs = 0.593, p = .002), and OCI-R total (r = 0.495, p = .014). PVD was positively associated with OCI-R ordering (r= 0.453, p = .026).  For the control group, DPSS was positively associated with BIS (r = 0.479, p = .028).

fMRI-task activation results

For the whole-brain analysis, there were no significant group differences for the five contrasts, at the selected multiple comparison corrected threshold. However, when considering the patient group alone, they showed an increased activation in the contrast Contamination>Healthy in the left ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (MNI: x = -20, y = 23, z = -15, ke = 18, p fwe = 0.015). When considering the encoding of Contamination vs healthy cues, all participants exhibited increased activation in the right thalamus (MNI: x = -14, y = 7, z = 3, ke = 15, p fwe = 0.037). All participants also presented increased activation on the right precuneus for the contrast Recognition Old > recognition New (MNI: x = 4, y = -59, z = 57, ke = 13, p fdr = 0.038).  

Conclusion

Conclusion

Past studies highlighted the importance of understanding contamination-driven avoidance in OCD to further improve the understanding of the disorder and treatment development.

Our results demonstrated that patients present higher activation of the vlPFC for contamination compared to healthy cues of the task, highlighting the abnormal salience of contamination cues in this disorder. Furthermore, our results demonstrate the even during the learning phase, the contamination-laden value exerts an effect, as seen by the activation of the right thalamus for all participants. During the recognition phase, a memory effect is captured by the right precuneus, where all the participants exhibited higher activation of this area when facing with old stimuli compared to new. Notably, no significant between-group differences emerged, which may be due to the clinical heterogeneity of the disorder.
