Introduction

Increasing magnetic field strength enhances fMRI sensitivity to BOLD contrast, yielding higher SNR0 and tSNR that can be exploited to achieve improved spatial and/or temporal resolution.

3D EPI suffers from pronounced susceptibility to physiological noise and artifact but is in priciple superior to SMS EPI at higher resolutions. It also offers greater efficiency in terms of SAR and acoustic noise.

Substantial technological advancements have been made to address the challenges of human fMRI at field strengths up to 11.7T and mitigate some of these limitations1. Among them prospective motion correction (PMC) using servo navigation2 which itself enables, thanks to precise k-space line matching across volumes, Phase Equalization Enhanced by Run-time Stabilization (PEERS)3.

Building on these developments, we present the first resting-state fMRI results at 11.7T using servo navigation with PEERS, and provide a comparison with 7T data.

Methods

Measurements were performed on the Iseult 11.7T scanner with a 8Tx-31Rx RF coil and on a Siemens Magnetom 7T Plus scanner with the Nova 8Tx-32Rx coil (Nova medical, Wilmington, MA, USA)

4 participants were scanned for resting-state fMRI at 11.7T. At 7T, 4 different participants were scanned using the same paradigm.

For each subject at 11.7T, a taylored pTx pulse was designed using 4 kT-spokes for the EPI acquisition, yielding and average FA NRMSE across subjects of 13%. Universal pulses were employed for 7T subjects.

At both 11.7T and 7T the imaging parameters were as follows: 3D EPI with servo navigators for prospective motion correction, whole brain 1.2 mm isotropic resolution, TR=53 ms, TRvol=2.12 s, TE=19/22 ms (11.7T/7T, gray matter's T2* for 11.7T, typical fMRI TE for 7T), FA=13° (Ernst angle for gray matter at 11.7T), AF=2x3 (GRAPPA with CAIPI), PF=6/8, 48x48 auto-calibration lines.

At 11.7T, 155 volumes were acquired while 280 volumes were acquired at 7T.

For each subject at 11.7T a 0.8 mm isotropic MP2RAGE T1w image was acquired for anatomical reference during preprocessing, although due to difficulties in the inversion pulse of subject 1, a previously acquired 7T 0.9 mm isotropic T1w was used instead for this subject.

At 7T, 0.8 mm isotropic MPRAGE T1w images were acquired.

Before reconstruction, the raw data of the EPI timeseries were stabilized using the PEERS phase equalization method. They were then fed back to the scanner for reconstruction.

Anatomical data was first passed to freesurfer 8's recon-all for segmentation and surface reconstruction and then, along with fMRI data, to fMRIprep for realignment, distortion correction, normalization and other preprocessing steps.

Individual default mode networks (DMN) were computed by extracting the timecourse of a 4 mm radius seed in the posterior cingulate cortex, cleaning it by removing nuisance confounds and low frequency fluctuations, and including it in a GLM using nilearn.

The same confounds, motion parameters, as well as their derivative and quadratic terms (24 parameters), from the realignment, as much as 15 anatomical compcor and the framewise displacement where also included in the GLM for nuisance confound regression.

DMN coverage is estimated by taking the number of voxels of the DMN maps in MNI space reaching the 3.1 z-score threshold within the DMN nodes of the MSDL atlas (where p > 0.1) as the proportion of the total number of voxels in those nodes.

The GLM was applied to the data smoothed using a 3 mm FWHM gaussian kernel is order to approach the spatial smoothness of connectivity atlases, and to perform group-wise averages.

Group connectivity matrices were constructed using the MSDL probabilistic atlas, again with the same nuisance regressors.

DMN maps, coverage and connectivity matrices were computed using different number of volumes in the timeseries (the last n volumes) to assess the stability of the results.

Results

Motion estimates derived from retrospective image realignment yielded extremely low values (mean RMS displacement of 0.04 mm at 11.7 T and 0.05 mm at 7 T), on the order of 0.05 mm (Figure 1). In contrast, motion parameters estimated during acquisition via prospective motion correction, based on servo-navigated FoV updates, showed larger RMS displacements (0.20 mm at 11.7 T and 0.19 mm at 7 T), consistent with values expected in the presence of moderate subject motion. This discrepancy indicates that PMC was effective in mitigating motion-related artifacts in the EPI time series.

The DMN was successfully identified at both 11.7T and 7T. For the same number of volumes, 11.7T data exhibited more extensive network coverage (Figure 2). Comparable DMN coverage was achieved with 145 volumes at 11.7T and 245 volumes at 7T, indicating improved sensitivity for functional network detection at 11.7T. This advantage is further supported by the connectivity matrices, which show clear network delineation at 11.7T with as few as 95 volumes, whereas 7T data with the same number of volumes fails to show distinct functional networks.

Peak tSNR values of approximately 40 were observed at both field strengths; however, substantially higher tSNR values were attained at 11.7T.

Conclusion

Although the tSNR at 11.7T reaches peak values comparable to those observed at 7T, the spatial distribution differs markedly between the two field strengths. This suggests that the noise regime at 11.7T is more strongly dominated by physiological noise4, in contrast to the predominantly thermal-noise-dominated regime observed at 7T. While this indicates a current noise limitation at 1.2 mm isotropic at 11.7T, it also highlights the potential to further increase spatial resolution, thereby reducing SNR0 and shifting tSNR toward a thermal-noise–dominated regime5.

We have demonstrated the feasibility of resting-state fMRI at 11.7T by employing both servo navigation and PEERS. Using an otherwise identical acquisition protocol to that employed at 7T (with the exception of TE), we directly compared the two field strengths and observed clear advantages of 11.7T for functional connectivity analysis. These results establish a technical foundation for exploiting the unique capabilities of 11.7T MRI and open new opportunities for high-resolution and high-sensitivity fMRI investigations at ultra–high magnetic fields.
