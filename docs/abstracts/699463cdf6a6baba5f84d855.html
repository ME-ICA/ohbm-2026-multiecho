Introduction

In the context of neurodegenerative disorders, myelin sheaths surrounding the axons are affected by physiopathological processes. Several techniques have been proposed to measure myelin content with MR imaging.

Throughout the numerous approaches, there is still no consensus on which method to use for clinical research to assess myelin changes.

Among other methods, T2 relaxometry (MESE), T2* relaxometry (MEGRASE Research Software Package) [1], inhomogeneous magnetisation transfer techniques (ihMT) [2, 3] and Chi separation [4] have been proposed. T2, respectively T2*, relaxometry relies on multi-echo spin echo, resp gradient echo sequences that provide a set of images at a fixed sampling rate along an exponential decay curve. The myelin water fraction (MWF) with short T2 values and the intra-extra cellular water fraction (IEWF) can be subsequently computed [5].

Inhomogeneous magnetization transfer (ihMT) allows to assess myelin with two variants, namely dual or sequential ihMT preparations and readouts, refered to as ihMT GRE and ihMT RAGE to compute ihMT ratios.

Finally, magnetic susceptibility source separation was also more recently proposed to compute diamagnetic susceptibility maps, called chiDia, from Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) acquisition to estimate myelin [4].

The aim of this study is to compare the MWF distribution of MESE and MEGRASE, ihMTR for ihMT GRE and ihMT RAGE and diamagnetic susceptibility chiDia for QSM in grey (GM) and white matter (WM) ROI for 6 healthy controls, and quantify these values in multiple sclerosis (MS) lesions.

Methods

Acquisition

After IRB approval (NCT05107232), brain images were acquired from six healthy volunteers (age range 28-57) at 3T (Magnetom Prisma, Siemens Healthcare, Erlangen, Germany) using five different MR pulse sequences. MEGRASE, ihMT RAGE and chiDia were also evaluated in a multiple scleros (MS) patient.

2D Multi Echo Spin Echo - MESE : 1x1x3mm3 resolution, 44 slices, TR/TE1=2720/9.7ms, ESP 9,7ms, 24 echoes, parallel imaging GRAPPA 2, 10min43s

3D Multi Echo Gradient and Spin Echo - MEGRASE research software package [1]: 1x1x3mm3 resolution, 44 slices, TR/TE1=1000/11.64ms, 32 echoes, CAIPIRINHA 3 acceleration, 9min.

3D Inhomogeneous Magnetization Transfer Gradient Echo - ihMT GRE [3]: 2x2x2mm3 resolution, TR/TE 345.0/2.62ms, prepulse: eight 1ms Tukey pulses with interpulses interval of 1.5ms, frequency offset 7kHz, 10min29s.

3D Inhomogeneous Magnetization Transfer Rapid Gradient Echo - ihMT RAGE [2] : 2x2x2 mm3 resolution, TR/TE=2000/4.01ms, prepulse: ten 5ms Tukey pulses every 100ms, frequency offset 7kHz, 12min14s.

QSM for chiDia : 1x1x1 mm3 resolution, TR/TE1=31/2.65ms, 6 echoes, parallel imaging GRAPPA 3, 290 Hz/Px bandwith, 6min18s

Data Analysis

MESE and MEGRASE images were preprocessed - denoising using total variation and registration to 3DT1 using FMRIB’s Linear Registration Tool (FLIRT). MWF maps for both relaxometry sequences were then estimated using regularized Non-Negative Least Squares (NNLS) with L-Curve method, including stimulated echo correction and flip angle optimization, according to [5].

The preprocessing of ihMT images consisted of a denoising using non-local means from Anima toolbox, and motion correction of the data using MCFLIRT. Due to its structural organization and composition, single-offset radiofrequency irradiation excites the macromolecular pool, inducing transitions among Zeeman magnetization, dipolar magnetization (arising from interactions between protons and lipid chains) and the aqueous pool. In contrast, dual-offset radiofrequency irradiation results in magnetization transfer only with the aqueous pool.

By combining these two types of images, myelin content - a major contributor to dipolar interactions can be estimated (ihMT ratio) by averaging both the single and dual offset, then normalizing it by the reference image (MT0), therefore subtracting both images and multiplying by a factor two. chiDia processing was done using the deep-learning approach by [4]. Local field perturbation was estimated after phrase unwrapping and echo combination to perform background field removal. R2* maps were generated and source separation was performed to generate paramagnatic (chiPara) and diamagnetic (chiDia) and total (chiTot) susceptibility maps.

We selected 6 regions of interest (ROI), including 3 in GM and 3 in WM, as well as the entire WM mask, segmented using Freesurfer. For the MS patient, lesions were segmented from the 3D FLAIR data using a neural network, U-Net. Then, the mean of 7 measures for each ROI were computed for each subject.

Results

Figure (a) and (b) shows higher ratios obtained for white matter as compared to grey matter. MEGRASE (blue) shows higher MWF as compared to MESE (orange). The latter shows lower MWF than the average values reported in the literature yet with varying methods. ihMT GRE (green) and RAGE (red) show less inter-subject variability than the other methods. IhMT GRE yields lower ihMT ratios and have less dispersion in all ROIs as compared to ihMT RAGE.

In Figure (c) and (d), ChiDia and ihMT RAGE (orange) show lower values in the mean of all lesions and in a single big lesion as compared to the contralateral ROIs and more sensitivity to differences as compared to the MWF obtained with MEGRASE.

Conclusion

MEGRASE yield higher MWF as compared to MESE and ihMT RAGE implementation with simultaneous ihMT preparation yields higher ihMT ratios as compared to ihMT GRE with sequential ihMT preparation.

Besides, the different sequences and metrics show variable inter-subject variability. In lesions, ihMT RAGE and ChiDia show stronger differences between lesional and contralateral non-lesional tissue as compared to MEGRASE, suggesting these approaches are more sensitive to detect tissue alterations in MS.
