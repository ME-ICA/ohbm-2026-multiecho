Introduction

Among the different non-invasive brain stimulation (NIBS) methods, transcranial ultrasound stimulation (TUS) offers the opportunity to modulate neurons in deep brain regions with high spatial accuracy.1 This accuracy, however, relies heavily on numerical simulations that predict the distribution of sound waves emitted by the transducer as they pass through various types of brain tissues. To ensure that stimulation is accurately focused and delivered with the correct intensity, it is essential to validate the focus location. One theoretically effective method for validating these factors is Magnetic Resonance Acoustic Radiation Force Imaging (MR-ARFI).2,3 This technique leverages changes in the MR signal phase, which occur as a result of submillimeter tissue displacements induced by ultrasound sonication. These changes provide critical information about the location and intensity of the ultrasound focus. Here we show an approach that combines the use of steerable transducers, neuronavigation, MR-ARFI and tailored MR hardware to enable TUS at 3 Tesla with closed-loop target validation. 

Methods

Our approach has several key elements that are essential for providing a smooth workflow from sonication simulation over transducer positioning and target validation to online motion tracking during stimulation. Central for target validation is the use of steerable transducers, i.e. sonication devices where the sonication focus can be changed without moving or repositioning the transducers. This flexibility ensures that any deviations between the MR-ARFI-derived focus and the simulated focus can be automatically corrected, thereby improving the precision and reliability of the stimulation. For MR-ARFI2,3 to be performed efficiently within clinically relevant time frames, high-sensitivity MR coils must be utilized in conjunction with MR-compatible TUS transducers. For this reason, we have developed a 16-channel receive coil tailored to the geometry used in TUS/MR studies (see figure 1). In our approach, we combine 256-element steerable transducers where each element is assigned a specific phase value to achieve the intended focus shape and position, respectively. The workflow (see figure 2) comprises an initial intake session where anatomical data and functional localisers are measured and suitable transducer positions are determined. In addition, ultra-short TE images are acquired in order to calculate the Pseudo-CT maps required for sonication simulation. The specific design of the transducer mounting setup (see figure 1) allows for easy manipulation of transducer positions from the outside of the MR coil, i.e. without getting in contact with the ultrasound gel. In the actual stimulation session, the intake-session transducer positions are reproduced using optical neuronavigation. Next, MR-ARFI data is acquired and any deviations between desired and actual sonication focus assessed. This difference is then used to adjust sonication parameters without changing transducer positions. After a final validation MR-ARFI scan, the sonication protocol is executed.

Results

After testing various implementations of the workflow approach as described above, we have found a solution where the neuronavigation software acts as a central hub for controlling all aspects of the experiment to be most useful. This software is used to import sonication simulations, provide the neuronavigation part essential for correct positioning of transducer units, import and display MR-ARFI results, assess deviations between intended and actual sonication focus positions and initialising the sonication paradigm. In addition, adjusting sonication parameters based on MR-ARFI feedback is also successfully implemented in the software. 

Conclusion

Here we have presented a novel prototype that provides a solution for the typical challenges faced with in TUS experiments. 256-element TUS transducers enable versatile target steering with changes in the positioning. Using a tailor-made MR receive coil provides the high sensitivity required for MR-ARFI acquisitions. A HASTE-based MR-ARFI sequence yields high-quality displacement maps3, and a unique transducer mounting mechanism enables swift and comfortable transducer positioning. By integrating these technologies in one system, it now becomes possible to achieve highly accurate and targeted deep brain stimulation, with real-time adjustments that ensure optimal application outcomes.
