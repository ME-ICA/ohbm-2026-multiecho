Introduction

Neurofeedback (NF) is a neuromodulation approach that has been widely studied for training stress resilience. The development of real-time functional magnetic resonance imaging (rt-fMRI) has renewed the translational potential of NF, particularly by enabling modulation of deeply located limbic regions involved in stress processing. However, the high cost, limited accessibility, and immobility of fMRI pose major barriers to scalability and clinical implementation. Moreover, most NF protocols to date are conducted in neutral or low-arousal environments that do not reflect real-world stress demands, thereby creating a significant translational gap.

Methods

To overcome the limited applicability of rt-fMRI, our lab developed an electroencephalography (EEG)-based model with improved spatial specificity, derived from simultaneous EEG–fMRI recordings, enabling the targeting of deep limbic activity. This approach yields Electrical FingerPrints (EFPs) of specific brain regions, including the amygdala (Amyg-EFP) and ventral striatum (VS-EFP). The EFP framework has been validated across multiple studies and is currently used for clinical applications.^1,2,3

To address the translational gap, we further developed an immersive, gamified virtual reality (VR) stress environment for closed-loop self-neuromodulation. Within this framework, the protocol trains both downregulation of amygdala-related activity and upregulation of VS-related activity, as both play a crucial role in stress coping and controllability.^4

The VR environment incorporates two distinct interactive feedback modalities, each designed to instantiate a different coping strategy during stress exposure: a protective-oriented modality, in which participants generate a defensive shield against a threatening agent, and an approach-oriented modality, in which participants actively engage the threat by shooting an arrow toward it. These contingencies allow us to examine whether these modalities drive different mental strategies and distinct neural regulation patterns.

Results

Initial usability and feasibility pilot data suggest differential associations between neural targets and coping modalities, indicating that individual differences in neural and behavioral responses may reflect distinct threat-response tendencies.

We hypothesize that under the protective feedback modality, participants will exhibit stronger downregulation of amygdala-related activity relative to ventral striatum (VS) upregulation, whereas under the approach-oriented feedback modality, neural regulation will be biased toward VS upregulation with comparatively weaker amygdala downregulation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this work introduces a novel, scalable, and ecologically valid neurofeedback (NF) platform designed to overcome the key limitations in NF field. By utilizing Electrical FingerPrints (EFPs)-derived from simultaneous EEG-fMRI recordings- we achieve improved spatial specificity with mobile EEG, enabling the targeted modulation of deep limbic structures like the amygdala and ventral striatum. Furthermore, the integration of this advanced signal processing with an immersive, gamified Virtual Reality (VR) stress environment bridges the critical translational gap by allowing participants to practice self-neuromodulation within a context that closely mirrors real-world stress demands.
 This innovative framework, which also explores the impact of distinct protective- and approach-oriented feedback modalities, paves the way for a more accessible, effective, and clinically relevant application of NF to enhance stress resilience.
