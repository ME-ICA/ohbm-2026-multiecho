Introduction

Excitation–inhibition (E/I) balance is a core organizing principle of cortical function, supported by coordinated glutamatergic and GABAergic signaling.1,2 Disruptions to this balance, linked to interneuron dysfunction and NMDA receptor hypofunction, have been implicated in schizophrenia-spectrum disorders. 3,4 While MRS allows in vivo quantification of inhibitory and excitatory metabolites, emerging EEG features such as aperiodic slope, entropy, DFA-based indices, and neuronal avalanches provide complementary systems-level proxies of E/I dynamics.5 However, the relationship between neurochemical and electrophysiological E/I markers remains unexplored in SSD. This study investigates multimodal E/I alterations and cross-modal coupling in SSD and healthy controls.

Methods

We studied 224 participants (113 SSD, 111 HC) from the Clinical Deep Phenotyping cohort. Participants completed resting-state EEG and single-voxel ¹H-MRS in separate sessions. MRS voxels were placed in the rostral ACC (58 HC, 63 SSD) and left DLPFC (53 HC, 50 SSD). GABA, Glx, and Glx/GABA ratios were quantified using MEGA-sLASER sequences and processed using Osprey/LCModel with standard tissue correction and quality-control procedures. Five-minute resting EEG was recorded with a 32-channel 10–20 montage and preprocessed using established pipelines. EEG-derived E/I markers included aperiodic exponent, beta/gamma power and peak frequency (FOOOF), functional E/I (fEI), multiscale entropy, microstate dynamics (7-class model), and neuronal avalanche metrics (branching ratio, avalanche exponent, κ). Metrics were computed globally, across scalp clusters, and at source level using LCMV beamforming constrained to ACC and lDLPFC ROIs.Group differences were evaluated using ANCOVAs controlling for age and sex. Cross-modal associations between MRS metabolites and EEG E/I markers were tested separately in HC and SSD via linear regression. Within SSD, relationships with PANSS symptoms and BACS cognition were examined.

Results

MRS metabolites did not differ between SSD and HC, with comparable GABA, Glx, and Glx/GABA levels in both ACC and lDLPFC (all pfdr > 0.05). In contrast, SSD showed widespread alterations across EEG-derived E/I markers, including a steeper global aperiodic exponent (q = 0.003), higher fEI (pfdr = 0.016), increased beta-range entropy (pfdr = 0.016), and reduced neuronal avalanche metrics—lower branching ratio, avalanche exponent, and κ (all pfdr < 0.02), along with altered microstate dynamics (PC1; q = 0.026). In healthy controls, lDLPFC GABA robustly predicted the global κ index (pfdr = 0.001, pfdr = 0.019), whereas no metabolite–EEG relationships were observed in SSD, indicating a loss of cross-modal E/I coupling. Finally, in the SSD group, neuronal avalanche parameters correlated with clinical and cognitive outcomes: higher avalanche exponents and branching ratios were associated with greater PANSS symptom severity and poorer BACS performance (all pfdr= 0.042), while no clinical associations emerged for MRS metabolites.

Conclusion

Our multimodal findings indicate that schizophrenia-spectrum disorders show preserved frontal MRS metabolite levels but widespread disruption in electrophysiological markers of excitation–inhibition (E/I) balance. While several EEG-derived indices, including aperiodic exponent, entropy measures, and neuronal avalanche dynamics, were significantly altered in SSD, the metabolite profiles in ACC and lDLPFC did not differ from healthy controls, consistent with recent meta-analytic evidence of region- and stage-dependent MRS variability. In healthy individuals, lDLPFC GABA reliably predicted large-scale propagation dynamics indexed by κ, but this cross-modal coupling was absent in SSD, suggesting a breakdown in how local inhibitory neurochemistry relates to global network behavior. The pattern of reduced κ, branching ratio, and avalanche exponent in SSD further points to subcritical and overly stabilized propagation dynamics, aligning with models of altered cortical microcircuitry and impaired large-scale integration in the disorder. Finally, the association between neuronal avalanche indices and both cognitive performance and symptom severity highlights the clinical relevance of large-scale dynamical imbalance. Together, these results suggest that network-level E/I dysregulation, and not regional metabolite concentration alone, may be a core systems-level feature of SSD
