Introduction

Very preterm birth (VPT; ≤32 weeks’ gestation) is associated with greater risks of cognitive, behavioural and emotion processing difficulties, partly attributable to altered whole brain functional organisation. To date, most functional MRI studies in VPT samples have relied on a static conceptualisation of functional activity, disregarding temporal fluctuations and state shifts. Further, not combining resting-state and task-based fMRI limits the potential to uncover domain-general patterns of functional activity, and establish robust and reproducible relationships with outcomes (Elliott et al., 2019). The aims of the current study were to (1) characterise the spatial organisation of domain-general intrinsic connectivity networks (ICN) across both task-based and naturalistic fMRI (passively watching a video), and (2) explore differences in features of dynamic functional activity between groups (VPT vs full term born children; FT) and condition (task based or naturalistic fMRI). 

Methods

As part of the Brain, Immunity and Psychopathology following very Preterm birth study (BIPP), 198 VPT and 88 FT children (median age: 9.33 years) underwent 3.0 Tesla fMRI, completing (1) an implicit negative emotion processing task adapted from Hariri et al. (2002) and (2) passively watched a low cognitive load movie (Inscapes; Vanderwal et al., 2015). Denoised Brain Oxygen Level Dependent (BOLD) signal from both fMRI runs (i.e. task and naturalistic fMRI) were combined, and decomposed into whole-brain intrinsic connectivity networks (ICN) using concatenated independent component analysis (ICA) across the whole sample, implemented with FSL MELODIC. Dual regression extracted subject-specific spatial maps and  timeseries from the group-level ICA output. Features of dynamic functional activity were extracted for both runs, and calculated in n-dimensional space for n number of non-noise ICNs identified, using  timeseries (Nickerson et al., 2017). Whole-brain features included (1) global energy, defined as the L2 norm (Euclidean distance from origin) of all data points per participant across all ICNs, and (2) metastability, the standard deviation of the L2 norm (de Alteriis et al., 2025). Linear mixed effects (LME) models were fitted to explore main effects and interaction between condition (task vs naturalistic fMRI) and group (VPT vs FT), on both features respectively, controlling for age, sex and motion (mean framewise displacement).

Results

29 non-noise ICNs were identified (11 of which are presented in Fig. 1A), including the somatosensory, default mode, retrosplenial and visual networks. For global energy, results revealed a significant condition by group interaction (F(1,609.4)=4.67, p=0.03; Fig. 1B): global energy level across ICNs was similar for both groups during task completion. Global energy was stable during task-based and naturalistic fMRI for the VPT group, and significantly higher in the FT relative to VPT participants during naturalistic fMRI (Inscapes). Only a significant main effect of condition was identified for metastability (F(1,604.8)=16.93, p<0.001; Fig.1C), with significantly higher metastability across all ICNs during naturalistic passive viewing relative to task for both groups.  

Conclusion

Results suggest that VPT birth alters fundamental properties of dynamic functional brain activity in childhood. In FT children, global energy increased from task-based to naturalistic fMRI, consistent with heightened spontaneous signal amplitude; in contrast, VPT children showed no condition-dependent modulation, suggesting a stimulus-invariant dynamic range, possibly constrained by altered structural development. Overall, whole brain metastability increased during naturalistic fMRI across groups, reflecting a shift toward less constrained brain states. These findings highlight disrupted flexibility of large-scale neural dynamics following very preterm birth and future work will further probe these at the regional level.
