Introduction

Stroke remains the second leading cause of mortality worldwide and a major contributor to disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs). Approximately 70-80% of survivors experience persistent motor impairment that substantially limits functional independence.1 Acupuncture has emerged as a promising adjunctive therapy, with systematic reviews demonstrating significant improvements in motor function when combined with conventional rehabilitation.2 However, the neural mechanisms underlying these therapeutic effects remain poorly understood, which hinders both mechanistic insight and clinical optimization. Although previous neuroimaging meta-analyses have identified preliminary patterns of brain activation following acupuncture,3 these studies were limited by small sample sizes, methodological heterogeneity, and insufficient consideration of stroke-specific motor impairment. Therefore, this coordinate-based meta-analysis is designed to synthesize functional and structural neuroimaging evidence, in order to reveal convergent patterns of brain activation following acupuncture in stroke patients with motor impairment.

Methods

This coordinate-based meta-analysis was conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines and was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO), CRD42023447636. We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Chinese databases (China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang, and VIP) from inception to August 2025. 

Inclusion criteria required: (1) peer-reviewed neuroimaging studies employing functional MRI (fMRI) or diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), (2) ischemic stroke patients with motor impairment, (3) acupuncture interventions (manual or electroacupuncture), and (4) whole-brain coordinates reported in standardized space (Montreal Neurological Institute or Talairach). Studies were excluded if they examined hemorrhagic stroke, employed sham acupuncture without real acupuncture comparison, lacked motor-focused outcomes, used diffusion tensor imaging without functional activation coordinates, or employed region-of-interest (ROI) analysis restricted to brain regions other than the primary motor cortex (M1). Two independent reviewers performed screening and data extraction. We accommodated both between-group comparisons (acupuncture vs control) and within-subject pre-post acupuncture designs. 

Coordinate-based meta-analysis was performed using Seed-based d Mapping with Permutation of Subject Images (SDM-PSI), version 6.214. We applied an uncorrected threshold of p < 0.05 with a minimum cluster extent of 10 voxels. Heterogeneity across studies was quantified using I2 statistics, with values <25%, 25-50%, and > 50% indicating low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively. Publication bias was assessed through Egger's regression test and the Excess Significance test, with visual inspection of funnel plot asymmetry for each significant cluster.

Results

Study Selection and Characteristics

The systematic search identified 828 records across all databases. Ultimately, 16 studies met all inclusion criteria (Figure 1). Of these, 15 were fMRI studies encompassing 325 ischemic stroke patients with motor impairment (12 within-subject pre-post acupuncture comparisons, 3 between-group comparisons; 12 resting-state fMRI, 3 task-based acupuncture stimulation paradigms), which were included in the coordinate-based meta-analysis. One DTI study met all inclusion criteria but was excluded from quantitative synthesis due to insufficient DTI studies for meaningful meta-analysis (minimum n = 3 required).

Meta-Analysis Results

This SDM-PSI meta-analysis identified six significant clusters with convergent activation across the 15 included fMRI studies (uncorrected p < 0.05, cluster extent k ≥ 10 voxels; Figure 2), which can be organized into three core neural networks: (1) Primary Sensorimotor Network: Right supramarginal gyrus (Brodmann area [BA] 40, 428 voxels, I² = 0.3%) at [48, -44, 42] (Z = 2.38, p = 0.009) and left postcentral gyrus (BA 3/4, 286 voxels, I² = 10.0%) at [-38, -20, 44] (Z = 2.44, p = 0.007) showed robust activation, indicating enhanced sensorimotor integration and tactile processing critical for motor recovery (Figure 2A, C). (2) Motor Planning and Coordination Network: Medial superior frontal gyrus (SFG)/supplementary motor area (SMA) （BA 8/9, 321 voxels, I² = 3.1%) at [8, 36, 44] (Z = 2.31, p= 0.010) and right cerebellar crus II (224 voxels, I2 = 0.6%) at [12, -76, -34] (Z = 2.34, p = 0.010) demonstrated bilateral activation supporting motor planning, bimanual coordination, and procedural learning (Figure 2B, D). (3) Attention and Body Schema Network: Left precuneus (BA 23, 173 voxels, I2 = 0.6%) at [-4, -54, 26] (Z = 2.05, p = 0.020) and left inferior parietal lobule (BA 40, 19 voxels, I2=0.1%) at [-42, -40, 44] (Z = 1.76, p = 0.039) revealed consistent activation supporting motor imagery, visuospatial attention, and body schema representation (Figure 2E, F).The extremely low heterogeneity across all clusters (I2<10%) indicates highly consistent findings across diverse study designs. Heterogeneity and Publication Bias

Heterogeneity analysis revealed very low I2 values across all clusters (range: 0.1-10.0%), indicating minimal between-study variability. All clusters fell well within the low heterogeneity threshold (<25%), with five of six showing I2<1%. The left postcentral gyrus showed the highest heterogeneity (I2=10.0%), likely reflecting variability in precise somatotopic localization across different acupuncture point selections, yet this still represents low heterogeneity. The left inferior parietal lobule exhibited the lowest heterogeneity (I2=0.1%), suggesting this region represents a particularly robust and replicable neural correlate of acupuncture treatment.  No evidence of publication bias was detected across all clusters (Egger's test: all p > 0.35; Excess significance test: all p  > 0.31). Funnel plots displayed symmetrical distributions of effect sizes.

Conclusion

This coordinate-based meta-analysis provides evidence that acupuncture modulates three core neural networks in stroke patients with motor impairment: (1) primary sensorimotor cortex supporting tactile integration, (2) motor planning regions including the supplementary motor area and cerebellum for coordination, and (3) attention networks facilitating motor imagery and body schema. The bilateral activations, particularly in supplementary motor areas straddling the corpus callosum, may reflect compensatory recruitment and interhemispheric rebalancing-key mechanisms in stroke recovery. The exceptionally low heterogeneity across all clusters substantially strengthens confidence in these findings as reliable and replicable neural signatures of acupuncture's therapeutic effects. However, critical gaps remain: the timing of acupuncture intervention relative to stroke onset could not be systematically examined due to insufficient reporting across included studies. This timing represents a potential key determinant of neuroplasticity and therapeutic efficacy.5 Additionally, while we aimed to synthesize both functional and structural neuroimaging evidence, only one DTI study met the inclusion criteria, precluding meta-analysis of white matter changes. 

Future research should use multimodal neuroimaging, standardized acupuncture protocols, and detailed reporting of intervention timing and white matter outcomes to fully elucidate the neural mechanisms underlying acupuncture's therapeutic effects in post-stroke motor rehabilitation.
