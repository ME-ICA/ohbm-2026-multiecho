Introduction

Connectivity-guided targeting has emerged as a promising strategy to improve transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) outcomes in treatment-resistant depression (TRD). Prior studies show that personalized stimulation sites with stronger anticorrelation to the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sACC) might outperform anatomical approaches1,2, underscoring the growing potential of personalized connectivity-guided targeting. We recently developed personalized targeting procedure, TAO-TMS (tree-based algorithm for optimized transcranial magnetic stimulation) (Kong et al., 2025). TAO-TMS targets exhibit higher reliability, closer scalp proximity and stronger anti-correlations to sub-genual anterior cingulate cortex3. To evaluate its clinical utility in real-world settings, we applied TAO-TMS in an open-label trial of naturalistic TRD patients (N = 20) in Singapore (NCT06341803), a clinically complex cohort characterized by high comorbidity, polypharmacy, and variable illness durations.

Methods

We conducted a preregistered open-label trial in 21 adults with treatment-resistant depression (NCT06341803). The sample exhibited substantial clinical complexity, with 86% meeting criteria for at least one additional psychiatric diagnosis, an average of 4.7 failed antidepressant trials. Participants underwent two MRI sessions (pre- and post-treatment) on a 3T Siemens Prisma scanner, each including a T1-weighted scan and two 10-minute resting-state fMRI runs. Data was preprocessed using the Computational Brain Imaging Group (CBIG) fMRI preprocessing pipeline. The scalp-based BeamF3 site was also identified at the pre-treatment session for comparison.

Participants received the Stanford Accelerated Intelligent Neuromodulation Therapy (SAINT) iTBS protocol over 5 consecutive days (10 sessions/day, 1,800 pulses/session at 90% depth-adjusted resting motor threshold)4, delivered via robotic neuronavigation maintaining coil position within 2 mm of the personalized TAO-TMS target. Clinical assessments were conducted at baseline, post-treatment, and at 1- and 3-month follow-ups. The primary outcome was response rate, defined as ≥50% reduction in MADRS score in at least one post-treatment assessment within four weeks.

Personalized TAO-TMS targets in left dorsal lateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) were derived from baseline rs-fMRI in each participant’s native space. Individual-specific cortical networks were estimated using the Multi-Session Hierarchical Bayesian Model (MS-HBM), and a tree-based algorithm selected the near-scalp site within attentional networks showing the strongest negative functional connectivity (FC) to the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sACC) (Figure 1A).

To evaluate the precision of personalized TAO-TMS targets relative to standard scalp-based localization, we compared the electric field (E-field) properties of the TAO-TMS target with the BeamF3 location using SimNIBS 4.1.0 for each participant. For each simulation, the “E-field hotspot” was defined as the set of cortical vertices representing the top 1% of E-field magnitude (Figure 1B left panel).

To assess target quality, two evaluation metrics were considered. First, network specificity (on-target) was defined as the percentage of the E-field hotspot area occupied by the target networks (dorsal and salience/ventral attention networks) (Figure 1B middle panel). Second, we computed the FC between the E-field hotspot and the sACC1,2 at baseline (Figure 1B right panel). More specifically, a rs-fMRI time course was computed based on a weighted average of DLPFC vertices’ time courses, where the weights corresponded to the local E-field strength. The DLPFC time course was then correlated with the sACC time course. A more negative hotspot sACC FC might indicate better target quality.

To examine treatment-related functional connectivity changes, two participants were excluded (one missed the post-treatment scan, one due to excessive motion), leaving 18 for analysis. Changes in hotspot sACC FC (post-treatment minus baseline) were correlated with percentage improvement in MADRS scores.

Results

Participants exhibited highly distinct network topography (Figure 1A top panel). All TAO-TMS target locations were close to the scalp (Figure 1A bottom panel) with an average distance of 14.1 mm. The mean distance between the personalized TAO-TMS targets and the BeamF3 targets was 13.4 mm (Figure 2A), with a maximum deviation of 31.5 mm. The considerable deviation highlights the potential for suboptimal engagement when relying on scalp-based measurements alone.

Participants demonstrated robust clinical improvement, with MADRS reduction of 57% ± 19% (N = 20; Figure 2B). Retrospective non-accelerated BeamF3 TMS at the same site yielded only 29% ± 34% reduction (N = 23, comparable sessions = 49 ± 29)5. Overall TAO-TMS yielded 70% response rate (Figure 2C). Among patients who met typical randomized-trial eligibility criteria (N = 11), response rate was 83%. Response rates immediately after acute treatment, at 1-month and 3-month follow-ups, were 65%, 50%, and 31.3%, respectively (Figure 2C).

TAO-TMS selects a personalized target within attentional networks in left DLPFC that exhibited the strongest negative FC with sACC, showed a 21.4% stronger on-target E-field engagement of attentional networks compared with the scalp-based BeamF3 targets (p = 0.012; Figure 2D). The E-field hotspot of TAO-TMS targets also exhibited an 87.6% stronger (more negative) hotspot sACC FC than BeamF3 targets at baseline (p = 0.012; Figure 2E). These results suggest that TAO-TMS targets might more strongly modulate the attentional networks and the sACC circuit. In 18 participants with pre- and post-treatment scans, however, changes in hotspot sACC FC were not significantly correlated with percentage MADRS reduction (r = -0.18, p = 0.476; Figure 2F).

Conclusion

Personalized TAO-TMS enables reliable, near-scalp targeting of left DLPFC sites with strong anticorrelation to the sACC, producing greater network focality than traditional scalp-based approaches. The protocol was feasible, safe, and associated with robust clinical improvement in treatment-resistant depression. These findings highlight the potential of individualized connectivity-guided TMS to enhance both network-specific modulation and therapeutic outcomes.
