Introduction

Resting-state functional MRI studies have long reported altered synchronization of brain activity in patients with temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE), the most common form of pharmacoresistant focal epilepsy in adults. These findings support the concept of TLE as a disorder of distributed brain networks rather than a purely mesiotemporal pathology. However, results across studies remain inconsistent, with no consensus on the spatial pattern, directionality, or clinical relevance of large-scale functional network disruptions.[1,2,3] These discrepancies are likely driven by small sample sizes, site-related variability, and heterogeneous analytical approaches. In this study, we conducted a data-driven, connectome-wide mega-analysis in a multicenter, retrospective cohort.[4] Our goal is to establish a robust and reproducible signature of intrinsic functional connectivity (FC) alterations in TLE with clear clinical relevance.

Methods

We analyzed 3T MRI data from 580 participants: 282 patients with unilateral TLE (133 males [47%]; mean ± SD age = 32.3 ± 10.0 years, range = 18–64; 147 [52%] with a left-sided seizure focus), and 298 age- and sex-matched healthy controls (152 males [51%]; mean ± SD age = 29.3 ± 8.0 years, range = 18–60). Multimodal MRI data (T1-weighted, diffusion, and resting-state functional MRI) were uniformly preprocessed using micapipe.[5] For each individual, a cortex-wide FC matrix was computed as Pearson correlation coefficients between regional time series. Site-related batch effects were corrected using ComBat with empirical Bayes while preserving effects of age, sex, and diagnosis. Edge-level FC differences between TLE patients and healthy controls were assessed using general linear models (age and sex as covariates), followed by network- and region-level contextualization (Figure 1A). Multiple comparisons were controlled using false discovery rate (PFDR < 0.050; Figure 1B-C). Partial least squares (PLS) analysis assessed relationships between FC changes and clinical features (age, age at seizure onset, epilepsy duration, hippocampal sclerosis, and antiseizure medication resistance). Longitudinal functional connectivity alterations in TLE patients with repeated MRI scans were tested using linear mixed-effects models. Finally, in a subset of TLE patients who underwent amygdalohippocampectomy, we examined presurgical FC predictors of postoperative seizure outcome using patient-specific surgical cavity masks derived from postsurgical T1-weighted MRI.

Results

Compared to healthy controls, TLE patients exhibited both increased (hyperconnectivity) and decreased (hypoconnectivity) FC (Figure 1A). At the edge level, hyperconnectivity comprised 1,973 edges linking 272 regions, whereas hypoconnectivity comprised 4,802 edges linking 354 areas. At the network level, hyperconnectivity primarily involved connections between the default mode and dorsal attention, frontoparietal, and ventral attentional networks, as well as between dorsal attention and limbic networks (Figure 1B); hypoconnectivity was most prominent between default mode and limbic networks, between ventral attention and somatomotor networks, and within the ventral attention network (Figure 1C). At the regional level, hyperconnectivity was most marked in the middle temporal, superior parietal, rostral middle frontal, and medial transmodal cortices (Figure 1B); whereas hypoconnectivity involved the mesiotemporal and anterior lateral temporal regions, medial superior frontal cortex, and caudal anterior/posterior cingulate cortices (Figure 1C). These patterns were highly consistent across all four datasets. Regarding clinical relevance, greater hypoconnectivity was seen in patients with older age, longer disease duration, hippocampal sclerosis, and antiseizure medication resistance (r = 0.23, P < 0.001, Pperm = 0.002; Figure 2A). Longitudinally, FC strength in patients declined over time for hypoconnected edges (t = −1.74, P = 0.021) and showed a trend level for hyperconnected edges (t = −1.09, P = 0.070; Figure 2B). Finally, in terms of postsurgical seizure outcome, patients with persistent postsurgical seizures had lower presurgical FC strength in spared–spared connections (t = −1.81, P = 0.039; Figure 2C). That is, reduced connectivity among unresected regions was associated with poorer surgical outcomes.

Conclusion

Leveraging advanced functional MRI analytics in a large multicenter dataset, we characterized the spatial architecture and inter-patient heterogeneity of TLE-related functional network imbalances. We further demonstrated the utility of the measure for individualized diagnosis and outcome prediction. Together, these findings advance understanding of the pathophysiological substrate of TLE and support the clinical potential of connectome-level biomarkers in focal epilepsy.
