Introduction

Pituitary adenomas can compress adjacent neurovascular structures including the optic apparatus, cavernous sinus, and internal carotid arteries.¹ MRI is essential for surgical planning,² but manual segmentation of the adenoma and surrounding anatomy is labor-intensive and subject to inter-observer variability.³ Although deep-learning models have shown strong performance in other brain tumor auto-segmentation tasks, progress in the sellar region remains limited by the lack of well-curated datasets.⁴ This study evaluates how MRI contrast phase (pre-contrast, post-contrast, and mixed pre/post) and training data volume influence nnU-Net segmentation performance of pituitary adenomas and surrounding structures.

Methods

Fifty-two coronal 3D anisotropic T1-weighted MRI scans of the sellar region (pre- and post-contrast) were retrospectively collected and manually segmented into eight anatomical labels. nnU-Net models were trained under three contrast conditions: PreOnly (pre-contrast), PostOnly (post-contrast), and PrePost (mixed pre/post), each using 15, 26, or 52 training images. Evaluation was performed on matching held-out test sets comprising five cases per dataset. Segmentation performance was quantified using the Dice similarity coefficient.

Primary analyses focused on central structures (adenoma, optic apparatus, pituitary stalk, and residual healthy pituitary [RHP]) due to their clinical relevance and consistent contrast sensitivity. Symmetric vascular structures (carotid arteries and cavernous sinuses) showed inconsistent trends and were excluded from the main contrast comparison. Group differences were assessed using Kruskal–Wallis tests with pairwise Wilcoxon post hoc comparisons, and generalisation trends were evaluated using Spearman correlation analysis.

Cross-contrast experiments were additionally conducted by evaluating PrePost-trained models on isolated PreOnly and PostOnly test sets across training sizes to assess contrast-domain robustness.

Results

Kruskal– Wallis tests comparing contrast phases within each structure and training size identified no statistically significant differences in Dice scores for matched-phase experiments (all p > 0.05). Phase-wise Dice trends and variability across training sizes are summarised in the Dice-versus-phase plots for central structures. In cross-contrast generalisation experiments, PrePost-trained models were evaluated on isolated PreOnly and PostOnly test sets. Performance gaps were defined as the absolute difference in Dice score between matched PrePost testing and cross-contrast testing. Across central structures, the magnitude of these gaps decreased with increasing training data volume for selected targets, and the corresponding trends are illustrated in the PrePost model generalisation plots.

Conclusion

Across central pituitary structures, nnU-Net segmentation performance was not significantly influenced by MRI contrast phase when training and testing were matched. Comparable Dice scores were observed for PreOnly, PostOnly, and mixed PrePost training across all evaluated training sizes. Pre-contrast–only models achieved performance similar to mixed-phase training for key structures such as adenoma, supporting the feasibility of reliable auto-segmentation in the absence of contrast-enhanced imaging. Cross-contrast experiments further showed that increasing training data volume reduced performance gaps when models were applied across contrast domains, indicating improved robustness with larger datasets. These findings demonstrate that contrast-independent auto-segmentation of pituitary adenomas and selected surrounding structures is achievable, with training data volume playing an important role in generalisation.
