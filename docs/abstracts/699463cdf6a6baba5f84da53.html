Introduction

Linear Dynamical System (LDS) network models allow to study spatio-temporal patterns in large-scale brain dynamics. One prominent example is the relationship between empirically measured anatomical connectvivity (AC) and neural activity covariance sturcture or functional connectivity (FC). LDS models usually predict that AC and FC matrices share the same eigenspaces (modes) and have eigenvalues related by simple algebraic relationships. Although they work remarkably well in many cases, they do not capture some prominent features of empirical FC data.

Here, we develop a new LDS model. We extend an existing linear model by keeping the axiom of shared eigenspaces between AC and FC matrices and the inverse-relationship of their eigenvalues, but adjust the AC eigenvalues conditioned on empirical FC data. This allows to study changes in the eigenspecta based on properties of the empirical FC data.

We test this model on resting state data from the Human Connectome Project.

Methods

Our model extends a linear model dx/dt = Ax + ε as proposed by (Galán 2008)^1. Here, the vector x denotes neural activity at different brain regions, A the connectivity of the network (modified by a diagonal self-connectivity) and ε the driving noise (multivariate Wiener process). The asymptotic covariance F of the time-continous evolution of the system can be computed using the continous-time Lyapunov equation. Assuming that the structural connectivity A is symmetic and that the noise is iid., A and F share the same eigenspaces and their eigenvalues are inversely related.

Given empirical FC data (eFC), we first fit the linear model by computing self-connectivity and variance of noise (minimizing L2 distance between eFC and Flin). We then fit the hybrid model by optimizing the eigenvalues of A (keeping its eigenvectors) so that the L2 distance between predicted functional data Fhyb (prediction of linear model with modified connectivity Ahyb) and eFC is minimized.

We tested the model on resting-state data obtained from the Human Connectome Project (3T HCP Young Adult rsfMRI/ TR=720ms, TE=33.1ms, 2.0mm isotropic voxelsize, 4 runs with approx. 15 min per run). We extracted time-series from 68-cortical regions as defined by the Lausanne-2008-Parcellation and perfomed global signal regression (GSR) to compensate for residual noise fluctuations due to e.g. physilogical noise. We computed subject-specific FC matrices as region-wise correlation-coefficients and averaged FC matrices and log-transformed AC matrices obtained from dMRI over 64 unrelated subjects.

We fitted linear and hybrid linear model on the group-averaged AC and FC data. Since the GSR affected in particular the first functional mode (highest eigenvalue), which was highly correlated to the first structural eigenmode, we fitted that mode separately in the linear model to avoid biases. We compared fits by Pearson correlation of offdiagonal entries.

For the detailed analysis, we grouped eigenvectors into (inter-hemispherically) symmetric or asymmetric vectors based on the sign of the cosine-similarity between the vector restricted to the left hemisphere and the vector restricted to the right hemisphere (symmetric for positive sign, asymmetric for negative sign).

Results

Figure 1 Part I compares the empirical data, the fit of the linear model and the fit of the hybrid model. The hybrid model shows an improved fit (ρ = 0.50 vs. ρ = 0.33). The eigenvalues of some modes are clearly decreased (e.g. second mode), while others are clearly increased (e.g. third to fifth mode). These patterns also hold at the single-subject level (not shown here). Differences in fit are in particular visible for off-diagnal terms capturing correlations between the two hemispheres.

Figure 1 Part II shows the changes of eigenvalues for the hybrid model and visualizes the highest modes (in terms of original structural eigenvalue) and the mode with the most prominent changes. Grouping modes into intrahemispherically symmetric and asymmetric (green and red/based on sign of cosine-similarity between hemispheres), the modes whose eigenvalues are increased (for AC and FC - these are a monotone function of each other in the model) are symmetric and the first modes whose eigenvalues are decreased or remain the same, are asymmetric.

Conclusion

Based on the axiom of shared eigenspaces between anatomical connectivity matrices and functional connectivity matrices, we introduced a new hybrid model, which allows to adjust eigenvalues based on functional connectivity data.

We tested this model on resting-state data from the Human Connectome Project.

Compared to a linear reference model, our model shows an improved fit to the data, and captures some features (e.g. diagonals in intra-hemispheric blocks) better than the reference model.

The model increases eigenvalues of hemerispherically symmetric modes and decreases eigenvalues of hemispherically antisymmetric modes, indicating that functional connnectivity data is more symmetric than anatomical data would suggest.

This could be explained by several mechanisms, in particular systematic underestimation of interhemispheric connections (e.g. due to difficulties of path-tracing algorithms around the corpus callosum), correlated noise or inputs between hemispheres or problems with the data acquisition.

We plan to investigate this in the future with extensions of the model adapting to the interhemispheric structure, introducing correlated noise and testing the model on different datasets.
