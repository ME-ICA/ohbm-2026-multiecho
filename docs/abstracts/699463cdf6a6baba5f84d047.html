Introduction

Microvascular dysfunction affects both the brain and heart, yet no imaging biomarker captures their shared network-level vulnerability1,2. Unified Structural–Functional Connectivity (USFC) is a recently developed multimodal “traffic map” that models functional load along the most efficient anatomical pathways3. While USFC predicts cognition in healthy adults, its relevance to clinical populations with systemic vascular stress remains unknown. To determine whether USFC identifies neuro-cardiac pathways linking cerebral network efficiency to coronary endothelial function and cognition in women with ischemia but no obstructive coronary artery disease (INOCA), a population characterized by coronary microvascular dysfunction and subtle cognitive decline.

Methods

Thirty-three women from the Women’s Ischemia Syndrome Evaluation (NCT03876223) study underwent multimodal brain MRI (structural, diffusion, and resting-state fMRI), invasive coronary function testing, cardiac MRI, and cognitive assessment. Structural connectivity (SC), functional connectivity (FC), and USFC were extracted for a predefined “backbone” of ten high-traffic midline pathways (Figure 1a). Support vector regression and logistic classification were used to predict cognitive and cardiac outcomes. Associations were corrected using FDR4.

Results

USFC significantly outperformed SC and FC in predicting both cognitive and cardiac measures (mean R² improvement: +20.1% vs FC, +16.6% vs SC; p<0.05) (Figure 1b-d). Specific pathways exhibited robust cross-system associations: Crystallized cognition was strongly predicted by connectivity between the left caudate and superior medial orbital gyrus (r = –0.78, p_FDR < 0.05) (Figure 1e). Coronary endothelial function was best predicted by the right calcarine–inferior occipital gyrus pathway (r = 0.70, p_FDR < 0.05) (Figure 1e). USFC showed superior discrimination of low vs high coronary blood flow (AUC = 0.622) relative to SC (AUC = 0.349) and FC (AUC = 0.425), using identical feature sets (Figure 1f&g).

Conclusion

USFC reveals a shared neuro-cardiac axis in microvascular disease, identifying occipital and frontostriatal pathways whose communication efficiency tracks both coronary physiology and cognition. These results demonstrate that integrated structural-functional traffic mapping provides more informative biomarkers than conventional connectomics and highlight USFC as a sensitive tool for detecting systemic vascular vulnerability.
