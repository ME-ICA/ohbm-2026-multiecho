Introduction

Brain age prediction uses neuroimaging data to estimate an individual’s biological brain age relative to their chronological age. The brain age gap (BAG) is defined as the difference between predicted brain age and chronological age.  BAGs have been used as a biomarker for cognitive decline and neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Traditionally, BAGs are calculated as a single global value, assuming homogeneous aging across the brain. However, brain aging is inherently spatially heterogeneous, with distinct regions and networks exhibiting distinct vulnerability to aging1. Global BAGs are therefore unable to directly ascertain anatomically specific aging patterns, limiting biological interpretability.

Recent advances in brain age research have increasingly focused on voxel-wise or region-based local brain age mapping1. These approaches leverage deep learning architectures to generate maps with the same spatial resolution as the original image. Local brain age provides an avenue to explore specific biological substrates of aging, such as white matter.  Neural circuitry plays a central role in brain aging and can be examined with diffusion MRI tractography, which offers a unique opportunity to characterize these age-related structural changes by enabling in vivo reconstruction of three-dimensional white matter streamlines2. Here, we introduce a tractography-based framework that projects voxel-wise local brain age gap (LBAG) estimates onto anatomically defined white matter bundles. To our knowledge, this is the first application of LBAG mapping directly within tractography-derived pathways. Using the Human Connectome Project3 dataset (N = 507), we generate a population-averaged LBAG map and characterize aging patterns across the white matter connectome. We identify reliable tract-level aging markers and validate biological sensitivity by projecting onto a standard white matter atlas to quantify the signal stability and sex-specific differences.

Methods

High-resolution structural MRI data were obtained from 507 cognitively healthy adults from the Human Connectome Project3 (214 males, 293 females). All participants were screened for neurological and structural abnormalities. The cohort age range was 36 to 80 years old with age distributions matched between males and females. 

LBAGs were estimated using a previously validated deep learning framework trained on structural-derived features. LBAGs were averaged across all participants, producing a population LBAG map. To anatomically contextualize these voxel-wise estimates, we used the O’Donnell Research Group (ORG) fiber clustering atlas, which includes 58 deep white matter tracts and 198 superficial fiber clusters derived from tractography of healthy HCP participants4. Rather than performing individual tractography for each participant, the ORG atlas streamlines were used to sample the population-averaged LBAG map as demonstrated in Figure 1. For each streamline point, the mean and standard deviation of LBAG were assigned from the respective maps, effectively treating the atlas as a biological sampling probe to quantify both aging signal magnitude and inter-participant variability along anatomically defined pathways.

For each tract, the mean LBAG (µLBAG) and standard deviation (σLBAG) were computed across sampled points. To capture both accelerated aging and relative resilience, the mean LBAG was used. Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) was defined as SNR=µLBAG/σLBAG. Tracts were ranked by (1) descending mean LBAG and (2) ascending standard deviation to identify high-reliability tracts exhibiting both strong signal magnitude and low variability, prioritizing pathways with stable and interpretable aging-related signals.

To assess biological sensitivity, sex-specific analyses were performed. We ensured that males and females had identical age distributions prior to comparison, resulting in matched subgroups of 187 males and 187 females spanning the same adult age ranging from 36 to 79 years old. Population-averaged LBAG maps were computed separately for males and females, and the Mann-Whitney U test compared LBAG distributions between sexes. These comparisons were first conducted across all white matter fibers. We then performed white matter tract clustering, computed the mean LBAG values, mean standard deviation value, and SNR estimation for each tract following the same procedure used in the population-averaged analysis described above. Sex differences were further evaluated to assess sex-specific differences in signal stability.

Results

Projection of the population-averaged LBAG map onto the ORG atlas revealed pronounced spatial heterogeneity in white matter aging across the connectome. Here, positive LBAG indicates relatively accelerated aging, while negative LBAG reflects relative preservation. Rather than uniform aging, distinct anatomical bundles exhibited either accelerated aging or relative preservation within the healthy cohort. The corresponding standard deviation map highlighted marked differences in signal stability between deep and superficial white matter. Deep white matter regions showed substantially higher inter-participant variability and smaller LBAG values, whereas superficial cortical pathways demonstrated lower variance and larger BAG magnitudes. This pattern indicates that superficial tracts provide more stable substrates for detecting aging-related microstructural changes.

The mean-variance ranking framework revealed pronounced heterogeneity in tract-level SNR. Superficial parietal-temporal (mean LBAG = 1.81), superficial frontal (mean LBAG = 1.921), and superficial parietal (mean LBAG = 1.45) pathways ranked highest, exhibiting large LBAG values combined with low inter-participant variability, indicative of consistent aging trajectories across the full cohort. In contrast, tracts such as the parietal corona-radiata (mean LBAG = -0.019), corpus callosum (mean LBAG = -0.03), and striato-occipital (mean LBAG = 0.03) demonstrated near-zero mean LBAG with high variance, reflecting low reliability and increased susceptibility to noise, partial volume effects, or biological heterogeneity. 

Sex-specific analyses demonstrated robust differences in white matter aging trajectories. Voxel-wise LBAG maps for male and female were computed using a previously validated machine learning framework. Across tract-defined regions, males (N = 187) exhibited significantly lower LBAG values than females (N = 187), indicating relatively younger-appearing white matter microstructure in this adult cohort (Mann-Whitney U = 226.0, p ‹ 4.510-9 ). Tractography visualizations further illustrate the spatial distribution of these sex-specific differences across white matter pathways (Figure 2). Importantly, when analyses were restricted to high-SNR tracts, the separation between male and female distributions remained robust. For example, the superficial frontal-parietal (female mean LBAG = 2.714, male mean LBAG = 1.513), superficial parietal-occipital (female mean LBAG = 2.567, male mean LBAG = 1.438), and superficial parietal-temporal tracts (female mean LBAG = 2.067, male mean LBAG= 1.263) all showed as regions with the largest sex-specific differences in aging signal stability.

Conclusion

This study presents a tractography-based framework for characterizing the spatial heterogeneity of brain aging within biologically defined white matter pathways. By projecting voxel-wise LBAG estimates onto tractography-derived streamlines, we move beyond global brain age metrics, enabling anatomically informed interpretation of aging patterns within continuous structural connectome. Our analysis reaffirms the spatial heterogeneous patterns of aging and suggests that these patterns extend to the brain’s white matter connectome. 

We identified a subset of high-stability tracts—predominantly superficial cortical pathways—that exhibit earlier, accelerated aging-related signals compared to other tracts. These tracts likely represent core anatomical substrates of normative white matter aging. In contrast, deeper or more heterogeneous regions, such as association fibers and long-range projection tracts, displayed lower signal-to-noise ratios, indicating that localized brain age estimates in these areas are more variable. Some tracts even exhibited negative SNR, including thalamo-frontal (mean LBAG = -1.04), striato-parietal (mean LBAG = -0.916), and thalamo-parietal (mean LBAG = -0.752), reflecting mean LBAG values below the cohort average combined with high variability. Notably, several weak-SNR tracts in our cognitively normal cohort, including the parietal corona-radiata, corpus callosum, and striato-occipital pathways, have been previously implicated in AD5. The near-zero mean LBAG and high variability observed here may reflect that AD vulnerable regions are often also vulnerable to age-related degradation, such that integrity of these regions varied with participant age. Future work examining SNR in these tracts in individuals with AD could reveal more pronounced and consistent aging patterns associated with neurodegeneration.

The biological sensitivity of this framework was further supported by sex-specific analyses. Lower LBAG values were observed in males compared with females across validated tract-level regions, and these differences persisted when analyses were restricted to high-SNR tracts such as the superficial parietal-occipital, superficial frontal-parietal, and superficial parietal-temporal pathways. These tracts exhibited substantially higher average LBAGs in females than in males, indicating more pronounced aging-related signals across individuals. The persistence of these effects within high-stability pathways suggests that the observed sex differences reflect biologically meaningful variation in white matter aging rather than noise-driven effects from less reliable regions. Together, these findings demonstrate that tract-informed LBAG mapping is sensitive to subtle, circuit-level heterogeneity in normative white matter aging.

In conclusion, this analysis establishes a normative reference for future studies of aging and neurodegenerative disease. This framework enables improved biological interpretability and lays the foundation for detecting early, pathway-specific deviations associated with pathological aging. Beyond its methodological contributions, this work highlights the potential of tractography-informed brain age mapping to reveal spatially nuanced patterns of vulnerability and resilience in the aging human connectome.
