Introduction

This study analysed source activation in motor adaptation in healthy controls and stroke patients. Motor adaptation is a process of learning a previously known skill in presence of an environmental perturbation and is hypothesised to be a model of recovery/rehabilitation1. 

Methods

Nine recovered stroke patients with a stroke affecting their right hand function (median age 57 [34-78], 1 female, 1-12 months post stroke) and 9 healthy adults (median age 56[38-63], 5 female) participated. A 64 channel EEG system (ANT Neuro, Enschede, Netherlands) was used to measure brain activity whilst subjects performed a reaching task with a  robotic arm (InMotion Technologies, Cambridge, MA, USA). 

The protocol was based on 2 reaching conditions (96 trials each). Familiarization trials were performed in a zero force-field followed by motor adaptation trials where the robot applied a force-field in the clockwise direction. Source activation was compared between trials in late familiarisation (low reaching error)  and early motor adaptation (high reaching error) using EEGlab2  and Fieldtrip3. EEG data were band-pass filtered between 0.5 Hz and 40 Hz, notch filtered at 50 and 25 Hz, segmented into epochs from -900 ms to 2500 ms with respect to the movement onset. Baseline was defined between -800 ms and -200 ms and removed from the data. The data were re-referenced to a common average and decomposed using Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to remove stereotypical artefacts. eLoreta algorithm was used to perform source reconstruction with source- and headmodel Fieldtrip templates. Source data was parcellated using Brainnetome atlas. Core motor network was defined as bilateral precentral and superior frontal gyri; motor learning network comprised  bilateral superior and inferior parietal lobule and cingulate and dorsal middle frontal gyri. The data for each network were averaged for ROIS within each parcel then for all parcels in predefined two networks for all trials in each condition per each subject.

Statistical analysis used permutation two sample t-test to compare stroke and control group within each condition for both predefined networks, using t max correction to control FWER (5000 permutations4).

Results

Figure 1A shows the example evolution of source activation (power) during right hand reaching movement during motor adaptation (single healthy participant). Time 0 = movement onset (activation in left sensorimotor cortex), after which the robot pushes the hand away from the expected trajectory and error correction takes place (prefrontal, anterior cingulate and posterior parietal cortex, t=400-1100ms). At the end of the movement, the participant focuses on setting the eyes on the target on the screen (occipital cortex).

The activation pattern after movement onset reflects movement preparation, execution, and reaction to perturbation processes. Figure 1 shows that core motor network activation peaks early right after movement onset in familiarisation, expressing motor control processes. Motor learning network activation follows the core network activation in this simple reaching condition and gradually decreases during movement. In motor adaptation, both core and learning network activation peaks around 0.7s post movement onset, during processing of the movement perturbation. 

There was no significant differences between stroke and controls in familiarisation nor motor adaptation conditions, neither in predefined core nor motor learning networks. 

Conclusion

There was no significant differences between groups in source activation of the core motor and motor learning networks.  This in line with our previous work showing similar performance of both groups on the behavioural level, yet in contrast with differences in connectivity on the sensor level5. Future directions include functional connectivity on the source level to better understand motor adaptation mechanisms.
