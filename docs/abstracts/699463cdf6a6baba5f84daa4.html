Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic has resulted in substantial neurological morbidity, with SARS-

CoV-2 infection significantly increasing the risk of dementia.1 SARS-CoV-2 Associated

Dementia (SCAD), represents the most prominent neurological outcome of COVID-19,

with the highest hazard ratio among reported central nervous system (CNS)

diagnoses.1 SARS-CoV-2 gains access to the CNS via ACE2 receptors in the olfactory

epithelium, conferring marked neuroselectivity.2 Anosmia, one of the most prevalent

symptoms of COVID-19,3 reflects early involvement of the olfactory system and

implicates the extended olfactory network (EON) as a primary route of viral neural

entry.4

The EON connects olfactory sensory neurons to the olfactory bulb and medial temporal

lobe (MTL) structures, which in turn project to regions implicated in Alzheimer’s Disease

and Related Dementias (ADRDs), including the hippocampus, orbitofrontal cortex, and

medial dorsal thalamus. Disruption of this network may link SARS-CoV-2 infection,

olfactory dysfunction, and SCAD.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provides a non-invasive method to examine

structural brain changes in SCAD. Voxel-based morphometry (VBM) allows whole-brain

assessment of gray-matter (GM) differences and is widely used in neurodegeneration

research. However, the neuroanatomical phenotypes associated with symptom severity

and anosmia in SCAD remain poorly understood.

Here, we applied VBM to T1-weighted MRI data from 1096 elderly individuals from an

indigenous Andes Amerindian population to identify GM phenotypes associated with

symptom severity and anosmia relevant to SCAD. We further assessed the similarity of

these SCAD neuroimaging phenotypes to established meta-analytic models of

Alzheimer’s Disease/Mild Cognitive Impairment (AD/MCI) and olfaction. By identifying

structural brain alterations linked to SCAD, this study aims to elucidate the

neuroanatomical pathways through which SARS-CoV-2 infection may contribute to the

development of dementia.

Methods

All analyses were conducted on primary MRI data acquired from the Interactions of

SARS-CoV-2 Infection and Genetic Variation on the Risk of Cognitive Decline and

Alzheimer’s Disease in Ancestral and Admixed Populations (ISAVRAD; isavrad.org) NIH

funded (U19AG076581) study. Specifically, T1-weighted MRI scans of 1096 elderly (≥60

years of age) participants from an indigenous Andes Amerindian population were

analyzed using VBM.

Preprocessing was carried out using FSL-VBM, which involved whole-brain extraction

with BET, tissue-type segmentation with FAST, and registration to MNI space using

FLIRT and FNIRT. A study specific brain template (MNI) was created ensuring an equal

number of subjects from each group. All images were registered to the study specific

template. Non-linear registration was refined using FNIRT and modulated GM images

were generated by applying Jacobian determinants. Gaussian smoothing (sigma = 3

mm) was applied to reduce noise.

Two general linear models (GLMs) were used for statistical analysis. The first model

included symptom severity (1-4; higher scores indicate more severe symptoms) as a

regressor, while the second model used anosmia scores (0-3; higher scores indicate

better sense of smell). Family-Wise Error (FWE) correction for multiple comparisons

was applied.

Phenotypic maps of GM changes associated with symptom severity and anosmia were

thresholded at p = 0.01 and visualized using Mango software. Significant regions

correlating with symptom severity and anosmia were identified, and interpretive tools in

Mango, such as Paradigm Class, Behavioral, and Disease analyses, were employed for

further interpretation.

To assess the relationship between the symptom severity VBM map and Alzheimer's

Disease/Mild Cognitive Impairment (AD/MCI), a meta-analytic AD/MCI VBM model was

used, and the correlation coefficient was computed to determine the degree of similarity.

Likewise, the anosmia VBM map was compared to a meta-analytic olfaction model

using the correlation coefficient. A novel and significant cluster in the anosmia VBM

analysis was further explored using Mango's BrainMap-derived (brainmap.org)

interpretive tools (Paradigm Class, Behavioral, and Disease analyses).

Results

Several brain regions showed significant correlations between GM volume and

symptom severity. Regions exhibiting decreased GM volume with increasing symptom

severity (atrophy) included the left Parahippocampal Gyrus (Amygdala), left Tuber, right

Fusiform Gyrus (BA 19), right and left Superior Parietal Lobule (BA 7), left Claustrum,

right Insula (BA 13), and right Postcentral Gyrus (BA 2). Regions showing increased GM

volume with increasing symptom severity included the left Anterior Cingulate Gyrus (BA

32 and 24), left Cuneus (BA 18), left Posterior Cingulate Gyrus (BA 23), right Posterior

Cingulate Gyrus (BA 30), and left Caudate Body. Paradigm Class, Behavioral, and

Disease analyses indicated that these regions are significantly associated (z ≥ 3) with

olfactory monitoring/discrimination, explicit and working memory, and diseases including

Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD), and Mild Cognitive

Impairment (MCI). The similarity between the symptom severity VBM map and the

AD/MCI meta-analytic map resulted in a coefficient of r = 0.271.

In the anosmia VBM analysis, regions showing increased GM volume with higher

anosmia scores (better olfactory function) included the left Claustrum, right Lentiform

Nucleus (Lateral Globus Pallidus), left Inferior Frontal Gyrus (BA 44), left Tuber, right

Cerebellar Tonsil, left Medial Frontal Gyrus (BA 10), and left Inferior Semilunar Lobule.

These regions were significantly associated (z ≥ 3) with olfactory

monitoring/discrimination, olfaction, language/speech, and diseases including AD, FTD,

and MCI. The similarity between the anosmia VBM map and the olfaction meta-analytic

VBM map resulted in a correlation coefficient of r = 0.114.

The left Inferior Frontal Gyrus (BA 44) was selected for further analysis due to its unique

presentation. Paradigm Class, Behavioral, and Disease analyses revealed significant

associations (z ≥ 3) with chewing/swallowing, language, speech, gustation, and

Schizophrenia.

Conclusion

Our results support the hypothesis that SARS-CoV-2 Associated Dementia (SCAD) is

characterized by distinct neuroimaging phenotypes. The symptom severity VBM

analysis revealed brain regions exhibiting both increases and decreases in GM volume,

with Paradigm Class, Behavioral, and Disease analyses linking these changes to

olfaction, memory, and dementia. The anosmia VBM analysis confirmed that severe

anosmia is associated with reduced GM volume, particularly in regions related to

olfactory processing and dementia. Notably, the left Inferior Frontal Gyrus (BA 44)

exhibited structural changes associated with gustation and chewing/swallowing, in

addition to language and speech functions, suggesting that SCAD related

neuroanatomical alterations may extend beyond olfactory dysfunction. Comparisons

with meta-analytic VBM maps of AD/MCI and olfaction yielded low correlation

coefficients, further indicating that this population exhibits unique structural brain

patterns associated with SCAD. Collectively, these findings highlight specific

neuroanatomical alterations underlying symptom severity, olfactory dysfunction, and

potentially broader sensorimotor functions in SCAD, providing insight into its neural

mechanisms.
