Introduction

Melancholic depression is a severe, subtype characterized by two core symptoms: psychomotor retardation and anhedonia. These symptoms suggest distinct dysfunctions within the striatal dopaminergic circuitry, particularly the putamen and reward systems[1]. Although accelerated intermittent Theta Burst Stimulation (aiTBS) is an approved treatment for depression, its ability to target symptom-specific neural circuits is poorly defined[2]. While the protocol targets the cortex, its therapeutic impact likely depends on modulating subcortical dopaminergic hubs. We proposed that aiTBS facilitates recovery by structurally remodeling the striatum. We hypothesized that this plasticity serves as a specific biomarker for psychomotor recovery, distinct from improvements in general mood or reward processing.

Methods

Participants and Study Design

The study cohort comprised 61 patients with Treatment-Resistant Melancholic Depression (DSM-5) and 77 age and gender matched healthy controls. While the full cohort was utilized for baseline comparisons to maximize statistical power, 50 patients were retained for the longitudinal analysis following quality control. These participants were randomized to receive active (n=25) or sham (n=25) aiTBS targeting the left DLPFC (20 sessions over 4 consecutive days).

Clinical Assessment

Psychomotor retardation was quantified using the CORE Assessment of Psychomotor Change, while anhedonia was assessed with the Snaith-Hamilton Pleasure Scale (SHAPS). Additionally, we monitored general depression severity using both the 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS-17). Data Calculation and MRI Preprocessing. To account for baseline variability, treatment response was calculated as percentage change. Distinct formulas were applied to accommodate data properties: clinical improvement was calculated as (Post - Baseline)/(Baseline + 1), while structural plasticity was calculated using the standard (Post - Baseline) / Baseline. T1-weighted images were acquired pre- and post-treatment using a 3T Siemens Prisma scanner. Longitudinal processing was performed using the CAT12 toolbox within SPM12, with modulated GM maps smoothed using an 8-mm FWHM Gaussian kernel. 

Statistical Analysis

Two-sample t-tests compared baseline grey matter volume (GMV) between the full cohort of 61 MDD patients and 77 HCs (FWE-corrected). Mean GMV was extracted directly from the anatomically defined Putamen ROI to ensure generalizability. Given the non-normal distribution of clinical subscales, Kendall correlations were conducted to examine the relationship between structural plasticity (Delta GMV) and symptom improvement (Delta CORE, Delta SHAPS, Delta BDI/HDRS). To verify mechanistic specificity, we compared the correlation coefficients between Active and Sham groups using Fisher’s r-to-z transformation.

Results

Baseline VBM analysis identified significantly larger gray matter volume in the right Putamen in patients relative to healthy controls (p < 0.05, FWE-corrected). This structural abnormality established the putamen as the specific anatomical target for the subsequent longitudinal analysis. In the longitudinal phase (N=50), we analyzed the relationship between structural plasticity and symptom improvement using the percentage change scores described above. 

In the active aiTBS group (n=25), we found a strong and significant negative correlation between the percentage change in right putamen volume and the percentage change in psychomotor retardation (r = -0.388, p = 0.009). Crucially, this indicates that greater structural expansion (volume increase) was strongly associated with greater symptom reduction. In contrast, this brain-behavior coupling was completely absent in the sham group (n=25; r = 0.021, p = 0.888). A Fisher’s r-to-z transformation confirmed that the correlation in the active group was significantly stronger than in the sham group (p < 0.001), establishing a treatment-specific interaction.

The observed plasticity exhibited high symptom specificity. In the active group, the change in putamen volume was not correlated with changes in general depression severity (HDRS-17: r = 0.077, p > 0.05).

Conclusion

Our study identifies compensatory structural plasticity in the right putamen—rather than normalization—as the specific driver of psychomotor recovery in melancholic depression. This hypertrophic response exhibited a double dissociation, tracking motor improvement independent of general mood. These findings validate an RDoC circuit-based framework, establishing right putamen plasticity as a precise, symptom-specific biomarker to guide neuromodulation treatments.
