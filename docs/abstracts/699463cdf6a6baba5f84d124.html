Introduction

Age-dependent templates (ADTs) have been proposed as an intermediate registration step to improve brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) spatial alignment accuracy. Intermediate templates have known transformations to standard templates and are intended to reduce registration error for images structurally distant from the standard target template. Arthofer et al.1 demonstrated improved registration accuracy using ADTs selected by chronological age. However, chronological age may not optimally capture individual neuroanatomical variability, and a more individualized metric to reflect biological age may provide a more accurate basis for template selection. The Spatial Pattern of Atrophy for Recognition of Brain Age (SPARE-BA) metric uses MRI to measure the extent of an individual’s age-related brain atrophy. An age is estimated by a model by Habes et al.2 based on the extent of brain atrophy detected in a T1-weighted scan, and this estimated age often deviates from a participant’s chronological age. We hypothesized that selecting ADTs based on SPARE-BA would improve registration accuracy compared to chronological age-based selection.

Methods

We conducted image registration using (i) T1-weighted (T1w) brain MRI scans only and (ii) both T1w and diffusion tensor imaging (DTI). The T1w-only analysis included 10 participants (age range: 34-86 years) from the Baltimore Longitudinal Study on Aging (BLSA) and the T1w+DTI analysis included 14 participants (age range: 34-94 years), 8 of whom were also included in the first analysis. SPARE-BA for each scan was calculated using the model by Habes et al.2

Each participant’s brain was registered to Oxford MultiModal Template 1 (OMM-1) using FSL’s MultiModal Registration Framework (MMORF)2 with each of the 37 ADTs (range: 45-81 years) serving as an intermediate template in separate registrations (Fig. 1a). MMORF parameters were based on the example/default settings in the documentation (including 5 iterations and a final warp resolution of 4 mm). This resulted in 37 T1w-alone registrations of each participant’s brain scans using ADT intermediates. We also performed registration directly to the standard template without ADT intermediates as a baseline comparison, totaling 38 T1w-alone registrations per scan.

We then performed registrations using each participant’s T1w+DTI scans as input to MMORF (14 participants, 8 of whom were included in the T1w-alone analysis). For computational efficiency, we subsampled intermediate templates, using the ADT for every 5th year (i.e., 45, 50,…, 80), resulting in 8 ADT registrations, and one registration directly to OMM-1 (9 T1w+DTI registrations per participant). 

Each participant’s T1w scan and the standard template were segmented into brain regions using MUlti-atlas region Segmentation utilizing Ensembles (MUSE),3 totaling 147 unique regions across all brains (Fig. 1b). We applied the T1w-alone and T1w+DTI deformation fields calculated during registration to transform each participant’s MUSE segmentation to the OMM-1 template space using nearest neighbor interpolation. For registrations with an ADT intermediate, we first concatenated the warp from subject space to the ADT with the provided warp from the ADT to OMM-1. To quantify registration accuracy, we calculated MUSE label Dice similarity coefficients between segmentations for all possible participant pairs within one registration type. 

For each of the 45 unique participant pairs (10 choose 2) with T1w-alone registrations, we computed MUSE label Dice similarity coefficients for every unique combination of registration protocol pairs (38 total registrations for participant 1 x 38 total registrations for participant 2), totaling 64,980 between-participant/registration combination comparisons. We also computed MUSE label Dice similarity coefficients between all 37 ADT registrations and the segmented OMM-1 for each participant.

We repeated the above for the T1w+DTI registrations for which we have 91 (14 choose 2) unique participant pairs, and 9 total registrations for each participant, totaling 7,371 between participant/registration combination comparisons.

We used paired t-tests to compare mean Dice coefficients across these conditions:

  1. Both participants use chronological age-matched ADTs.
  2. Both participants use SPARE-BA-matched ADTs.
  3. Both participants directly registered to OMM-1.

We analyzed T1w-alone and T1w+DTI registrations separately to assess whether SPARE-BA based template selection improved registration accuracy compared to chronological age-based selection in each modality.

Results

Across all 64,980 participant-pair comparisons, mean Dice coefficients across all regions of interest (ROIs) ranged from 0.575 to 0.695 (mean=0.644, SD=0.0192) when registering T1w-alone. Across all 7,371 T1w+DTI registration participant-pair comparisons, mean Dice coefficients for all ROIs ranged from 0.538 to 0.683 (mean=0.634, SD=0.0176; Figure 2a).

Our primary hypothesis that SPARE-BA-based ADT selection would outperform chronological age-based selection was only supported by our T1w+DTI results. When both participants use SPARE-BA-matched ADTs in T1w-alone registration, mean Dice was 0.6411 (SD=0.0189) compared to 0.6378 (SD=0.0213) for chronological age-matched ADTs (paired t-test, t(44)=-1.0115, p=0.3173). When we use SPARE-BA-matched ADTs for both participants’ scans in T1w+DTI registration, mean Dice was 0.6495 (SD=0.0096) compared to 0.6479 (SD=0.0111) for chronological age-matched ADTs (paired t-test, t(90)=-3.2537, p=0.0016).

We then compared the chronological age-matched and SPARE-BA-matched groups with Dice coefficients computed from registrations directly to OMM-1. T1w-alone registration directly to OMM-1 resulted in marginally higher average Dice overlaps (mean = 0.6622, SD = 0.0145) compared to when both participants had chronological age-matched ADTs (paired t-test, t(44)= -9.0901, p-value < 0.0001), and when both participants had SPARE-BA-matched ADTs (paired ttest, t(44)= -9.5829, p-value < 0.0001; Figure 2b). In contrast, T1w+DTI registration directly to OMM-1 resulted in a marginally lower average Dice coefficient (mean=0.6413, SD=0.0135) compared to chronological age-matched ADTs (paired t-test, t(90)=6.2157, p<0.0001) and SPARE-BA-matched ADTs (paired t-test, t(90)=8.6746, p<0.0001; Figure 2c).

Conclusion

Our findings suggest modality-dependent effects of ADT-mediated registration. For T1w-alone registration direct registration to the standard template outperformed both ADT mediated approaches, contrary to our hypothesis and to previously reported improvements with ADT intermediates.1 However, for T1w+DTI registration, ADT intermediates improved registration over direct registration, and SPARE-BA-based template selection had a small but statistically significant advantage over chronological age-based registration, partially supporting our original hypothesis.

Several factors may explain the divergence from Arthofer et al.’s findings for T1w-alone registration. First, because DTI is more sensitive to white matter microstructure, and white matter undergoes more rapid changes after middle age,5 it is reasonable to infer that age dependent templates likely better represent these white matter changes across age groups and thus improve registration. Second, our sample sizes (N=10 for T1w-alone and N=14 for T1w+DTI) may not capture the full range of neuroanatomical variability across the lifespan. While we attempted to select a variety of age and SPARE-BA combinations, our small subsample from all BLSA data does not reflect every imaging phenotype. Third, we looked at mean Dice overlap across all ROIs, including individual small cortical ROIs, which could explain the relatively low (<0.7) average dice coefficient values.

In conclusion, our results partially support using age dependent templates (ADTs) as intermediates in brain MRI registration. Our results suggest that the benefit of ADT intermediates is modality-dependent, as they do not improve T1w-alone registration to OMM-1 but do improve T1w+DTI registration accuracy. Furthermore, SPARE-BA may offer a modest advantage over chronological age for ADT selection in multimodal registration contexts.
