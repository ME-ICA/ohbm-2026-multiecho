Introduction

Introduction: Epigenetic regulation of the oxytocin receptor gene (OXTR), particularly DNA methylation (DNAm), has been linked to insecure attachment, high trait anxiety, and suicidality1. However, its role in panic disorder (PD) and brain network dysfunction remains unclear. By advancing imaging-epigenetic biomarkers, this study aims to move toward precision psychiatry in PD. Within this approach, patient stratification and intervention are informed by the convergence of molecular, neural, and clinical biomarkers.

Methods

Methods: We investigated OXTR DNAm and its structural connectivity in 563 patients with PD and 210 healthy controls (HCs). Peripheral blood OXTR promoter (-934) DNAm at the three CpG sites was quantified by pyrosequencing. In a neuroimaging subset (PD=85, HCs=110), diffusion MRI tractography reconstructed the structural connectome. Network-based statistics tested the diagnosis-by-OXTR DNAm and diagnosis-by-clinical vulnerability effects. Associations with separation-related life events (SLEs) using the Cochrane Life Events Inventory2, the Korean version of Anxiety Sensitivity Inventory-Revised (ASI-R)3, and the Korean version of Scale of Suicidal Ideation (SSI)4 were analyzed. A bagging ensemble regression model was used to evaluate pharmacological treatment response prediction in patients with PD. 

Results

Results: Patients with PD exhibited significantly lower OXTR DNAm levels than HCs. Within PD, reduced DNAm was correlated with more frequent SLEs, higher respiratory fear sensitivity on ASI-R, and elevated SSI total scores. Diagnosis-by-OXTR DNAm interaction effect analyses revealed reduced connectivity across the hippocampus, amygdala, orbitofrontal, and precentral regions converging on the extended fear network. Importantly, OXTR DNAm-associated structural connectivity in the orbitofrontal and lateral occipital cortices robustly predicted the 8-week pharmacological treatment outcomes in patients with PD.

Conclusion

Conclusion: These findings indicate that peripheral OXTR hypomethylation reflects insecure attachment-related SLEs, anxiety sensitivity, and suicidality, and maps to disrupted extended fear network connectivity. It prospectively informs pharmacological treatment responses in patients with PD. In conclusion, OXTR DNAm-linked imaging-epigenetic markers may help identify high-risk PD subgroups, including those with elevated suicidality, supporting precision psychiatry.
