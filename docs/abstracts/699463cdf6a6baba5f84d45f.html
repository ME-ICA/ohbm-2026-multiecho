Introduction

Task-based (tb) fMRI is a common procedure for mapping eloquent brain areas for pre-operative evaluation of brain tumor resections. When tb-fMRI is limited, resting-state (rs) fMRI can serve as an alternative approach for presurgical mapping. The feasibility of rs-fMRI localization of functional networks has been demonstrated by using seed-based correlation (SBC) analysis and independent component analysis (ICA).^1 While SBC is an intuitive approach, it requires a priori knowledge of the seed location which has relied on either anatomical knowledge or tb-fMRI activations.^1 ICA is a data driven approach, but it typically relies on knowledge of functional connectivity networks to identify the component of interest.^2 This study aimed to evaluate two recently developed automated analysis pipelines in clinical rs-fMRI software, with SBC and ICA, respectively, by comparing their language and motor mapping results with task-based fMRI.

Methods

Automated SBC analysis and ICA pipelines were developed using hand motor and language atlases created with presurgical tb-fMRI of 291 and 281 brain tumor patients, respectively. fMRI data for each set were processed using SPM12 to generate activation t-maps which were binarized and pooled to create probabilistic atlases by overlaying them for the two functional areas: hand motor and language.^3 

A separate dataset, including tb-fMRI of 25 patients for hand motor and 28 patients for language functional areas (all with rs-fMRI), acquired on clinical 3T scanners, was used to test the analysis pipelines. The rs-fMRI was performed using GRE-EPI (TR/TE=2000/25 ms, voxel size = 3.4 x 3.4 x 4 mm3, 180 dynamics) with eyes open and fixated. A clinical fMRI software (nordicBOLD/nordicMEDiVA, NordicNeuroLab Inc., Milwaukee, WI USA) was used to process the tb-fMRI and generate t-maps with a 50% AMPLE threshold.^4 The rs-fMRI dataset was processed using the analysis pipelines developed in this work, on the nordicMEDiVA platform, to generate functional connectivity (FC) maps. Image preprocessing included motion correction, nuisance regression, template to patient space warping, band-pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz), spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM), and volume censoring (FD > 0.5 mm). Regional homogeneity (ReHo) maps were generated before spatial smoothing. FC was calculated using two analysis methods: (1) automated seeding with 10 candidate seeds, each a sphere (r = 6 mm) centered at local ReHo peaks masked by an atlas, and (2) ICA using 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and N number of components, where N is data specific. For method 1, seeds were selected in the contralateral hemisphere for hand motor mapping and in either anterior or posterior primary language area (PLA), whichever was more distant to the tumor, for language mapping. Output maps from automated seeding and ICA were sorted using weighted goodness of fit (WGOF) as the template matching algorithm to recommend an FC map that best fits the network of interest.^5 Euclidean distances between peaks of FC and peaks of tb-fMRI activations in the ipsilateral hemisphere (motor) or the PLA closer to the tumor (language) were compared between the two methods. Within the same regions, a watershed segmentation on the t-maps and z-maps with 50% AMPLE threshold was applied to generate multiple sub-regions. The initial markers for the watershed segmentation were set as the local peaks within the region of interest determined by the probabilistic template. A minimum distance between local peaks of 8 mm and minimum segmented region size of 5 voxels was enforced to remove or combine small segments. The center of mass (COM) was computed for each watershed segment and the minimum distance between the tb-fMRI activation COM and rs-fMRI FC COM were compared between the two methods.

Results

Both rs-fMRI analysis methods were able to localize the functional areas of interest, which overlapped with tb-fMRI activations under AMPLE threshold, in all testing patients. For the COM distance measurements, SBC results were overall smaller than ICA with no statistically significant differences in either hand motor or language mapping (p > .05).

Conclusion

Software was developed to implement automated SBC and ICA rs-fMRI analysis pipelines using probabilistic atlases of hand and language areas to rank recommended FC maps for brain tumor patients. The areas of recommended FC overlapped with tb-fMRI activations in all patients with extents dependent on threshold selection. For both methods, probabilistic template matching recommended FC maps with peaks and COM reasonably adjacent to those of the tb-fMRI activation maps. The distances of FC-to-tb fMRI peaks ranged between 10-15 mm, whereas that of COMs ranged between 6-8 mm. The smaller COM distances indicate that there were multiple functional foci in these areas. Overall, SBC analysis and ICA results were comparable, with SBC showing slightly closer agreement with tb-fMRI.
