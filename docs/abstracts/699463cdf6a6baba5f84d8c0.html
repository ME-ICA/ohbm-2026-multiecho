Introduction

Psychotic-like experiences (PLEs) are subclinical psychotic experiences that are present in the absence of a psychotic disorder. The clinical course of PLEs is heterogeneous, with some children exhibiting PLEs at risk of developing psychotic or other psychiatric disorders. Recently, research has begun to uncover factors, i.e., distress and persistence, that may indicate a greater clinical importance of PLEs.1 Identifying the neural bases of these factors is essential to understanding the processes underlying PLEs and their clinical outcomes.

Previous research has identified associations between PLEs and resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC), highlighting the roles of attentional and salience networks. However, the overall literature is mostly inconsistent, with different networks and contrasting directionality of relationships being implicated. This lack of reliability may stem from reliance on group-averaged parcellations, which do not account for inter-individual variability in networks. Individual-specific RSFC yield metrics are more reliable, stable, and behaviorally relevant than those derived from group-averaged approaches. Additionally, evidence suggests that individual-specific methods uncover associations that are typically masked by the group-averaged approach, which is especially relevant for PLEs, as effect sizes often reflect small RSFC impairments.

The present study examines associations between RSFC metrics and distress/persistence, and their interaction using longitudinal data from the ABCD Study in school-age youth. Specifically, group-averaged and individual-specific metrics are evaluated to examine the role of inter-individual variability in network connectivity and spatial extent and to compare methods. We hypothesize that individual-specific metrics of attention-related networks (e.g., Salience Network) would be associated with stronger and/or unique correlations with distress and the persistence of PLEs than group-averaged metrics.

Methods

Participants

The ABCD Study is a large-scale study tracking 9- to 10-year-olds recruited from 21 research sites across the US. MRI data were obtained at baseline, and PLE characteristics were defined using data from three waves: baseline (n=11,878), 1-year follow-up (n=11,235), and 2-year follow-up (n=6,571). The analyses included data from 1,040 participants with usable RSFC data.

Measures

Participants completed the previously validated Prodromal Questionnaire-Brief Child Version (PQ-BC). Consistent with previous research, distress scores were calculated as the total number of 21 questions endorsed, weighted by their level of distress. A dichotomous distress factor was coded as 1 for individuals with >= 1.96 SDs above the distress mean, and all other scores were coded as 0. A dichotomous persistence factor was coded as 1 if individuals endorsed distressing or non-distressing PLEs for two or more of the three waves of data, with transient PLEs coded as 0.

MRI Processing

            Surface generation and sampling of functional data to anatomical surfaces followed previously established procedures. ABCD data were processed through the DCANBOLDproc pipeline. High motion timepoints were identified after filtering FD > 0.3mm. Timepoints were interpolated using least squares spectral estimation. Timeseries were demeaned/detrended. Nuisance regression was applied, and the timeseries were bandpass-filtered (0.008 < f < 0.1). Spatially normalized anatomical data were segmented, and surfaces were generated via FreeSurfer. Outputs were converted to the Connectivity Informatics Technology Initiative file format. Brain Imaging Data Structure formatted MRI data were registered to a standard Montreal Neurological Institute template. FMRI data were projected to the individual’s surface and registered to the fs_LR_32k template.

Group-Based RSFC. Fourteen networks from the Gordon parcellation2 were examined: default mode (DMN), visual (VS), fronto-parietal (FPN), dorsal attention (DAN), ventral attention (VAN), salience (SAL), cingulo-opercular (CON), dorsal somatomotor (DSM), ventral somatomotor (VSM), auditory (AUD), anterior medial temporal (AMT), posterior medial temporal (PMT), medial parietal (MP), and parieto-occipital (PO). The Fisher Z-transformed averaged pairwise Pearson correlations within- and between- each network were derived.

Individual-Specific RSFC. Metrics were created using an empirically validated template-matching technique.3 Pairwise vertex-to-vertex time series correlations were computed, resulting in a 59412x59412 matrix for each subject. The top 5% of connections were retained and binarized. Dice coefficients were calculated for each vertex, and the vertex was assigned to the network with the highest Dice coefficient. Contiguous vertices were removed if the total surface area was less than 30 mm². Vertices were then parcellated based on network identity and proximity. Average time series for each parcel were extracted, and Pearson correlation matrices were computed between all parcel time series and Fisher Z-transformed. The average surface area per network was calculated. Network-level connectivity was calculated by averaging parcel-wise correlations to produce a matrix of within and between-network connectivity metrics.

Statistical Analyses

The analyses used hierarchical linear models, nested by family unit and research site. Models examined the main effects of distressing and persistent PLEs, and their interaction. For surface area analyses, models were conducted for each of the 14 networks to assess average surface area. For both the group-averaged and individual-specific RSFC metrics, 14 within-network and 91 between-network models were evaluated. Multiple comparison corrections were applied. Covariates included age, sex, and scanner type; additionally, surface area analyses included surface smoothness estimates, and RSFC analyses included the total number of retained frames.

Results

Group-Averaged RSFC 

Using RSFC metrics derived from traditionally defined group-averaged networks, there were no models that passed significance threshold. One a priori RSFC network showed a trend-level association, whereby there was a trend-level main effect of persistence for within-network CON RSFC (ß=-0.052, 95%CI: -0.113,0.009, p=.096), such that the persistent PLEs showed lower within-network CON RSFC compared to transient PLEs.

Individual-Specific Surface Area

 There was a main effect of greater distressing PLEs associated with lower surface area for VAN regions (ß=-0.073, 95%CI: -0.134,-0.012, p=.02). There was also an interaction for SAL (ß=-0.065, 95%CI: -0.127,-0.003, p=.04) network regions, characterized by a stronger effect of distress related to reduced surface area for persistent PLEs compared to transient PLEs.

Individual-Specific RSFC

For individual-specific RSFC, there were several main effects of distress, including that increased distress was associated with increased within-network SAL RSFC (ß=0.072, 95%CI: 0.012,0.133, p=.02) and increased between-network SAL-CON RSFC (ß=0.110, 95%CI: 0.047,0.172, p=.001). Greater persistence was associated with lower within-network FPN RSFC (ß=-0.084, 95%CI: -0.146,-0.023, p=.008) and a trend towards lower within-network CON RSFC (ß=-0.050, 95%CI: -0.109,0.009, p=.099). Lastly, there was an interaction of distress and persistence, whereby for between-network PMT-PO connectivity (ß=-0.093, 95%CI: -0.155,-0.031, p=.003), there was a stronger effect of distress related to lower connectivity for persistent compared to transient PLEs. 

All of these effects were either significantly stronger (i.e., SAL-CON, PMT-PO) or trend-level stronger (ps<.10, SAL, FPN) for the individual-specific estimates compared to the group-averaged estimates.

Conclusion

The present study examined both group-averaged and individual-specific neural metric associations with psychosis spectrum symptoms, specifically distressing and persistent PLEs. Distress, persistence, and their interaction showed associations with individual-specific surface area and RSFC for networks supporting salience attribution-related processes (i.e., CON, SAL, and VAN). Results suggest a potential role for impairments in bottom-up and top-down attentional processes in contributing to distressing PLEs, especially persistent ones. Results also support the importance of examining both network size and variation in functional connectivity, as these metrics showed several non-overlapping associations with PLEs. Notably, the study indicates that individual variation in surface area and RSFC metrics may be more sensitive to early psychosis-spectrum symptoms, revealing associations not evident in group-averaged parcellations. These findings provide support for disruption in salience processing as a particularly informative metric associated with the development of clinically significant early psychosis spectrum symptoms.
