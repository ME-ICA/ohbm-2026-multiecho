Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and schizophrenia spectrum disorder (SCZ) share several genetic factors, which might cause overlapping social deficits^1. The central nervous system contains neurons and glial cells (including astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and microglia), which are composed of gray matter and are crucial for brain development and pathological processes. Although the structural imaging data could assist us in estimating the gray matter volume of an individual’s cortex, the number, density, and soma size of neurons, as well as the ratio of neuron to non-neuron cells, remained unclear. Previous studies have provided evidence indicating that SCZ has reduced neuronal cell number and density^2 and increased small pyramidal neuron size^3; ASD has increased the size and density of microglia cells^4. This study aimed to compare the resting-state functional connectivity of the region of interest (ROI) identified in our previous work as having volumetric differences between ASD and SCZ.      

Methods

SCZ, ASD, and HC were aged 18-28. SCZ group included schizophrenia, schizophreniform disorder, and first-episode psychosis according to the Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth Edition (DSM-IV). The diagnostic criteria for ASD are based on the DSM-IV and DSM-5. Structural and rs-fMRI images were acquired on a 3T MRI scanner (Siemens Magnetom Tim Trio) using a 32-channel phased-arrayed head coil. All participants were verbally instructed to remain still to complete a 6-minute eyes-closed rs-fMRI scan (180 echo-planar imaging (EPI) volumes; TR=2000ms; TE=24ms; flip angle=90°; field of view (FOV)=256×256mm2; matrix size=64×64; 34 axial slices acquired in an interleaved ascending order; slice thickness=3mm; voxel size=4×4×3mm3). Wakefulness was checked by verbal responses at the end of scans. Anatomical images were acquired using a T1-weighted magnetization prepared rapid gradient echo sequence (TR=2000ms; TE=2.98ms; TI=900ms; flip angle=9°; FOV=256×256mm2; matrix size=256×256×192; voxel size=1mm3 isotropic). CONN toolbox (https://web.conn-toolbox.org/) implemented in SPM12 (http://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/spm/software/spm12/). The general linear model was introduced to control covariates, including age and sex.

Results

The results of our previous work showed that the 16 ROIs were larger in ASD than SCZ, but there was no significant difference between individuals with psychiatric disorders and healthy controls. These 16 ROIs included the right angular gyrus, right fusiform gyrus, bilateral inferior temporal gyrus, bilateral lingual gyrus, right medial frontal cortex, right opercular and orbital parts of the inferior frontal gyrus, right precuneus, bilateral parahippocampal gyrus, left posterior orbital gyrus, right subcallosal area, right temporal pole, and right transverse temporal gyrus. This study included 62 SCZ (52 males), 26 ASD (23 males), and 89 HC (78 males). The mean age (standard deviation, SD) for SCZ was 22.93 (2.56), for ASD was 21.93 (2.90), and for HC was 22.55 (2.86). The ASD group was the youngest, and the SCZ group had the lowest Full-scale IQ. The results of CONN show statistically significant functional connectivity differences across the three groups (size p-FEW < 0.01 and peak p-FEW < 0.05), including bilateral frontal lobes, left middle frontal gyrus, anterior and posterior divisions of the cingulated gyrus, right lingual gyrus, and left operculum cortex.

Conclusions: We found significant differences in salience and visual network between the three groups^5, and that alterations in functional connectivity of the frontal and lingual gyri were concurrent with gray matter volume alterations. The results suggested that the volumetric alteration was not directly reflecting neural activity.  

Conclusion

We found significant differences in salience and visual network between the three groups^5, and that alterations in functional connectivity of the frontal and lingual gyri were concurrent with gray matter volume alterations. The results suggested that the volumetric alteration was not directly reflecting neural activity.
