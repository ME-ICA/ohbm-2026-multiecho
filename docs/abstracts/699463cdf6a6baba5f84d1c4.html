Introduction

Surgery is the most effective treatment for patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy when the epileptogenic zone can be precisely identified and removed [1]. Resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) is a promising approach to delineate the epileptogenic zone, as it is non-invasive and easy to implement in clinical settings. Brain activity metrics derived from resting-state fMRI such as Regional Homogeneity (ReHo), Amplitude of Low-Frequency Fluctuations (ALFF), and Fractional Amplitude of Low-Frequency Fluctuations (fALFF), have been shown to provide good sensitivity to localize the epileptogenic zone 1, Additionally, while most rs-fMRI studies have quantified these metrics in a static manner over entire scans, dynamic approaches assessing temporal variations of the metrics within the scan can provide complementary information about the epileptogenic zone 2. However, the mechanisms at origin of these dynamic fluctuations remain unclear; it has been suggested they may be caused by variations in the occurrence of interictal epileptic discharges (IEDs) during the scan 2.  

Assessing the influence of IEDs on rs-fMRI metrics would require the simultaneous acquisition of EEG during the fMRI scan. However, conventional EEG recorded at the surface of the scalp provides little information about the precise localization of IEDs in brain areas where rs-fMRI metrics are quantified. The current study thus leverages the recording of intracranial EEG using implanted electrodes during the fMRI scan to directly examine how temporal variations in the occurrence of IEDs affect dynamic ReHo, ALFF, and fALFF values.

Methods

This study involved 41 patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy who underwent simultaneous intracranial EEG-fMRI (iEEG-fMRI).

MRI scans were performed with a 3T GE Discovery scanner with the following parameters: T1-weighted anatomical MRI (TE 3.8 ms, TR 9.3 ms, flip angle 12 degrees, field of view of 24 cm, matrix 320 x 256 x 64, and voxel size 0.47 mm x 0.47 mm x 2.00 mm) and EPI fMRI (TE 30 ms, TR 1500 ms, flip angle 65 degrees, field of view 24 cm, matrix 64 x 64, and voxel size 3.75 mm x 3.75 mm x 5.00 mm, 1 hour scan duration). During the fMRI scan, IEEG data was recorded at either 10 kHz or 20 kHz using a SynAmpsRT system and Scan 4.4 software. Following gradient artifact correction by average artifact subtraction (AAS), IEDs were manually marked in a bipolar montage consisting of adjacent contacts of each iEEG electrode.

fMRI preprocessing included motion correction, slice timing, brain extraction, smoothing, filtering, and normalization, with independent component analysis (ICA) used to remove fMRI artifacts.

ALFF, fALFF, and ReHo were calculated in 3x3x3 voxel ROIs centered around each iEEG electrode contact. Each pair of ROIs corresponding to each iEEG bipolar pairs were averaged to achieve a direct correspondence between rsfMRI metrics and iEEG channels. Dynamic variability of ALFF, fALFF, and ReHo was also calculated by computing each metric within 120-second sliding windows with 50% overlap. For each iEEG channel, the number of IEDs occurring within each window was also counted. To examine the association between dynamic rs-fMRI values and corresponding fluctuations in IED counts across windows, a linear mixed-effects model with each rs-fMRI metric as the dependent variable and IED count as a fixed effect were applied. Only iEEG channels that exhibited at least one IED were included in the regression. A random intercept was included for each patient to account for inter-subject variability in baseline metrics levels, allowing us to model the repeated measurements from multiple iEEG channels within patients while assessing the overall population-level relationship between rs-fMRI metrics and IED rates.

Results

In terms of the relationship between dynamic IED numbers and ALFF, fALFF, and ReHo values, we observed a positive correlation between the number of IEDs and all metrics. Figure 2 illustrates these relationships using the linear mixed-effects regression described above. Specifically, the top left panel shows ALFF versus IED numbers, the top right panel shows fALFF versus IED numbers, and the bottom left panel shows ReHo values versus IED numbers. All metrics demonstrated a significant positive correlation with p-values of 0.0013, 0.0075, and 0.0109 for ALFF, fALFF, and ReHo, respectively.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that dynamic resting-state fMRI metrics, including ALFF, fALFF, and ReHo, are positively associated with temporal variations in IED counts during the scan in patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy. This provides a clear interpretation for observed dynamic rs-fMRI fluctuations and indicates that rs-fMRI can capture meaningful information on local brain activity associated with IED occurrence, which could help delineate the epileptogenic zone in these patients. However, this dependence of rs-fMRI metrics on IED rates could also contribute to inter-scan variability that may limit the reliability of rs-fMRI. This will be investigated in future studies.
