Introduction

Schizophrenia is widely considered a disorder of dysconnectivity, with diffusion MRI studies consistently identifying white-matter abnormalities across major tracts. Yet findings remain heterogeneous, likely reflecting clinical variability and limitations of tensor-based metrics, which cannot resolve crossing fibers and may obscure tract-specific pathology.

Fixel-based analysis (FBA) provides an advanced diffusion MRI framework capable of resolving multiple fiber populations within a voxel (“fixels”), enabling fiber-specific assessment of white-matter micro- and macrostructure. FBA quantifies fiber density (FD), fiber-bundle cross-section (FC), and their combined measure (FDC), offering greater anatomical specificity and sensitivity than voxel-averaged approaches. Prior studies, including our own, show that FBA can detect subtle and spatially circumscribed alterations in schizophrenia; yet results remain variable, and key gaps persist.

One major open question concerns how fiber-specific white-matter alterations unfold across the course of schizophrenia. Illness duration is often used as an index of cumulative neurobiological burden, yet few studies have examined fiber-level trajectories across broad time scales. Furthermore, the clinical significance of these alterations remains unclear. Although symptoms are routinely assessed with the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), the relationship between fiber-specific disruptions and multidimensional symptom severity has been insufficiently explored.

To address these gaps, we apply advanced FBA methods to a large schizophrenia cohort spanning diverse illness durations and incorporates PANSS symptom dimensions to investigate structure–symptom relationships. By integrating a fiber-resolved imaging approach with comprehensive clinical characterization, this work aims to advance understanding of white-matter pathology and its relevance to symptom expression in schizophrenia.

Methods

Diffusion MRI and clinical data were obtained from the Taiwan Aging and Mental Illness (TAMI) database, including 301 healthy controls (HC) and 218 individuals with schizophrenia (SCZ). Illness duration in the schizophrenia group ranged from less than 1 year to more than 40 years. For descriptive analyses, patients were stratified into five duration-based subgroups: <2 years, <5 years, 6–10 years, 11–20 years, and >20 years. Clinical symptom severity was assessed using the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), including positive, negative, and general psychopathology subscales. All data collection procedures were approved by the institutional review board, and informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Diffusion-weighted images were acquired on multiple 3T MRI systems across participating sites using standardized TAMI acquisition protocols. Images were visually inspected for artifacts and underwent preprocessing in MRtrix3. Preprocessing included noise reduction, Gibbs ringing removal, motion and eddy-current correction, susceptibility distortion correction, and bias field correction. Datasets with excessive motion or signal dropout were excluded based on predefined quality control criteria. Following preprocessing, images were upsampled to improve anatomical detail and fiber orientation modeling.

Single-shell three-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution (SS3T-CSD) was applied to model fiber orientation distributions (FODs) for white matter while accounting for gray matter and CSF compartments. Intensity normalization was performed using mtnormalise to achieve consistent FOD scaling across participants. A study-specific population FOD template was constructed from a representative subset of participants, and each subject’s FOD image was registered to the template using nonlinear FOD-guided registration. Fixels were segmented from the template and subject images, and fixel correspondence was established for subsequent statistical analysis.

Three fiber-specific metrics were computed: fiber density (FD), reflecting intra-axonal microstructure; fiber-bundle cross-section (FC), reflecting macroscopic tract morphology; and the combined measure (FDC), representing overall fiber bundle capacity. Whole-brain fixel-wise analyses were performed using connectivity-based fixel enhancement (CFE) with 5,000 non-parametric permutation tests. Group comparisons between SCZ and HC were family-wise error (FWE) corrected at p < 0.05.

To examine clinical relevance, fixel-wise regression analyses were conducted relating each metric (FD, FC, FDC) to illness duration, treated as a continuous variable. Additional models assessed associations between fixel-wise metrics and PANSS total and subscale scores. All statistical models included age, sex, and intracranial volume as covariates. For visualization, significant fixels were mapped onto template tractography. To contextualize fiber-level findings, tract-of-interest analyses were further conducted using masks derived from TractSeg, allowing extraction of mean fixel metrics within major white-matter pathways. Correlation analyses were then performed between tract-averaged fixel measures and PANSS dimensions to illustrate structure–symptom associations.

This comprehensive pipeline integrates advanced diffusion modeling, fixel-wise statistics, and clinical symptom profiling to characterize fiber-specific alterations in schizophrenia and their relevance to illness duration and symptom severity.

Results

Whole-group fixel-wise analyses revealed robust reductions in FD, FC, and FDC in schizophrenia relative to healthy controls (FWE-corrected). These differences were distributed across commissural, limbic, cerebellar, and thalamocortical pathways, with prominent effects in the fornix, splenium, superior and middle cerebellar peduncles, optic radiations, and superior thalamic radiation. The overlap of FD, FC, and FDC reductions indicates concurrent microstructural and macrostructural compromise.

Illness-duration subgroup analyses showed a clear stage-related pattern. Patients within the first two years of illness exhibited relatively focal reductions, mainly involving limbic and interhemispheric fibers. Individuals with 6–10 years of illness demonstrated broader involvement across posterior association and thalamocortical pathways. The 11–20-year group showed the largest FC and FDC reductions, consistent with progressive macrostructural loss. Together, these results suggest that fiber-level abnormalities emerge early but expand to long-range projection systems as illness duration increases.

To test clinical relevance, tract-averaged FDC values were regressed against PANSS positive, negative, general, and total scores across 71 white-matter pathways. Although effect sizes were modest, associations were systematic. Negative symptoms showed the strongest and most consistent relationships, with lower FDC in the anterior thalamic radiation, cingulum bundles, superior longitudinal fasciculus, and uncinate fasciculus. Positive symptoms showed weaker associations, primarily in splenial and thalamo-occipital tracts. General psychopathology scores correlated with widespread reductions across long-range association fibers, and PANSS total scores exhibited broad, low-magnitude negative relationships across multiple commissural and projection pathways.

Forest-plot visualization highlighted the consistency of these effects: most tracts showed negative regression slopes across all PANSS dimensions, indicating that greater symptom severity relates to reduced fiber-bundle capacity. This pattern aligns with the whole-group and duration-based findings, supporting a model in which fiber-specific white-matter deficits in schizophrenia are both progressive and clinically meaningful.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive fiber-specific characterization of white-matter alterations in schizophrenia using fixel-based analysis across whole-group, illness-duration, and symptom-based dimensions. Consistent with prior diffusion literature, patients showed widespread reductions in FD, FC, and FDC, affecting commissural, limbic, and thalamocortical pathways. Importantly, the convergence of microstructural (FD) and macrostructural (FC) abnormalities highlights a coordinated vulnerability of long-range projection systems that is not detectable with voxel-averaged methods.

Illness-duration analyses revealed a clear stage-dependent pattern. White-matter disruptions were evident even within the first two years of illness, suggesting that fiber-level alterations emerge early in the disease course. Mid-duration patients exhibited broader but attenuated abnormalities, whereas individuals with 11–20 years of illness showed the strongest FC- and FDC-based changes, consistent with accumulated macrostructural loss. These findings suggest that schizophrenia involves not only progressive degeneration but also periods of relative reorganization, aligning with models proposing dynamic rather than strictly linear neuropathology.

The PANSS–white matter analyses extend these findings by linking fiber-specific abnormalities to symptom expression. Although effect sizes were modest, negative symptoms showed the most consistent associations with reduced FDC across anterior thalamic, fronto-limbic, and long-range association pathways. Positive and general symptoms demonstrated weaker but systematic relationships involving splenial and posterior thalamocortical fibers. These results indicate that symptom burden reflects distributed disruptions in fiber-bundle capacity rather than focal tract abnormalities, supporting a network-level interpretation of clinical heterogeneity in schizophrenia.

Together, the whole-group, illness-duration, and PANSS regression results provide converging evidence for a fiber-resolved model of dysconnectivity in schizophrenia. FBA reveals early-emerging and stage-dependent alterations that map onto key clinical dimensions, offering a more nuanced account of white-matter pathology than traditional diffusion measures. These findings underscore the value of fiber-specific metrics for understanding disease mechanisms and highlight the potential of integrating clinical and diffusion-based features to improve stratification and phenotyping in schizophrenia research.
