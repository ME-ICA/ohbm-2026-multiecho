Introduction

Gliomas are aggressive primary brain tumors characterized by rapid malignant progression, high recurrence, and mortality rates. The 5-year overall survival rate of glioma patients is below 35%.4 It grows along white matter (WM) tracts, attacking brain WM structural network and further leading to reorganization of neural connectivity. Thus, gliomas are increasingly recognized not only focal diseases, but also network-based diseases.1 In addition to focal symptoms, gliomas cause various cognitive deficits according to their locations and growth rate. For example, gliomas located in prefrontal cortex may lead to cognition dysfunctions.4 Previous studies have examined the lateralized effects of glioma on the brain structural network using hemispheric comparisons,2 and the impact of focal lesions on various cognitive functions has also been extensively studied. However, it remains unclear how glioma-induced structural network reorganization relates to cognitive performance at the network level in the glioma patients. To reveal glioma-induced network alternations, we constructed the brain WM structural connectome for each patient, separated it into the ipsilesional and contralesional hemispheric connectomes, and applied clinical behavioral tasks to examine the association between these connectivity alternations and cognitive performance of the patients. We hypothesized that gliomas may lead to brain network reconfiguration and attack specific networks related to cognitive functions.

Methods

Subjects. We recruited 35 presurgical glioma patients (21M/14F, age = 43.09 ± 3.53 years old) in the Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center (SYSUCC). Patients completed the following tasks: the Psychomotor Vigilance Task (PVT), Go/No-Go task, and Rule Shift task. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of SYSUCC. Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects prior to the study in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Data acquisition. All the MRI data were acquired on a GE 3.0T Discovery MR750 scanner in the SYSUCC. High resolution brain structural images (sMRI) were acquired using a T1-weighted 3D MP-RAGE sequence (TR = 7.25 ms, TE = 2.96 ms, flip angle = 12°, FOV = 256 × 256 mm, data matrix = 256 × 256, voxel size = 1mm3, and 192 slices). The diffusion-weighted MRI (dMRI) data were acquired with a single-shot spin-echo diffusion-weighted EPI sequence (TR = 6,000 ms, TE = 70 ms, flip angle = 90°, FOV = 224mm × 224 mm, data matrix = 112 × 112, voxel size = 2mm3, inter-slice gap = 0, and 74 axial slices). 

Lesion segmentation and data preprocessing. Brain lesions were manually segmented using ITK-snap on the T1-weighted images and registered to the MNI152 1mm template. The dMRI and T1-weighted sMRI data were preprocessed using MRtrix3 and FSL. The dMRI data were denoised and corrected for head motion, eddy currents, and bias fields. Skull stripping was performed. The brain sMRI data underwent bias field correction, followed by five-tissue-type (5TT) segmentation. The AAL3 atlas was warped to the diffusion native space for regional analysis.5 Finally, the structural connectome was generated based on the registered AAL3 with 170 regions, each region being defined as a node. The edge was defined as the connection between two regions. For each subject, we generated weighted, undirected WM structural connectivity matrices by using two distinct weighting schemes separately, (1) the edge was weighted as the raw end-point tract count and (2) the edge was weighted as the mean fractional anisotropy (FA) value averaged across the tract. According to the location of gliomas, we separated each matrix into two 80-by-80 connectivity matrices, which correspond to the contralesional and ipsilesional hemispheres separately. The tracts that crossed into the opposing hemisphere were excluded.

End-point tract count and FA analysis. The mean values of edges for the contralesional hemisphere were calculated to determine the 99.9% confidence interval (CI). The corresponding mean values of each edge in the ipsilesional hemisphere were also calculated. The values that fell outside this 99.9% CI were considered significantly different from the contralesional counts. The ipsilesional edges with values exceeding the upper limit of the CI were classified as significantly increased edges, while those with values falling below the lower limit were classified as significantly decreased edges. For the network based on the FA, we only included those ipsilesional connections which were found to be significantly altered in the end-point tract count. The value difference for either the end-point tract count edges or the FA-weighted edges between the ipsilesional and contralesional hemispheres was calculated as the value of the ipsilesional edge minus the value of the corresponding contralesional edge.2

Results

Changes of WM structural connectivity

Compared with the contralesional hemisphere, the ipsilesional hemisphere showed significant alterations in WM structural connectivity. We observed significant altered edges either in the network based on the tract count or in the network based on the FA. Specifically, using the end-point tract-count analysis, we identified 731 significantly altered edges, including 293 decreased and 438 increased connections. In contrast, FA-weighted WM connectivity showed a predominance of decreased integrity, including 202 decreased edges and only 50 increased edges.

These significantly altered edges were then classified according to their corresponding brain lobes to indicate the changes in inter-lobar structural WM connectivity. Our analysis revealed widespread decreased inter-lobar connections in the end-point tract count. Conversely, the pattern of increased edges of end-point tract count was primarily located in the frontal-to-subcortical / cerebellar pathways. Furthermore, among these increased connections, only a very small subset exhibited a corresponding increase in FA. Behavioral-structural correlation

Spearman’s correlation analysis was performed to examine the relationship between behavioral task performance and the changes in WM structural connectivity. The analysis focused on the relative differences in edge values (tract count or FA) between the ipsilesional and contralesional hemispheres. Specifically, the decreased edges refer to ipsilesional edges with values significantly lower than their corresponding contralesional edges, while the increased edges are defined analogously. Fig. 1 shows that behavioral task performance was significantly correlated only with the value differences of decreased edges. No significant correlation was found between the value differences of significantly increased edges and behavioral task performance (p > 0.05). By analyzing the network based on the tract count, we revealed that glioma disrupts widespread structural networks centered on the frontal lobe, leading to corresponding sensory and motor deficits. In specific, we found that PVT miss rate was significantly negatively correlated with the decreased edge values in the dorsal attention network (IFGoperc-ANG) and visual network (IFGoperc-SOG), while Go trial accuracy was positively correlated with the relative sparing of sensorimotor integration and cortico-striatal motor connectivity. Furthermore, by analyzing the network based on the FA, we found that glioma compromises the integrity of WM connections, leading to higher cognitive deficits. We found No-Go trail accuracy was positively correlated to the FA value of key fronto-striatal pathways underlying response inhibition. For the Rule Shift task, although the accuracy on the incongruent trials depended on the preserved tract count and FA within the conflict-monitoring and fronto-thalamic cognitive control networks, FA abnormalities were found to correlate with behavioral performance across a greater number of edges than tract count reductions.

Conclusion

Conclusions

The present study analyzed the alternation of ipsilesional edges compared with contralesional ones and revealed glioma severely compromises the white matter structural network integrity. The observed spontaneous compensatory reorganization, characterized by increased tract counts, is predominantly in subcortical connections. However, this reorganization appears constrained due to an accompanying degradation of integrity (FA) across all affected white matter. Behavior-structure correlation analysis revealed that the sensory and motor deficits is related to the quantity of fiber tract loss, while higher cognitive functions, such as inhibition and conflict resolution, were associated with the degradation of FA. These findings suggested that white matter fiber tract integrity, rather than the quantity of tract counts, plays key roles for maintaining higher-order cognitive function after glioma injury.
