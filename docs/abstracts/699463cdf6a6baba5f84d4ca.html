Introduction

Introduction

Mental health problems frequently emerge during childhood and adolescence and can have enduring effects into adulthood. Nearly one in seven adolescents meets criteria for a psychiatric disorder, and conditions such as ADHD, anxiety, and depression show moderate to high heritability1,2. Because these years represent a critical window for brain maturation, structural and functional changes during this period may reflect emerging vulnerability to mental health problems. Prior neuroimaging studies have identified regional alterations associated with early-onset disorders, supporting the role of the brain as an intermediate phenotype linking genetic liability to symptoms.

Recent imaging-transcriptomic work has further demonstrated that spatial patterns of neural abnormalities can be related to regional variation in cortical gene expression, as characterized by the Allen Human Brain Atlas (AHBA) 3. These studies provide evidence linking micro-level transcriptional architecture to macro-level brain abnormalities. However, most existing work has focused on single disorders, relied on cross-sectional comparisons, or examined already-diagnosed adolescents, limiting the ability to characterize neurodevelopmental processes that accompany the emergence of symptoms.

Understanding the molecular correlates of onset-related neurodevelopmental changes is critical for clarifying mechanisms of psychiatric vulnerability during adolescence. Longitudinal, multimodal approaches are therefore needed to identify the transcriptional signatures associated with brain changes that precede or accompany the onset of diverse mental health problems.

Methods

Method

Data were drawn from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study, a large longitudinal cohort of children aged 9–10 years at baseline. Mental health trajectories were defined using DSM-oriented scales from the Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL). Participants with T-scores <65 at baseline and ≥65 at the two-year follow-up were classified as exhibiting onset of ADHD, anxiety, conduct, depressive, oppositional defiant, or somatic problems. Individuals with a prior psychiatric history or missing usable MRI data at either time point were excluded. The final sample included 79 individuals with first-onset ADHD, 116 with anxiety problems, 56 with conduct problems, 133 with depressive problems, 62 with oppositional defiant problems, 176 with somatic problems, and 2553 controls with consistently low symptom scores.

We analyzed multimodal MRI data processed using the standardized ABCD imaging pipeline  4. Imaging features included measures derived from structural MRI and multiple functional MRI paradigms. In total, 2040 cortical features were examined across modalities.

Longitudinal neurodevelopmental differences were evaluated by modeling follow-up imaging measures while adjusting for baseline values and covariates including age, sex, socioeconomic indicators, and scanner type at both time points. This approach isolated group-related developmental deviations while accounting for initial between-group differences. Significance was assessed using multiple-comparisons correction applied within and across modalities. To evaluate robustness, we conducted resampling analyses in which case–control subsets were repeatedly re-estimated and resulting spatial patterns were compared to full-sample maps.

For imaging-transcriptomic analyses, we focused on cortical features to enable alignment with regional gene expression from the Allen Human Brain Atlas (AHBA). For each mental health problem, only imaging measures exhibiting significant developmental alterations were retained for transcriptomic analysis. Gene expression data, comprising over 15,000 genes sampled across cortical regions, were aggregated to match the imaging parcellation.

Partial least squares (PLS) regression 5 was used to relate spatial patterns of neurodevelopmental alteration to regional gene expression profiles. For each problem, a PLS model was fit using regional imaging effects as the response and gene expression levels as predictors. Statistical significance of the brain–gene associations was evaluated using spatially constrained permutation tests that preserve hemispheric topology, as well as additional tests based on phenotype-label permutation. These procedures assessed whether observed associations exceeded chance expectations under both spatial and non-spatial null models.

Genes were ranked by their loadings on the first latent component, which captured maximal shared variance between imaging and gene expression. The highest and lowest loading genes (top and bottom 2%) were selected as those most strongly associated with the neurodevelopmental pattern. Gene set enrichment analyses were conducted to identify overrepresented biological pathways and functional annotations, including gene ontology categories and canonical signaling pathways. Analyses were repeated across multiple gene-selection thresholds to evaluate stability of enriched pathway profiles.This multimodal framework integrates longitudinal neuroimaging with transcriptomic variation to characterize the molecular correlates of brain changes emerging during the onset of mental health problems in adolescence (Figure 1).

Results

Results

We compared six groups of children who developed mental health problems (ADHD, anxiety, conduct, depressive, oppositional defiant, and somatic symptoms) with a large control group. Analyses were adjusted for demographic and scanner-related covariates.

Neurodevelopmental changes
 After correction for multiple comparisons, significant neurodevelopmental differences emerged only in the ADHD and conduct groups. No significant alterations were detected for anxiety, depressive, oppositional defiant, or somatic symptoms. In ADHD, developmental differences were observed in task-related functional activation, reflected by increased or decreased changes in beta weights across cortical regions relative to controls. In the conduct group, widespread increases in resting-state variability were identified, indicating altered temporal dynamics of intrinsic activity. Other modalities showed no significant effects. 

Associations between gene expression and neurodevelopmental patterns
 To identify potential molecular correlates of these alterations, we applied partial least squares (PLS) regression to relate spatial patterns of neurodevelopmental change to cortical gene expression. For both ADHD and conduct problems, the first PLS component showed a strong spatial correspondence between regional brain change and gene expression gradients (ADHD: rₛ ≈ 0.64; conduct: rₛ ≈ 0.65), with significance confirmed by conventional and spatially constrained permutation tests. Genes were ranked according to their PLS loadings, and the highest- and lowest-weighted subsets were retained for enrichment analysis. These components captured the dominant transcriptional axes aligned with the observed neurodevelopmental patterns.

Enrichment analysis
 Enrichment analyses revealed distinct biological themes for ADHD and conduct problems. In ADHD, positively weighted genes were enriched for biological processes related to ion transport, neuronal excitability, and synaptic signaling. Negatively weighted genes were enriched for processes involved in neurodevelopmental organization and synaptic structure. In the conduct group, positively weighted genes were enriched for pathways related to inositol metabolism and nucleotide regulation, whereas negatively weighted genes were associated with membrane and organelle organization, ribosomal processes, and synaptic pruning mechanisms. Enrichment patterns remained broadly consistent across alternative threshold choices, indicating robustness of the transcriptomic signatures(Figure 2).

Conclusion

Conclusion

Our multimodal findings show that ADHD and conduct problems are characterized by distinct yet biologically interpretable neurodevelopmental alterations, reflected in changes in task-related activation and resting-state signal variability. These alterations were systematically aligned with cortical gene expression gradients related to excitatory signaling, inositol metabolism, and synaptic organization, highlighting both disorder-specific and partially overlapping molecular pathways. The convergence on processes involved in synaptic structure and plasticity supports the notion of a shared neurobiological basis for externalizing problems. More broadly, these results demonstrate the value of integrative imaging-transcriptomic approaches for identifying mechanistic pathways underlying the emergence of adolescent mental health problems and for informing the development of developmentally sensitive, precision-targeted interventions.
