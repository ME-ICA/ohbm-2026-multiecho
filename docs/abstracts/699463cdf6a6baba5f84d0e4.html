Introduction

Moebius syndrome is a rare congenital disorder defined by non-progressive facial palsy and horizontal gaze palsy, caused by hypoplasia or absence of the facial (VII) and abducens (VI) nuclei. Brain imaging studies in Moebius syndrome remain scarce and mostly limited to small case series describing morphological abnormalities on conventional MRI. Advanced imaging has provided insights beyond conventional MRI, with diffusion tensor morphometry revealing regional volume reductions within gaze-control regions in Moebius syndrome1 . However, tensor-based methods remain limited in resolving the complex fibre architecture of the pontine tegmentum, and the microstructural organization of intrinsic brainstem pathways - particularly those connected with the VI and VII nuclei - remains poorly characterized in vivo. This study aimed to characterize fibre-specific micro- and macrostructural abnormalities associated with Moebius syndrome using advanced diffusion MRI.

Methods

In this prospective study, fifteen children with Moebius syndrome (8 males; mean age 8.58 ± 3.86 years), phenotyped in a dedicated multidisciplinary clinic, underwent 3.0 Tesla MRI. The imaging protocol included 3D T1-weighted imaging and diffusion-weighted imaging (40 non-collinear directions, b = 1000 s/mm²). To characterize fibre-specific white matter abnormalities across the whole brain, a whole-brain fixel-based approach2 was employed to compute fibre density (FD), fibre cross-section (FC), and their combined measure (FDC), and to compare patients with thirty healthy controls (17 males; mean age 10.47 ± 2.85 years). Preprocessing was performed in MRtrix3 and followed the recommended pipeline3, including denoising, Gibbs ringing removal, correction for eddy-current and head-motion artefacts with outlier slice replacement, brain extraction, and bias-field correction. After applying the single-shell, single-tissue pipeline, whole-brain statistical comparisons were conducted using general linear models controlling for age, with 5000-permutation non-parametric testing (FWE-corrected, p < 0.05). Brainstem subregion volumes were segmented using FreeSurfer (v7.3) and analysed between groups with age-adjusted general linear models (FDR-corrected). Tract-of-interest analyses examined associations between clinical measures and tract-specific abnormalities using age-adjusted linear regression (FDR-corrected).

Results

Patients showed significant reductions in FDC within pathways both efferent from and afferent to the VI and VII nuclei, compared to healthy controls (FWE corrected, p < 0.05). These included the intrinsic fibres of cranial nerves VI and VII, the medial longitudinal fasciculus, the superior and inferior cerebellar peduncles, the corticonuclear tract including the frontal eye field, and the anterior thalamic radiations. Volumetric analysis of brainstem subregions demonstrated reduced significant volumes of the medulla, pons, midbrain, and superior cerebellar peduncles in patients (FDR-corrected, p < 0.05). FDC abnormalities within the cranial nerve VI tract, medial longitudinal fasciculus and cerebellar peduncles were significantly associated with reduced visual acuity (FDR-corrected, p < 0.05). 

Conclusion

This study showed widespread fibre-specific white matter abnormalities in Moebius syndrome, extending well beyond the VI and VII nuclei to involve infra- and supratentorial motor pathways. Although the aetiology of Moebius syndrome remains debated, the presence of brainstem calcifications at the level of the VI and VII nuclei supports the hypothesis of a prenatal disruptive mechanism at the presumed site of injury. Taken together, these findings suggest that Moebius syndrome may serve as a model for investigating tracts of the intrinsic brainstem and illustrate the broad impact of early pontine disruption on white matter development within the brainstem and beyond. They further underscore the clinical relevance of fibre-specific metrics and offer a framework for studying other neurodevelopmental conditions affecting brainstem pathways.
