Introduction

Cognitive and mental health variability emerge long before the onset of clinically detectable impairment, yet the biological processes that contribute to these early differences remain insufficiently understood. Midlife represents a particularly important transitional period marked by subtle neurobiological and systemic changes that may shape later vulnerability in both cognitive performance and mental health. Circulating proteins provide a promising avenue for probing systemic biological states that may reflect early alterations in brain integrity.

While prior research has primarily focused on individual protein associations with cognitive and mental health decline, module-based proteomic approaches, where group functionally related proteins are organized into biologically coherent clusters, provide a more stable and interpretable framework for studying complex brain–behavior relationships1. Importantly, it remains unclear whether peripheral proteomic networks relate to both cognitive and mental health outcomes through shared or distinct effects on brain structure, and whether such pathways are already detectable in healthy, community-dwelling adults during midlife, when effect sizes are expected to be subtle but potentially meaningful.

In this study, we aimed to characterize the overlapping and domain-specific contributions of plasma proteomic networks to cognitive and mental health functioning in a lifespan cohort of healthy adults. Using plasma Olink proteomics, we investigated whether structural brain measures mediate associations between proteomic module expression and functional outcomes, including Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI) scores, verbal memory, visual memory, and executive function. We hypothesized that variation in systemic proteomic profiles would be associated with individual differences in cognitive and mental health outcomes, and that these relationships would be partially mediated by regional cortical structural variation.

Methods

The overall study design, including details on the dataset and the analysis workflow, is summarized in Figure 1. Participants were drawn from the Lifespan Study conducted between December 2021 and December 2024, which recruited community-dwelling healthy adults across two age strata: 104 young adults aged 21–30 years (24.1±2.67 years, 44 males) and 139 middle-aged adults aged 40–60 years (52.1±6.83 years, 52 males). All participants completed cognitive and neuroimaging assessments during the same study interval. Domain-specific cognitive performance—verbal memory, visual memory, and executive function—was assessed in both age groups, whereas Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI) was collected only in the middle-aged group.
 `         Plasma samples were analyzed using Olink Explore HT panel, and relative protein expression values (NPX) of 5416 proteins were used for downstream analyses. As a first step, each protein was tested for an association with cognitive domain or NPI total scores using linear regression models adjusting for age, sex, handedness and education. Proteins showing nominal significance at p<0.05 were selected for protein–protein interaction mapping. These significant proteins were projected into a PPI network using established STRING Database version 12 using the default threshold of medium confidence (score threshold = 400). Louvain clustering algorithm was applied to detect modules of highly interconnected proteins. Modules containing fewer than 3 proteins were excluded for future analyses. Module functions were then determined based on enrichment of Gene Ontology (GO) terms. For each module, we summarized proteomic variation using the module eigenprotein, computed as the first principal component (PC1) of standardized protein values within that module2. 

Structural MRI data were processed using FreeSurfer, and cortical thickness and grey matter volume was derived across 68 regions based on the Desikan-Killiany atlas. Mediation analyses were performed in the middle-aged group only, adjusting for age, sex, handedness, education and estimated Total Intracranial Volume (eTIV). For each module eigenprotein, we tested whether cortical thickness or grey matter volume mediated its association with NPI total scores and cognitive domain T-scores. Indirect effects were estimated using a quasi-Bayesian approximation with 1,000 iterations. 

Results

Across the protein co-expression modules, a consistent pattern emerges. Immune-related biology shows the highest total number of proteins within each domain, with 38 immune-related proteins in NPI, 22 in Verbal Memory, 33 in Visual Memory, and 45 in Executive Function. This aligns with evidence that systemic and neuroimmune processes broadly shape cognition and behavior3. 

In contrast, neuronal and synaptic processes show substantial total protein representation only in the cognitive domains. Verbal Memory includes 37 neuronal or synaptic proteins, Visual Memory includes 7, and Executive Function includes 13, while NPI-related modules contain none. This pattern is consistent with evidence that disruptions in synaptic integrity and neuronal circuit function are key contributors to deficits in domain-specific cognitive performance4.

In the middle‑aged group, mediation analyses demonstrated that cortical thickness partially mediated the relationship between inflammatory proteomic Module 2 and NPI total score. Specifically, Module 1 showed a significant indirect effect via right banks of the superior temporal sulcus (Average Causal Mediated Effect (ACME) = −0.064, p = 0.026, Figure 2A).The superior temporal sulcus is a key region supporting social perception, communication, and interpretation of others’ actions and intentions, aligning well with mental health–related behavioral variation captured by the NPI.

By contrast, metabolism‑related Module 2 exhibited positive mediated effects through parietal and cingulate structural measures (Figure 2B). Module 2 had significant indirect effects on NPI total score via right supramarginal gyrus volume (ACME = 0.083, p = 0.022) and left rostral anterior cingulate thickness (ACME = 0.071, p = 0.03). The supramarginal gyrus, located in the inferior parietal lobule, contributes to language processing and integration of sensory information, with structural variation linked to emotional and cognitive symptoms. The anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) is a key node in networks supporting emotion regulation, decision‑making, and cognitive control, and variations in its thickness have been associated with mental health variations in aging and clinical populations. 

No significant mediation effects were observed for verbal memory, visual memory, or executive function. The presence of brain-mediated proteomic effects on NPI but not cognition should be interpreted cautiously and may reflect a combination of greater sensitivity of mental health measures to early systemic biological variation and limited statistical power to detect cognitive effects in this sample.


Taken together, these findings suggest that specific inflammatory and metabolic proteomic signatures may contribute to behavioral disturbances through their association with cortical structural variation. This aligns with evidence that peripheral immune activation and metabolic dysregulation can influence brain systems involved in emotion and cognitive control5. These results support the idea that systemic biological profiles are meaningfully linked to brain integrity and behavioral outcomes in midlife.

Conclusion

In summary, our findings indicate that systemic proteomic signatures, particularly those related to inflammation and metabolism, are linked to behavioral outcomes through their influence on cortical structure. Immune-related proteins appear to have broad effects across behavioral and cognitive domains, whereas metabolism- and synaptic-related proteins show more domain-specific relationships, especially with cognitive performance. Together, these results highlight the role of peripheral biological processes in shaping brain integrity and behavior in midlife, supporting the notion that early molecular signatures may provide targets for intervention to preserve cognitive and emotional health.
