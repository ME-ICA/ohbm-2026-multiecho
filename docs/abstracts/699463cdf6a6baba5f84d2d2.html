Introduction

Robust links between mental illness and neuroimaging have been difficult to find. Our definitions of the disorders themselves may contribute to this difficulty. If we had validated measures of individual symptoms, instead of scales that measure severity at the level of disorders, then we could look for neuroimaging correlates at the symptom level, which may be more robust.^1 The preliminary HiTOP internalizing scales provide a variety of symptom specific subscales, but they have a 1-year assessment window.^2 This long assessment window makes them poorly suited to comparison with computational psychiatry and neuroimaging, which provide snapshots of the brain’s state on the scale of hours. We validated a version of the scales assessing symptoms over the previous 2 weeks, using a subset of scales that were most relevant for depression and anxiety.

In our validation, we first assessed the test-retest reliability of the scales with the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC). This is a ratio of the variance within an individual’s responses at multiple time points to the total (between + within) variance. We next assessed the measurement invariance between the original sample used to validate the HiTOP scales (assessing the previous year) and two online samples we collected (assessing the previous two weeks). Measurement invariance assesses the relationship between the items in a questionnaire and the total score on that questionnaire. If two versions of a questionnaire are measuring the same underlying concept, then the relationship between individual items and total score should not significantly differ. 

Lastly we tested convergent and divergent validity. Convergent validity means that a scale which claims to measure a concept should agree with other validated scales measuring the same concept. Divergent validity means that a scale which claims to measure some concept should specifically measure that concept, and be uncorrelated with unrelated concepts.

Methods

In our preregistered study (https://osf.io/5sn3p/overview), we validated the following HiTOP subscales:

Anhedonic Depression

Anxious Worry

Appetite Gain

Appetite Loss

Cognitive Problems

Hyposomnia

Indecisiveness

Insomnia

Panic

Separation Anxiety

Shame/Guilt

Situational Phobia

Social Anxiety

Well-Being

We additionally collected the Patient Health Questionnaire-8 (PHQ-8)^3, General Anxiety Disorder-7 (GAD-7)^4, and Barkley Adult ADHD Rating Scale-IV (BAARS)^5 as well as questions about demographics and mental health history.

We collected two data samples, one representing the US general population (GenPop; n=497, n female=266, mean(std) age = 54.6(21.1)) and a second composed of individuals who endorsed symptoms of depression, anxiety, or ADHD (Enriched; n=305, n female = 206, mean(std) age = 48.6(18.2)). Data was collected by YouGov from their panel of participants. We collected responses from both populations about 1 week apart so that we could assess test-retest reliability. To determine if we were measuring the same constructs as the original scale despite changing the assessment window from 1 year to 2 weeks, we also requested access to the validation sample from the original publication of the HiTOP sample (n = 496, n female=284, mean(std) age = 31.7(11.7)). We assessed measurement invariance between the GenPop, Enriched, and HiTOP validation samples. Finally, we tested several convergent and divergent validity hypotheses to further validate the scales.

All code is available at https://github.com/mariezelenina/HiTOP. Data will be made publicly available prior to presentation of these results.

Results

Our test-retest reliability analysis showed at least good (95% confidence interval of ICCs (2,1) > 0.75) for most scales in a combined analysis of GenPop and Enriched samples. The exceptions were:

BAARS Hyperactivity (ICC [95%CI] = 0.77 [0.72, 0.80])

HiTOP Situational Phobia (ICC [95%CI] = 0.79 [0.75, 0.82])

HiTOP Appetite Loss (ICC [95%CI] = 0.78 [0.75, 0.82])

HiTOP Hyposomnia (ICC [95%CI] = 0.76 [0.72, 0.80])

Across all three samples, all 7 out of 14 HiTOP scales tested were invariant at the configural and metric levels, at least. One scale could not be assessed because it consisted of only 2 items. For the remaining 6 scales, we were still able to find an invariant subset of items, listed at the end of this section.

All of the preregistered convergent and divergent validity hypotheses were confirmed (Figure 1).

Conclusion

We found that our subset of depression and anxiety related HiTOP internalizing scales had good reliability or just missed the threshold for good reliability (0.75) with the lowest lower 95% confidence interval being 0.72. 

Reliability alone does not suffice, because the scale might not be measuring the desired concept. We found that 7 out of the 14 subscales are measuring the same concept in both a 1-year assessment window and a 2-week assessment window and across general and enriched samples. For the other 6 subscales with three or more items, we were able to find an invariant core of items. These invariant cores seem on their face to be more tightly focussed than the original scales. For example, the social anxiety scales invariant core is focussed on the avoidance of social action while the excluded items are related to more passive feelings of social discomfort. The excluded items on the Anhedonic Depression scale are related to absence of laughter and low energy, which are distinct symptoms. These modifications may make the invariant cores more suited for the purpose of measuring individual symptoms than the original scales.

All of the preregisterred convergent and divergent validity analyses were confirmed. This provides evidence that some of the subscales are specific measures of the intended concepts.

Our work did have some limitations. The original HiTOP validation sample and our GenPop and Enriched samples were collected at least 3 years apart. Changes in the popular conception or understanding of these mental health disorders may have contributed to the lack of measurement invariance for some of the original scales. Due to limits on how many questions we could ask each participant, we were not able to collect information to assess the convergent and divergent validity of all of the HiTOP subscales.

All together, this work shows that these depression and anxiety related scales from the HiTOP internalizing scales can be collected with a 2-week assessment window. We showed that these scales are reliable and valid. With the shorter assessment window they are likely to be suitable measures for use in neuroimaging or computational psychiatry studies.
