Introduction

Brain morphology analyzed at the group average level has returned low effect sizes in major depressive disorder (MDD) compared with bipolar disease or schizophrenia, yet, there exists clinical subgroups (e.g., recurrent disease, early disease onset, use of antidepressants) that tend to show stronger abnormalities. In addition, adolescent patients show a different type of abnormality with rather surface area (SA) abnormalities compared to adult patients with cortical thickness (CT) abnormalities.1,2 So far, data-driven clustering approaches have gained no clear delineations, likely due to a combination of generally low effect sizes and clinical heterogeneity.3 Here we apply clustering that (other than single-dimensional clustering techniques) groups features and subjects at the same time. The specific algorithm FABIA (Factor Analysis for Bicluster Acquisition)4 factorizes the data into a set of latent biclusters where each bicluster is defined by both its rows and columns. We consider this approach useful when wants to find subgroups of features and subjects that exhibit a similar correlation of features (‘structural covariance’) within in the group, more strongly than outside the group. Of note, other features may have average deviations, but in a non-correlated manner. Eventually, the algorithm also leaves a group of subjects not particularly distinguishable (‘background’), and generally, subjects can have multiple cluster memberships.

Methods

Samples and input features: We collected data sheets from quality controlled regional FreeSurfer segmentation results (68 cortical thickness [CT] markers, 68 surfacce areas [SA] markers, 16 subcortical volumes) within the ENIGMA MDD working group (32 sites, total 6205 subjects, 3017 healthy controls [HC] and 2543 MDD patients). Two samples were held outside for out-of-sample experiments. 645 subjects were excluded based on quality control. Detection and replication split: A 70% vs. 30% split was performed within MDD and HC group, each matched for age and sex, for separate clustering operations (Fig. 1). Site effect correction and demographic model: In site effects and demographic effects were estimated in half in of the 70% of HC using ComBat-GAM (ICV, sex and age [with non-linear expansion] as covariates), and multiple linear regession. Both models were applied to the rest of HC and all patients in a two-stage manner (site effects, then demographic model). The final clustering was performed on residual feature values of 4051 individuals (3249 adults and 802 adolescents). Biclustering settings: The number of biclusters (k=4) was determined by testing solutions from k=2 to k=20, selecting based on bicluster stability measured by similarity in 10-fold approach. Key settings of FABIA used were: sparseness α=0.01, 10,000 iterations, sample loading sparseness spz=0.5 and extraction threshold thresZ=0.5 for bicluster assignment.

As a pre-experiment, smaller groups with neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer’s and Huntington’s disease) and schizophrenia were investigated along with a specifically matched HC group. These analyses (not detailed here) provided proof-of concept, that FABIA detects extreme morphological subgroups in a data-driven way. 

Post-hoc characterization of (exclusive) bi-clusters: Key clinical variables were compared between resulting bi-clusters (including the background group), i.e. age and sex distribution of MDD and HC, recurrent vs. first episode, age at onset, use of antidepressant. In addition, driving features were identified, and ANOVA of the residuals were estimated, separately for the feature groups, between bi-clusters and the background to characterize key morphological features.

Results

A number of 4 biclusters was identified as by pre-analyses optimal. The driving features were mostly reproduced in the replication sample, showing good stability of cluster-driving regions in the independent 70% and 30% subsamples and moderate stability of associated proportion of MDD and sexes (Fig. 1). The driving features in the detection sample were (see star symbols in Fig. 2): (#1) SA of left and right superior frontal gyrus, SA of left and right rostral middle frontal gyrus, (#2) left and right lateral ventricle volumes, (#3) left and right lateral ventricle volumes, left and right putamen, (#4) SA of left and right pericalcarine cortex SA, and left and right lingual gyrus. Fig. 2 also shows comparative maps between the bi-clusters and the background group.

Clinical differences were found for age (lowest in bicluster #1 and #3), % of MDD (lowest [more HC] in bicluster #4), sex distribution (highest proportion of women [68.7%] in bicluster #2 and highest proportion of men [40.6%] in #4), sex-by-diagnosis distribution (highest proportion of male controls [19.6%] and lowest of female MDD patients [35.3%] in #4), and age at onset in adults (overall result, with #1 trending as lowest), Beck Depression Inventory values (highest in #2), and a trend in Hamilton Disease Rating Scale values (highest in #3, lowest in #4). No differences between clusters were found for: % of MDD using antidepressants, distribution of recurrent/first episode in MDD patients, % of MDD patients in remission, number of episodes. No differences of the site distribution between biclusters were found.

Conclusion

Bi-clustering of a large group of MDD patients and HC demonstrated four biclusters and a background group. Against the background cluster, cluster #2, #3 and #4 demonstrated sub-average CT values with a prefrontal emphasis as found in a large meta-analysis,2 whereas cluster #1 demonstrated widespread CT increases. SA results demonstrated #1 standing out, with widespread low SA, as reported particularly for adolescent MDD.2 This converged with lower AO in #1, potentially representing a morphological subtype. Remarkably, typically reported lower CT (as observed in adult MDD) seems to split into one type with the ‘typical’ lower prefrontal pattern (#2, yet normal or higher subcortical volumes) and one with the lower subcortical volumes (#3, yet more widespread CT pattern). #4 (more HC in the detection sample) presented a combination of low prefrontal CT, yet high paralimbic CT and subcortical volumes, potentially demonstrating a compensation pattern. Further analyses will focus on separate clustering of adults and adolescents, and external validation through unseen datasets.
