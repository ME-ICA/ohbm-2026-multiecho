Introduction

Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) is an established neuromodulatory treatment for obsessive–compulsive disorder (OCD), yet clinical response remains highly variable.1 Deep TMS (dTMS) using the H7 coil is FDA-cleared for OCD and is designed to stimulate medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) and anterior cingulate cortex (ACC),2 key hubs of the cortico–striato–thalamo–cortical (CSTC) circuit implicated in OCD pathophysiology.3 However, stimulation targets are typically defined using coarse anatomical heuristics (e.g., 4 cm anterior to the motor hotspot), without accounting for individual differences in electric field (E-field) distribution or network engagement. Here, we combined E-field modeling and resting-state functional MRI to test whether dTMS-induced modulation of ACC-centered CSTC connectivity, and to use regional E-field strength to predict symptom improvement in OCD. 

Methods

Forty-two patients with treatment-resistant OCD (DSM-5 diagnosis confirmed by SCID-5; Yale–Brown Obsessive–Compulsive Scale [Y-BOCS] ≥ 20) participated in a six-week randomized, double-blind trial of dTMS targeting mPFC/ACC. Participants received either active or sham stimulation (34 active, 8 sham). The first ten participants were treated using a double-cone (DC) coil (MagPro R30), while the remaining thirty-two received stimulation with the H7 coil (Figure 1). Stimulation parameters were identical across coils. For the DC coil, the target was defined as the midpoint between the motor hotspot and the nasion. For the H7 coil, the stimulation target was positioned 4 cm anterior to the motor hotspot, consistent with FDA-cleared protocols for deep mPFC/ACC stimulation.

Active stimulation consisted of 28 sessions of 20 Hz dTMS (100% motor threshold; 40 trains; 50 pulses per train; 2,000 pulses per session), each preceded by five minutes of individualized symptom provocation to engage OCD-related CSTC circuits. Sham stimulation followed an identical schedule using a sham coil. Resting-state functional MRI and Y-BOCS assessments were acquired at baseline (v1) and at the treatment endpoint (v2). Clinical response was defined as a ≥35% reduction in Y-BOCS score.

Behavioral effects were assessed using a permutation-based 2×2 factorial ANOVA with stimulation condition (active vs. sham) and coil type (H7 vs. DC) as between-subject factors. Seed-based functional connectivity analyses focused on ACC and mPFC subregions. Individualized E-fields were simulated from T1-weighted structural MRI using SimNIBS⁴ and normalized by global mean E-field strength. Dose–response relationships between regional E-field strength and clinical improvement were assessed using Spearman correlations with max-statistic permutation correction.

Results

Responder rates differed markedly by coil type and stimulation condition. In the H7 group, 13 of 26 participants receiving active stimulation met responder criteria (≥35% Y-BOCS reduction), whereas no responders were observed in the H7 sham group (0/6). In contrast, response rates were low for the DC coil, with only one responder in the active DC group (1/8) and none in the DC sham group (0/2). Permutation ANOVA revealed a significant main effect of coil type, with greater Y-BOCS reduction following H7 compared to DC stimulation (F = 5.85, p = 0.019), and a significant interaction between coil type and stimulation condition (F = 9.17, p = 0.004). The main effect of Active versus Sham stimulation was not significant (F = 2.19, p = 0.154). Post hoc tests revealed that active stimulation resulted in a larger symptom reduction than sham stimulation within the H7 group (mean difference = 18.6%), whereas no active–sham difference was observed for the DC coil, indicating coil-specific efficacy.

At the network level, compared to sham, H7 dTMS (v2–v1) significantly increased functional connectivity (FC) between pregenual ACC and left caudate nucleus (Figure 2A), a core CSTC pathway, while decreasing FC with the left inferior parietal lobule and increasing FC with the left superior frontal gyrus (GRF corrected: voxel p < 0.01, cluster p < 0.05).

Dose–response analysis demonstrated that individual variability in induced E-field strength within the right supracallosal ACC was predictive of clinical outcome (Figure 2B). Higher E-field strength in this region was associated with greater Y-BOCS reduction (Spearman ρ = −0.54, p = 0.003), surviving family-wise error correction (FWER < 0.05) using a max-statistic permutation approach.

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate that the clinical efficacy of dTMS in OCD depends critically on coil geometry and its ability to engage ACC-centered CSTC circuitry. H7 dTMS selectively enhances ACC–striatal functional connectivity, and individual differences in ACC E-field strength predict symptom improvement. Together, these results support a move toward connectivity- and E-field–guided personalization of TMS targeting in OCD.
