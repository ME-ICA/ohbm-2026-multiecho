Introduction

Recent convergence between artificial intelligence and neuroscience has revealed that deep neural networks (DNNs) develop representations that align closely with the human visual system. However, conventional brain encoding studies typically rely on dense linear mappings to translate these artificial features into brain activity, while neglecting the explicit spatial constraints of neural processing, for instance, population receptive field (pRF) of the visual cortex. These models fail to capture how information integration evolves across the cortex, thereby obscuring the critical transition from localized visual analysis to holistic semantic abstraction.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as OpenCLIP, offer a promising computational substrate to investigate this continuum by inherently aligning visual representations with abstract concepts in a shared latent space. However, standard VLMs lack direct biological interpretability. It remains unclear how to effectively model retinotopic spatial organization and explicitly delineate the functional hierarchy transitioning from unimodal processing to multimodal integration within these architectures.

In this study, we introduce a biologically constrained vertex-wise encoding model based on the OpenCLIP backbone, leveraging the joint mechanism of image-text alignment and perceptual similarity. Crucially, we incorporate two novel attention mechanisms (spatial and layer attention) to bridge the gap between ANN latent features and the cortical organization of retinotopy and visual hierarchy. This study aims to 1) estimate the population Receptive Fields (pRFs) directly from the model’s attention maps, validating their biological plausibility against empirical data; and 2) dissociate the cortical hierarchy of visual versus semantic processing, revealing a transition from visual-dominant representations in the visual cortex to semantic-dominant encoding in high-order association areas.

Methods

Dataset and Preprocessing: We leveraged the large-scale Natural Scenes Dataset (NSD) [1], using high-resolution 7T fMRI responses from four subjects (subj01, 02, 05, 07) who viewed 10,000 natural images from the COCO dataset. Functional brain imaging data were preprocessed and modeled using GLMsingle to estimate beta weights associated with each scene image. These responses were projected onto the fsaverage surface space (10,242 vertices per hemisphere), which was utilized for all subsequent model training and parameter estimation. Only significantly predicted vertices (FDR corrected, p<0.05) were plotted on the fsaverage surface. 

Encoding Backbone and Feature Extraction: We utilized the OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 architecture (pretrained on LAION-2B) as the backbone, leveraging its robust multimodal alignment capabilities [2]. To capture the full spectrum of visual processing, we extracted hierarchical feature representations of natural scene images from all intermediate transformer blocks. This layer-wise strategy preserves the step-by-step transformation of information, ensuring that both low-level structural features and high-level semantic abstractions are available for voxel-wise modeling.

Biologically Constrained Attention Mechanisms: To linearly map these hierarchical features to brain activity for each scene image, we introduced two specialized, learnable attention mechanisms designed to mimic biological constraints. 1) Spatial Attention: We learned a voxel-specific weight matrix applied to the visual feature grid (16*16 image patches). Crucially, these weights are softmax-normalized to generate a 2D spatial probability distribution for each voxel. This functions as a learnable receptive field, explicitly constraining the spatial extent of information integration. 2) Layer Attention: We integrated a mechanism that assigns trainable weights to the 12 transformer blocks. This mechanism allows the model to automatically select the optimal level of information abstraction, ranging from low-level visual features to high-level semantic concepts, tailored to the specific processing streams of each voxel.

Biological interpretability: To validate the biological plausibility of encoding model, we estimated population Receptive Field (pRF) parameters directly from the spatial attention maps. We calculated the sub-pixel center of mass of the attention weights to determine coordinates (x,y) of the maximum weights. The coordinates converted into standard pRF parameters: polar angle (θ= arctan2(y, x)) and eccentricity ((e =√(x² + y²)). We quantified the alignment between model-derived estimates and the empirical data provided by the NSD pRF experiments using Pearson correlation. Furthermore, to capture hierarchical feature processing, we specifically investigated layer attention profiles for cortical vertices in V1d, V1v, hV4, and prefrontal regions, illustrating distinct profiles and feature selectivity across the cortical hierarchy.

Model comparison: To delineate the cortical hierarchy and interaction between modalities, we systematically compared the encoding performance of OpenCLIP against a suite of unimodal baselines, including unaligned vision models (DINO-v2, ViT), and language models (GPT-2, BERT). Specifically, to dissociate the distinct contributions of visual and linguistic components, we contrasted the encoding performance of OpenCLIP-ImageEncoder and OpenCLIP-TextEncoder, demonstrating a functional gradient from early visual cortex (ImageEncoder) to associated region in frontal and parietal cortices (TextEncoder). Furthermore, we operationally defined Vision-Language Integration Areas as cortical regions exhibiting high encoding accuracy in both modalities (r > 0.3) and predictive parity between encoders (rdiff < 0.05). The semantic selectivity of these integration areas were delineated using fLoc localizers, comprising 10 recognition tasks spanning categories such as words, bodies, faces, places, and objects.

Results

The proposed biologically constrained model achieved state-of-the-art encoding accuracy across the visual cortex (peak Pearson r = 0.879; Fig. 1A), while demonstrating robust performance extending beyond visual areas to encompass frontal, parietal, and temporal regions. Furthermore, the learned Layer Attention mechanism successfully reconstructed the intrinsic cortical hierarchy without explicit topological supervision (Fig. 1E). As detailed in the representative spatial and layer attention profiles (Fig. 1B), this hierarchy manifested as a distinct layer preference: early visual areas (e.g., V1) preferentially attended to shallow transformer layers (Layers 1–4) to extract low-level features, whereas higher-order regions such as the prefrontal cortex (PFC) shifted their attention toward deeper layers (Layers 8–12), reflecting a transition from image feature extraction to semantic abstraction. 

To validate the model’s biological interpretability, we estimated population receptive field (pRF) parameters, specifically the polar angle and eccentricity, directly from the Spatial Attention maps of encoding models (Fig. 1B). Intriguingly, model-derived pRF estimates exhibited striking topographic correspondence with empirical ground truth provided by NSD-pRF experiments (Fig. 1C-D), accurately reproducing key features such as polar angle phase reversals (e.g., distinct selectivity in V1v vs. V1d) and foveal-to-peripheral eccentricity gradients (scale 1-6). Quantitatively, this alignment of pRF estimates was robust across all subjects, achieving significant correlations for both polar angle (peak r = 0.80) and eccentricity (peak r = 0.49; all p < 0.001).

To validate the necessity of cross-modal alignment, we benchmarked our framework against a suit of unimodal baselines (Fig. 2A). The image-text aligned OpenCLIP-ImageEncoder significantly outperformed the unaligned self-supervised DINO-v2 (peak rdiff = 0.299) and ViT (peak rdiff =0.708), while the OpenCLIP-TextEncoder surpassed pure language models, including GPT-2 (peak rdiff = 0.226) and BERT (peak rdiff = 0.280). Crucially, a direct comparison between Image and Text Encoders revealed a functional gradient of modality preference, shifting from specific image features in the early visual cortex to abstract semantic concepts in frontal and parietal regions (Fig. 2B). 

At the whole-brain level (Fig. 2B), scatter plots illustrate a clear transition from strict visual dominance in the occipital cortex to visual-language convergence in the frontal and parietal lobes. This gradient was quantitatively mirrored at the granular ROI level (Kastner 2015 atlas [3]; Fig. 2C): the marked performance gap in early visual areas (V1–V3) progressively diminished along the ventral stream, ultimately closing in high-level association cortices where language models achieved predictive parity with vision models. Furthermore, we identified specific Vision-Language Integrated Areas (rdiff < 0.05, r > 0.3 in both modalities) that functionally recruit both visual and semantic features for holistic processing. These integration areas were annotated using NSD-fLoc functional localizer tasks (e.g., faces, bodies, places, objects, words). Detailed ROI analysis confirmed that the ImageEncoder’s specific advantage was limited to the early visual cortex and gradually diminished along the dorsal and ventral streams, whereas the Image- and Text-encoders showed comparable performance in the integration areas.

Conclusion

This study establishes a biologically constrained encoding framework that harmonizes state-of-the-art deep learning representations with the cortical organization of retinotopy and visual hierarchy. By integrating Spatial and Layer Attention mechanisms into the OpenCLIP architecture, we demonstrate that a model fine-tuned for joint image-text alignment and perceptual similarity can robustly predict brain activity with high fidelity while mirroring the hierarchical principles of biological vision systems. Incorporating these biological constraints did not impair computational performance; rather, it enhanced the model’s capacity to generalize and interpret complex neural responses, particularly regarding the neural mechanism underlying the visual-language integration, providing rigorous validation for the Neuro-AI hypothesis that goal-driven multimodal models converge toward brain-like representational strategies.

A pivotal contribution of this study is the unsupervised estimation of retinotopic properties, specifically polar angle maps and eccentricity gradients, directly from the encoding model’s attention weights. The high fidelity of our model-derived pRF estimates relative to empirical NSD-pRF data suggests that the spatial topography of the visual cortex is fundamentally organized to optimize information integration for downstream visual and semantic processing. This finding positions our encoding framework as a powerful tool for computational neuroscience, offering a non-invasive window into functional architecture that complements and validates traditional fMRI mapping techniques.

Finally, our identification of Vision-Language Integrated Areas challenges the strict modular segregation of sensory perception and semantic abstraction. The observed functional gradient suggests that high-level ventral temporal, dorsal lateral and parietal regions act as continuous integration hubs, where visual features and linguistic concepts map onto a shared representational space rather than being processed in isolation. This continuum perspective offers a compelling mechanism for how the human brain seamlessly integrates multimodal inputs to construct a coherent understanding of the world. Future work will extend this framework to temporal dynamics, investigating how this visual-semantic integration unfolds over time.
