Introduction

Modeling individualized resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) dynamics remains challenging due to short time series, noise, and high inter-subject variability. Hierarchical recurrent neural networks (RNNs), particularly shallow piecewise-linear RNNs (shPLRNNs), have recently been proposed as interpretable dynamical systems models capable of extracting low-dimensional latent structure shared across subjects while capturing individual variations. Such models promise improved generalization, subject-level dynamical representations, and the ability to synthesize realistic surrogate fMRI time series. However, their performance and limitations on large real-world neuroimaging datasets remain insufficiently characterized. Here, using 1,423 samples from the MACS cohort, we systematically evaluate the capacity of an interpretable hierarchical, shallow piecewise-linear RNN (hier-shPLRNN) ^1  to reconstruct rs-fMRI dynamics, generalize to unseen subjects, and encode behaviorally meaningful inter-individual differences. Our analyses aim to clarify the capabilities and boundaries of these models as tools for individualized brain mapping.

Methods

Healthy-participant rs-fMRI data from the Marburg-Münster Affective Disorders Cohort Study (MACS; n = 1,423) ^2 underwent standard preprocessing, including slice-timing and motion correction, band-pass filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz), scrubbing of motion-contaminated frames and cortical parcellation into 68 regions. For each participant, the ROI × time series was modeled using a hier-shPLRNN, in which a low-dimensional, subject-specific parameter vector (20 dimensions; determined via subject-wise PCA to explain ≥95% variance) was mapped through shared group-level dynamical weights capturing cohort-wide latent structure. Training of group and individual parameters used generalized teacher forcing to stabilize learning under potentially chaotic dynamics.

Model generalization was evaluated on held-out subjects by fine-tuning only the individual parameter vectors while keeping group-level weights fixed, thus testing the transferability of learned cohort dynamics to unseen individuals. Reconstruction accuracy was quantified using Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) between empirical and simulated time series and Pearson correlation between empirical and model-generated functional connectivity (FC). Subject identification analyses compared simulated FC to empirical FC patterns across all participants.

To assess the behavioral relevance of the learned latent representations, we tested associations between individual parameters and demographic/cognitive variables (sex, age, BMI, IQ, years of schooling) using FDR-controlled mass-univariate ANOVA/OLS models with covariate adjustment. Complementary nested cross-validated machine-learning pipelines evaluated multivariate predictive power and compared performance against PCA-derived features and empirical rs-fMRI data.

Results

In-sample simulations moderately reproduced empirical rs-fMRI dynamics, with mean Pearson correlation r = 0.63 and DTW = 0.17, indicating that the hier-shPLRNN successfully captured salient cohort-level temporal structure. For held-out subjects, per-subject fine-tuning of individual parameters (with fixed group weights) reduced correspondence (r = 0.41; DTW = 0.24), demonstrating only partial generalisability of learned dynamics to unseen individuals. Reconstruction accuracy varied widely and was strongly modulated by FC “template similarity,” with more typical subjects showing substantially higher empirical-simulated FC correspondence.

Subject identification accuracy based on simulated FC was low (Top-1 = 4.44%; Top-3 = 8.01%; Top-5 = 10.20%), reflecting limited subject specificity within the learned latent codes. Individual parameter vectors showed statistically significant but small effects in mass-univariate analyses, relating to sex, age, and BMI after FDR correction, but effect sizes were weak. Machine-learning analyses using RNN-derived parameters achieved only modest predictive performance (e.g., sex BACC = 0.67; age MSE = 121.48), and performance substantially declined for test subjects. Across all benchmarks, empirical rs-fMRI features consistently outperformed hier-shPLRNN, indicating that although the hierarchical model provides compact and interpretable representations, it currently captures only a limited fraction of subject-specific variability.

Conclusion

The hier-shPLRNN’s key advantage is its simultaneous learning of shared group-level dynamics and individual parameter vectors, which regularises subject-level estimation and is therefore well-suited to rs-fMRI time series. The model retrieves meaningful cohort structure and low-dimensional subject factors, but current individualisation remains modest: generalisation to unseen subjects and subject identification are limited, and predictive links to demographics/cognition are small. Enhancing subject specificity will likely require richer latent parameterisations, stronger regularisation, contrastive/domain-adaptive objectives, multimodal integration and, where feasible, longer scans for increased statistical power.
