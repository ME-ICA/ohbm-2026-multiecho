Introduction

    Traditionally viewed as a predominantly motor structure, the human cerebellum is now recognized to support a broad range of non-motor functions, including cognition and emotion.1 Cerebellar magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has become increasingly important in many neuro-psychiatric disorders. However, unlike the cerebral cortex, the cerebellum’s compact spatial scale and densely folded architecture contrast sharply with the richness of functions it supports,1,2 making accurate normalization and cross-subject alignment particularly challenging.

    To address these challenges, the Spatially Unbiased Infratentorial (SUIT) toolbox was developed to provide a dedicated cerebellar template and voxel-wise normalization framework.2 While SUIT has been widely used for both functional and anatomical studies of the cerebellum, its isolation algorithm often requires manual correction and performs suboptimally for neonatal, aging, and clinical populations.

    Here we introduce SUITPy, a fully revised Python-based re-implementation of SUIT designed to be robust across the lifespan. SUITPy incorporates a new U-Net based cerebellar isolation model trained on an expert-validated dataset spanning the entire lifespan and clinical cases, achieving substantially higher fidelity in separating the cerebellum from adjacent cortex. Building on these masks, SUITPy provides a Cerebellum Only normalization pipeline that improves anatomical alignment over Whole Brain pipelines. The toolbox further includes flatmap visualization and a comprehensive set of anatomical and functional cerebellar atlases, offering a unified platform for high-precision cerebellar functional and anatomical MRI analysis.

Methods

Cerebellar isolation

    At first, we built a dataset of hand-corrected cerebellum masks, which includes 140 subjects covering the whole lifespan (age 0-76 years). For each subject, the correctness of the mask delineation was reviewed by at least two independent experts to ensure high labeling accuracy. The hand corrected masks included white matter and gray matter of the cerebellum and the brainstem structures from the medulla oblongata inferiorly and the midbrain superiorly. 

    Based on this manually corrected dataset, we trained a deep learning architecture to generate the cerebellum mask for automatic cerebellar isolation. Raw T1w and T2w images were first affine-registered and resampled to the 1 mm isotropic MNI template,3 ensuring consistent orientation, scale, and resolution for uniform cropping. To reduce computational cost and focus on the region of interest, we cropped each brain scan to a fixed 128×128×128 voxel bounding box centered on the cerebellum and brainstem in MNI space. A 3D U-Net was employed for isolation, taking the co-registered T1w and T2w volumes as a two-channel input. The network comprised four encoder and four decoder blocks with increasing/decreasing channel numbers, instance normalization, and Leaky ReLU activations, connected via skip connections. The output was a voxel-wise probability map in MNI space, which was transformed back to native space using the inverse affine transform and binarized to produce the final cerebellar mask.

    To evaluate the isolation performance of this proposed 3D U-Net model, we calculated the 95th percentile Hausdorff Distance and compared our model exclusively with SUIT.2 And all models’ performances are compared with the baseline model, which is the average of the manual masks from the training sets.

Cerebellar normalization

    The original SUIT toolbox employed a normalization to a Cerebellum Only template. Here, the MRI image was cerebellum isolated using the above U-Net based isolation model and then registered to the cerebellum-specific template using ANTs antsRegistrationSyN to the whole brain template tpl-MNI152NLin2009cSymC_T1w.nii.gz,4 either using a skull-stripped version (whole-brain template) or the non-skull-stripped version (whole-head template).3 For a cerebellum-only pipeline, we used a version of the template that contained only the cerebellum. We used N4 bias-correction on the original image before normalization in each of the pipeline.

Evaluation of normalization

    To evaluate the performance of different normalization pipelines, we calculated the correlation coefficient between the template and the resliced image. The resliced image was generated by warping the U-Net masked T1 image into the MNI152NLin2009cSymC space using the transformation estimated from each pipeline.2 In order to test the performance of each pipeline in lobule parcellation, we use ACAPULCO to get the individual lobule parcellations at first.5 Then the lobule parcellation was resliced into the MNI152NLin2009cSymC space using the transformation estimated from each pipeline.2 The generalized dice score was calculated for each pair of subjects within the same dataset, yielding a subject x subject similarity matrix. For each subject, a summary dice score was obtained by averaging its off-diagonal entries. This provides a measure of how consistently the lobular parcellation for a given subject aligns with those of the remaining cohort.

    For the statistical analysis, we assessed normalization performance at both the subject and dataset levels. At the subject level, we applied paired t-tests to compare correlation or dice score across pipelines. At the dataset level, we first computed the average correlation or dice score across all subjects within each dataset, then performed a two-way ANOVA with pipeline and dataset as fixed factors.

Results

1. Cerebellar isolation with multi-modality MRI using 3D U-Net

    To improve the isolation of the cerebellum from the surrounding tissues, we trained a U-Net deep learning algorithm on the hand-segmented data from 140 subjects and 8 datasets. Importantly, the datasets spanned the entire lifespan (0-76 years), utilized different imaging resolutions and modalities (T1 or T2 weighted images), and also included cerebellar degeneration patients.

    We first compared the new U-Net algorithm with the original SUIT isolation pipeline on datasets for which SUIT is applicable, including BCP, high-resolution MDTB, MDTB, HCP-YoungAdult, and music-sdtb, as shown in Fig. 1a, the proposed 3D U-Net consistently achieved significantly lower 95th-percentile Hausdorff distances than SUIT across these datasets. 

    Importantly, the proposed U-Net algorithm also generalized to datasets for which the original SUIT pipeline fails to produce reliable results, as indicated by performance worse than the baseline reference, such as dHCP (ages 0–0.09 years). Specifically, SUIT failed to generate valid cerebellar masks for HCP-Development (ages 10–21 years) and cerebellar degeneration (ages 34–76 years) datasets, whereas the proposed U-Net produced accurate and stable isolations in these datasets. Together, these results highlight the robustness of our U-Net isolation model to diverse anatomical variations and imaging protocols, positioning it as a reliable and generalizable solution for cerebellar isolation across the full age range and pathologies.

2. The cerebellar mask helped the spatial normalization

    To assess whether the U-Net derived cerebellar mask improves spatial normalization, we implemented the Cerebellum Only normalization pipeline, in which the U-Net mask was first applied to isolate the cerebellum from the raw image, followed by normalization to a cerebellum-only template. For comparison, we also applied two commonly used strategies, i.e., Whole Brain and Whole Head normalization.

    The impact of the cerebellar mask on spatial normalization was then evaluated by examining the correlation between the template and the resliced images using different pipelines. The Cerebellum Only pipeline showed significantly higher correlation compared to the other two pipelines that did not incorporate a cerebellar mask (Fig.1b). This suggests that the U-Net mask helped the overall structural alignment of the whole cerebellum.

    To further evaluate the consistency of the pipelines across datasets, we calculated the average correlation for each dataset. We observed a clear performance gap between pipelines with and without the U-Net isolation mask. A two-way ANOVA revealed a significant main effect of pipeline (p = 2.54 × 10–67), but no significant main effect of dataset (p = 0.854) and no significant pipeline × dataset interaction (p = 0.852). These results indicate that the performance advantage of the Cerebellum Only pipeline, reflecting the benefit of cerebellar isolation, was consistent across datasets.

    Beyond voxel-wise template alignment, we evaluated the effect of the Cerebellum Only pipeline on the consistency of cerebellar lobular alignment across individuals. We therefore obtained individual lobule parcellations and then resliced to the template space using the transformation estimated from three pipelines. This allowed us to quantify inter-subject lobular correspondence using the generalized dice score (Fig.1c). At the subject level, the Cerebellum Only pipeline yielded significantly higher dice score than Whole Head and Whole Brain, indicating more accurate and spatially consistent lobule alignment. At the dataset level, a two-way ANOVA revealed significant main effects of dataset (p = 3.61 x 10-15), pipeline (p = 3.70 x 10-63), and significant dataset × pipeline interaction (p = 5.02 x 10-26). This pattern shows that Cerebellum Only pipelines improve lobular alignment in all datasets, but the magnitude of this improvement varies across datasets.

Conclusion

We present SUITPy, a fully revised implementation of the widely used SUIT toolbox for cerebellar MRI analysis. SUITPy incorporates a lifespan-generalizable U-Net based cerebellar isolation model trained on a manually curated dataset spanning diverse ages and clinical populations, enabling robust cerebellar segmentation across heterogeneous data. Building on these masks, normalization to a Cerebellum-Only template yields substantially improved structural alignment and more consistent cerebellar lobular correspondence across subjects, underscoring the importance of cerebellar isolation for accurate cerebellar normalization. Together with integrated reslice, flatmap, and cerebellar atlas modules, SUITPy provides a comprehensive and extensible framework for high-precision cerebellar functional and anatomical MRI analysis.
