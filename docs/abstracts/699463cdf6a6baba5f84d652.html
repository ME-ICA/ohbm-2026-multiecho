Introduction

Characterizing brain venous angioarchitecture is fundamental to understand the mechanism behind functional MRI. A critical challenge in high-resolution laminar fMRI is the "pial bias," where signal contributions are heavily weighted towards the surface rather than local neuronal activity due to draining and pial veins1. To resolve this, it is necessary to characterize the individual macrovasculature and draining veins, which introduce significantly higher inter-subject variability than the relatively homogenous capillary network2.

While recent acquisition advances, such as Skipped-CAIPI 3D EPI3 allow for fast, whole-brain imaging of the venous vasculature, the quantification of its features remains a bottleneck. Accurate segmentation in T2*-weighted imagery is historically difficult; traditional morphology-based methods often lack the sensitivity to identify small vessels amidst complex tissue contrast and intensity inhomogeneities. To address these limitations, we propose an extended deep-learning framework tailored for the precise segmentation and quantification of venous structures in T2* data.

Methods

In this abstract, we tested our segmentation framework on two T2*-weighted image – one post-mortem human brainstem image with 50 µm isotropic resolution4 and an in-vivo whole-brain scan with 350 µm isotropic resolution5.

Data preprocessing: 

To suppress spatial intensity inhomogeneities, we applied N4 bias field correction with a deliberately dense B-spline fitting grid (with small control-point spacing, e.g., 3 mm for post-mortem image, 10 mm for in-vivo image). This configuration reduces contrast variations across fibres and subcortical tissue, while preserving vesselness features that reflect the fine-scale vascular signal. In practice, vessels appear as high-resolution, elongated structures with sharper local intensity gradients than surrounding tissue. Because N4 models only smooth, slowly varying intensity fields, these fine-scale vascular features are largely unaffected. This preprocessing step, therefore, enhances segmentation consistency without eroding the vessel-specific signal (Figure 1(a)).

Vessel segmentation:

To address the segmentation challenges posed by the complex intensity profiles and susceptibility artifacts inherent in high-resolution T2*-weighted imaging, we extended the VesselBoost framework using a subject-specific domain adaptation strategy. Our approach utilizes a 3D U-Net backbone, initially pretrained on 14 Time-of-Flight (TOF) MRA volumes from the StudyForrest project. This pretraining establishes a strong prior for vascular geometry before exposure to the noisier T2* domain.

To adapt the model to the specific contrast of our target data—comprising both post-mortem samples and in-vivo scans—we implemented a localized fine-tuning step. For each subject, we extracted a representative thin slab and generated proxy segmentation masks within this volume using a Frangi vesselness filter. These Frangi-generated pseudo-labels, which yielded sparse and spatially fragmented segmentations, served as targets to fine-tune the model, allowing it to learn the local intensity mapping of the T2* acquisition. The adapted model was then applied to the remaining volume for full-specimen inference, effectively combining the robust vessel features learned from TOF data with subject-specific contrast adaptation (Figure 1(b)).

Results

The preprocessing step effectively reduced subcortical intensity inhomogeneities while preserving fine vessel contrast, enabling robust segmentation across the brainstem specimen. The deep-learning model accurately delineated small vascular structures, capturing both surface and penetrating vessels with minimal false positives. As illustrated in Figure 2, segmented vessels (red overlay) appear continuous and spatially consistent across fibre-rich regions.

Conclusion

We here demonstrate an effective workflow for vessel segmentation in high-resolution T2*-weighted MRI by integrating ANTs–based preprocessing with deep-learning inference. These combined steps provide a robust framework for extracting detailed vascular structures from susceptibility-weighted images.
