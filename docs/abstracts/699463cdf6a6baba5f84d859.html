Introduction

Brain volume alterations occur in obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), especially within the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical (CSTC) loop1. However, there is inconsistency in the findings, likely due to small sample sizes and heterogeneity in the methodology. The Enhancing NeuroImaging Genetics through Meta-Analysis initiative (ENIGMA) addresses the issue of sample size by pooling MRI data from diverse samples worldwide. Studies using FreeSurfer in the ENIGMA OCD sample found altered GM volume in the basal ganglia, thalamus, striatum, anterior cingulate cortex, prefrontal cortex and the hippocampus2,3. Voxel-based morphometry (VBM) may provide complementary information to FreeSurfer by localising focal alterations in subcortical GM and WM regions4. 

The current study applies a standardised ENIGMA-VBM pipeline in the largest OCD sample to date. We aim to investigate volumetric differences in brain volume between people with OCD and controls, and clinical variables' association with the volumetric differences.

Methods

Fifty-two cohorts from the ENIGMA-OCD working group participated, comprising 5036 individuals, including the adult sample (mean age=32.2, 2081 patients, 2105 controls), and pediatric sample (mean age=14.0, 434 patients, 416 controls). 

Sites performed the VBM analysis locally using the standardised ENIGMA-VBM tool (https://sites.google.com/view/enigmavbm), developed by the authors based on a DARTEL VBM pipeline in SPM. Statistical T-maps were generated from each cohort and meta-analysed using Seed-based d-Mapping5 to reveal voxel-wise differences between OCD and controls in GM and WM volume. Briefly, SDM meta-analyses effect size maps with a random-effects model, where site is modelled as a weighed random effect, applies family-wise error (FWE) correction for multiple comparisons using threshold-free cluster enhancement (TFCE) with statistical thresholding of p < 0.025.

In addition, we examined associations of GM and WM volume with various clinical variables: age of onset, illness severity, medication, comorbid anxiety disorders, and comorbid depressive disorders.

Results

Adult patients showed significantly lower GM volume (peak Hedges’ g=0.18) in the left anterior cingulate, bilateral insula, medial prefrontal cortex, orbitofrontal cortex and temporal pole. and lower WM volume in widespread regions around the corpus callosum, extending to frontal WM (g=0.16). Pediatric patients showed lower frontal GM volume (g=0.33) in the prefrontal cortex but no detectable WM alterations (Figure 1).

In both adult (peak g=-0.12) and pediatric samples (peak g=-0.26), psychotropic medication use of any kind was significantly associated with a larger decrease in GM volume in brain areas similar to those highlighted in the case-control analyses. Increased illness severity measured by the total score of the Y-BOCS (adult sample) or CY-BOCS (pediatric sample) was associated with lower regional GM volume widely distributed across most of the brain in both adult (peak g=-0.11) and pediatric samples (peak g=-0.20).

Conclusion

These results reveal brain volume alterations in OCD mapped at a voxel-level, with adult samples showing a different pattern than pediatric samples. Medication and illness severity appear to be important factors in these changes. However, although our methodology allows us to preserve data privacy and includes a greater number of sites, a limitation was that we did not have direct access to individual participant-level data, which would provide further insights into the effects of clinical variables. Therefore, disentangling these two clinical variables’ effects remains a challenge.
