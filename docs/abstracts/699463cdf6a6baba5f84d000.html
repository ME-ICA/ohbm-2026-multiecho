Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) has a strong genetic basis, yet the neural mechanisms linking semantic processing with social cognition and the extent to which they reflect familial liability remain insufficiently understood. Although prior research has emphasized social-cognitive systems¹, growing evidence indicates a bidirectional relationship between semantic and social processes, with reliable disruptions in ASD². Unaffected siblings (SIB), who share genetic and early environmental backgrounds with probands³, provide a valuable model for identifying heritable intermediate traits. This study tested whether neural systems supporting semantic control, particularly the left inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) and middle temporal gyrus (MTG), as well as perceptual processing regions such as the cuneus, exhibit endophenotypic patterns and dimensional associations with social functioning. 

Methods

Participants were 36 individuals with ASD, 36 SIB, and 37 typically developing (TD) controls. During fMRI, participants completed a semantic judgment task, with analyses focused on the left IFG, MTG, and cuneus. Social functioning was evaluated using the Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS), focusing on the Social Awareness (SA) and Social Communication (SC) subscales. Group differences and correlations between regional activation and SRS-SA/SC scores were examined to test endophenotypic characteristics and dimensional continuity.

Results

A robust endophenotypic gradient emerged in the IFG activation: ASD showed reduced activation compared with TD, and SIB showed intermediate responses, consistent with diminished engagement of semantic control mechanisms⁴. MTG activation showed a similar pattern, although SIB did not differ significantly from TD. In contrast, both ASD and SIB displayed heightened cuneus activation relative to TD, suggesting increased reliance on perceptually driven processing⁵. Across familial groups and the full sample, lower IFG and MTG activation was significantly associated with higher SRS-SA scores. For SRS-SC, reduced MTG activation was significantly associated with higher scores, while IFG activation showed only a marginally similar trend. These findings suggest associations between semantic-control activation and social functioning.

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate that both familial liability and continuous variation across the autism spectrum shape the neural substrates of semantic control and social processing. The observed endophenotypic gradient, together with robust brain–behavior associations, suggests heritable neural features that may contribute to social–semantic difficulties in ASD.
