Introduction

The circumpapillary retinal nerve fiber layer (cpRNFL) preserves the topographic organization of retinal ganglion cell (RGC) axons as they converge onto the optic nerve head. The widely used six-sector model i.e., temporal, temporal-superior (TS), temporal-inferior (TI), nasal, nasal-superior (NS), and nasal-inferior (NI) reflects major anatomical fiber bundles entering the disc, supported by classical structure–function relationships with mathematical modeling of nerve-fiber trajectories 1,2. Sectoral differences in axonal density and metabolic load might contribute to heterogeneous vulnerability across lifespan and disease. For example, some studies with aging and neurodegeneration (Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s) showed nasal and central rather than far-periphery RGC loss, possibly parasol and midget cells, respectively, reflected by thinning in temporal, and inferior-and-superior cpRNFL (i.e., papillomacular, and inferior-and-superior arcuate retinal nerve fiber bundles, respectively) 3,4. Please note that the center for cpRNFL is the optic disc, which is located in the nasal hemiretina; much closer to the nose than the fovea. 

We therefore hypothesized that sectoral cpRNFL would reveal fine-tuned associations with cortical and white-matter structure including (i) strong associations of arcuate-rich temporal cpRNFL sectors with brain grey-/white-matter, especially in occipital regions, (ii) contralateral patterns for nasal sectors reflecting hemifield organization in primary visual cortex/optic radiation, and (iii) approximate retinotopical organization in V1, temporal fibers (including fovea and macula) being represented more exteriorly closing to occipital pole and nasal fibers being represented more interiorly along calcarine sulcus. 

Methods

We used behavioral, neuroimaging and ophthalmological data from a large population-based LIFE-Adult Study (https://www.health-atlas.de/projects/5) cohort including N=966 (440 F, 526 M) at age of 53.72 ± 16.50 years for the brain grey-matter and N=702 (348 F, 354 M) at age of 50.53 ± 17.04 years for the white-matter evaluation. Spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (OCT, Heidelberg Spectralis) resulted in six-sector cpRNFL thickness measures for each eye at micron level resolution. Brain MRI data was acquired with a 3T Siemens Verio® scanner. T1-weighted (MPRAGE, 1-cubic-mm resolution) images were (pre)processed using CAT12 toolbox within voxel-based morphometry (VBM) framework (https://neuro-jena.github.io/cat/) to estimate grey-matter density (GMD). Diffusion-weighted images (twice refocused SE EPI, 1.7-cubic-mm resolution) were (pre)processed using FSL’s diffusion and tract-based spatial statistics (TBSS) pipelines (https://fsl.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/fsl/docs/diffusion/tbss.html) to derive fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD). Whole-brain voxel-wise regressions predicted GMD, FA, or MD from sectoral cpRNFL thickness while adjusting for age, sex, and OCT scan radius (plus total intracranial volume for VBM). We applied voxel-level uncorrected p < 0.001 and cluster-level FWE correction at p < 0.05 as significance thresholds. Additionally, we used Bonferroni correction on top of cluster-level p values for multiple testing of the six-sectors for each seprate eye. Nasal-sector hypotheses were evaluated by small-volume correction in bilateral occipital cortex. 

Results

Sector-level cpRNFL thickness showed robust and anatomically coherent associations with brain structure. Grey matter analyses showed that TI and NI cpRNFL sectors exhibited strong positive associations with GMD in calcarine/visual cortices including occipital pole, calcarine sulcus, precuneus, parahippocampal and lingual/fusiform gyrus (e.g., right NI cpRNFL, Figure 1, voxel-level uncorrected p < 0.001 and cluster-level FWE corrected p < 0.05) and right dominant nasal cpRNFL sector positive associations with the contralateral occipital regions with small volume correction. 

White-matter analyses revealed strong FA (positive) and MD (negative) associations for temporal, TI and TS cpRNFL sectors along the optic radiations, inferior longitudinal fasciculus, inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, forceps major, and thalamo-occipital projections (voxel-level uncorrected p < 0.001 and cluster-level FWE corrected p < 0.05). TS cpRNFL sector was specifically correlated with limbic pathways such as cingulum and fornix including hippocampus and amygdala in addition to optic radiations (Figure 1). 

Conclusion

Sector-level cpRNFL thickness might provide anatomically specific and targeted markers of retina–brain relationships. TI and NI sectors by possibly carrying dense arcuate and papillomacular RGC inputs, showed strong grey- and white-matter associations. Contralateral associations in nasal sectors confirmed our previous results showing right side dominancy 5. These findings altogether, demonstrate that sectoral cpRNFL may offer improved anatomical fidelity and may serve as a sensitive, noninvasive biomarker of distributed neural architecture and early neurodegenerative or age-related alterations in the brain. Future work using machine-learning approaches applied to ultra–high-field brain imaging and region-specific analyses may further refine these associations and help uncover more fine-grained retinotopic mapping between the retina and the brain.
