Introduction

The cerebellum plays a crucial role in motor, cognitive, and affective functions, and atypical cerebellar development has been associated with a broad range of neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions. Despite its strong clinical relevance, cerebellar research has historically lagged behind studies of the cerebrum, partly because the field lacks standardized, high-resolution surface atlases that capture cerebellar morphology during early life. Developmental cerebellar surface atlases spanning birth through early adulthood are essential for characterizing normative growth trajectories, identifying deviations linked to disease, and enabling consistent cross-study comparisons. To support these needs, we leverage high-resolution MRI data from two large-scale datasets, Baby Connectome Project (BCP) and Human Connectome Project Development (HCP-D), covering individuals from birth to early adulthood. Using these comprehensive datasets, we construct the first set of cerebellar cortical surface atlases spanning 0-22 years, providing new opportunities to systematically investigate cerebellar organization and its developmental dynamics.

Methods

T1-weighted and T2-weighted structural MRI images from 893 participants (totaling 1,166 scans) from two datasets were included in this study: BCP1 (240 participants, 513 scans, ages 0~6 years), HCP-D2 (653 participants, ages 6~22 years). All MR images were processed using iBEAT V23 and Sun et al.4, including cerebellar extraction, tissue segmentation, hemispheric separation, and surface reconstruction. After that, all cerebellar surfaces were mapped onto spherical surfaces for the consistent topology. Average convexity, mean curvature, and myelin content (T1w/T2w ratio) were computed as features to characterize cerebellar foliation patterns across ages. We further constructed longitudinally consistent developmental cerebellar surface atlases using the approach in Li et al.⁵ Surface alignment quality was evaluated using across-subject feature similarity in terms of Pearson’s correlation coefficient.

Results

Figure 1 illustrates the average convexity and myelin patterns of our cerebellar surface atlases for both left and right hemispheres at representative ages, i.e., 1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 18 months, 2, 4, 6, 10, 15, and 20 years. As shown, the constructed atlases capture the developmental progression of cerebellar structure, reflecting age-related changes in cerebellar morphology.

To assess the quality of the constructed cerebellar surface atlases, we evaluated cross-subject alignment by registering individual cerebellar surfaces to atlases and measuring the consistency of average convexity across participants. Average convexity serves as a geometric descriptor of cerebellar foliation, and improved alignment of this feature indicates better correspondence of fine-scale anatomical patterns. As shown in Figure 2 (a), after registration, we observed a substantial increase in cross-subject alignment, demonstrating that these atlases provide a coherent geometric reference and effectively reduce inter-individual variability.

In addition, we examined how key cerebellar surface properties evolve across development. Specifically, we extracted average convexity, mean curvature, surface area, myelin content, and surface thickness from each cerebellar surface and summarized their age-related trajectories. As shown in Figure 2 (b), these features exhibit biologically meaningful trends, further supporting the developmental validity of the constructed atlases.

Conclusion

We introduce the first set of cerebellar surface atlases from 0-22 years of age, which provide a longitudinally consistent representation of cerebellar development from birth to early adulthood. These atlases capture the evolving organization of cerebellar foliation and surface morphology across ages, offering detailed and anatomically coherent depictions of early-life maturation. By serving as a standardized developmental reference, these atlases enable more accurate characterization of normative cerebellar growth, support the detection of atypical trajectories, and improve comparability across studies and cohorts. This resource will advance both basic and clinical research on cerebellum and its role in neurodevelopmental conditions.
