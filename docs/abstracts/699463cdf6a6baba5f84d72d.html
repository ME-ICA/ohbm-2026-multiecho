Introduction

Population aging is accelerating, intensifying the need to understand how the brain maintains cognitive function across adulthood. Resting-state functional connectomics suggests that healthy aging is characterized by reduced segregation: within-network connectivity in the Default Mode Network (DMN) tends to decline, while interactions between the DMN and executive systems such as the Frontoparietal Network (FPN) become less anti-correlated and can follow non-linear trajectories with inflections around midlife. Recent perspectives stress strong inter-individual variability and a shift from a deficit-only view toward aging as an adaptive optimization process. The SENECA framework formalizes this idea, proposing a midlife reconfiguration that balances energetic cost and efficiency through greater control-network integration and tighter regulation of DMN activity. In parallel, deep-learning models and saliency mapping have been used to predict age from functional connectomes, yet these interpretability outputs are rarely embedded in neurocognitive theories. One of our previous work using multivariate latent-variable modeling further indicates that core cognitive performance in the CamCAN cohort can be summarized along two broad dimensions: an executive/fluid axis (e.g., hotel task, Cattell, and executive-loaded language production) and a semantic axis (e.g., semantic abstraction and sentence comprehension), providing an interpretable cognitive reference for brain-age mechanisms. Here, we adopt a theory-driven saliency perspective to test whether model-derived importance patterns capture the predicted midlife transition between segregation and integration.

Methods

We analyzed resting-state fMRI from 615 healthy adults (18–88 years) from the CamCAN cohort. Data were preprocessed with fMRIPrep; a Schaefer-400 atlas was registered to each subject’s T1 using ANTs (SyN), and denoising combined high-pass filtering, motion correction, WM/CSF regression, global-signal regression, and iterative scrubbing. Whole-brain functional connectomes were computed as Pearson correlations. The upper triangle (79,800 edges) was vectorized and used to train a hybrid multilayer perceptron comprising an age regression encoder part and a decoder to regularize hidden representations by recreating the input connectivity patterns. Model attributions were derived with SmoothGrad by averaging absolute input gradients across noisy resamples and min–max scaling each saliency map. Saliency was summarized within and between the 12 resting-state networks of the Ji et al. partition, related to nodal integration/segregation metrics (participation coefficient; within-module z), and probed with counterfactual interventions that replaced targeted edge sets with a young-adult connectome template (<45 years). Cognition was summarized by a latent embedding obtained on eight neuropsychological measures spanning language, executive control, and memory (naming, verbal fluency, proverb comprehension, sentence comprehension, tip-of-the-tongue, hotel multitasking, Cattell fluid intelligence, story recall). The multilayer perceptron and the latent embedding model were fit on the training split and applied to held-out participants by applying the learned embedding, with loadings quantifying each task’s contribution.

Results

The model generalized well to held-out data (test R² = 0.80, MAE = 7.04 years) and preserved the out-of-sample relationship between predicted age and a global mean-connectivity gradient, indicating that overall connection strength carries age signal but does not fully explain predictions. In parallel, raw functional connectivity showed a small but significant decrease with age (spin test ρ = −0.068), consistent with a connectome that becomes globally less connected across adulthood.

Focusing on topological roles, healthy aging was characterized by a non-linear (midlife-accelerating) rise in connector nodes (integrative hubs; high integration and segregation) together with an increase in peripheral nodes (low integration/segregation), i.e., a shift toward a network that is more integrated yet less cohesively structured, with clear departures from linearity. Subsystem analyses showed heterogeneous reconfiguration: DMN connector nodes increased sharply after midlife while DMN peripheral nodes also increased, indicating concurrent hub-driven integration and local “peripheralization” within DMN. The Sensorimotor network displayed robust age-related growth in connector nodes (linear + non-linear components), and the Cingulo-Opercular network showed a predominantly non-linear increase in connector nodes, both consistent with increasing cross-module embedding of these systems later in life. In contrast, the Frontoparietal network showed a decrease in connector nodes alongside an increase in peripheral nodes, marking a redistribution of integrative load away from FPN connector architecture with age.

Saliency analyses converged with these node-level findings: nodal importance increased with age, and saliency was preferentially associated with integrative hubs (higher participation coefficient), not with locally specialized/segregated nodes (within-module z). Moreover, the positive link between participation and saliency weakened with age, suggesting that the model’s reliance on highly integrative long-range hub structure is strongest earlier in adulthood. Counterfactual “rejuvenation” manipulations showed that restoring specific DMN-FPN patterns could reduce predicted age, with the strongest effects in older participants. Finally, the dominant latent cognitive score derived from the neuropsychological battery followed a non-linear decline with age, with a significant negative linear effect and an additional negative quadratic term indicating steeper decreases in older adulthood (model R² ≈ .55).

Conclusion

Together, our findings indicate that healthy aging is not captured by a uniform loss of functional connectivity, but by a structured and non-linear reconfiguration of integrative roles across brain systems. Indeed, predictions of chronological age were primarily driven by the topology of integration: model saliency emphasized long-range connections and connector hubs, particularly those bridging the Default Mode and Frontoparietal networks, rather than locally specialized nodes. At the system level, changes in topological roles revealed a redistribution of integrative load after midlife, with increasing connector-node involvement in DMN, Sensorimotor, and Cingulo-Opercular systems and a concomitant shift toward more peripheral organization within FPN. This heterogeneous pattern supports a transition in integration dynamics from long-distance DMN-FPN coupling toward more local DMN-SMN-CON integration with advancing age. Counterfactual “rejuvenation” further demonstrated that restoring targeted connectivity patterns can selectively reduce predicted brain age, with the strongest effects in older adults, providing a mechanistic bridge between explainable deep learning outputs and neurocognitive theory. Finally, the dominant latent cognitive score declined non-linearly with age, aligning cognitive aging trajectories with the observed connectome-level pivot. Overall, results converge on a midlife inflection toward greater integration and reduced segregation, consistent with the SENECA account of economical, synergistic reconfiguration, and illustrate how saliency-based interpretation can ground brain-age models in system-level principles of functional network organization.
