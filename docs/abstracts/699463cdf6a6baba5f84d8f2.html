Introduction

With the rapid advancement of clinical neuroscience and Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs), there is an increasing demand for convenient, patient-friendly EEG acquisition methods suitable for diverse environments, including home-based settings. Traditional gel-based EEG systems, while reliable, are often cumbersome and time-consuming, whereas dry electrode systems tend to suffer from elevated noise levels. In previous study, we found that wetting the hair with water (hereafter referred to as the wet condition) significantly reduces electrode impedance and improves both spectral power and functional connectivity measures relative to dry electrode application. In the present study, we aimed to systematically evaluate two EEG denoising performance under the wet condition.

Methods

We recruited 22 healthy participants and recorded resting-state EEG during eyes-open and eyes-closed conditions under dry, semi-dry(wet) and gel electrode setups within a single-session experimental design. Each condition’s state recording consists of 5 minutes with eyes open and 5 minutes with eyes closed (Figure 1). As explained, only the wet and gel data will be used in this study. EEG data were initially acquired at 500 Hz, downsampled to 250 Hz, and band-pass filtered between 0.5 and 45 Hz. Denoising performance was evaluated based on the percentage of variance removed, spectral power estimated using the Welch method, and functional connectivity similarity. Functional connectivity was quantified using the phase-locking value (PLV), in the frequency domain.

Averaged gel-based EEG data were computed from denoised gel recordings, using two established algorithms: HAPPE1, an ICA-based pipeline, and GEDAI3, a GVD-based algorithm developed by our group. The resulting gel-based EEG data were treated as a reference (ground truth). We then assessed and compared the performance of two denoising approaches applied to wet EEG recordings, denoted as wet_HAPPE and wet_GEDAI. The primary objective was to identify the optimal denoising method for wet EEG data, validate the GEDAI algorithm, and assess its suitability for future real-time neurofeedback applications. 

Results

The results showed no significant difference in the proportion of variance removed between wet_GEDAI and wet_HAPPE, although both removed significantly more variance than the averaged gel reference data. In terms of spectral power, a substantial overlap is observed across all three conditions. Topographic analyses of relative power, as shown in figure 2, indicated that wet_GEDAI yielded spatial distributions intermediate between the gel reference and wet_HAPPE. Furthermore, within-group correlation analyses of relative power and PLV revealed stronger similarity between the gel reference and wet_GEDAI than between the gel reference and wet_HAPPE.

Conclusion

In conclusion, GEDAI demonstrated superior performance for denoising wet EEG data, while also offering faster processing speed, supporting its potential utility for real-time neurofeedback applications. In future work, we plan to include additional denoising algorithms for comparison, such as ASR and pyLossless. At the same time, microstate features will also be evaluated across methods.
