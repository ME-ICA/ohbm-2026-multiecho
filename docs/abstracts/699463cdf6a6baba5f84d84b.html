Introduction

Compulsivity and impulsivity are important endophenotypic traits in a broad range of psychiatric disorders and are closely linked to alterations in fronto-striatal neurocircuitry.1 Resting-state functional MRI (RS-fMRI) is an essential tool in identifying underlying abnormalities in connectivity (RSFC) and resting-state networks (RSNs).

Methods

This RS-fMRI study investigated compulsivity (Y-BOCS total score) and impulsivity (UPPS-P scale total and sub-scale scores) in compulsive-impulsive disorders, including obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), trichotillomania (TTM), methamphetamine use disorder (MUD), and gambling disorder (GD). OCD and TTM were combined forming a compulsivity (COMP) meta-group, while MUD and GD were combined forming an impulsivity (IMP) meta-group. A total of 221 participants were included for initial clinical and demographic variables, 126 of which were incorporated in imaging analyses, comprising of 32, 28, 35, 24 and 7 participants for the control, OCD, TTM, MUD and GD groups, respectively. After preprocessing using the HALFpipe pipeline2, RSFC of striatal seeds to whole brain was compared between meta-groups using AFNI‘s 3dttest.3 Seeds included ventral striatum inferior & superior, dorsal caudate, ventral & dorsal rostral putamen (VRP & DRP), dorsal caudal putamen,4 and whole RSNs.5 Striatal seed-based analyses compared resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC) between meta-groups and diagnostic groups, and associations with Y-BOCS and UPPS-P sub-scores, with further analyses utilising whole RSFC network masks as seeds.

Results

Compared to the COMP meta-group, the IMP meta-group had higher scores for both compulsivity, t(150) = -2.818, p = 0.005, and impulsivity, t(72) = -5.51, p < 0.001. Education, IQ, and employment was significantly higher in the COMP than the IMP meta-group, while smoking and alcohol was more frequent in the IMP than the COMP meta-group (all p-values < 0.05). Age, sex, depression and clinical severity (CGI-S) did not differ significantly between the two groups (p>0.05).

The COMP meta-group displayed greater resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC) compared to the IMP meta-group between the ventral rostral putamen (VRP) seed and the left middle superior and temporal gyrus, but decreased RSFC with the putamen and caudate. Relatively increased intrinsic connectivity in the IMP compared to the COMP meta-group was found both within the sensorimotor network regions and between the default mode network (DMN) and sensorimotor network regions.

Compulsivity was associated with decreased RSFC in the COMP meta-group relative to the IMP meta-group of both the VRP and dorsal rostral putamen (DRP) seeds with various regions of the DMN (mostly anterior cingulate cortex, left superior medial gyrus and middle temporal gyrus; as shown in Figure 1A-B). This finding was also replicated when comparing the diagnostic groups OCD and TTM to the MUD group. Increased impulsivity, on the other hand, was associated with increased connectivity between the VRP seed and both the bilateral temporal thalamus and mid-orbital gyrus when comparing the COMP to the IMP meta-group (see Figure 1C).

Conclusion

We identified novel RS-fMRI abnormalities in RSFC networks between patients with compulsive-impulsive disorders, and with increasing compulsivity and impulsivity in primarily compulsive groups. Connectivity abnormalities with the rostral putamen were particularly apparent. Abnormal connectivity of the rostral putamen requires further replication as a potential biomarker of pathology and treatment intervention outcome.1
