Introduction

Contour integration, in which the visual system extracts local elements from cluttered backgrounds and integrates them into a coherent global contour, is a critical step before object recognition. Previous studies have suggested that multiple cortical regions across the visual hierarchy are involved in contour integration. However, the dynamic interactions among these cortical regions across the visual hierarchy during contour integration remain to be further elucidated.

Methods

To investigate the neural dynamics and mechanisms of contour integration, we conducted two fMRI experiments with complementary designs. In Experiment 1, we employed a 3 Tesla scanner combined with a sandwich masking paradigm to trace the temporal dynamics of cortical responses. Eighteen participants performed a contour detection task while viewing Gabor fields (with or without an embedded contour) presented at four durations (33, 67, 133, 267 ms) between masking stimuli. Blood oxygenation level-dependent (BOLD) signals were compared between contour present and absent conditions to derive psychophysical performance and the underlying cortical activation time courses across visual areas V1, V2, V3, V3B, and LO. 

In Experiment 2, We leverage recent advances in ultra-high-field (UHF) imaging to examine the interplay between different cortical areas underlying contour integration at a finer spatial scale. Compared to 3T, UHF (7T) fMRI provides submillimeter resolution, allowing voxels to be distinguished at different cortical depths within the visual cortex. Based on known anatomic laminar circuits from electrophysiological studies, we can further explore functional connectivity across cortical layers to probe feedforward and feedback processing. Specifically, we examined how ventral, dorsal, and parietal visual cortices interact to enable the perceptual grouping of discrete elements into a coherent contour. Furthermore, we examined how top-down attention modulates this distributed processing network. A 2 × 2 × 4 block experimental design was used with the factors Attention (attented vs. unattended), Contour (present vs. absent) and Orientation (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°). Eighteen human participants maintained central fixation and performed a contour detection task (attended condition) or an even-odd judgement task (unattended condition). To identified the key brain regions involved in contour processing, we used multivariate pattern analysis MVPA to discriminate BOLD responses related to contour present versus absent at different attention conditions across visual cortex. We also decode the global contour orientation at different attention conditions. 

Results

The results from both experiments revealed a hierarchical and feedback-dependent cortical process for contour integration. In Experiment 1, we observed a distinct temporal sequence of cortical activation during contour integration: the higher visual area LO responded earliest to contours (at 67 ms), followed by V3B (133 ms), and subsequently by early visual cortices (267 ms). While all regions exhibited generally increasing activation with longer stimulus durations, this staggered engagement suggests that contour processing involves feedback from higher- to lower-level visual areas. In Experiment 2, MVPA results showed that occipital and parietal cortices were engaged in contour representation during the contour detection task. Notably, when attention was diverted to a concurrent odd-even task, the representational strength of contours was significantly reduced across these regions. Critically, early visual areas V1/V2 and intraparietal regions IPS2/IPS3 exhibited attention-dependent representation, encoding contours only at attended condition—a pattern consistent with modulation by top-down feedback. In addition, IPS displayed orientation-invariant coding, and this invariance itself was modulated by attention. Further laminar and functional connectivity analyses will help clarify the specific synergistic mechanisms across these regions and across cortical layers. 

Conclusion

Together, these findings delineate a temporally staged and spatially distributed network, critically regulated by attention and feedback, that supports the integration of contour elements into a coherent percept.
