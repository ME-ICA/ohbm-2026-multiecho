Introduction

The mindfulness body scan practice induces selective attention to different body parts and engages distinct subregions of the sensorimotor network in a time-varying manner.1 A natural explanation is that these sensorimotor areas are differentially recruited according to the body area being attended to, but this remains untested. Here, we provide supporting evidence for this hypothesis. We functionally localize foot, hand, and face sensorimotor areas through three voluntary motor tasks and show that functional connectivity (FC) strengths from these areas change dynamically during the body scan across three corresponding conditions: lower limbs, upper limbs, and face. We hypothesize that selective attention to different body areas in the body scan practice modulates connectivity strengths in brain regions linked to voluntary movement of those areas.

Methods

21 Taiwanese adults (mean age = 49.4 ± 2.9), all experienced mindfulness meditators, participated in this study. The protocol was approved by Joint IRB at Taipei Medical University, and written informed consent was obtained.

Participants completed a single session in a 3T PRISMA MRI scanner, including T1-weighted anatomical imaging and a 9-minute continuous fMRI session, with 3-minute body scan intervals in the fixed order: lower limbs, upper limbs, and face. Subjects were told to close their eyes and focus nonjudgmentally on physical sensations in the designated body area during each interval. To localize sensorimotor areas linked to voluntary movement, subjects then completed a 312-second motor task with three 20-second intervals each for hand prehension, ankle dorsiflexion, and facial muscle contraction (Fig. 1a).

MarsBar was used to extract the following seed regions of interest (ROIs), corresponding to sensorimotor activation during the voluntary movement task: ankle dorsiflexion (755 voxels; peak at MNI -02 -04 58), hand prehension (693 voxels; peak at MNI -48 -22 52), and facial muscle contraction (205 voxels; peak at MNI -50 06 02). These seed ROIs were then imported to CONN, where FC was quantified using voxel-wise seed-based correlation (SBC).

Statistical analysis was performed with a Friedman test (χ²2 19, p < 0.001) to assess differences across the three body focus conditions, followed by post-hoc pairwise comparisons (T20, p < 0.05) using paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with false discovery rate (FDR) correction.

Results

The ankle ROI showed significant FC differences between the lower limbs and both upper limb and face conditions to the left angular gyrus (72 voxels; peak at MNI -52 -56 14) (χ²2 19 = 16.67, p < 0.001, W = 0.40) (Fig. 1a-b). Pairwise comparisons showed greater FC for the lower limbs compared to both upper limb and face conditions. 

The hand ROI showed significant FC differences between the face and both lower and upper limb conditions to the left cuneal cortex (89 voxels; peak at MNI -06 -80 30) (χ²2 19 = 10.67, p < 0.01, W = 0.25) (Fig. 2a-b), as well as between all three body-focus conditions to the right temporal occipital fusiform cortex (84 voxels; peak at MNI 28 -48 -09) (χ²2 19 = 20.67, p < 0.0001, W = 0.49) (Fig. 2c-d). Pairwise comparisons showed greater FC to the left cuneal cortex for the face compared to both lower and upper limb conditions and greater FC to the right fusiform in the order of face, upper limbs, lower limbs.  

There were no significant differences in FC between conditions from the face movement ROI.

Conclusion

The dynamic FC changes between body focus conditions from the motor task seed ROIs (foot and hand) support the hypothesis that body-specific sensorimotor areas underlie selective attention shifts during the mindfulness body scan. In particular, FC differences from the foot seed ROI to the angular gyrus suggest attentional demands specific to body-focus areas,2, 3 and FC differences from the hand seed ROI to the cuneus and fusiform areas suggest distinct functions related to internal body image and familiar object perception.4, 5 These findings suggest that the body scan practices strengthen functional connections between the sensorimotor and visual networks, enhancing the integration of sensory information and bodily representation according to the body area focused on.
