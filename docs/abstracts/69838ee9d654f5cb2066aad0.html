Introduction

Brain development is driven by multiple transient mechanisms that evolve rapidly in both time and space. These processes manifest at the microscopic level —through changes in tissue composition, microstructure, and cytoarchitectonic organization— and at the macroscopic level, with the emergence of cortical folding, vascularization, and differentiation between gray and white matter.1 While histology provides detailed descriptions of these mechanisms, current whole-brain datasets covering the third trimester of gestation rely on in utero MRI or MRI of premature infants, both of which are limited to coarse spatial resolutions.

We developed a comprehensive methodology for the acquisition and reconstruction of ex vivo multiparametric mesoscopic MRI data (100–200 µm isotropic) using an 11.7 T preclinical scanner. This data bridges the resolution gap between histology and in vivo imaging, and allowing in-depth study of these neurodevelopmental processes at the whole-hemisphere scale. Six brains have been acquired so far, between at 15 and 39 post-conceptional weeks (PCW), and three of them have been fully reconstructed and released as open data.2

Multiparametric quantitative mapping provides information about tissue composition and microstructure, each parameter having a specific sensitivity profile: T1 is strongly influenced by macromolecular tissue content including myelin, T2 is particularly sensitive to cell density, T2* notably reflects myelination and iron deposition,3 diffusion MRI provides estimates of fibres and neuritic density. Histological slides acquired on the same brains will provide validation for MRI findings.

This contribution presents this unprecedented dataset4 and demonstrates its potential for the study of fetal neurodevelopment, through a first characterization of neurodevelopmental processes at key stages with full 3D coverage and rich multiparametric contrasts.

Methods

Inclusion criteria

We included brains from normotrophic fetuses from 15 PCW to term, following in utero or neonatal death, excluding any major brain lesion, malformation, or genetic disease known to strongly affect brain development. This study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was granted by the parents, and handling of human brains was duly reported to the competent regulatory authority (CODECOH declaration DC-2022-5118).

Preparation

A first imaging session was performed using 3 T MRI in the context of a virtual autopsy. The hemispheres were separated and one hemisphere was dedicated to imaging at 11.7 T, the other being reserved for histology with immunohistochemical labelling (not part of this abstract). The hemisphere dedicated to imaging was fixed by immersion in formalin (12% PFA) for 2 months at ambient temperature, then hydrated and doped by immersion in a solution of PBS / Azide / gadolinium (2 mM) for 1 month. Whole-hemisphere T2-weighted imaging was acquired at 7 T (400–500 µm resolution), giving a geometric reference of the whole hemisphere. Afterward, hemispheres larger than the working space of the preclinical 11.7 T scanner (50 mm diameter) were cut into blocks that were placed in custom-fitted 3D-printed containers filled with non-magnetic oil (Fluorinert). All tissue processing was compatible with subsequent histological processing: the blocks were serially sectioned, and H&E-stained slides were acquired post-MRI on the same tissue, to enable direct comparison of MRI and histological modalities.

11.7 T acquisition

Each block was acquired on a preclinical 11.7 T scanner (Bruker BioSpec 117/16) with a multiparametric protocol at mesoscopic resolution (100–200 μm isotropic): high-resolution T2-weighted imaging for anatomical reference; quantitative T1, T2, and T2* relaxometry, as well as multi-shell high-angular-resolution diffusion imaging at 200 µm resolution (b = 1500, 4500, 8000 s/mm2 with 25, 60, 90 directions) enabling the reconstruction of biophysical models (diffusion tensor, NODDI) and tractography. Each field of view represented 150 hours of acquisition time.

Reconstruction

To reconstruct a complete volume from these blockwise acquisitions, we developed a five-step processing pipeline (see Fig. 1): intra- and inter-field of view registration, inter-block registration, global deformation correction, and image fusion.5

Results

Three fully reconstructed brains aged 18, 27, and 31 post-conceptional weeks (PCW) were released to the community as an open-access dataset (see Fig. 2a): doi.org/10.25493/X3K8-Y9W.

A first descriptive analysis of neurodevelopmental processes at these 3 key stages follows.

The 18 PCW brain was small enough to be imaged in its entirety. This developmental stage is characterized by globally short T1 values, consistent with a tissue composition dominated by extracellular matrix, i.e. high macromolecular content. Periventricular zones exhibited short T2 values, indicating regions of high cell density typical of proliferative zones. The putamen showed clusters of short T2 values suggestive of early cellular aggregation. The cortical plate (CP) displayed a trilaminar appearance in some regions but retained a predominantly monolayer organization elsewhere. This pattern is not extensively documented in the literature and the trilaminar aspect may reflect the arrival of thalamocortical afferents. In addition, the CP exhibits a radially oriented microstructure highlighted by the orientation distribution function map (see Fig. 2b). Diffusion maps showed high FA in proliferative and periventricular regions corresponding to white matter segments I and II. Proliferative zones also exhibited high diffusivities with substantial variability; in particular, areas near fiber-crossing hubs displayed lower diffusivity values. These regions have been described as more vulnerable to ischemia and hypoxia.

27 PCW

At 27 PCW, proliferative zones were smaller than at 18 PCW, while the subplate zone (SP) was more prominent. The boundary between the SP and the intermediate zone (IZ) was visible only on the T2 map (see Fig. 2b). Diffusivity maps helped delineate the basal ganglia and complement high-resolution T2-weighted imaging. The corona radiata showed alternating layers of low and high diffusivity. The low-diffusivity layer overlapped with the IZ and SbZ, corresponding to white matter segment II (particularly the sagittal strata), where tangential and radial fibers coexist. Overall, the CP displayed low diffusivities and a well-organized radial structure.

The 31 PCW brain was particularly informative because it presented both transient fetal compartments and increasingly mature anatomical organization. Proliferative zones persist but were smaller than at 27 PCW. In contrast, the CP now displayed a six-layer architecture similar to the adult cortex (see Fig. 2b). The subplate zone had markedly decreased, and the IZ can now be considered nascent “white matter”. Globally, T1 values were higher than at 18 PCW, consistent with more mature tissue composition. T2* maps revealed remarkable detail in microvascular architecture, providing insights into emerging vascular organization.

Conclusion

This first descriptive analysis is not attempting to be exhaustive, but to highlight the potential of this first-of-its-kind dataset to serve as a reference for the 3D imaging study of fetal neurodevelopment. With the future addition of more brains covering the developmental timeline, complete reconstructions of the fibre architecture including tractography, co-registered histology, and segmentations of the major brain structures, we ambition to turn this dataset into a rich neurodevelopmental atlas.
