Introduction

Youth is a critical neurodevelopmental window marked by striking individual differences in behavior1–3. These behavioral variations largely stem from evolving brain functional variability1,4–6. Functional variability manifests in two distinct forms: connectivity architecture and functional topography7,8. Both show heterogeneity across the cortex from birth, aligning with the sensorimotor-association axis9–13. However, research on the dynamic spatiotemporal changes in youth of these patterns remains limited. Furthermore, most studies rely on group-level atlases, which often obscure precise individual features14,15. In contrast, individualized functional parcellation captures specific topography and predicts behavior with greater accuracy9,10,16. Therefore, there is a critical need to systematically describe spatiotemporal patterns of functional variability in youth using individualized approaches to better understand cognitive differentiation.

Methods

Our study utilized high-quality functional MRI data (23 min scan duration) from the Human Connectome Project Development (HCP-D). Following rigorous quality control, the final cohort comprised 601 participants (ages 6-22 years). We employed an iterative parcellation algorithm to generate Individualized Functional Parcellations (IFP), mapping 87 homologous functional regions for each subject16,17. Inter-individual variability in functional parcellation (vIFP) was quantified as the spatial dissimilarity between subjects (calculated as 1 - Dice coefficient). Based on these individualized regions, we computed functional connectivity (FC) profiles. Inter-individual variability in functional connectivity (vFC) was defined as 1 - r, where r represents the inter-subject Pearson correlation of FC profiles. Crucially, to ensure these metrics reflected more accurate inter-individual differences, we explicitly regressed out the confounding effects of intra-individual variability12. To characterize developmental trajectories, we applied a sliding window approach across age-sorted individuals (window size = 10; step size = 5). Age-related trends were modeled using Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) to capture non-linear spatiotemporal patterns.

Results

vIFP exhibited a heterogeneous cortical distribution: higher in association area and lower in sensorimotor area (Fig. 1a). This aligns with prior evidence on functional variability9,12. Whole-brain average vIFP increased continuously throughout youth (Fig. 1c), indicating prolonged function individual differentiation. At the ROI level, 54 of 87 regions showed significant age effects (Fig. 1b). Most regions exhibited age-related increases, with the dorsal and ventral attention networks showing the largest effects. Notably, vIFP trajectories displayed clear non-linearity. We found a critical turning point around 14–16 years. Before this period, growth rates were low or negative, with individual variability narrowing; afterwards, vIFP increase accelerated across 41 of 54 regions (Fig. 1d).

IFP-based FC offers a more precise fingerprint than group-level atlas. Spatially, vFC mirrored the vIFP pattern (Fig. 1e). Longitudinally, whole-brain average vFC showed a weak, non-significant upward trend (Fig. 1g). However, 39 regions exhibited significant developmental effects (Fig. 1f). The dorsal attention network showed broad changes, with mixed age-related increases and decreases. Crucially, ROI-level vFC trajectories shared the distinct 14–16 year turning point observed in vIFP (Fig. 1h).

We leveraged individualized homologous functional regions to map the distribution and development of functional variability. We found that both vIFP and vFC were prominent in association area and lower in sensorimotor area—a distribution consistent with prior work that validates the reliability of our approach. Temporally, global variability increased with age. Attention networks played a critical role in these changes. While vIFP showed more widespread significant age effects than vFC, most notably, both metrics shared a pivotal developmental window. After 14–16 years, inter-individual variability in functional increase accelerated. This surge may link to shared environmental influences, advanced cognitive maturation, and the onset of psychiatric risks during youth.

Conclusion

By employing individualized functional parcellation, we mapped the spatiotemporal evolution of brain functional variability across youth. Our results confirm that inter-individual variability in functional parcellation(vIFP) and connectivity (vFC) were prominent in association area and generally expand with age. Crucially, we found 14–16 years as a pivotal turning point, marking the onset of accelerated functional individualization. These findings not only validate the necessity of precision mapping in developmental research but also provide new neurobiological evidence linking this variability surge to adolescent cognitive maturation and psychiatric vulnerability.
