Introduction

Launched in 2016, the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study is a 21-site U.S. cohort following ~11,800 youth from ages 9–10 into early adulthood with repeated brain and behavioral assessments. The ABCD Study provides curated, de-identified data with harmonized measures of mental and physical health, neurocognition, substance use, and sociocultural context, alongside neuroimaging, biospecimens, and wearable/mobile data. Since the initial public release in 2018, the ABCD study has generated a rapidly growing, cross-disciplinary body of publications spanning multiple research questions. To date, however, there has been no systematic, data-driven synthesis of this literature to characterize how the ABCD study resources are being used, which topics have been most prominently investigated, and where important research gaps remain. To address this need, we assembled a comprehensive corpus of ABCD Study publications and applied bibliometric and topic-modeling approaches to map the thematic structure, temporal evolution, and citation impact of the ABCD study research. This work provides a scalable framework for tracking research trends of the past decade of ABCD and identifying emerging opportunities as the ABCD Study enters its second decade. 

Methods

We assembled a body of ABCD publications from the ABCD Consortium’s active publication database in May 2025. We downloaded full-text PDFs of peer-reviewed journal articles through PubMedCentral, using Pubget.1 For each article, we extracted publication year, journal, country, authors, and citation counts. Citation counts were obtained from the Semantic Scholar API and cross-checked with OpenAlex2,3; discrepancies were resolved by manual review. We computed descriptive bibliometric indicators and mapped country productivity and co-authorship networks. For topic modeling, we used GROBID to extract titles, abstracts, and main text while excluding references and headers.4 Text was lower-cased, tokenized, lemmatized, and stripped of numbers, punctuation, URLs, and email addresses. We removed standard stopwords and a custom list of frequent academic terms (e.g., “methods,” “results,” “supplementary”) so that the model emphasized substantive scientific content. Using the Gensim Python library, we built a term dictionary and filtered out very rare words and terms appearing in more than half of publications.5 We then trained Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) models with varying numbers of topics (i.e., from 10-22 topics) and selected the final solution based on the mean c_v coherence across folds and team interpretability. Topic labels were assigned by two authors from highest-probability terms and refined with the full team. Results

We identified 1,293 ABCD publications (2018–May 2025). Publication volume increased each year, with work appearing in 352 journals across developmental neuroscience, psychiatry, psychology, pediatrics, genetics, and general science. The most frequent outlets included Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience, JAMA Network Open, Journal of Adolescent Health, and Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging. Most papers were authored by relatively small teams (median = 6 authors), with only a few very large consortia papers (e.g., > 100 authors). ABCD publications originated from 26 countries, led by the United States (978 papers) followed by China, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Australia. Across 214 primary institutions, leading contributors included the University of California San Francisco, Washington University in St. Louis, Yale University, the University of California San Diego, and the University of Michigan. Co-authorship network analysis revealed 27 clusters and >14,000 author–author links, highlighting dense collaborative structure. LDA topic modeling yielded a 20-topic solution with good coherence and relatively balanced representation (mean ≈ 65 papers/topic; model structure and sa;ient terms shown in Figure 1, methods). The 20 topics grouped into four broad domains: (i) ABCD methods and data modalities (Genetics, Neurocognition, Substance Use, Diffusion MRI, Functional MRI, SST Task, MID Task, and General Methods); (ii) clinical conditions (ADHD & Autism, Depression/Hippocampus, Obesity/COVID, Eating Disorders, Pain & Psychosis, Suicide & Trauma); (iii) contextual factors (Socioenvironmental Factors, Discrimination & Cultural Factors, Prenatal Factors); and (iv) adolescent behaviors (Sleep, Screentime & Puberty, Peer Victimization & Bullying). Conclusion

Our analysis shows that ABCD publications are contributing significantly to methods development in developmental cognitive neuroscience. Topic modeling revealed four broad domains, with many highly cited papers clustering in a General Methods topic and in domains such as ADHD/Autism, Functional MRI, Genetics, Screentime/Puberty, and Socioenvironmental Factors.At the same time, the corpus shows relative underrepresentation of work on peer victimization/bullying, eating disorders pain/psychosis and prenatal factors, indicating actionable gaps for future ABCD research. Because the topic themes largely reflect methodological language from full texts, future work aiming to model results-focused themes may need to restrict input to papers’ Discussion sections.
