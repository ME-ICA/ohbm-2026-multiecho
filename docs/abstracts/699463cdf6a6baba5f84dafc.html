Introduction

Panic disorder (PD) is characterized by recurrent panic attacks and heightened interoceptive sensitivity, often accompanied by maladaptive cognitive and emotional regulation. Mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) has shown clinical efficacy in PD; however, its neural mechanisms—particularly structural brain changes—remain insufficiently understood. Previous neuroimaging studies suggest that mindfulness practice is associated with structural alterations in brain regions involved in emotion regulation and self-referential processing.^1,4 Recent advances in neuroscience emphasize the role of the extended fear network, including prefrontal–limbic regions, in panic symptom regulation.^3 The present randomized controlled trial (RCT) investigated whether a neuroscience-informed MBCT (NMBCT) induces both clinical improvement and brain structural changes in patients with PD, compared with a waitlist control condition.

Methods

Forty-three patients diagnosed with PD were randomized to either an 8-week NMBCT intervention (n = 23) or a waitlist control group (n = 20). Clinical symptoms were assessed at baseline and post-intervention using validated scales, including the Panic Disorder Severity Scale (PDSS), Albany Panic and Phobia Questionnaire (APPQ), Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II), Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), Anxiety Sensitivity Index-Revised (ASI-R), and Self-Compassion Scale–Short Form (SCS-SF).

High-resolution T1-weighted MRI scans were acquired at both time points. Cortical thickness, surface area, and subcortical volume were analyzed using FreeSurfer longitudinal pipelines. Region-of-interest analyses focused on prefrontal and limbic regions implicated in PD, including the medial prefrontal cortex, dorsomedial prefrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, insula, precuneus, cuneus, and entorhinal cortex. Longitudinal changes were examined using repeated-measures ANOVA (RMANOVA) and linear mixed-effects models (LMM), controlling for age and sex.

Results

Compared with the waitlist group, the NMBCT intervention group demonstrated significant group-by-time interactions indicating greater improvement in panic severity, agoraphobic avoidance, anxiety sensitivity, depressive symptoms, and self-compassion (all p < 0.05). Structural neuroimaging analyses revealed significant longitudinal structural brain changes in the intervention group, including alterations in cortical thickness and surface area within the medial prefrontal cortex, insula, precuneus, cuneus, and entorhinal cortex, as well as volume changes in prefrontal regions. Several prefrontal and insular structural changes showed parallel trajectories with clinical symptom improvement.

Conclusion

This RCT provides convergent clinical and neurostructural evidence that a NMBCT program produces meaningful symptom improvement and brain structural plasticity in patients with PD. The findings support mindfulness-based interventions as mechanistically grounded treatments that modulate brain regions involved in emotional regulation and interoceptive processing, highlighting their potential role in precision psychiatry for PD.
