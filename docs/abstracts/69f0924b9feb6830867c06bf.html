Introduction

Neuromaps1 is a popular open-source Python framework designed to simplify comparisons between brain maps and coordinate spaces in humans. The expanding body of non-human primate (NHP) research, driven by their close neuroanatomical similarity to humans and value for understanding of brain organization and connectivity,2 presents a need to extend the original Neuromaps framework to incorporate non-human coordinate spaces. Given the lack of standards across this large number of imaging coordinate spaces, it is critical that the Neuromaps framework be adapted to handle the numerous commonly used templates and coordinate spaces across several species. Here, we present Neuromaps-PRIME, an extension of Neuromaps that enables transformations across NHP spaces and facilitates cross-species comparisons, demonstrating example use cases for transforming parcellations across the available spaces. We further incorporate annotations associated with the various spaces as part of Neuromaps-PRIME, while remaining flexible to accommodate user-provided additions.

Methods

In Neuromaps-PRIME, we extend the set of transforms available through Neuromaps by adding five rhesus macaque template spaces: Yerkes19, MEBRAINS, D99, CIVETNMT, and NMT2 (symmetric), leveraging volumetric and surface transforms made publicly available by the RheMAP resource.3 Surface transformations between these coordinate spaces were computed using the CIVET software environment.4 Additionally, to support cross-species transformations,5 we incorporated transformations between the fsLR (human) and Yerkes19 (NHP) spaces.

To support this complex and growing network of spaces, we designed a graph-based architecture in which each node corresponds to a distinct brain template space, and each edge represents the transformation between two spaces (Figure 1). Nodes include the name of the template (e.g., 'MEBRAINS'), the relevant species (e.g., 'macaque'), and a descriptive summary (e.g., ‘MEBRAINS 1.0 Macaque atlas'), along with the native surfaces and volumes. Edges represent the composite transforms (volumes) or projected spheres (surfaces). Figure 1 illustrates this graph, including all presently incorporated spaces and high-quality transformations included in the architecture.

In addition to the transformations, Neuromaps-PRIME accommodates an extensible set of annotations (e.g., receptor maps). We implemented a linked-metadata standard that associates spaces and their definition files with their associated annotations. This definition can be updated directly in Neuromaps-PRIME and also by tool users in real-time, allowing them to update the nodes and edges in the network without having to explicitly change the codebase.  This approach separates configuration from implementation, making the incorporation of new atlases or maps a straightforward process that does not require additional development of the graph architecture. 

The surface and volumetric transformations require specific versions of multiple tools (e.g. Connectome Workbench, ANTs). A robust execution framework is therefore required to ensure transformations can be reliably performed across diverse computing environments. To address this and alleviate the challenges that arise with installing and using these different tools, we integrated NiWrap, a portable wrapper for neuroimaging tools. The codebase for Neuromaps-PRIME can be found at https://github.com/childmindresearch/neuromaps-prime. 

Results

The Neuromaps-PRIME framework integrates multiscale, multimodal annotations across NHP and human brains, enabling comprehensive comparative analyses of brain organization within and between species. By extending the original Neuromaps framework, it provides access to diverse NHP brain datasets alongside specialized tools for NHP-specific analyses.

Beyond supporting extensibility, the graph-based structure at the core of Neuromaps-PRIME allows the system to dynamically construct multi-step transformations where direct mapping between two nodes is not available. For example, transforming between ‘fsLR’ and ‘MEBRAINS’ requires chaining transformations from ‘fsLR’ to ‘Yerkes19’, and then from ‘Yerkes19’ to ‘MEBRAINS’. When no direct transform between two template spaces exists, the engine computes the shortest path that exists — without unnecessary modality changes across surface or volume — and combines the necessary intermediate transforms before ultimately invoking low-level functions to generate new transforms on the fly. These newly created transforms can optionally be inserted back into the graph, providing a cached resource that accelerates future queries by avoiding repeated multi-step computations.

Extending the original Neuromaps toolbox, Neuromaps-PRIME enables users to project atlases across different spaces and across species. Figure 2 visualizes the projection of the Yerkes19 Markov atlas across all available spaces (NHP: D99, MEBRAINS, NMT2Sym; human: fsLR), demonstrating both surface and volume-based transformations. Additionally, Neuromaps-PRIME supports transformations of the human brain annotations, e.g., metabolic or microstructural maps, to NHP. The intuitive graph structure supports the flexible integration of additional atlases in the future, such as RheMAP-Surf surface transformations.

Neuromaps-PRIME is shipped in a straightforward Python API for finding and applying transformations between atlas spaces alongside a user-friendly configuration, allowing new spaces and transformations to be added seamlessly. 

Conclusion

Alongside a curated library of NHP and human brain annotations, we provide a robust framework capable of performing comparative, cross-species neuroimaging research. With Neuromaps-PRIME, we showcase the extensibility of a graph-based approach for integrating surface and volume transformation workflows, particularly in the translation of brain imaging atlases to new contexts. This framework’s straightforward implementation and ability to dynamically compute transformations as necessary make it a powerful extension of the original Neuromaps toolbox.

Unifying brain annotations across different macaque coordinate spaces (white matter tract tracing, gene expression, neurotransmitter receptors, morphological development) into a single, intuitive API allows researchers to streamline their use of existing datasets for their own analyses. While only macaque templates are currently implemented in Neuromaps-PRIME in addition to the human templates already included in the base version of Neuromaps, future work will incorporate the inclusion of other NHP templates, such as from the chimpanzee and marmoset.

Neuromaps-PRIME bridges gaps in comparability across studies, supports the integration of emerging spaces, and provides a scalable platform for future extensions, ensuring that researchers can efficiently map and compare brain data across templates. Adapting Neuromaps for the growing non-human research landscape will accelerate this work, reduce duplication, strengthen claims, and increase scalability.
