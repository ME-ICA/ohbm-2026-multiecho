Introduction

Deep learning applied to structural MRI holds substantial potential for supporting dementia diagnosis, yet its clinical translation is limited by the opacity of model decision-making. While explainable AI methods attempt to address this issue, most approaches do not incorporate neuroanatomical context or undergo systematic validation of explanation quality. This work introduces a framework that merges CNN relevance maps with morphological brain features using unsupervised clustering and evaluates explanations by clinical review.

Methods

We collected a multi-cohort dataset of 3,253 T1-weighted MRI scans from six publicly available sources, spanning cognitively normal (CN) individuals, patients with amnestic mild cognitive impairment (MCI), Alzheimer’s disease (AD), and frontotemporal dementia (FTD). A multi-class convolutional neural network with a DenseNet architecture was trained to classify MRI scans into CN, AD/MCI, or FTD categories. After training, we generated voxel-level attribution maps using Layer-wise Relevance Propagation to identify image regions that contributed most strongly to each prediction. For every participant, we extracted regional relevance densities based on an anatomical atlas and combined these with morphological metrics, i.e., regional volumes and cortical thickness, normalized using W-scores to adjust for age, sex, intracranial volume, and scanner variability.

Using these features, we constructed three explanation spaces: one based exclusively on relevance maps, one based solely on morphological characteristics, and a context-enriched space that combined both. Hierarchical clustering with Ward linkage was applied to the context-enriched explanation space. Finally, we created three forms of post-hoc explanations: simplified cluster-level patterns, example-based explanations showing similar patients and expected disease trajectories, and textual descriptions summarizing key neuroanatomical abnormalities. Six neurologists and radiologists provided qualitative evaluations of the interpretability and clinical usefulness of the explanations.

Results

The context-enriched explanation space yielded superior clustering performance compared with spaces based on relevance or morphology alone, demonstrating higher cluster homogeneity and closer correspondence between cluster boundaries and diagnostic labels. The example-based explanations produced within this space generated clinically coherent patterns matching known trajectories of neurodegenerative progression. Text-based summaries offered concise interpretations of neuroanatomical abnormalities supporting individual model predictions. In qualitative assessments, clinicians found the explanations potentially useful for supporting diagnostic reasoning, noting that they offered meaningful anatomical context, though they emphasized the importance of careful clinical interpretation.

Conclusion

By integrating CNN relevance maps with neuroanatomical morphology, this framework produces context-rich, interpretable explanations for dementia classification from MRI. The combination of quantitative clustering evaluation and expert review provides a path toward more transparent and clinically trustworthy AI tools within neuroimaging. The approach may be extended to other neurodegenerative disorders and imaging modalities, supporting broader adoption of explainable AI within the neuroscience community.
