Introduction

The adolescent period is a time of accelerated cognitive and social development as well as of vulnerability for impulse-control disorders that may contribute to psychiatric disorders in adulthood. It is believed that, in early adolescence the amygdala dominates emotional processes, and that acquisition of normative socio-cognitive skills during adolescence depends on remodeling of prefrontal cortical (PFC) functional connectivity with the amygdala. We previously reported1 that in the adult macaque, mesoscale connections of the amygdala contact sensory, motor, prefrontal, limbic, subcortical regions, and does so in a patchy (mesoscale) and topographic way, revealing surprisingly extensive and systematic influence. Here, we hypothesize that, in juvenile macaques (ages 2-4), along with maturation of columnar organization, inputs from amygdala to PFC become more patchy and that the connectivity shifts from a predominantly feedforward to predominantly feedback. 

Methods

Three juveniles (2-4 years old), Monkey A and Monkey D, and two adults (6 years old), Monkey K and Monkey M. Two juvenile monkeys were studied each at a single age, and 2 were studied longitudinally with repeated sampling every 6 months (2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4 years of age). We used a novel focal (submillimeter) stimulation method in ultrahigh field MRI (INS-fMRI)2, to map brainwide mesoscale networks (1 stim site:1 network). Data were collected in a Siemens 7T Magnetom (custom 4 channel receive RF coil over prefrontal cortex, 10-15 slices, single shot, TR = 200 ms, in plane resolution of 350 × 350 μm2 × 1mm).

Results

Summary. We found, following stimulation of the same site in amygdala, over ages of 2-4, that the number of voxels brainwide fell significantly, consistent with the presence of pruning. In prefrontal cortex in areas 8, 9, and 10, from age 3 years, we found the appearance of patches of voxels and a potential shift of strongest voxels from middle layers to superficial layers.

Preliminary evidence for brainwide mesoscale connectional pruning from ages 2 to 3. Figure 1 illustrates data from three (2, 2.5, 3 year) timepoints in one monkey. Representative slices are shown from anterior to posterior (A) at each age (C-E). The brainwide number of voxels is observed to decline significantly from age 2 to the 2.5 and 3 year timepoints (F). This decline provides, at mesoscale resolution, preliminary evidence of pruning of brainwide functional connections. 

Preliminary Evidence for Presence of functional clusters in superficial layers of prefrontal cortex at age 3. Data was acquired using surface 5-coil array placed over prefrontal cortex. Functional scans were conducted in a Siemens 7T Magnetom, using 0.3mm voxels in plane. Data from a single 3 year old monkey is shown in Figure 2. Activations in prefrontal cortex (A) are observed following stimulation in basal nucleus of the amygdala (B). Six slices from prefrontal areas (C) are shown below (D-I, bottom row: enlarged). Voxels are observed through areas 8, 9, and F7 and appear to be more prominent in superficial layers. Clustering may also be present in superficial layers of area 8 (G) and F7 (H). Further analysis at other ages will be examined for presence of clustering and timing of their emergence.

Conclusion

  These observations form the basis for inquiry into how these mesoscale (‘columnar’) organizations might be established during the juvenile years. On the basis of pruning literature from sensory cortex development, our preliminary data support hypothesis is that we may see more diffuse distributions in early ages (e.g., age 2), followed by the appearance of clustered and topographic mapping of connections in later years (e.g. age 4). Evidence for laminar remodelling is also observed. Further quantification will be conducted to support these preliminary conclusions.
