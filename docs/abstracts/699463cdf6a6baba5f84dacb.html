Introduction

Negative symptoms are core and enduring features of schizophrenia that contribute to poor functional outcomes (Kirkpatrick et al., 2006). Negative symptoms in schizophrenia are multi-dimensional, with extensive research supporting a two-factor structure comprising Motivation and Pleasure (MAP) and Expression (EXP) dimensions (Blanchard & Cohen, 2005; Strauss et al., 2018). This two-dimensional structure has been influential, shaping negative symptoms conceptualization in the DSM-5, defining treatment targets, and guiding both clinical assessments and pathophysiological investigations (Marder & Galderisi, 2017; Kring et al., 2013). A better understanding of the structural brain abnormalities associated with negative symptoms and related receptors may help clarify their pathophysiology and guide the development of more targeted treatments (Marder et al., 2011; Galderisi et al., 2021). To advance our understanding of the neural circuitry associated with negative symptom dimensions, this study examined 1) correlations between cortical thickness, and MAP and EXP negative symptom dimensions, and 2) associations between the resulting brain-symptom correlations and cortical receptor density maps. We hypothesized different regional patterns of negative correlations between cortical thickness and EXP and MAP symptom dimensions, and unique relationships between these correlation patterns and cortical receptor densities.

Methods

Data from 22 participating sites (n = 1678) were included as part of a meta-analysis. Each site performed linear regressions examining correlations between MAP and EXP symptom dimensions and cortical thickness. Random effects meta-analyses were performed using the z-transformed partial correlation coefficients from the site-level analyses. Multiple comparisons were corrected using false discovery rate (FDR; pFDR < 0.05). Results for which p < 0.05 but pFDR > 0.05 were denoted as nominally significant. Spin permutation tests were used to examine the significance of the association between brain-symptom correlation maps from the meta-analyses and published receptor density maps (Hansen et al. 2022).

Results

While there were no FDR-significant correlations, the MAP dimension was correlated nominally and positively with mean lingual (r = 0.052, p = 0.046), paracentral (r = 0.051, p =0.037), postcentral (r = 0.051, p = 0.039), superior parietal (r = 0.057, p = 0.022), and transverse temporal (r = 0.035, p = 0.035) gyri thickness. The EXP dimension was nominally and negatively correlated with mean lateral orbitofrontal (r = -0.052, p = 0.034) gyrus thickness.

Regarding analyses of receptor densities, the MAP dimension brain-symptom correlation maps were negatively associated with receptor density maps of 5HT1a (r = -0.23, p = 0.022), 5HT4 (r = -0.21, p = 0.033), A4B2 (r = -0.25, p = 0.017), and MOR (r = -0.23, p = 0.022), and positively associated with that of GABAa (r = 0.216, p = 0.025). The EXP dimension brain-symptom correlation maps were negatively associated with receptor density maps of 5HT1a (r = -0.22, p = 0.038), 5HT2a (r = -0.20, p = 0.035), 5HT4 (r = -0.38, p = 0.002), A4B2 (r = -0.28, p = 0.03), CB1 (r = -0.27, p = 0.022), D2 (r = -0.24, p = 0.03), M1 (r = -0.24, p = 0.0315), mGluR5 (r = -0.315, p = 0.018), and MOR (r = -0.27, p = 0.027), and positively associated with 5HTT (r = 0.22, p = 0.036).

Conclusion

Our meta-analyses of cortical thickness and symptom dimension correlations found no FDR-significant correlations, though there were a few noteworthy nominally significant correlations. There was one correlation in the frontal region for EXP and five for MAP that spanned all four lobes. As hypothesized, the correlation for EXP was negative, but contrary to our hypothesis, the correlations for MAP were positive instead. However, as these were nominally significant and not robust to multiple comparison correction, they need to be interpreted with caution. As for brain-symptom and receptor density associations, 5HT1a, 5HT4, A4B2 and MOR were shared associations across MAP and EXP dimensions. The positive association of GABAa was unique to MAP and 5HTT was unique to EXP. Furthermore, negative associations with 5HT2a, CB1, D2, M1 and mGluR5 were also unique to EXP. Despite no FDR-significant correlations between cortical thickness and symptom dimensions, the pattern of nominally significant correlations for EXP and MAP showed some unique associated receptor density profiles. While further work is needed to validate the brain mechanisms underlying negative symptom dimensions, our findings provide at least partial support for distinct neural circuitry associated with the EXP and MAP dimensions.
