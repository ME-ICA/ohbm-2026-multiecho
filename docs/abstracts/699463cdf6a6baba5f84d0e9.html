Introduction

Brain imaging studies for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) have not always yielded consistent findings (Cortese, 2021), potentially owing to measurement bias in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scanners (Yamashita et al., 2023). This study aimed to elucidate the functional brain characteristics in children with ADHD by addressing measurement bias in multi-site MRI data using the harmonization method, traveling-subject (TS) method. The TS method is an innovative approach for correcting differences between MRI machines. Using the TS method, sampling bias and individual differences can be controlled for the same participant across MRI scans from multiple institutions, and the regression model extracts only the measurement bias between models, enabling more accurate data correction in multi-site functional brain studies (Yamashita et al., 2019).

Methods

MRI data were obtained from 15 adult traveling subjects, 220 typically developing (TD) children (mean age = 11.98 ± 3.30 years; 84 females), and 110 children diagnosed with ADHD (mean age = 10.27 ± 2.23 years; 8 females). Imaging was conducted across four scanners—two located at the University of Fukui, one at the University of Osaka, and one at Chiba University (Yamashita et al., 2023).

Preprocessing and connectivity analysis were performed using the CONN toolbox. We calculated framewise displacement (FD) and removed volumes with FD > 0.5 mm in proprocess. Functional parcellation followed Yeo’s 7-network atlas and Schaefer’s 100-ROI parcellation derived from the same network model (Schaefer et al., 2018). We examined within- and between-network functional connectivity as well as the Amplitude of Low-Frequency Fluctuations (ALFF).

Then we applied TS harmonization to correct the differences in MRI scanners in these indices. Group differences between ADHD and TD participants were evaluated using mixed-effects models, adjusting for age, sex, handedness, intelligence quotient (IQ), head motion, and gray matter volume.

Results

For between-network connectivity, children with ADHD exhibited increased connectivity between the visual and somatomotor networks (β = 0.158, p = 0.007); however, this effect did not remain significant after FDR correction (FDR-corrected p = 0.152). For intrinsic network connectivity, ADHD children showed significantly higher functional connectivity within the dorsal attention network (β = 0.159, p = 0.006, FDR-corrected p = 0.040; Figure 1AB). Additionally, higher ALFF was observed in the limbic network in the ADHD group (β = 0.131, p = 0.006, FDR-corrected p = 0.040; Figure 1CD).

Conclusion

By applying TS harmonization to minimize multi-site MRI bias, this study revealed altered functional connectivity in children with ADHD. The dorsal attention network is associated with top–down attentional control, visuospatial processing, cognitive regulation, and antagonistic interactions with the default mode network. The limbic network comprises the orbitofrontal cortex and anterior temporal cortex and is part of an extended default network, which is considered to be associated with various attention problems. The findings suggest hyperconnectivity within the dorsal attention network and elevated ALFF in the limbic network, potentially reflecting atypical attentional regulation in ADHD.
