Introduction

Structural brain abnormalities in schizophrenia are well-documented but exhibit substantial inter-individual variability, partly reflecting the disorder’s complex genetic architecture. Polygenic risk scores (PRSs) quantify cumulative common variant liability; however, conventional genome-wide PRSs lack biological specificity. We hypothesized that biologically-constrained PRSs derived from schizophrenia-enriched gene sets explain more variance in brain structure than genome-wide PRSs.

Methods

Structural MRI and genotype data were sourced from 28,302 participants in the UK Biobank dataset.^1 PRSs were constructed from gene ontology terms that were significantly enriched for schizophrenia, as identified in the gene set analyses reported by latest genome-wide association study (GWAS) for schizophrenia.^2 Associations between PRSs and cortical thickness (CTh), surface area (SA), and grey matter volume (GMV) were evaluated using linear regression models, as well as a LASSO model that jointly incorporated all gene-set-specific PRSs. Results

Genome-wide PRSs were significantly associated with reduced global CTh (R̄²=0.0003, p=0.015) and GMV (R̄²=0.0005, p=0.001). In contrast, none of the gene-set-specific PRSs showed significant associations after multiple comparison correction. LASSO regression models incorporating 23 gene-set PRSs explained variance ranging from R̄²=3.76×10⁻⁷ to 0.0004 across brain measures (p-FDR>0.12) but did not outperform genome-wide PRS models (R̄² up to 0.0005, p-FDR = 0.001–0.015). 

Conclusion

While genome-wide PRSs were reliably associated with reduced CTh and GMV, constraining PRSs to mechanistically relevant gene sets did not improve explanatory power. Advancing our understanding of schizophrenia-related brain changes will likely require more comprehensive functional genomic resources and integrative models that bridge molecular mechanisms with neuroanatomical outcomes.
