Introduction

Humor processing involves a shift from cognitive comprehension (resolving incongruity) to affective appreciation (the feeling of amusement) (Vrticka et al., 2013). Although the amygdala is central to this experience, the effective connectivity mechanisms governing this transition remain unclear. The primary purpose of this research is to elucidate the amygdala's directional influence in facilitating this cognition-to-emotion shift. To achieve this, we synthesized data from two of our previous studies using dynamic causal modeling (DCM) and parametric empirical Bayes (PEB), specifically selected to capture complementary stages of this process. We integrated our prior work on humor and creativity (Study 1), which focuses on mechanisms of "cognitive comprehension and appreciation," with our prior work on rewards (Study 2), which isolates "affective appreciation." By synthesizing these datasets, we aim to map the specific pathways through which the amygdala routes information from executive networks to reward centers.

Methods

Study Selection: 

We integrated data from two event-related fMRI studies (N = 90 total) employing dynamic causal modeling (DCM) with parametric empirical Bayes (PEB). Study 1 (N = 60) focused on cognitive comprehension and appreciation, examining the processing of creativity (Alternate/Aesthetic Uses) and humor (Incongruity-Resolution/Nonsense) (Chan et al., 2023). Study 2 (N = 30) focused on affective appreciation, examining the consumption of erotic (primary), sexual humor (social), and monetary (material) rewards (Chan & Chou, 2022).

Synthesis Protocol:

To bridge these domains, we established a Synthesis Protocol that aligned the "Punchline" stage (Study 1) with the "Outcome" stage (Study 2), defining this window as the critical period for comprehension and appreciation. We formulated a cross-domain integration framework to map effective connectivity onto two distinct neural circuits sharing the Amygdala as the central hub:

  • Cognitive circuit: Connecting the Amygdala to executive targets (inferior frontal gyrus [IFG], precuneus) for processing novelty and incongruity comprehension.
  • Reward circuit: Connecting the amygdala to appreciation targets (nucleus accumbens [NAc], lateral orbitofrontal cortex [lOFC]) for processing sensory and material value.

We utilized DCM-PEB to quantify the directed connection strengths (Hz) across these circuits, testing how the amygdala modulates downstream targets depending on task demands.

Image Analysis: 

The fMRI data were preprocessed and analyzed using SPM12 (Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging) implemented in MATLAB. The DCM-PEB was used to infer effective connectivity (EC) among key regions in the dopaminergic reward system, including the ventral tegmental area (VTA), nucleus accumbens (NAc), ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC), precuneus, inferior frontal gyrus (IFG), and amygdala. DCM analysis was performed by specifying a DCM per participant in the individual-level analysis. The parametric empirical Bayes (PEB) analysis was used to estimate group-level connectivity patterns.

Results

The synthesis reveals a dynamic, context-dependent role for the amygdala in facilitating the shift from comprehension to appreciation (see Figure 1).

1. Facilitation of processing stages: 

Across both datasets, the amygdala consistently modulated the transition from detection (novelty, incongruity, or reward presence) to evaluation and appreciation, flexibly engaging executive or valuation targets depending on task demands.

2. Domain-specific routing:

2.1 Cognitive comprehension and appreciation (Study 1): In tasks requiring the resolution of ambiguity (creativity and humor), the amygdala exerted significant excitatory influence on the IFG and precuneus. This connectivity was critical for both resolving expectation violations (e.g., "getting" the joke) and supporting the subsequent appreciation of the resolved content via distinct pathways: receiving inputs from the VTA (evaluating humorous amusement) and projecting to the VTA (evaluating aesthetic pleasure).

2.2 Affective appreciation (Study 2): In tasks involving direct reward consumption, the amygdala shifted its effective connectivity profile. For primary erotic rewards, it engaged the lOFC (sensory appreciation). For monetary rewards, it targeted the NAc (material appreciation). For sexual humor rewards, it recruited inputs from the VTA and the vmPFC (funniness appreciation).

2.3 Integration: Sexual humor, which requires both comprehension and appreciation, uniquely recruited inputs from the VTA and vmPFC into the Amygdala, illustrating the integration of social cognition with reward value.

Conclusion

This synthesis supports a unified neurobiological model, positioning the amygdala as a flexible "switchboard" essential for high-order appreciation. We demonstrate that the amygdala not merely as an emotion generator, but as a dynamic hub that adapts its effective connectivity based on the nature of the experience. Specifically, it exerts excitatory influence on frontoparietal executive targets to facilitate cognitive comprehension and appreciation in humor and creativity, while distinctly engaging reward targets (lOFC, NAc) to process affective appreciation in sensory and material contexts. These findings elucidate the neural mechanisms by which the brain integrates surprise, understanding, and pleasure, providing a network-level explanation for how we navigate the complex transition from cognition to emotion.
