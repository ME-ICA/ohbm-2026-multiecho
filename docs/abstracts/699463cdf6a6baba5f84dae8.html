Introduction

Pharmacological treatments for Methamphetamine Use Disorder (MUD) show limited efficacy, making neuromodulation a compelling alternative. However, current non-personalized TMS protocols, which rely on group-average coordinates, result in high individual variability in therapeutic outcomes. This study aimed to identify the personalized peak coordinates of functional dysregulation within the amygdala-prefrontal circuitry during drug-cue reactivity. We use this novel approach to establish a precise individualized dual-target TBS strategy to restore functional balance in MUD patients.

Methods

We analyzed task-based fMRI data from 60 participants (mean age ± SD: [35.86 ± 8.47] years) with MUD during a drug-cue exposure task. The Left Medial Amygdala (lmAmyg) (Brainnetome Atlas) was selected as the seed region based on its highest subcortical activation during cue exposure (mean activation ± SD: [0.27 ± 0.29]). A Group-level Psychophysiological Interaction (PPI) analysis first identified two critical prefrontal nodes: the Frontopolar Cortex (FPC) (Positive Connectivity, MNI: [-30, 40, -16], size: 669 voxels) and the Left Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (DLPFC) (Negative Connectivity, MNI: [-44, 38, 6], size: 325 voxels). Crucially, a personalized analysis was then conducted for each subject to define the optimal TMS target:

  1. FPC Target: The precise MNI coordinate corresponding to the maximum positive connectivity to the lmAmyg was extracted within the FPC cluster for each individual.
  2. DLPFC Target: The precise MNI coordinate corresponding to the maximum negative connectivity to the lmAmyg was extracted within the Left DLPFC cluster for each individual.

Results

At the group level, amygdala-centered connectivity analysis revealed two dissociable prefrontal clusters: a positive functional connection with the frontopolar cortex and a negative connection with the left DLPFC. Subject-level analyses demonstrated substantial inter-individual variability in both the strength and spatial distribution of these connections. While frontopolar–amygdala coupling was consistently positive at the group level, its magnitude and localization varied across individuals. Amygdala–DLPFC connectivity showed heterogeneous patterns, with a subset of participants exhibiting pronounced negative coupling.

Conclusion

This study introduces a novel methodology that leverages individualized connectome-based targeting to move beyond fixed-site approaches in neuromodulation for MUD. By identifying the subject-specific coordinates of the most functionally dysregulated connections (FPC: positive/hyperactive; DLPFC: negative/hypoactive), we establish a robust neurobiological framework for a personalized dual-target TBS protocol. This approach offers a critical strategy for restoring the functional balance of the craving and control circuitry, holding immense potential for significantly improving clinical outcomes in addiction treatment.
