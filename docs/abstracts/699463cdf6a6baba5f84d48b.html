Introduction

Schizophrenia is a highly complex, heterogeneous disorder that poses significant challenges for identifying its neurobiological mechanisms and optimizing treatment response.^1 Advancing precision psychiatry and personalization of medication strategies requires defining clinically meaningful subtypes and characterizing their distinct neurobiological and functional signatures.^2 In this work, we identified clinical subtypes: two data-driven-Aware and Unaware, differentiated by the Scale to Assess the Unawareness of Mental Disorders (SUMD) alongside three hypothesis-driven groups based on the Positive and Negative Symptom Severity Scale (PANSS): Low Positive Low Negative (LPLN), Low Positive High Negative (LPHN) and High Positive High Negative (HPHN). We investigated resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) biomarkers, their discriminative power, and their alignment with normative neurotransmitter receptor architectures. Fluidity and ALFF emerged as robust functional markers capable of differentiating clinical schizophrenia profiles across both subtyping approaches. To investigate the pharmacological effect of the functional signatures, we integrated estimates of receptor occupancy obtained through PK–PD modelling regarding normative anatomical map of receptor densities. ^3 Our analysis showed that D2 and 5-HT2A receptor targeting significantly modulated receptor–neuroimaging coupling, with notable effects on ALFF-related intrinsic fluctuations. These findings highlight how combining symptom-based stratification, functional imaging, and pharmacological modelling can uncover mechanistic differences across schizophrenia subtypes and support the development of personalized treatment strategies for this multifaceted disorder.^1

Methods

We analyzed multimodal data from male schizophrenia patient (age 18–50) diagnosed according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Health Disorders (DSM-IV) that have been stable for more than 10 years. Our findings are based on clinical (n=92), neuroimaging (n=86), and pharmacodynamic (n=83) data of the same cohort.   Cortical parcellation used the Schaefer 7-network, 100-region atlas with inclusion of subcortical ROIs.^4 From minimally preprocessed BOLD we extracted 819 features (ALFF, fALFF, regional fluidity, static and dynamic functional connectivity metrics, integration/segregation indices, and derived summary measures) at regional, network and whole brain level. Two data-driven subtypes (Aware vs Unaware) were defined with k-means clustering of a 43-dimensional clinical dataset (cluster number selected by silhouette score, Calinski-Harabasz, Davies-Bouldin, and inertia indices). We defined hypothesis-driven groups, LPLN, LPHN, and HPHN by thresholding the summed positive and negative items of the PANSS scale. Nonparametric test Mann–Whitney U with FDR correction defined significant regional features to distinguish the subtypes. Nested cross-validated SVM (5-fold inner for model selection, 3-fold outer for performance) pipeline with normalization, feature selection, permutation tests, and SHAP for feature importance was applied to the 819 neuroimaging features with subtype labels as ground truth.  Mechanistic relationships among symptom dimensions, receptor burden, and cortical dynamics were probed using structural equation modeling (SEM), incorporating latent factors for clinical severity, receptor load, and functional dysregulation. CFA-based measurement models were followed by MLR-estimated structural paths, with model fit evaluated via RMSEA, CFI, TLI, and χ²/df, mediation effects were estimated with 2000-sample bootstrapping. Receptor–function associations were examined with normative PET-derived density maps (D₂, D₁, 5-HT₂A, 5-HT₁A, 5-HT6) using Partial Least Squares (PLS) to compute latent variables capturing receptor–neuroimaging covariance and subject-level coupling scores. Individual receptor occupancy was calculated by pharmacokinetic–pharmacodynamic modeling based on medication dose, plasma concentration, and Ki values, and correlations between occupancy and PLS coupling scores were evaluated using Pearson’s r. Differences in receptor coupling, occupancy effects, and neuroimaging–receptor interactions across subtypes were further assessed using Mann–Whitney U tests for data-driven distinction and Kruskal Wallis with post-hoc Mann–Whitney U tests for hypothesis-driven subtypes.  

Results

Clustering of clinical data revealed two robust data-driven subtypes, Aware and Unaware, separated by the items on the Scale to Assess Unawareness of Mental Disorders (SUMD).  In parallel, we define hypothesis-driven subtypes based on PANSS positive and negative symptom thresholds defined as LPLN, LPHN, and HPHN, Fig.1 (A). Across 819 resting-state fMRI–derived features, regional fluidity emerged as the most discriminative marker of data-driven subtypes, with the Aware group demonstrating systematically higher mean fluidity than Unaware subjects, Fig.1 (B). This pattern suggests greater dynamical flexibility in Aware patients and a more rigid functional organization in Unaware patients. A nested cross-validated support vector machine (SVM) classification pipeline using all neuroimaging features effectively distinguished Aware from Unaware patients, achieving a mean accuracy of 0.76 ± 0.07, balanced accuracy of 0.72 ± 0.09, macro-F1 score of 0.72 ± 0.09 and mean area under the curve (AUC) of 0.78 ± 0.13. When limiting the model to the most statistically discriminative features, performance remained above chance, underscoring the robustness of a compact set of functional signatures. Multiclass classification among the PANSS-based LPLN, LPHN and HPHN groups yielded lower separability (accuracy ~0.44), reflecting the more graded and overlapping neurofunctional profiles across symptom-based categories. Feature importance analyses revealed that while no single biomarker dominated, distributed sets of functionally related metrics including fluidity, low-frequency oscillatory power (ALFF) measures collectively supported subtype distinction. Receptor–function integration using partial least squares (PLS) revealed coherent subtype-specific β-patterns linking normative neurotransmitter receptor density maps to individual functional profiles. Spatial maps show distinct receptor contributions across subtypes, with D2 and 5-HT2A receptors having divergent β-weights (FDR-corrected) between subtypes shown in Fig.1 (C). Individual coupling scores derived from receptor–function alignment were significantly correlated with estimated receptor occupancy (p < 0.05) in subtype-specific ways: D2 occupancy–coupling associations were strongest within the Aware subgroup, indicating that higher dopaminergic engagement was tied to stronger alignment between regional receptor density and intrinsic activity patterns; whereas 5-HT2A occupancy–coupling associations were particularly prominent in specific PANSS-driven groups, such as LPLN, suggesting serotonergic modulation may differentially shape the functional organization of patients with low positive and low negative symptoms Fig.1 (D). Structural equation modeling (SEM) provided mechanistic insight into the interrelations among pharmacological load, neurofunctional dynamics, and clinical symptomatology. The model demonstrated excellent fit (CFI = 0.985; RMSEA = 0.047) and revealed that neurophysiological mechanisms captured by fluidity significantly predicted clinical dimension (β = –0.26, p = 0.041). Pharmacological factors did not show direct associations with clinical outcomes, supporting the interpretation that neurofunctional dynamics mediate the effects of receptor-level variation on symptom expression. These multimodal results indicate that functional neuroimaging features differentiate clinically meaningful subtypes aligned with individual variations in receptor engagement. By combining clinical, imaging, and pharmacological dimensions, we uncover mechanistic heterogeneity emphasizing the potential of integrated biomarkers to guide precision psychiatry.

Conclusion

Multimodal integration of clinical phenotyping, resting-state fMRI metrics, and receptor-informed modeling reveals reproducible schizophrenia subtypes with distinct neurobiological signatures. Regional fluidity consistently emerged as a robust discriminator of clinical insight subtypes, reflecting differential neurofunctional flexibility across large-scale brain configurations. PLS-derived receptor–neuroimaging coupling demonstrated that dopaminergic (D2) and serotonergic (5-HT2A) systems interact selectively with intrinsic brain dynamics in a subtype-specific manner, with D2 coupling most pronounced in Aware patients and 5-HT2A coupling linked to PANSS-defined subgroups. Structural equation modeling further elucidated mechanistic pathways, showing that neurophysiological flexibility mediates the influence of receptor pharmacology on clinical severity, suggesting that dynamical brain features serve as a functional intermediary between neurochemical systems and clinical expression. Together, these findings highlight the utility of combining multimodal neuroimaging, receptor-informed modeling, and mechanistic network analysis to stratify patients, identify biomarkers, and advance personalized therapeutic prediction. This work supports a hypothesis-driven, receptor-informed framework for biomarker development that integrates network dynamics, pharmacology, and clinical phenotypes, and is compatible with computational modeling approaches for precision psychiatry and personalized treatment in schizophrenia.^5
