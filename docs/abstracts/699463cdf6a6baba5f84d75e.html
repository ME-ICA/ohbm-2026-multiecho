Introduction

Recent work has shown that co-activation patterns (CAPs), which cluster individual fMRI frames into recurring whole-brain activation states, offer a compact representation of dynamic brain activity. In this study, we use CAPs to characterise alterations in the dynamic properties of these states in first-episode schizophrenia (FES) compared with healthy controls (HC). Building on the results of Yang et al.1, who reported CAP abnormalities in chronic schizophrenia, we examine whether early-stage alterations follow similar or distinct patterns. We further evaluate the discriminative value of CAP-derived features using an information-leakage-controlled machine learning pipeline. We find that FES subjects switch more frequently between states and spend proportionally more time in states resembling the Default Mode Network (DMN), with results consistent across frequency band and clustering granularity.

Methods

The rs-fMRI data was acquired using the T2*-weighted gradient echo-planar imaging sequence (TR: 2000 ms, TE: 30 ms, ﬂip angle: 70°, voxel size: 3 × 3 × 3 mm^3, ﬁeld of view: 192 mm × 192 mm, matrix size: 64 × 64, 37 axial slices, slice order: alternating increasing, 300 volumes). Preprocessing followed the default CONN pipeline, minus slice-timing correction.

The dataset comprised 73 HC and 119 FES subjects.

To analyse the different frequency bands, the preprocessed data were band-pass filtered into: 0.01 - 0.027 Hz band (band 4), 0.027 - 0.073 Hz (band 5), and the canonical 0.01 - 0.08 Hz band. Time series were parcellated using the Schaefer atlas 2 with 400 ROIs and 7 cortical networks with the addition of 8 subcortical ROIs from the AAL atlas.

Analyses were performed in MATLAB (The Mathworks, inc.) version 2024a, using primarily in-house scripts.

Co-activation patterns computation: Time frames from all HC were concatenated and k-means clustering was applied to them, with cluster counts ranging from 2 to 21. Candidate solutions were evaluated using silhouette scores and anticorrelation strength between cluster pairs. Based on these criteria, CAP sets with 6, 8, 10, and 12 clusters were retained, and each CAP map was normalised to unit standard deviation. 

Cohort alignment and statistical analyses: To analyse spatiotemporal differences between HC and FES subjects, we assigned  the frames of the FES cohort to the obtained CAPs using Pearson’s correlation and then averaging to obtain the CAPs of the FES cohort.
Nine dynamic measures were then computed per subject and CAP: Persistence, Fraction of time (FT), Counts, In-degree, Out-degree, Transition Probability, Resilience, Mean Return Time, and Coefficient of Variation. Spatial CAP maps were visualised, and group differences in FT and Persistence were assessed across frequency bands using two sample t-tests (p-values Bonferroni corrected). 

Classification: We implemented a leakage-controlled pipeline using a linear support vector machine (C = 1). In each iteration, 50% of HC were randomly selected to redefine CAPs specifically for classification analysis to avoid data leakage.
Frames from the remaining HC and all FES subjects were assigned to these CAPs, and spatial features were extracted.

Only subjects not used in the classification-only CAP definition were included in leave-one-out evaluation. After removing the test subject, the training set combined CAP-defining and CAP-assigned subjects, with HC up-sampled using SMOTE to balance classes. This process was repeated 50 times for each frequency band, and mean balanced accuracy and standard deviation are reported.

Results

Statistical analysis of dynamic measures:

Persistence, defined as the average amount of frames spent in one CAP before switching to another CAP, was significantly lower in the FES cohort than in the HC cohort for all CAPs across all bands and all clustering solutions.

On the other hand, the results related to FT, defined as the amount of frames spent in one CAP out of all the frames, varied based on cluster numbers and frequency.

For k=6 and k=8, FES subjects spent significantly more time in DMN-like CAPs and significantly less time in Somatomotor (SMN)-like CAPs compared with HC, with results consistent across all frequency bands. For k=10 and k=12, FES subjects continued to show increased FT in DMN-like CAPs, but the SMN-related differences were not found; DMN effects appeared only in band 4 (0.01-0.027 Hz).
The CAPs were matched to the canonical networks of Yeo et al.(2011)3 via a Multiple Linear Regression, using the binarized mask of the 7 canonical networks as regressors.

Classification results: 

Overall, performance remained modest and did not exhibit a consistent relationship with either frequency or clustering granularity.
The highest balanced accuracy was observed for the canonical band with k=10 (62.31 % ± 4.41 %), closely followed by band 4 with k=6 (61.57 % ± 4.30 %) (Figure 1). Performance slightly varied with clustering granularity and frequency bands. Band 4 showed a monotonic decline in accuracy with increasing k, reaching 57.05 % ± 4.1 % at k=12. Band 5 exhibited decreasing accuracy from k=6 to k=10, with improvement at k=12, whereas canonical band showed increasing accuracy up to k=10 before declining at k=12.

Conclusion

FES subjects showed more frequent transitions between CAP states and increased occupancy of DMN-like states, together with reduced time in SMN-like states, relative to HC. These findings are consistent with recent evidence of unstable and dysregulated brain dynamics in FES4 and support the view that large-scale network engagement is already altered at onset 5.

The dependence of FT effects on clustering granularity and frequency band, which were robust across all bands for coarser CAP solutions, but restricted to the slowest band for finer solutions, underscore the impact of methodological choices on the characterisation of CAP dynamics. Future work systematically probing these parameters may help clarify how dynamic network alterations emerge at the onset of schizophrenia.

In contrast, classification performance remained modest and largely insensitive to CAP computation parameters. Balanced accuracy ranged from 57.05 to 62.31 % across bands and cluster numbers, with only minor differences between configurations. Despite using a similar pipeline, we did not observe accuracies as high as reported by Yang et al.1, although our results are closer to their supplementary findings on the COBRE dataset. This discrepancy likely reflects the functional differences between FES and chronic schizophrenia cohorts, though additional methodological factors (absence of feature selection, no age/sex matching) may also contribute. Finally, we observed that CAPs had to be defined separately for statistical analyses (using all HC) and for classification experiments (using half of HC); using all HC to define CAPs led to pronounced overfitting and poor generalisation of classifiers.

Together, these results indicate that while CAP dynamics capture meaningful group-level alterations in FES, their subject-level predictive utility remains limited, highlighting the need for further methodological refinement in feature extraction and classification design.
