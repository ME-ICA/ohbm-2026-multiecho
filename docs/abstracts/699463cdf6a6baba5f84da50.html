Introduction

Adolescence is a vulnerable developmental period when symptoms of psychiatric illness often first emerge. Frequently, these symptoms entail hypersensitivity to threats, pain, and other negatively valenced stimuli. Extensive work in rodents and non-human primates has identified the habenula (Hb), a small structure near the posterior thalamus, as highly responsive to aversive environmental stimuli and important for harm avoidance behaviors.1 However, the small size of the human Hb (~30mm3 per hemisphere) necessitates high-resolution fMRI sequences and careful analytic procedures to accurately measure in vivo Hb BOLD signals.2 Extending our work to precisely map human Hb resting-state functional connectivity,2 here we describe an improved procedure for defining the Hb in fMRI studies. Using this approach, we compared Hb connectivity between adolescents with clinically significant psychiatric symptoms, predominantly related to depression and anxiety, and age-matched healthy controls. We also explored associations with clinical symptom severity.

Methods

We recruited 132 adolescents (age 12-20), including 24 healthy controls and 108 with primarily mood and anxiety symptoms. Diagnoses were confirmed by clinician via semi-structured interview. All subjects completed questionnaires to assess depression (BDI), anxiety (MASC), and anhedonia (TEPS) severity. MRI was performed on a 3T Siemens Skyra scanner using protocols similar to the Human Connectome Project (HCP) Lifespan studies, including anatomical T1w MPRAGE and T2w SPACE (0.9mm isotropic) as well as resting-state fMRI (2.3mm isotropic, TR=1s, 600 volumes, 5× multiband acceleration). Preprocessing followed standard HCP pipelines,3 including multirun ICAFIX denoising and MSMAll surface alignment, followed by additional  anatomical CompCor denoising, mild spatial smoothing (FWHM=4mm), and bandpass filtering (0.1-0.01Hz). Bilateral Hb ROIs were defined at the participant level by automated anatomical segmentation using combined T1w, T2w, and T1w/T2w intensity ratio images.4

Figure 1 illustrates our new Weighted Shape Optimization method for defining participant-level Hb ROIs at functional resolution. This approach builds on the Shape Optimization method described in our prior study and found to significantly enhance sensitivity to Hb BOLD signals.2 Weighted Shape Optimization improves on this approach by accurately reflecting the spatial overlap and probabilistic tissue weights of input anatomical Hb segmentations in the output Hb ROIs.

  1. Semi-automated Hb segmentation, based on anatomical T1w, T2w, and “myelin-enhanced” T1w/T2w ratio (Figure 1A) images,4 yielded probabilistic left and right Hb segmentations at 0.9mm isotropic anatomical resolution in native participant space (Figure 1B).
  2. Voxel indexes (i.e. images with one unique integer value per voxel, shown as different colors in Figure 1C) created at 2.3mm isotropic functional resolution in MNI template space were simultaneously upsampled to anatomical resolution and warped to native space (Figure 1D) using FSL FNIRT with nearest-neighbor interpolation.
  3. Upsampled, native-warped voxel indexes from Step 2 were masked with participants’ anatomical Hb segmentations from Step 1 to identify all index values corresponding to the Hb (Figure 1E).
  4. Hb index values identified in Step 3 were used to identify corresponding Hb voxels from the original 2.3mm MNI index (Figure 1F).
  5. Each voxel in 2.3mm MNI space was weighted (Figure 1G) based on the fraction of voxels in the upsampled, native-warped voxel index that contained Hb tissue (i.e. survived masking) at that index times the mean intensity (i.e. Hb tissue probability) of segmented Hb voxels at that index. Weights were normalized by scaling the minimum intensity to 0 and maximum intensity to 1 within each Hb ROI.

Timeseries values for the resulting Hb ROIs were extracted from unsmoothed, denoised fMRI data via CONN toolbox and weighted by estimated voxel Hb tissue probability. Subject-level resting-state functional connectivity was calculated using Connectome Workbench as the z-transformed Pearson correlation between extracted bilateral Hb timeseries and whole-brain fMRI data in in 32k CIFTI space. Connectivity maps were generated via FSL PALM and evaluated using permutation-based two-sample t-tests (10,000 permutations per test) to examine differences between the clinical and healthy control cohorts and Pearson correlations to examine associations with clinical symptom scales. All analyses were controlled for age and sex. Results were evaluated at the cluster-mass corrected pFWE<0.05 level with a cluster-determining threshold of Z>2.33.

Results

Relative to healthy controls, adolescents in the clinical cohort exhibited significantly stronger Hb connectivity throughout the bilateral medial parietal lobe, including the posterior cingulate cortex, precuneus, and retrosplenial cortex. Additionally, stronger Hb connectivity was observed for the medial posterior brainstem at the level of the inferior pons extending through the superior medulla, as well as clusters within the right cerebellar lobule IX and crus II. Group difference findings are displayed in Figure 2, with and without significance thresholding. Associations with clinical symptoms were not significant, although unthresholded maps indicated a strong spatial correspondence between anxiety severity and increased Hb connectivity with default network regions, including medial parietal regions, similar to the pattern seen in our unthresholded group contrast results (Figure 2, bottom).

Conclusion

The method described here improves on previous techniques for defining the Hb in human neuroimaging studies, preserving details about probabilistic voxel tissue weighting to inform the resulting extracted Hb ROI timeseries for use in fMRI studies. This approach has been designed to scale readily to a wide range of anatomical and functional MRI resolutions, and is also applicable to binary Hb segmentations. Scripts to generate Weighted Shape Optimized Hb ROIs are freely available through the lead author's GitHub repository. Using this approach, we found significantly stronger Hb resting-state functional connectivity with parietal portions of the default network. Notably, our previous work in healthy young adults from the HCP public dataset indicated a consistent delineation between task-positive regions, which had strongly positive Hb connectivity, and task-negative regions including the default network, which had weakly negative Hb connectivity.2 These findings suggest that increased Hb-default network connectivity may be an early neuroimaging biomarker for psychiatric symptomatology, particularly anxiety.
