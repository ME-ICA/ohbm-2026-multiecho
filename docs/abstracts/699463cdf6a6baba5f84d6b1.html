Introduction

Accelerated intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS) has demonstrated efficacy in treating treatment-resistant depression. Prior pre-post fMRI studies have uncovered the neural mechanisms underlying its effects but have largely neglected the dynamic changes occurring during and immediately after each iTBS session.^1,2 This is notable since the superior efficacy of accelerated protocols is assumed to partially arise from multiple, spaced sessions, which foster long-term potentiation, long-lasting heightened cortical excitability, and cumulative nonlinear clinical improvements. ^3 Understanding these temporal dynamics is crucial for elucidating the mechanisms driving the rapid and robust effects of accelerated iTBS. This study aimed to monitor hemodynamic response dynamics throughout treatment and examine their relationship with daily clinical outcomes.

Methods

A 22-year-old right-handed male diagnosed with major depressive disorder (MDD) participated in the study and underwent accelerated iTBS treatment based on the Stanford Neuromodulation Therapy (SNT) protocol. This treatment involved 50 iTBS sessions (10 sessions/day over five consecutive days, with a 50-minute interval between sessions), targeting the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) at 90% of resting motor threshold (RMT) via neuronavigation using individual structural images. Hemodynamic responses (changes in cortical oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin concentration, HbO and HbR) were recorded before (180-sec), during (582 sec), and after (180-sec) each session. 

Depressive severity was assessed using the 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D-17) and the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) before treatment, one day post treatment, and two weeks post treatment. Self-report questionnaires, including the Participant Health Questionnaire 9 (PHQ-9) and the International Positive and Negative Affect Schedule Short Form, were administered daily. Stimulation-related side effects, such as pain and fatigue, were collected after each iTBS session using a verbal analog scale. 

Generalized least squares (GLS) regression was performed to assess temporal dynamics across sessions and treatment days. Pearson’s correlation examined the relationship between hemodynamic responses and clinical outcomes.

Results

The participant exhibited a 50% reduction in HAM-D-17 (from 30 to 15) following the treatment meeting the predefined response criterion with acceptable side effects (Supplementary 2.1). However, the MADRS reduction (from 42 to 22, 47%) fell short of the response threshold. Two weeks post-treatment, HAM-D-17 and MADRS scores increased to 27 and 34.

Significant changes in hemodynamic responses were observed in both the stimulated dlPFC (HbO: F2, 50=18.093, p<0.001, partial ƞ2=0.270; HbR: F1.555, 50=38.853, p<0.001, partial ƞ2=0.442) and contralateral dlPFC (HbO: F2, 50=16.839, p<0.001, partial ƞ2=0.256; HbR: F2, 50= 21.520, p<0.001, partial ƞ2=0.305) (Fig. 1B). 

Analysis of the changes across the 50 measurements revealed a progressive reduction in local hemodynamic responses in HbR changes during-stimulation (r=-0.29, p=0.045) and HbO changes post-stimulation (r=-0.4, p =0.004), except for post-stimulation HbR changes (r=-0.32, p=0.024) (Fig. 1C(a), 1C(c), 1C(d), solid line). Similar negative effect of daily sessions were observed in the contralateral dlPFC for HbR changes during (coefficient =-0.024, t=-3.21, p=0.002, 95% CI: -0.039, -0.009) and after iTBS (coefficient=-0.031, t=-2.88, p=0.006, 95% CI: -0.053, -0.009), as well as for the post-stimulation HbO changes (coefficient=-0.028, t=-2.06, p=0.045, 95% CI: -0.056, -0.001) (Fig. 1C(e), 1C(g), dot line).

A marginally significant anti-correlation was observed between daily after-iTBS HbO changes in the stimulated dlPFC and daily PHQ-9 scores (r=-0.88, p=0.051) (Fig. 1A)

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates the feasibility of using concurrent fNIRS to track the instantaneous hemodynamic responses to accelerated iTBS in a single subject. Accelerated iTBS induced significant hemodynamic changes in the bilateral dlPFC with progressive reductions observed across all sessions, daily sessions, and treatment days, suggesting a cumulative effect. Future studies with larger sample sizes and sham controls are necessary to confirm these findings and validate the observed trends.
