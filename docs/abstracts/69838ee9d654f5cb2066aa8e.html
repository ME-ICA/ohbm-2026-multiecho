Introduction

Gilles de la Tourette syndrome (TS) is a neurodevelopmental disorder presenting with sudden involuntary movements called tics. In addition to motor symptoms, psychiatric comorbidities such as obsessive-compulsive behaviors are commonly observed. In patients with severe, treatment-refractory symptoms, deep brain stimulation (DBS) can be an effective treatment option. Since its first application in TS, multiple subcortical targets have been explored. Most common targets are intralaminar thalamic nuclei and internal globus pallidus (GPi)1. Stimulation of the common targets yields broadly comparable clinical improvement.2 Recent studies with novel targets such as external globus pallidus (GPe)3 and subthalamic nucleus (STN)4 also report promising outcomes. 

DBS effects in TS are thought to be mediated by cortico–basal ganglia–thalamo–cortical (CBGTC) circuits. Electrophysiological recordings have demonstrated coupling between thalamic and pallidal targets during tic generation.5 Considering the comparable clinical outcomes and the electrophysiological evidence from humans, we hypothesize that DBS for TS targeted in thalamus, pallidum and subthalamic nucleus might improve tics via a common network. Here, starting on the voxel level and moving to white matter pathways, we show the subcortical tic-response network shared across targets. Furthermore, we introduce an open-source subcortical white-matter pathway atlas and anatomical modeling framework designed to model subcortical pathways that are difficult to reconstruct using conventional diffusion-based methods.

Methods

We retrospectively analyzed imaging and clinical data from 115 patients with TS who underwent bilateral DBS implantation across 12 international centers. Targets included thalamus (n = 43; Centromedian, Ventrooralis intermedius, Parafascicular Nucleus regions; CM/Voi, CM/Pf), pallidum (n = 56; anteromedial GPi, posteroventrolateral GPi, or GPe), and STN (n = 16). Electrode localization and electric field (E-field) modeling were performed using Lead-DBS. Patient-specific E-fields were transformed into standard stereotactic space (ICBM 2009b nonlinear asymmetric template). Clinical outcome was quantified as percent change in tic severity scores, measured by the Yale Global Tic Severity Scale (YGTSS), harmonized to 6 months timepoint when available. 

To characterize local DBS effects, voxel-wise DBS response maps were computed for thalamic and pallidal cohorts by correlating E-field magnitude with clinical improvement. The standard pipeline in the Lead-DBS Sweetspot Explorer was used for this analysis. The results highlighted three main local tic-response peaks per thalamus and pallidum. STN patients were initially left out as an independent test cohort. To test for a shared network, spatial similarity between STN E-fields and the combined thalamic–pallidal response map was correlated with STN clinical outcomes.

To visualize network-level effects, DBS response maps were recalculated for the full cohort (n = 115), exploiting the wide inter-target distribution of electrodes. Tract-like clusters of positively correlated voxels were identified. These clusters were anatomically interpreted with the help of the subcortical white matter atlas developed in our lab and was discussed with anatomy experts (M.P., A.F.S.), converging on three main pathways. The trajectories of the tract-like voxel clusters in were traced using curves in 3D Slicer. Uniformly distributed streamlines were generated around the curves using the newly-developed openly-available CurveToBundle tool. Connectivity between patient E-fields and these functional tract models was quantified and correlated with clinical outcomes at group and target-specific levels. 

Our analyses were supported by tract models derived from extensive anatomical literature. The connections in between TS-DBS targets are not widely available in 3D pathway atlases due to the limitations of standard tractography algorithms in the subcortex. We therefore modelled the potential inter-target pathways manually. We first reviewed human and non-human primate anatomical literature to identify subcortical pathways potentially relevant to TS-DBS. Using CurveToBundle, we manually translated these pathways into standard template space by drawing 3D curves and generating artificial streamlines constrained by anatomical waypoints. Functional tract models were then derived by fitting curve-based bundles to the tract-like response clusters observed in the voxel-wise analyses.

Results

This retrospective, multi-center study analyzed imaging and clinical data from 115 patients with Tourette syndrome who underwent bilateral DBS implantation targeting thalamus (n = 43; CM/Voi, CM/Pf), pallidum (n = 56; amGPi, pvGPi, GPe), or the subthalamic nucleus (STN; n = 16). This cohort represents the largest TS-DBS imaging dataset assembled to date. The mean age at surgery was 29.5 years ± 10.4. Across the cohort, DBS was associated with a mean post-operative tic reduction of 45.1% ± 26.6 at a median follow-up of 6 months (interquartile range, 6–8 months).

Three main tic-response peaks were identified in thalamus and pallidum. These were located in anteromedial GPi, mid-dorsal GPi and mid-ventral GPi in pallidum (n=56) and in the ventromedial/ventrolateralis anterior nuclei, rostrodorsal CM and ventral regions of parafascicular nuclei in thalamus (n=43).

Spatial similarity between electric fields (E-fields) of an independent STN cohort (n=16) and the combined thalamic–pallidal tic response map significantly correlated with tic improvement in STN patients (R = 0.53, p = 0.034), providing out-of-sample evidence for a shared subcortical network.

When recalculated across the full cohort (n = 115), the DBS response map formed continuous, tract-like clusters extending between pallidum, STN, and thalamus. These clusters followed the trajectories of three major subcortical systems: ansa lenticularis, fasciculus lenticularis, and efferent pathways of intralaminar thalamic nuclei. Modeling these tract-like patterns as anatomically constrained bundles demonstrated that stronger stimulation of this network correlated with greater tic improvement across targets (R = 0.39, p < 0.001). 

To refine the model, we applied DBS fiber filtering, retaining streamlines significantly associated with clinical improvement while preserving overall tract topology. Connectivity to the top 5% of significant streamlines correlated with tic improvement across cohorts, including at the whole-sample level after target correction (R = 0.47, p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴). Although these analyses are hypothesis-generating due to inherent circularity, they demonstrate that a single tract-based model can account for DBS response across three distinct targets. 

Another important outcome of this study is the Subcortical White Matter Atlas. To define potential tic related pathways, we constructed anatomically curated white-matter tract models based on human and non-human primate anatomical and histological literature. This process yielded an openly available subcortical DBS atlas capturing detailed pallidothalamic pathways, including projections to and from the CM-Pf complex, as well as broader systems such as striatopallidofugal and cerebellothalamic tracts. The atlas integrates decades of tracing and postmortem evidence into tract models in standard template space and is openly available (https://osf.io/f8y7u/). To enable this modeling, we developed CurveToBundle, a novel tool that generates anatomically constrained tract models from manually defined trajectories, extending prior anatomically driven approaches. The open-source tool can be accessed in the SlicerNetstim extension (https://github.com/netstim/SlicerNetstim.git) in 3D Slicer (https://www.slicer.org/).

Conclusion

By combining voxel-wise DBS response mapping with anatomically curated tract modeling, we identify a shared subcortical white-matter network underlying tic improvement across thalamic, pallidal, and STN targets in Tourette syndrome. Our findings suggest that modulation of specific sub-components of pallidothalamic and intralaminar thalamic pathways mediates tic improvement. The accompanying open-source subcortical atlas and CurveToBundle tool provide a resource for future network-based DBS studies across indications.
