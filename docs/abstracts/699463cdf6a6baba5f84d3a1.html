Introduction

Superficial white matter (SWM) bundles form dense cortico-cortical pathways that support lateralized language functions, yet their microstructural organization has remained difficult to quantify. Diffusion MRI and microstructure models such as NODDI provide neurite-density proxies like intra-cellular volume fraction (ICVF), enabling population-scale analysis of SWM microstructure.1 However, SWM lies adjacent to cortical gray matter and cerebrospinal fluid, so ICVF estimates in these regions are strongly affected by partial-volume effects (PVE), especially in images of large cohorts that use lower spatial resolution. As a result, inferences about hemispheric asymmetry and aging in SWM microstructure may be confounded by measurement artefacts rather than reflecting true biology.

Here, we contrast the Human Connectome Project (HCP; young adults) and the UK Biobank (UKB; mid-to-late adulthood) to ask how PVE and age jointly shape our understanding of hemispheric asymmetry in Broca-related SWM bundles. Specifically, we compare heritability and bundle-wise genetic correlation of ICVF in short- and medium-range SWM bundles coursing beneath pars opercularis and pars triangularis. We introduce a per-bundle Gaussian mixture model (GMM) phenotyping step that explicitly targets PVE and test whether this correction restores the expected left-lateralized organization and reveals interpretable age-dependent changes in hemispheric coupling. 

Methods

Diffusion MRI and genotype data were drawn from two large cohorts. HCP includes approximately 1,200 young adults (22-35 years) scanned at 1.25~mm isotropic resolution with a three-shell protocol (b = 1000/2000/3000 s/mm2). UKB includes tens of thousands of participants aged 45-77 years scanned at 2~mm isotropic resolution with two shells (b = 1000/2000 s/mm2) and genome-wide SNP data after standard quality control.2 Only participants of European ancestry were retained in UKB to match available LD-score reference panels.

Whole-brain anatomically constrained tractography was performed in native space. Streamlines were segmented into short- and medium-range SWM bundles using the ESBA/GeoLab pipeline,3 yielding 550 reproducible bundles per subject with consistent surface-based labeling of gyral crowns. For each subject x bundle, we sampled NODDI-derived ICVF along streamlines and aggregated values into an empirical distribution representing the bundle’s microstructural profile.

To mitigate PVE, we applied a per-bundle Gaussian mixture model. For every subject x bundle distribution, we fitted both a single Gaussian and a two-component GMM and selected the model with the lower Bayesian Information Criterion. When the two-component model was preferred, the bundle phenotype was defined as the mean of the higher-ICVF (tissue-like) component; otherwise the single-component mean was used. This step suppresses low-ICVF modes attributable to gray matter and CSF contamination while preserving variability in the SWM core. 

Subject x bundle phenotype matrices were then constructed separately for HCP, uncorrected UKB, and PVE-corrected UKB. Missing entries arose when a subject’s tractogram lacked a reliable match to a given atlas prototype. We applied completeness filtering followed by K-nearest-neighbours imputation. Prior to imputation, all bundle phenotypes were z-scored to place them on a common scale, ensuring that differences in absolute magnitude across bundles did not bias the KNN distance metric and that each variable contributed comparably during imputation. In HCP, heritability and genetic correlations were estimated using pedigree-based variance-components models that incorporate the twin/sibling kinship structure. In UKB, we performed genome-wide association studies for each bundle phenotype and used LD Score Regression4 to estimate SNP-heritability and pairwise bundle-wise genetic correlations (rg), restricting to traits exhibiting significant heritability. Representative bundle-wise genetic-correlation maps for HCP and uncorrected UKB are shown in Figure~1a-b, illustrating the influence of acquisition resolution and partial-volume contamination on apparent hemispheric symmetry. Panel~1c shows the output of the proposed GMM-based PVE correction, included here to demonstrate the methodological effect of isolating the tissue-like component prior to downstream analyses.

We examined ageing through the analysis of hemispheric coupling, and we focused on SWM bundles coursing beneath pars opercularis and pars triangularis (Op + Tr). In UKB, we computed Pearson correlations between Op + Tr bundle phenotypes within and across hemispheres, across subjects grouped into overlapping age bins spanning 45--77 years. For each coupling curve (left intra-hemispheric, right intra-hemispheric, inter-hemispheric), we summarised the slope as the percentage change between youngest and oldest bins and obtained 95% confidence intervals via 1,000 non-parametric bootstrap repetitions of the age-binning procedure.

Results

Across both cohorts, ICVF in SWM bundles was robustly heritable. In HCP, pedigree-based heritability of short-range SWM ICVF averaged around 0.42 (range ≈0.25–0.61), consistent with strong genetic control of microstructure in young adults. In UKB, SNP-based heritability estimates were lower (mean ≈0.20, range ≈0.10–0.32), as expected from polygenic architectures, coarser dMRI resolution, and summary-level GWAS modelling.

The spatial pattern of genetic correlations around Broca’s region differed strikingly across cohorts and phenotyping strategies.In HCP, bundle-wise genetic-correlation maps showed a clear left-lateralized organization, with strong within-hemisphere rg and weaker cross-hemispheric coupling, consistent with classical left-hemisphere dominance in language networks reported in meta-analytic studies5. In uncorrected UKB phenotypes, however, bundles appeared spuriously symmetric, with inflated bilateral coupling. As illustrated by Figure 1c, the left-lateralized organization re-emerged in UKB after PVE correction, with strong ipsilateral coupling in Op+Tr bundles and substantially reduced cross-hemispheric correlations.

After correction, Broca-related SWM bundles exhibited a pattern more consistent with HCP. Ipsilateral genetic correlations within left Op+Tr bundles remained high, whereas inter-hemispheric rg decreased markedly relative to the uncorrected case. At the same time, absolute coupling magnitudes dropped from ≈0.8 to ≈0.35–0.45, and the variance of mean coupling across bootstrap age bins decreased, indicating that PVE had introduced a large shared-noise component that inflated apparent covariance and symmetry.

Age-dependent trajectories of hemispheric coupling are shown in Figure 2. These curves highlight (i) the spuriously flat or negative trends in uncorrected phenotypes and (ii) the biologically interpretable divergence of inter- and intra-hemispheric coupling after PVE correction, including a modest increase in inter-hemispheric coupling and asymmetrical intra-hemispheric slopes. After PVE correction, inter-hemispheric coupling increased modestly with age (∆ ≈ +5.0%), while intra-hemispheric coupling decreased slightly in the left hemisphere (∆ ≈ −2.1%) and showed a small positive trend in the right hemisphere (∆ ≈ +1.8%). Confidence bands narrowed, reflecting more stable estimates. Together, these results suggest that aging modulates hemispheric coupling in SWM in a subtle but interpretable way that becomes visible only once PVE contamination is removed.

Conclusion

Our cross-cohort analyses demonstrate that partial-volume effects profoundly influence inferences about hemispheric asymmetry and aging in superficial white matter. In the high-resolution HCP cohort, Broca-related SWM bundles reproduce the canonical left-dominant organization expected for language pathways, with strong ipsilateral genetic correlations and comparatively weaker cross-hemispheric coupling. In contrast, the same bundles appear nearly symmetric in the lower-resolution UKB data when PVE is ignored, and age-related trends become weak, noisy, or inconsistent with established models of language lateralization.

Figure~1 illustrates how these discrepancies arise from methodological rather than biological factors. By modelling each bundle’s ICVF distribution with a Gaussian mixture model and isolating the tissue-like component, the PVE-driven low-ICVF contamination is suppressed. This correction restores the expected lateralized organization in UKB, reduces spurious covariance introduced by shared noise sources, and greatly improves correspondence with the HCP reference pattern. The shift from an apparently bilaterally symmetric architecture to a left-dominant one after PVE correction underscores the extent to which raw SWM phenotypes are shaped by resolution, cortical proximity, and mixing with gray matter and CSF.

Figure~2 shows that PVE correction also clarifies the relationship between age and hemispheric coupling in Op+Tr bundles. Without correction, inter- and intra-hemispheric correlations show flat or contradictory slopes that could be misinterpreted as genuine decline of lateralization. After correction, a more coherent picture emerges: inter-hemispheric coupling increases modestly across adulthood, while intra-hemispheric coupling exhibits asymmetric trajectories in the two hemispheres. These results suggest that hemispheric specialization in SWM is preserved into later adulthood, but that subtle age-related adjustments occur in the balance between within- and cross-hemispheric interactions.

Methodologically, our findings highlight the central importance of phenotype definition in imaging–genetics studies of SWM. Bundle-level PVE-aware modelling improves atlas matching, stabilises covariance estimates, and prevents contamination-driven similarity from being conflated with biological covariation. Conceptually, the results indicate that SWM lateralization reflects a robust organizational principle that persists across the adult lifespan, and that accurate microstructural phenotyping is essential for uncovering age-related modulation rather than masking it. More broadly, the approach provides a framework for harmonising microstructural genetics across cohorts with markedly different acquisition protocols, enabling more reliable integration of population-scale datasets.
