Introduction

Extremely preterm (EP) birth is associated with higher risks of perinatal white matter (WM) injury, potentially causing abnormal neurologic and neurocognitive outcomes [1]. MRI biomarkers distinguishing individuals with and without neurologic disorder guide research on EP birth antecedents, clinical correlates, and prognoses.  In this study, we initiate a database of fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) measures in a cohort of extremely preterm born children who are now 15 years old. In addition, we compare these quantitative diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) metrics between sex, and between those with atypical development (autism spectrum disorder, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, or cognitive impairment) and those who are neurotypically developing. 

Methods

The subjects and imaging protocol are part of the ELGAN-ECHO Study (Extremely Low Gestational Age Newborns–Environmental Influences on Child Health Outcomes). Participants  included individuals aged 14–16 years born EP underwent 3.0-T MRI evaluation from 2017 to 2019 in this prospective multicenter study. The cohort included 114 females and 143 males. 66 were identified as expressing an atypical development (including autism spectrum disorder, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, or cognitive impairment) whereas 160 were classified as having neurotypical development.

 The diffusion protocol included a 16 direction diffusion tensor acquisition with B = 1000. Spatial resolution was typically 1.5x1.5x2 mm, but varied slightly by site. Processing included converting from DICOM to NIfTI, FSL-based analysis [2] including brain extraction, eddy current correction and tensor calculation, registration to the MNI152 template, and returning Harvard-Oxford anatomic regions and JUH White Matter regions back to the subject’s native space, where FA and MD were averaged over each of the available anatomic regions from these atlases. These values were corrected for site effect (COMBAT). This resulted in a set of regional FA and MD values for each region in our 226 subjects. In addition to presenting the results for this overall cohort, we performed statistical analysis of this cohort by sex and developmental status.

Results

We show tables of the results of the FA and MD regional values for this overall cohort, the cohort separated by sex, and the cohort separated by developmental status. These sex and developmental status data are accompanied by the statistical assessment of between group differences. As this information is designed to be ‘descriptive’ we do not control these analyses for multiple comparisons. 

Conclusion

We presented a large database of diffusion properties at 15 years of age derived from a cohort of extremely preterm born infants. Within this cohort, sex differences are seen in the FA (female greater than male) in the: whole brain, total cerebral white matter, internal capsule, superior longitudinal fasciculus, uncinate fasciculus and pallidum. MD sex (female less than male) differences were seen in: total cerebral white matter, internal capsule, superior longitudinal fasciculus, caudate, putamen and pallidum. Between typically and atypically developing subjects, FA differences (atypical less than typical) were seen in: whole brain, cerebral cortex, subcortical gray matter overall, hippocampus, pallidum, putamen and amygdala. No MD differences were seen between these groups. Future work will seek to reduce limitations related to lack of normal-gestation comparison group and resolution of the heterogeneous ‘atypical’ developmental group into more diagnostic relevant clinical categories.
