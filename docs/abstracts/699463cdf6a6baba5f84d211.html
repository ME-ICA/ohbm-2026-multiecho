Introduction

As a critical non-human primate model with high similarity to humans in genetics, brain organization, and behavior, the macaque serves as an indispensable bridge between basic neuroscience and clinical medicine. Constructing a high-precision macaque brain atlas is essential for analyzing complex neural circuits, understanding advanced brain functions, and advancing translational research in neuropsychiatric disorders. The core challenge in achieving this goal lies in integrating brain imaging data across different scales and modalities: while macroscopic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) can noninvasively capture three-dimensional whole-brain structures, its resolution is limited (millimeter-scale); whereas microscopic histological staining (e.g., Nissl staining) can reveal cytoarchitectural details, it suffers from tissue deformation and information loss during two-dimensional section preparation. Existing methods are primarily designed for single modalities or scales, making it difficult to overcome the intensity, texture, and scale disparities arising from fundamental differences in imaging principles and resolutions. Current mainstream strategies typically treat super-resolution and image registration as two independent serial tasks, which not only risks error accumulation but also limits overall performance due to the lack of synergy between tasks. To overcome these limitations, we propose a synergistic optimization framework that jointly optimizes super-resolution and registration tasks. This end-to-end approach enhances both detail restoration capability and anatomical alignment accuracy, thereby overcoming the constraints of independent task processing in multimodal, multiscale brain image integration.

Methods

We first constructed a multimodal macaque brain dataset (R05) comprising Nissl-stained sections, blockface imaging, and 3D MRI, and established standardized quality control and preprocessing pipelines. Building on this foundation, we propose an end-to-end joint optimization framework consisting of two core modules: a registration module based on spatial transformer networks (STN) and a super-resolution module based on feature transfer. The entire network adopts a progressive architecture that proceeds from coarse to fine and from shallow to deep, gradually aligning input images and achieving 4× super-resolution reconstruction for high-precision registration (Figure 1a). 

The registration module employs an enhanced VoxelMorph architecture as its backbone (Balakrishnan et al. 2019), composed of a four-layer encoder-decoder (U-Net) (Ronneberger et al. 2015). This module takes concatenated source and target images as input, directly predicts dense deformation fields, and achieves differentiable image warping through spatial transformer networks. We use LeakyReLU as the activation function to accommodate the regression of both positive and negative displacements in the deformation field. Normalized mutual information is adopted as the unsupervised similarity loss, combined with deformation field gradient smoothness constraints, ensuring registration robustness and anatomical plausibility. 

The super-resolution module innovatively integrates an attention-based (Vaswani et al. 2017) feature search and transfer mechanism (Dosovitskiy et al. 2021), enabling the extraction of texture features from high-resolution reference images and their transfer to low-resolution target images. Its core components include: a VGG-19-based encoder for extracting multiscale features; a search-and-transfer module activated during training, which performs patch matching in feature space via an attention mechanism to transfer high-frequency textures from reference images to low-resolution targets; and a progressive upsampling generator that fuses multiscale features and texture information through PixelShuffle operations and residual blocks to achieve 4× super-resolution reconstruction. Additionally, we use a learnable texture generation module (LTG) (Ruan et al. 2025) that learns the mapping from reference and low-resolution images to desired textures, enabling direct synthesis of high-frequency texture features during inference. This module uses a composite loss function, including L1 reconstruction loss, cross-modal texture loss based on Sobel operators, perceptual loss based on VGG features, and WGAN-GP adversarial loss, collectively ensuring pixel accuracy and visual realism of the reconstructed images. This design eliminates reliance on computationally intensive feature search modules and significantly improves computational efficiency. 

To effectively coordinate the joint training of registration and super-resolution tasks and avoid interference between network branches, we employ an alternating optimization strategy: only registration module parameters are updated in odd-numbered training epochs, and only super-resolution-related module parameters are updated in even-numbered epochs. This approach effectively prevents mutual interference between the two tasks during joint training. Through this synergistic optimization mechanism, super-resolution provides rich high-frequency detail features for registration, while registration supplies precise geometric constraints for the super-resolution process. The two modules mutually reinforce each other, collectively achieving significant performance improvements.

Results

Our synergistic optimization framework demonstrated superior performance on the R05 macaque brain dataset, achieving key quantitative metrics of 0.4776 MI, 31.87 dB PSNR, and 0.9426 SSIM—significantly outperforming baseline methods, including VoxelMorph and DFMIR (Figure 1b).

Through comparative analysis of multiple super-resolution strategies (including reference-free blind super-resolution, reference-based super-resolution, and collaborative super-resolution integrated with registration), we found that reference-guided methods more effectively restore high-frequency details in low-resolution MRI. As shown in Figure 2a, the texture details reconstructed through the feature transfer mechanism exhibit high consistency with the anatomical features of the original MRI, rather than representing artificially generated textures by the network. This result validates the effectiveness of the attention-based feature search module in our study—this module performs dense patch matching in feature space, accurately selecting and transferring texture information from high-resolution reference images that is anatomically consistent with the target MRI, thereby ensuring the anatomical authenticity of the super-resolution results.

Furthermore, the detail enhancement provided by super-resolution plays a crucial role in improving registration accuracy: low-resolution MRI struggles to capture fine anatomical landmarks due to spatial resolution limitations, whereas after 4× super-resolution, the enhanced texture resolution enables the spatial transformer network (STN)-based registration module to identify more precise anatomical landmarks, achieving high-precision spatial alignment across multimodal data (Figure 2b). Figure 2c presents the final 3D reconstruction results, demonstrating that the framework effectively addresses practical challenges in histological data processing: tissue gaps and minor distortions inevitably introduced during section preparation are not excessively deformed but maintained within anatomically reasonable ranges under the constraints of deformation field smoothness regularization, while simultaneously achieving precise alignment across multimodal data.

Moreover, the alternating optimization strategy ensures stable joint training, while the progressive architecture successfully achieves 4× image reconstruction. The framework maintains consistent performance across different brain regions and demonstrates robust generalization capabilities in comprehensive 3D reconstruction of the complete multimodal dataset.

Conclusion

This study addresses the dual challenges of cross-modal and cross-scale data integration in macaque brain imaging by proposing an innovative synergistic optimization framework. By constructing an end-to-end unified architecture, we achieve joint optimization and mutual enhancement of registration and super-resolution tasks, significantly surpassing the performance of traditional methods (such as the linear + nonlinear registration workflow of ANTs and the independent registration model of VoxelMorph) while maintaining high computational efficiency.

The core innovation of the framework lies in breaking the traditional serial processing paradigm by deeply integrating a spatial transformer network (STN)-based registration module with a feature transfer-based super-resolution module. The super-resolution module provides registration with high-resolution features rich in anatomical details through feature search and transfer mechanisms and a learnable texture generator (LTG). In turn, the registration module offers strict geometric constraints for the super-resolution process via estimated spatial transformation relationships. These two modules mutually reinforce each other through a synergistic optimization mechanism, fundamentally overcoming the error accumulation issues inherent in independent processing. Furthermore, the alternating optimization strategy and progressive architecture collectively ensure the stability of joint training while successfully achieving 4× image reconstruction.

The framework demonstrates the ability to avoid generating tissue artifacts and maintain reasonable deformation ranges, a characteristic that grants it significant potential for clinical applications in neuroanatomical research. These capabilities also lay a solid foundation for constructing high-precision macaque brain atlases and bridge the gap between microscopic cellular structures and macroscopic brain organization, providing key technical support for analyzing neural circuit connectivity and understanding brain function mechanisms. 

Although this study is validated on macaque brain data, the framework's architecture possesses inherent generalizability. Its modular design can be adapted to different species (such as mice and humans) and imaging modalities (e.g., PET, fMRI, spatial transcriptomics images), requiring only adjustments to feature extraction modules and loss function weights for specific data characteristics to enable transfer applications. Future work will focus on three directions: First, extending the framework to more complex human brain imaging data to address greater individual variability and more intricate anatomical structures; Second, integrating additional omics data layers (e.g., spatial transcriptomics, proteomics data) to construct a unified brain spatial coordinate system for multimodal, multi-omics fusion, establishing deeper connections between cell types, neural connectivity, and whole-brain function; Third, further enhancing computational efficiency through model compression and hardware acceleration.

In summary, this study demonstrates that synergistic optimization of complementary tasks yields superior results compared to serial or independent processing. The developed framework not only addresses current technical bottlenecks in brain imaging data integration but also opens new avenues for integrative neuroscience research, ultimately contributing to the construction of more accurate brain atlases and deepening the understanding of brain organization and function across scales.
