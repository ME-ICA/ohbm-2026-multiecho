Introduction

Prior work has shown that transcallosal white matter connections in human posterior cortex exhibit protracted development into adulthood1. However, it remains unclear how this development unfolds at a finer spatial scale relative to cortical functional organization. Interhemispheric white matter pathways are usually grouped into anterior-posterior sections of the corpus callosum (CC), with the posterior fifth of the CC referred to as the splenium. Prior work has also shown that there is an anterior-posterior gradient of axonal properties along the CC, in which central axons interconnecting sensorimotor representations are larger in caliber and more sparsely packed compared to the splenium2. Work in humans suggests that T1 relaxation decreases from posterior to anterior CC3, but the splenium may have a distinct organizational pattern. Developmentally, studies examining the protracted maturation of the transcallosal pathway between bilateral parietal cortices have yielded mixed results4,5. This inconsistency likely stems from the fact that the splenium relays axonal connections ranging from a broad range of areas (temporal, occipital, and parietal cortices). Crucially, even within the parietal cortex, there are several visual field maps along the intraparietal sulcus representing the dorsal visual stream, all of which likely undergo distinct developmental trajectories. Here, we perform a diffusion MRI (dMRI) study examining how white matter properties of interhemispheric pathways vary along the dorsal visual stream, whether this variation maps onto known CC structural gradients, how these pathways might undergo distinct developmental trajectories, and whether this finer-grained dissection of CC connectivity correlates with behavioral data related to the function of the dorsal visual stream. 

Methods

A dMRI dataset was acquired from 50 children (ages: 6-13; 22 females) using a Siemens Prisma 3T MRI. dMRI data were acquired with multiple b-values (seven images for b = 0 s/mm2; 25 directions for b = 1250 s/mm2; 50 directions for b = 2500 s/mm2) with 1.5mm isotropic voxels. Data were acquired with anterior-posterior phase encoding and corrected for susceptibility-induced distortion using FSL TOPUP. To identify interhemispheric white matter tracts terminating in distinct visual field maps of parietal cortex, we defined the visual field maps in each participant’s native intraparietal sulcus (IPS). Specific adjustments were made to IPS-4 and IPS-5 to equalize their size relative to IPS maps 0-3. Interhemispheric white matter tracts were generated through constrained spherical deconvolution-based probabilistic tractography (MRtrix 3.0.4) with a left hemisphere IPS map as the seed region, and its homologue in the right hemisphere as target. Outlier streamlines (those three standard deviations from the core) were excluded using the L2 norm from the mean streamline coordinate of each CC tract. Diffusion properties were analyzed along tracts normalized by percentile, with 50% denoting the center of the CC. All participants underwent behavioral testing to quantify performance on the following tasks: test of word-reading efficiency (TOWRE-2), rapid automatized naming tests (RAN), and a line-bisection task to measure visuospatial bias (VSB) in degrees of visual angle.

Results

We were able to identify transcallosal tracts interconnecting each of the IPS visual field maps from 0 to 5. The cores of each tract from IPS-0 to 5 innervate along the splenium in a ventral-to-dorsal axis, consistent with the inferior-to-superior organization of visual field maps along the IPS. A clear gradient emerges when examining diffusion properties (either FA or MD) within the portion of each tract contained within the CC (Figure 1A). Specifically, across tracts from IPS-0 to IPS-5, MD values significantly increase (e.g., from 6.4x10-4 mm2/s in IPS-0 to 6.8x10-4 mm2/s in IPS-5), with FA values exhibiting a similar gradient of increase (from 0.82 in IPS-0 to 0.85 in IPS-5). Developmentally, FA was largely stable across the examined age range (6 to 13 years). In contrast, MD values decreased significantly with age (Pearson coefficients between -0.34 and -0.44, p-values between 0.02 and 0.001). To relate behavior to white matter properties in a holistic manner, we created generalized linear models with the mean MD of each inter-hemispheric IPS tract as predictors and a given behavioral task as the predicted variable. As shown in Figure 1B, the tests of reading (TOWRE, RAN-letters, RAN-numbers) mapped similarly onto white matter tracts (positive beta weights for IPS tracts 1, 4, & 5, negative for 0, 2, & 3), while visuospatial bias mapped uniquely onto tracts (positive betas for 0 and 5, negative for 1 & 4). Each model was statistically significant in describing behavior (predicted-measured fit p-values from 0.02 to 0.0007). As a control analysis, we average MD over all tracts within a given individual to derive a single interhemispheric parietal white matter metric. In this control GLM, this grouped white matter pathway failed to predict behavior, and performed significantly less than the individualized IPS tract models even when adjusting for model complexity. 

Conclusion

The observed increase in MD across fiber tracts from IPS-0 to 5 is consistent with prior work showing that myelin content decreases as one travels further from sensory into higher-level association cortex. However, prior work has also observed that fiber density increases as one travels from posterior to anterior CC3. Consistent with general developmental increases in myelination, we observed that MD decreases across all tracts with increasing age. Crucially, these data suggest that the increasing MD gradient observed from IPS-0 to IPS-5 tracts may be driven by differences in axonal myelination, rather than fiber packing, given that increased density would have predicted decreasing MD along the dorsal stream. The interhemispheric tracts quantified here also demonstrate functional specificity, with different tracts mapping onto distinct visual behaviors. Critically, behavior could only be modeled when corpus callosum white matter was functionally defined into its constituent visual field map connections (IPS-0 to 5). Examining inter-hemispheric white matter properties en masse failed to significantly predict behavior. Collectively, these results demonstrate a finer-scale, behaviorally-relevant organization of connectivity within the splenium which is not observable when treating this region of the CC as a monolith.
