Introduction

Once thought to be a purely motor structure, the cerebellum is now recognised as a contributor to higher-order cognitive and socio-emotional functions (Pierce et al., 2023). Emotional prosody, referring to the nonverbal aspects of speech, is a key component of social communication, allowing us to infer others’ feelings and intentions. In social interactions, it can be processed explicitly, when attention is directed toward the emotional tone, or implicitly, when emotional information is extracted despite attention being focused on another feature. Yet, the cerebellum has often been overlooked in these paradigms, either due to a limited field of view in fMRI or a primary focus on cortical regions. Consequently, the cerebellum’s role in modulating vocal emotion processing under different attentional demands remains poorly understood (Frühholz et al., 2012; Pierce & Péron, 2020). Here, we investigated how explicit versus implicit attention shapes neural responses to emotional prosody in both healthy adults and individuals with chronic cerebellar stroke.

Methods

Fifteen individuals with chronic cerebellar stroke and fifteen age- and education-matched healthy controls (mean age = 62.06 years, SD = 10.10; mean education = 17 years, SD = 3.29) participated. For the patients, the time since stroke at data acquisition was 26.81 months (SD = 24.87, range = 5-89). During 3T BOLD fMRI, participants listened to vocal stimuli expressing anger, happiness, or neutrality under two attention conditions. In the explicit task, participants identified the expressed emotion; in the implicit task, they categorized speaker gender while ignoring emotional tone. Data were preprocessed in SPM12 and CONN, including realignment and unwarping, slice-timing correction, artifact detection and scrubbing, segmentation, normalization to MNI space, spatial smoothing (8mm FWHM), and temporal denoising with band-pass filtering (0.01-0.1 Hz). Whole-brain activation patterns were analyzed using multivoxel pattern analysis (MVPA). Task-dependent network interactions were examined via generalized psychophysiological interaction (gPPI) analyses between cerebellar and basal ganglia seed regions (32 ROIs total) to characterize task-specific modulation of cerebro-cerebellar and cerebello-striatal connectivity.

Results

Compared to healthy controls, cerebellar stroke patients showed task-dependent hyperactivation patterns during vocal emotion processing. During implicit processing, patients exhibited greater recruitment of bilateral superior temporal gyrus and cerebellar lobules I–VIII. During explicit emotion categorization, patients showed increased engagement of precuneus, cuneus, anterior superior temporal gyrus, sensorimotor cortex, and inferior frontal gyrus subdivisions. These patterns suggest compensatory reliance on auditory and cerebellar mechanisms during implicit processing, and increased recruitment of attention- and control-related networks when emotional content was task-relevant. Together, these results are consistent with increased cognitive effort and strategic resource allocation in patients. 

Task modulated functional connectivity analyses using cerebellar and striatal seed regions revealed distinct connectivity profiles. Right cerebellar lobule IX showed positive coupling with bilateral putamen and vermis (lobule XII) during implicit emotion processing (p < .05 FDR-corrected), while no significant connectivity emerged during explicit processing, suggesting that implicit and explicit vocal emotion processing rely on distinct cerebello-striatal network dynamics.

Conclusion

Our findings support the growing evidence of the cerebellum's role in emotion processing, extending its function beyond motor coordination to its contribution to the dynamic allocation of attentional resources. The differential involvement of cerebellar lobules depending on task demands and attentional focus suggests an organised functional differentiation, with explicit processing engaging higher-order associative regions and implicit processing relying on more automatic mechanisms. 

Beyond advancing theoretical models of cerebellar function, this work underscores the need to consider socio-affective deficits in cerebellar patients and provides a basis for the development of targeted interventions to improve emotional communication after stroke.
