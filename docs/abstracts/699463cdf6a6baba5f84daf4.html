Introduction

Previous MRS studies have identified altered visual cortex metabolites in glaucoma compared to healthy controls, but glaucoma is a heterogeneous disease with varied visual field (VF) loss patterns. Whether different glaucoma phenotypes exhibit distinct metabolic signatures remains unknown. This study used data-driven clustering to classify glaucoma patients by VF loss patterns and investigated whether these phenotypes demonstrate different metabolic profiles in the visual cortex.

Methods

Forty-nine participants with bilateral primary open-angle glaucoma (62.8±9.5 years) underwent 24-2 Humphrey VF testing. Model-based clustering (mclust) objectively identified phenotypes based on regional VF parameters. Proton MRS (3T Siemens) targeting the visual cortex was performed, with metabolites including N-acetylaspartate (NAA), GABA, glutathione (GSH), and lactate (Lac) quantified using Osprey. MRS data from six participants (three per group) have been analyzed to date, with remaining data processing ongoing. Given the small sample size, independent t-tests were used for preliminary comparisons, and results should be interpreted with caution pending validation with the complete dataset. Pearson correlations examined relationships between metabolites and mean deviation (MD).

Results

Two phenotypes were identified: central-peripheral loss (n=22, central MD: -20.46±7.14 dB, peripheral MD: -17.52±5.86 dB) and peripheral-loss-only (n=27, central MD: -7.59±5.31 dB, peripheral MD: -9.95±3.64 dB), with no significant demographic differences. Preliminary MRS analysis revealed that the central-peripheral-loss group exhibited significantly elevated GABA (p=0.009) and lactate (p=0.020), but reduced glutathione (p=0.036) compared to the peripheral-loss-only group. NAA showed a trend toward reduction in the central-peripheral-loss group (p=0.097). GABA was negatively correlated with MD (r=-0.854, p=0.030), while NAA showed positive correlations (r=0.832, p=0.040).

Conclusion

Data-driven phenotyping reveals that glaucoma subtypes with different VF loss patterns exhibit distinct metabolic signatures. The central-peripheral-loss phenotype showed elevated GABA and lactate with reduced GSH, suggesting different pathophysiological mechanisms underlying varied clinical presentations. This phenotype-based approach may provide new insights into glaucoma heterogeneity beyond traditional patient-control comparisons. Updated results with the complete dataset will be presented.
