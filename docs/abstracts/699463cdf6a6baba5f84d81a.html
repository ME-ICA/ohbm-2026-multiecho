Introduction

Direct ZTE fMRI in humans has recently been demonstrated at a limited resolution of ΔV=(3mm)3 and ΔT=3s/volume1; but its widespread translation to clinical systems is currently a partially unfulfilled goal2. Even at the imaging rate of 3 s/volume, significant undersampling is applied, resulting in loss of sensitivity at higher temporal resolutions1.

In UTE/ZTE applications, radial sampling allows for minimizing the time between RF pulse and signal acquisition. However, non-uniform sampling density and reduced sampling efficiency with 1-spoke/TR rate limit the temporal resolution attained by radial sampling. Alternative k-space sampling trajectories, such as radial helical cones have been explored to further improve the sampling efficiency of center-out radial sampling3,4. Compared to radial sampling, helical-cones trajectory provides improved PSF distribution and enables higher undersampling rates, while reducing coherent undersampling artifacts. Since ZTE fMRI is acquired at a high sampling rate with dwell times as short as td=2μs, helical cones trajectory can be readily adapted to ZTE fMRI to enhance temporal resolution. 

This study presented first human ZTE fMRI results with helical-cones trajectory. The helical-cones offered improved sampling efficiency and signal robustness which allowed for further enhancement of temporal resolution. The proposed helical cones trajectory, was benchmarked against ZTE fMRI with radial sampling and GRE-EPI during visual and motor tasks and at rest. 

Methods

In ZTE, the readout gradient Gr is ramped up before the rectangular RF pulse and held constant until signal acquisition is complete. The center of k-space is reached at the midpoint of the RF pulse while the signal acquisition can only begin after a system- and coil-dependent delay of a few tens of microseconds. The acquisition delay results in a gap at the k-space center, since the first samples of each spoke are missing. 

At the time point of TE after the center of the RF pulse, the acquisition of the radial part starts. This time point is marked as t=0 in Figure 1a. At this time point, two amplitude-modulated sinusoidal gradients GOsc⊥1 and GOsc⊥2 were applied orthogonally to the radial readout gradient Gr, forming a helical cone readout. The gradient waveform of the sinusoidal gradients was calculated as,

                                                   GOsc⊥1(t) = GOsc•t•sin(2πfOsct)/Tenc            (1)

                                                   GOsc⊥2(t) = GOsc•t•cos(2πfOsct)/Tenc           (2)

                                                                    Gz(t) = Gr                                    (3)

Where GOsc is the peak amplitude of the oscillating gradient waveform and fOsc is the frequency of the oscillation. Tenc is the encoding time of one spoke.

View-sharing was applied by merging spokes from temporally adjacent views to generate undersampled images with different acceleration factors (Fig.1c,d,e): N radial spokes were grouped into m views (sub-groups) so that each view sampling k-space homogeneously following a spiral phyllotaxis pattern. 

All fMRI measurements were performed at a 3T clinical MRI system (PrismaFit, Siemens, Erlangen) using a 20-channel receive-only head coil. 5 healthy volunteers participated in visual task, motor task and resting-state fMRI measurements. Visual stimulation was delivered using a flickering checker-board pattern with a block design paradigm of 42s=21s(off)+21s(on) and the visually instructed motor stimulation was given in the same block design, with left-right hand switch introduced between the blocks, creating a two-task condition. 

Measurements were performed using both ZTE-R and ZTE-HC (TR/TE=2/0.06 ms, Tenc=1.6ms, α=3°, FOV=(192mm)3, ΔV=(3 mm)3, Nspokes=1500, GOsc=10mT/m, fOsc=2kHz for HC, Tacq=3s/volume, Nspi=125 SPI-acquisitions). All measurements were repeated using a BOLD EPI acquisition: TR/TE=3000/30ms, α=90°, Nslices=54, FOV=192x192x162mm3, ΔV=(3mm) 3, 86 volumes, 3s/volume. 2-fold- and 5-fold-accelerated images were acquired simultaneously during the visual-task measurements. All images were co-registered to the MNI-152 atlas and analyzed using General Linear Model (GLM) and Independent Component Analysis (ICA) in FSL.

Results

Figure 2a presents the clustered group-level analysis results for the visual task across GRE-EPI, ZTE-R, and ZTE-HC. The Z-score maps were all clustered with Z≥2 and overlaid on the MNI-152 2mm brain atlas. For all three methods, the largest clusters were localized in the primary visual cortex. The largest GRE-EPI cluster was located bilaterally across the occipital lobe (Peak Z=3.39, 2412 voxels), with a total cluster mass of 5874.5. Compared to GRE-EPI, in ZTE-R, relatively minor clusters survived. The largest cluster was explicitly located in the center lingual gyrus (Peak Z=3.00, 180 voxels). The largest cluster of ZTE-HC was found over the right occipital pole and surrounding the lingual gyrus (Peak Z=3.03, 933 voxels). ZTE-HC achieved a total cluster mass of 2572. As expected, GRE-EPI showed a larger cluster than both ZTE methods. Compared to the ZTE-R, ZTE-HC yielded a 175.9% larger cluster size and a 188.7% higher cluster mass (i.e., the sum of corrected Z-scores within the cluster), with a dominant cluster in the occipital lobe. 

Figure 2b shows the normalized mean time courses for GRE-EPI, ZTE-R, and ZTE-HC, overlaid on the event design. The corresponding power spectrums were computed by FFT and presented in Figure 4e, with all 3 dominant spectral peaks at 0.0233 Hz, which is the frequency bin closest to the event frequency 0.0238 Hz (1/42s). The correlation coefficients between the three mean time courses and the hemodynamic response function (HRF) were 0.715 for GRE-EPI, 0.765 for ZTE-R, and 0.732 for ZTE-HC. Cross-correlation between the ZTE and GRE-EPI time courses indicated that the ZTE signal preceded the GRE-EPI signal by one volume. 

Figure 2c presents clustered group-level analysis results of motor task for the three sequences, with the blue colormap representing the left-hand task and the red-yellow colormap representing the right-hand finger tapping. For all sequences, with Z≥2.3, large clusters were located in postcentral gyrus and precentral gyrus. The clusters also followed the contralateral control principle: the clusters associated with the left-hand task were mapped to the right motor and somatosensory cortex, and those associated with the right-hand task were mapped to the left side. For the left-hand task, the matching cluster observed with GRE-EPI had a size of 911 voxels (Peak Z=3.81), and a cluster mass of 2387.5. In comparison, the matching cluster of ZTE-R had 400 voxels (Peak Z=3.33) with a cluster mass of 1088.3. Two clusters were detected in the ZTE-HC result with 310 and 102 voxels respectively, resulting in a total cluster mass of 1039.2 and a Peak Z-score of 3.16. For the right-hand task, as in the left-hand task, GRE-EPI detected a large activation cluster with 895 voxels. In comparison, ZTE-R showed only a small cluster of 90 voxels, which was significantly smaller than the left-hand task result. The largest cluster of ZTE-HC was located in the left motor cortex, consisting of 429 voxels. The normalized mean time courses of the left-hand task using the 3 methods were provided in Figure 2d.

Under 2-fold/5-fold undersampling during the visual task (Figure 2e), the cluster size in ZTE-R decreased by 22%/76%, and ZTE-HC was more robust against undersampling, showing smaller reductions under 5-fold (35%/43%). Primary visual, auditory and sensory motor networks were found in resting-state analysis (data not shown).

Conclusion

In this study, we demonstrated the feasibility of ZTE fMRI using a helical cone trajectory on a clinical 3T MRI system and benchmarked it against radial ZTE and GRE-EPI for visual and motor functions and resting-state connectivity in 5 volunteers. The increased sampling efficiency with helical cones enabled an acquisition rate of 0.6 s/volume. In the visual stimulus experiments, both ZTE-R and ZTE-HC showed consistent activation in the primary visual cortex. The ZTE cluster distribution appeared more focal, located on the right half of the visual cortex, unlike the bilateral distribution observed in the GRE-EPI. The mean time course of clusters closely followed the event block design. Yet, the ZTE results showed a smaller time delay than the EPI, which can be attributed to arterial inflow being a dominant signal source. Although body coil excitation is expected to minimize inflow effects, the MRI system had a body coil approximately 50 cm long, which may not cover the heart when an average adult volunteer is positioned with eyes aligned with the isocenter. A local Tx-coil configuration may still enhance inflow effects if the same receive-coil sensitivity can be achieved. 

ZTE also demonstrated large clusters in both the left and right motor cortices following the finger-tapping task. This suggests that combined inflow and T1-related intensity changes may be smaller than the BOLD signal following the neuronal activity. Nevertheless, the corresponding cluster locations and time courses within the clusters demonstrate the sensitivity of ZTE-HC to task-related local functional activity in the brain, which has so far been demonstrated only for ZTE with radial sampling. 

Higher sampling efficiency in ZTE-HC led to larger clusters than in ZTE-R. The advantage of helical cone trajectories is highlighted in high-temporal-resolution analyses, with acquisition rates up to 0.6 s/volume. Although Z-scores decreased with increased acceleration rates, ZTE-HC remained capable of locating the largest cluster with the highest Z-score in the expected cortical region. At the same time, ZTE-R failed to detect the task-related activation in the 5-fold accelerated case. In the motor task experiment, both ZTE methods yielded comparable results for the left-hand finger-tapping task. However, on the right, ZTE-HC located a central cluster in the left motor cortex, whereas ZTE-R barely detected any large clusters in the expected cortex. Similarly, ZTE-HC outperformed ZTE-R by 5-fold, demonstrating that ZTE-HC had a better ability to preserve sensitivity to local functional activation at higher undersampling rates.
