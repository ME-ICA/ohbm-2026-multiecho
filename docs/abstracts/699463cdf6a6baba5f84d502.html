Introduction

Cerebral ischemic disorders remain difficult to diagnose at an early stage, as structural alterations are often subtle or absent in routine imaging 1. Positron emission tomography provides a sensitive assessment of cerebral metabolism and perfusion, enabling the detection of hypometabolic regions before overt anatomical damage becomes visible. However, PET imaging is limited by high cost, prolonged acquisition time, and restricted accessibility, particularly in emergency and primary care settings where rapid clinical decision making is required 2. Learning based MRI to PET synthesis has emerged as a promising approach for inferring metabolic information from widely available MRI data. Nevertheless, most existing methods assume access to high quality multi sequence MRI and rely on unconstrained data driven mappings. In real world clinical practice, MRI acquisition is frequently restricted to thick slice protocols or incomplete modality sets. Furthermore, commonly used MRI sequences such as T1 weighted, T2 weighted, diffusion weighted imaging, and FLAIR encode heterogeneous tissue contrasts, which substantially complicates robust and generalizable PET estimation from a single modality 3. In this work, we focus on PET generation from single modality thick slice MRI under realistic clinical constraints. We propose a unified framework that models local MRI to PET relationships through structured linear transformations, enabling stable PET estimation without modality specific architectural customization. By explicitly constraining the mapping between MRI and PET, the proposed approach is applicable to multiple MRI modalities within a single formulation, including T1 weighted, T2 weighted, DWI, and FLAIR images. Through systematic evaluation across different MRI modalities, we demonstrate that metabolically informative PET images can be consistently generated from diverse single modality inputs.

Methods

Dataset and Preprocessing. As illustrated in Fig. 1(d), in collaboration with partner hospitals, we collected a multimodal imaging dataset comprising 303 patients with subacute and chronic cerebral ischemia. For each subject, T1 weighted MRI and PET images were available, together with undersampled MRI modalities including T2 weighted, DWI, and FLAIR. The dataset was randomly divided into a training set of 242 subjects and a test set of 61 subjects using an 8:2 split to ensure reliable model training and evaluation. For preprocessing, all MRI and PET images were acquired at the same time point, ensuring strict spatiotemporal correspondence. To account for resolution differences across modalities, all MRI images were registered to the PET space using the Similarity transformation implemented in the ANTs 4 toolkit, achieving voxel level alignment. Skull stripping was then performed on the T1 weighted images using FSL 5, and the resulting brain masks were applied to the registered T2, DWI, and FLAIR images to consistently remove non brain tissues and eliminate potential interference from skull signals. Subsequently, zero valued background slices were cropped, yielding paired three dimensional volumes with a fixed size of 128×128×64. All registration and skull stripping results were visually inspected and confirmed by experienced clinicians. In addition, to construct undersampled T1 representations, fully sampled T1 images were also registered to the corresponding undersampled modality spaces using the same Similarity transformation.

Model Architecture. As illustrated in Fig. 1(e), the proposed framework comprises three key stages: generation of the pre mapping and initial estimation, construction of the transformation matrix A, and bidirectional reconstruction of PET and MRI using A. Given a single modality MRI input, two parallel branches are employed. In the first branch, the MRI is processed by an initial estimation network based on FuseUNet to produce an initial PET estimate, denoted as PET′. In the second branch, the MRI is passed through an intensity mapping module composed of only ten learnable parameters, yielding a pre mapped representation MRI′ that stabilizes subsequent linear modeling. Next, both MRI′ and PET′ are partitioned into N three dimensional patches and concatenated according to patch indices to form an N×N block level representation. The diagonal and off diagonal components of this representation are separated and processed by two dedicated transformation matrix generators, each implemented with a FuseUNet backbone. The outputs of these two branches are then combined to construct the full transformation matrix A. During reconstruction, the transformation matrix A is multiplied with the MRI patch representation to obtain the final PET estimate in a single step. Meanwhile, the corresponding MRI reconstruction is achieved by solving the associated linear system using a Gauss iteration scheme, with the initial PET estimate PET′ serving as the initialization.

Implementation Details and Evaluation Metrics. All experiments were implemented using PyTorch 2.1 with CUDA 12.4. Training and evaluation were conducted on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1×10−4. The model was trained for 200 epochs without learning rate decay to maintain optimization stability. Quantitative evaluation was performed voxel wise within the brain mask using PSNR, SSIM, NMSE, NMAE, and NCC. Qualitative assessment was based on visual inspection of synthesized PET images and corresponding error maps across different MRI modalities.

Results

Fig. 2(a) presents qualitative PET generation results from different single-modality MRI inputs, including T1-weighted, T2-weighted, DWI, and FLAIR images, across five representative cases. Visually, PET images synthesized from all modalities successfully capture the global metabolic distribution patterns observed in the ground-truth PET, including cortical uptake heterogeneity and subcortical metabolic contrast. Error maps further illustrate that most reconstruction deviations are spatially localized and remain relatively low in magnitude, without introducing structured artifacts. For each case, images correspond to the same patient scanned at different time points, covering the subacute (left) and postoperative chronic (right) stages. Error maps illustrate voxel wise differences between the synthesized PET and the ground truth PET.

Among the evaluated modalities, PET images generated from T2-weighted MRI exhibit the most consistent visual agreement with the ground truth, showing reduced error intensity in cortical and deep gray matter regions. In contrast, PET synthesis from FLAIR demonstrates comparatively larger deviations, particularly in regions with subtle metabolic gradients, while DWI-based generation shows moderate performance with increased local variability. These observations suggest that modality-specific tissue contrast and signal-to-noise characteristics play an important role in downstream PET estimation quality.

Quantitative results are summarized in Fig2. (b). PET generated from T2-weighted MRI achieves the highest PSNR (30.11), SSIM (0.956), and NCC (0.979), alongside the lowest NMAE (0.184) and NMSE (0.048), indicating superior structural fidelity and intensity consistency. T1-weighted and DWI inputs yield comparable performance with slightly reduced accuracy, whereas FLAIR-based generation shows lower overall metrics, consistent with the qualitative error patterns. Collectively, these results demonstrate that reliable PET synthesis can be achieved from multiple MRI modalities, with T2-weighted MRI providing the most favorable balance between structural preservation and metabolic accuracy.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the feasibility of generating metabolically informative PET images from single modality thick slice MRI across multiple commonly used clinical sequences. By explicitly modeling local MRI to PET relationships within a unified framework, the proposed approach enables stable PET estimation from T1 weighted, T2 weighted, diffusion weighted, and FLAIR inputs without modality specific customization. The ability to synthesize PET from heterogeneous MRI modalities under realistic acquisition constraints highlights the potential of this framework as a practical surrogate for metabolic imaging. Such modality agnostic MRI to PET synthesis may support earlier assessment of cerebral ischemia and broaden access to metabolic information in clinical settings where PET is not routinely available. Future work will explore extending the framework to temporally misaligned or weakly paired MRI PET data, as well as incorporating additional clinical priors to improve robustness across disease stages. Further validation on larger multi center cohorts and prospective clinical studies will be essential to assess the translational potential of the proposed approach in real world diagnostic workflows.
