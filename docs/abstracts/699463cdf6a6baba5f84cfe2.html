Introduction

The neuroanatomy of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) is heterogeneous1, complicated by overlapping influences from normal aging and other confounders. Conventional neuroimaging methods2 often fail to disentangle these effects, posing challenges for early Alzheimer's disease (AD) detection.

Methods

Leveraging T1-weighted MRI data from the Beijing Aging Brain Rejuvenation Initiative (BABRI, n=918) and the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI, n=1293), we developed a conditional variational autoencoder3 (CVAE) framework to systematically separate MCI-specific neuroanatomical alterations from aging-related changes and unknown confounders. The model extracts latent features linked to cognitive impairments and was evaluated for cross-cohort generalizability.

Results

The CVAE-derived MCI-specific features showed strong correlations with deficits in episodic memory, attention, and executive function (p<0.001), revealing atrophy in temporal, frontal, limbic, and cerebellar networks. These associations were robustly replicated across cohorts. Predictive modeling based on these features outperformed whole-brain atrophy (AUC=0.83 vs. 0.74; p<0.001) and CSF biomarkers (AUC=0.83 vs. 0.77; p<0.001) for conversion to AD.

Conclusion

This study establishes a novel neuroimaging framework that effectively isolates disease-specific neurodegeneration from physiological aging, providing an interpretable tool for personalized MCI stratification and advancing mechanistic understanding.
