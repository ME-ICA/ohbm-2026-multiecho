Introduction

The hierarchical models of cortical processing divide the cortex into unimodal regions—characterized by organized topographic maps such as the somatosensory homunculus and visual retinotopy—and multimodal association areas that integrate information across domains.

Recent discoveries challenge this framework. The identification of the somato-cognitive action network (SCAN)1 revealed that primary motor and somatosensory cortices contain inter-effector regions interdigitated with classic body-part representations. These zones exhibit functional connectivity to distributed cognitive control networks, including the cingulo-opercular network (CON), demonstrating that even primary sensorimotor cortex participates in domain-general cognition. This raises a compelling question: might analogous cognitive integration zones exist within early visual cortex?

The early visual areas (V1, V2, V3) classically contain orderly retinotopic maps dedicated to only low-level visual processing. Here, we systematically mapped the functional organization of human early visual cortex using state-of-the-art neuroimaging data and report the discovery of a visuo-cognitive zone (VCZ) that challenges models of strictly hierarchical visual processing.

Methods

We analyzed data from the Human Connectome Project (HCP). The main analyses utilized naturalistic movie-watching 7T fMRI data (1-hour recording, 178 participants, preprocessed with HCP pipelines).2 Using a clustering analysis, V1, V2, and V3 were partitioned into foveal, parafoveal, peripheral, and far-peripheral subregions. Functional connectivity maps for each subregion were characterized through whole-cortex vertex-wise BOLD signal correlations.

Interregional timing structure was assessed using lagged cross-correlation with convex interpolation.3 Event-locked activation was examined at salient movie timepoints (movie-to-rest transitions). Resting-state HCP data (1-hour recording, 178 participants, preprocessed with HCP pipelines) were also analyzed to examine state-dependent functional connectivity patterns.

For structural connectivity, we analyzed HCP diffusion-weighted imaging data preprocessed via the MRtrix pipeline (50 participants).4 Anatomically constrained tractography (ACT) with SIFT2 optimization was performed to weight biologically plausible fibers.

Results

We identified a distinct zone corresponding to the far-periphery of V2/V3—termed the visuo-cognitive zone (VCZ)—that exhibited drastically different functional connectivity compared to the rest of early visual cortex. During naturalistic movie-watching, VCZ showed robust connectivity to SCAN inter-effector regions, the cingulo-opercular (salience) network, and the parietal memory network (PMN). Additionally, VCZ demonstrated strong functional connectivity with subcortical structures implicated in salience processing, including the heads of the caudate and putamen.

Anatomically, VCZ displayed reduced retinotopic representation5 yet high myelin content comparable to adjacent early visual areas, with sharp myelin boundaries at the V3 border—consistent with its position within early sensory cortex.

VCZ responded selectively to salient events in movies, co-activating with the CON network. Latency analysis revealed that PMN exhibited significant negative lag values (leading VCZ), whereas inter-effector and CON regions showed positive lags, with VCZ positioned near zero. This temporal structure suggests that VCZ receives top-down modulations from higher-order regions before relaying information to downstream salience and sensorimotor targets.

During rest, VCZ’s functional connectivity exhibited pronounced state-dependent reorganization: while the connectivity with PMN diminished, the coupling with visual areas (namely LO3 and LIPv) and the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) increased. This pattern implicates a shift toward bottom-up visual processing in the absence of cognitively engaging stimuli. Across participants, increases in functional connectivity to regions activated during movie-watching correlated strongly with decreases in connectivity to regions engaged at rest.

Structural connectivity analyses revealed that ventral VCZ was connected to ipsilateral and contralateral dorsal VCZ and peripheral V1. This pattern underscores the dual nature of VCZ, exhibiting both early visual characteristics (myelin architecture, eccentricity-based structural connectivity) and cognitive functional properties.

Conclusion

The discovery of VCZ and its flexible, state-dependent functional connectivity calls for a re-evaluation of current models of visual cortical organization. Rather than functioning as a strictly retinotopic, bottom-up processing region, the far-peripheral territories of V2/V3 host a specialized interface that flexibly links early sensory representations to higher-order cognitive and action systems.

VCZ simultaneously exhibits: (1) anatomical signatures of early visual cortex, (2) functional signatures of a cognitive hub during naturalistic stimulation (selective responses to salient events, coupling to SCAN, CON, and PMN), and (3) state-dependent connectivity reorganization (cognitive network coupling during movie-watching; visual/oculomotor coupling at rest). Furthermore, latency patterns suggest that VCZ serves as an intermediate stage in the cortical hierarchy and receives top-down modulations.

These findings parallel recent descriptions of interdigitated cognitive representations in primary sensorimotor cortices and suggest a broader organizational principle: cognitive integration zones may be embedded within primary cortical areas, challenging current models of cortical function.
