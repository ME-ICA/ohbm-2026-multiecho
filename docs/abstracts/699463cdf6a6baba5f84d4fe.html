Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) progression involves synaptic dysfunction and network disruptions that often precede gross structural atrophy. Resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) can serve as an important tool for mapping these early functional deficits. While the triple transgenic (TG) 3xTgAD mouse model is widely used to study amyloid and tau pathology, most functional characterizations have relied on cross-sectional designs. In this project, we characterize the functional changes in brain regions using the Brainsync transform for rs-fMRI1, for comparing the functional signal cross-sectionally, between normal controls and TG, as well as longitudinally, at 3 and 6 months in TG subjects. We analyzed an open-access set of rs-fMRI preclinical data in 3xTgAD mice (n=12) and wild-type controls (n=10) collected at 3 and 6 months of age2,3. This interval represents a critical window transitioning from prodromal markers to established amyloid pathology. 

Methods

Subjects and Longitudinal Design

We analyzed open-access data from a cohort of Triple Transgenic Alzheimer’s Disease (3xTgAD) mice (n=12) and age-matched wild-type (WT) controls (n=10)2,3. As part of the previous study, all subjects underwent resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) at two critical developmental time points: 3 months of age (representing prodromal pathology) and 6 months of age (representing established disease in TG population). Imaging was performed on a high-field preclinical MRI scanner with animals lightly anesthetized to maintain sedation while preserving neurovascular coupling. 

Our analysis of this data proceeded as follows. Our rodent preprocessing pipeline was used to perform basic preprocessing of the rs-fMRI data for each subject, which involved brain extraction from T2, temporal filtering (0.005 Hz - 0.1 Hz), and spatial smoothing (FWHM 0.6 mm). We then used Group Brainsync1 across all subjects to generate a group atlas and compared each subject's brain to the atlas using functional distance to the atlas as a test statistic. We used the ranksum test for the comparison and corrected for multiple comparisons using FDR. Next, we compared each subject at 3-month scans to 6-month scans using the Brainsync transform1. The difference statistic was then compared between TG subjects and normal controls using the ranksum test, and FDR correction was applied. 

Results

In the cross-sectional comparison between TG and normal controls at 3 months, differences were observed throughout the brain, with the most prominent differences in the hippocampus, amygdala, cerebellum, and parietal cortex. 

In the longitudinal comparison between 3-month and 6-month rs-fMRI scans, the most pronounced differences were observed in the hippocampus, amygdala, and parietal cortex, indicating functional degeneration over time in these areas. 

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate that functional connectivity in the 3xTgAD mouse undergoes progressive deterioration, specifically affecting memory-related networks. This trajectory mirrors the known trajectory of AD-related degeneration in humans and highlights the utility of longitudinal rs-fMRI in tracking early-stage pathology using TG rodent models.
