Introduction

Epileptic seizures emerge from dynamic fluctuations in cortical excitability, yet the large-scale mechanisms that precede seizure onset remain poorly understood. A central concept in epilepsy research is the balance between excitation and inhibition (E/I), which shapes network stability and influences the brain’s susceptibility to ictal transitions. Recent progress in electrophysiology has highlighted the aperiodic component of the EEG power spectrum as a promising non-invasive marker of E/I balance, offering a window into broad shifts in cortical population activity. Although intracranial studies suggest that epileptic regions may be constrained by inhibitory influences during interictal periods, it remains unclear how E/I dynamics evolve across the pre-ictal transition at the whole-brain level.

Understanding these dynamics is essential because seizure generation reflects not only local hyperexcitability but also large-scale network reconfiguration. Moreover, cortical microstructure and neurotransmitter receptor architecture are increasingly recognized as important constraints on functional dynamics, yet their relationship to pre-ictal E/I balance has not been established. Bridging these scales may help clarify why global compensatory mechanisms fail at seizure onset and may provide novel targets for prediction or intervention.

In this study, we investigated time-resolved changes in E/I balance using high-density EEG and source-reconstructed cortical activity in patients with focal drug-resistant epilepsy. We quantified dynamic fluctuations in the aperiodic exponent prior to seizures and examined their relationship with directional connectivity, cortical thickness, and neurotransmitter receptor distributions. Our goal was to determine whether pre-ictal E/I shifts are focal or widespread, how they shape inter-regional communication, and which structural or neurochemical features modulate these dynamics.

Methods

Participants
We analyzed a cohort of patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy who underwent presurgical evaluation including high-density EEG, clinical video-EEG, and structural MRI. Only individuals with at least one seizure captured during high-density EEG were included. Electroclinical data, imaging findings, and clinical history were reviewed to classify seizure onset zones.

EEG Acquisition and Preprocessing
High-density EEG was recorded with 128 channels at 512 Hz and re-referenced to the vertex. Standard preprocessing included down-sampling, 0.1–45 Hz filtering, epoching, automatic artifact rejection, bad-channel interpolation, and ICA-based removal of non-neural components. For consistency, we extracted 13 min of artifact-free pre-ictal data preceding each seizure, and 450 s of awake resting-state interictal activity. Channels and epochs were visually inspected to ensure quality.

Cortical Source Imaging
T1-weighted MRI scans were processed using FreeSurfer for cortical segmentation and thickness estimation. Individual cortical surfaces and electrode positions were imported into Brainstorm. A three-layer boundary element model was generated, and source activity was reconstructed using a weighted minimum-norm approach. Source estimates were parcellated into 68 Desikan–Killiany regions. Epileptic regions were identified using clinical information, ictal/interictal source localization, and expert evaluation.

Spectral Parameterization and E/I Index
We used the SPRiNT pipeline to estimate time-resolved power spectra in sliding 60-s windows (90% overlap). For each window, power spectra (1–40 Hz) were decomposed into periodic and aperiodic components using the specparam algorithm. The aperiodic exponent—the slope of the 1/f-like component—served as a proxy for E/I balance. This yielded 120 time points in the pre-ictal window and 66 in the resting-state condition. Oscillatory power was also extracted for delta, theta, alpha, beta, and gamma bands.

Effective Connectivity Analysis
Directed connectivity was estimated through spectral Granger causality using a multivariate autoregressive model (order 10). The same sliding-window structure as SPRiNT was applied to obtain dynamic inward and outward connectivity profiles. Connectivity matrices were thresholded proportionally at the 95th percentile to ensure comparable network density across subjects. Time-averaged connectivity metrics were computed separately for pre-ictal and interictal periods.

Structural and Neurochemical Features
Cortical thickness values were obtained from each subject’s FreeSurfer parcellation. Regional neurotransmitter receptor densities (including GABA, glutamate, acetylcholine, opioid, and serotonin systems) were extracted from publicly available PET meta-analytic atlases aligned to the Desikan–Killiany parcellation.

Statistical Analysis
Linear regression was applied to each region’s pre-ictal time series to quantify slopes of the aperiodic exponent and oscillatory power changes. Slope differences were tested between conditions (pre-ictal vs. interictal) and between region types (epileptic vs. non-epileptic). Associations between E/I balance and connectivity were assessed using Spearman correlations with FDR correction. Structure–function relationships were tested by correlating cortical thickness with aperiodic exponent values. Spatial correlations between receptor density maps and the group-level exponent map were evaluated with spin-permutation tests (5000 rotations) to account for spatial autocorrelation.

Results

Pre-ictal E/I Dynamics
Across the 13-min pre-ictal window, the aperiodic exponent showed a robust upward trend, indicating a progressive shift toward reduced cortical excitability. This increase was evident in both epileptic and non-epileptic regions, with no significant slope differences between them. Compared with interictal resting state, pre-ictal slopes were significantly steeper, confirming that the observed dynamics were specific to the seizure-proximal period. Delta and theta power also increased as seizures approached, while alpha, beta, and gamma bands showed no consistent pre-ictal trend.

Effective Connectivity Patterns
Time-dependent changes in directed connectivity did not display consistent monotonic trends. However, when connectivity was averaged across the pre-ictal interval, clear group differences emerged. In the delta band, epileptic regions exhibited a larger number of outward compared with inward connections. Outward connectivity was also significantly higher in epileptic than in non-epileptic regions. Similar topological differences were observed in the theta band. These patterns were absent during the interictal state, suggesting that directionality asymmetries are specific to the pre-ictal transition.

Coupling Between E/I Balance and Connectivity
Higher aperiodic exponent values (reflecting reduced excitability) correlated with a larger number of outward connections in non-epileptic regions during the pre-ictal period. In epileptic regions, the correlation trended in the opposite direction, though this did not survive correction. No significant relationships were found in the interictal condition. These results indicate that pre-ictal shifts in E/I balance are linked to reconfigurations in directional communication, particularly in networks outside the seizure-onset zone.

Structure–Function Relationships
Cortical thickness and pre-ictal aperiodic exponent values showed a positive association in non-epileptic regions, suggesting that structurally thicker cortex supports stronger inhibitory-dominant dynamics. This relationship was absent within epileptic regions, consistent with local microstructural disruption or altered circuit organization. The strength of this structure–function coupling remained stable across the pre-ictal time course.

Neurotransmitter Receptor Correlates
Spatial correlations between neurotransmitter receptor maps and the group-level aperiodic exponent revealed a significant negative relationship with muscarinic cholinergic receptor density. Regions with lower muscarinic density displayed stronger inhibitory-dominant shifts. No other receptor system showed significant associations after spatial permutation correction.

Summary
Together, the results reveal that seizure onset is preceded by a widespread shift toward inhibition-dominated cortical dynamics, accompanied by altered network directionality and region-specific structural and neurochemical associations. These findings support a model in which global compensatory processes attempt—but ultimately fail—to stabilize the system before the ictal transition.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that seizure onset is preceded by a widespread, progressive shift in cortical dynamics consistent with reduced excitability, reflected by a systematic increase in the aperiodic exponent across both epileptic and non-epileptic regions. This large-scale shift indicates that the pre-ictal transition is not confined to the seizure-onset zone but instead involves coordinated changes across the cortex. The simultaneous rise in low-frequency power further suggests that slowing of cortical activity is a hallmark of the approach to seizure.

Pre-ictal directionality analyses revealed that epileptic regions exhibit disproportionately stronger outward connectivity compared to inward connectivity, highlighting their role as drivers within the network during the transition to ictal activity. Importantly, the relationship between E/I balance and connectivity differed across region types: non-epileptic cortex showed tighter coupling between increased inhibition and outward communication, whereas epileptic regions displayed a disrupted or inverted pattern. These divergent relationships point to distinct dynamical regimes that characterize healthy versus pathological tissue during seizure emergence.

Structural and molecular features provided additional context for these network-level dynamics. In non-epileptic regions, thicker cortex was associated with stronger inhibitory-dominant states, suggesting that intact local microarchitecture supports compensatory mechanisms aimed at stabilizing the system. In contrast, the absence of such coupling within epileptic regions likely reflects pathological reorganization or loss of structural integrity. The observed link between muscarinic receptor density and the spatial distribution of the aperiodic exponent further implicates cholinergic neuromodulation in shaping pre-ictal cortical stability.

Together, these findings support a framework in which the brain engages widespread compensatory processes—mediated by inhibitory-dominant states, large-scale low-frequency dynamics, and structural and neurochemical constraints—to delay or prevent seizure onset. The failure of these mechanisms in epileptic regions may constitute the critical tipping point that pushes the system into the ictal state.

By integrating high-density EEG, source modeling, directed connectivity, cortical morphology, and neurotransmitter receptor mapping, this work provides a multi-scale characterization of pre-ictal transitions. The dynamic aperiodic exponent emerges as a promising biomarker for capturing evolving E/I balance and could serve as a candidate feature for future seizure forecasting frameworks or adaptive neuromodulation strategies. Overall, our results highlight the importance of whole-brain approaches to understanding seizure initiation and open new avenues for predictive and therapeutic interventions in focal epilepsy.
