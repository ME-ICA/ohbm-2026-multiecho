Introduction

Naturalistic stimuli are increasingly used to map the functional organization of the developing brain, given their unique potential to capture complex social cognition in ecologically valid contexts while maintaining high participant compliance^1. However, standard analyses often passively map neural responses only to annotated movie events, assuming that participants invariably attend to identical scene content which thus dictates neural processing. This overlooks the active and potentially individual specific role of attention. In the animated film Partly Cloudy, the narrative intertwines two social dimensions: the mental/affective states of a cloud character (Gus) and the physical/somatic states (e.g., pain, action) of a stork character (Peck)^1. We propose that the maturation of these distinct social brain networks is gated by the child's active attentional selection. Specifically, we hypothesize that fixating on the 'affective agent' (Gus) engages mentalizing networks, and we contrast this with the networks activated when attention is focused on the 'somatic agent' (Peck). By integrating simultaneous eye-tracking with data-driven brain state modelling, this study aims to demonstrate how gaze information relates to functional neural dynamics in 4-7-year-old children. This approach is vital for developmental cohorts, where high attentional variability serves as a key driver for individual differences in neural specialisation.


Methods

Participants & Acquisition: Three children (individual ages: 4.62, 4.97, and 6.99 years) were recruited from the Brain Health in Gen2020 Study. Data were acquired on a GE 3T SIGNA™ Premier system with a custom-built MR-compatible eye-tracking camera (12M-I, MRC systems, Heidelberg DE) as shwon in Figure 1A. During fMRI data acquisition, participants watched a naturalistic video composed of four short clips over a total of 10:50 minutes. The current analysis focused on the animated short Partly Cloudy (265s). fMRI data were acquired with a gradient echo EPI sequence with the following parameters: multiband factor (MB=3), TR=1000ms, TE=30ms, voxel size=3mm isotropic.

Preprocessing: Preprocessing was performed with fMRIPrep, including rigid body motion correction and registration to MNI152NLin2009cAsym space. Automatic removal of motion artifacts using independent component analysis (ICA-AROMA) was performed after spatial smoothing with an 4mm FWHM gaussian kernel. Residual white matter and cerebrospinal fluid signals were regressed out using Nilearn. 

Neural State Modelling: We extracted time series from brain regions defined with the Schaefer-200 parcellation (Yeo-7 networks). To capture rapid neural dynamics, we computed frame-wise co-fluctuation matrices for each TR^3. These matrices served as input for a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) analysis. Model stability analysis determined an optimal solution of K=6 states. Three states were identified as artifacts and excluded based on their strong temporal correlation with motion estimates, as assessed by framewise displacement (FD).

Connectivity & Gaze Analysis: To characterize the remaining 3 dynamic states, we derived a representative functional connectivity matrix for each state by averaging the frame-wise co-fluctuation matrices assigned to that state. Simultaneously, we mapped gaze coordinates to dynamic Areas of Interest (AOIs) for Gusface, Peckface, their bodies and other environment objects using automated object detection (YOLO, shown in Figure 1C-D)^4. We calculated a "Gaze Delta" for each state to quantify specific shifts in fixation probability relative to the global baseline.

Results

The HMM analysis parsed the fMRI data into 6 distinct states, visualized as raster plot in Figure 2A. After excluding states linked to motion artifacts, three robust functional states (S1, S2, S6) remained.

State 6 revealed a topology dominated by Ventral Attention/Salience (VAN; Z=0.85) and Limbic (Z=0.88) networks (Figure 2B), suggestive of "bottom-up" capture by emotional saliency. Temporally, this state appeared with higher frequency during narrative climaxes. Gaze analysis confirmed a time-locked preference for the affective character, showing a +9.1% increase in fixation on Gus's face relative to the global baseline (Figure 2C).

In contrast, State 1 showed dominant coupling in Dorsal Attention (DAN; Z=0.61) and Somatomotor (Z=0.75) networks (Figure 2B), reflecting "top-down" visual tracking of physical action. This was accompanied by a specific preference for the character experiencing physical sensation, evidenced by a +2.0% increase in fixation on Peck's body relative to baseline (Figure 2C).

Finally, State 2 served as a baseline viewing mode, characterized by low connectivity strength and non-specific gaze patterns.

Conclusion

This study suggests a double dissociation between neural connectivity and active gaze targets. By utilizing gaze as a decoding tool, we identified that the brain toggles between a "Mental-Affective" mode (State 6, linked to Limbic/DMN) and a "Physical-Somatic" mode (State 1, linked to DAN/SomMot). These findings highlight that social brain specialization is driven by active visual sampling attending to "internal states" (Gus) versus "bodily states" (Peck)—rather than passive scene exposure. This gaze-informed framework resolves ambiguities in content-based analyses, offering a mechanism for how young children actively disentangle the mental and physical aspects of social narratives.
