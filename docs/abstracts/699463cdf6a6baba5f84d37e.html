Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD), resulting from progressive degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc), remains a debilitating movement disorder. Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) is an established intervention, yet clinical outcomes are highly variable, with approximately one quarter of patients experiencing suboptimal motor improvements (<30 %). This variability highlights the critical need for refined patient selection strategies. Current reliance on subjective clinical assessments, such as the MDS-UPDRS and Levodopa responsiveness, limits predictive accuracy. Neuroimaging, offering objective and scalable solutions, is therefore a preferred avenue for biomarker development. The degeneration of Nigrosome-1 within the SNpc is a pathological hallmark of PD1. Imaging techniques, specifically Susceptibility-Weighted Imaging (SWI)2, visualize this structure via the Swallow-Tail Sign (STS)3; the loss of which is a highly accurate diagnostic biomarker. Accuracy improved markedly when combined with neuromelanin-sensitive MRI and T2 mapping (AUC 0.958; sensitivity 97%)4. Advanced modalities like Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) further enable detailed grading of Nigrosome-1 integrity5. Despite these diagnostic advances, the role of Nigrosome-1 integrity in predicting DBS outcomes remains unknown. This study aims to determine whether pre-operative Nigrosome-1 integrity is associated with (1) baseline clinical features and (2) postoperative motor outcomes following DBS in PD patients. Identifying such an association could establish Nigrosome-1 integrity as a critical biomarker, shifting the paradigm toward personalized neuromodulation strategies and optimized therapeutic success.

Methods

This study retrospectively analyzed 70 consecutive patients initially identified as suspected PD and deemed eligible for Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) surgery at the University Health Network (UHN) between February 2022 and February 2024. Following exclusion of two patients subsequently diagnosed with essential tremor, the final cohort comprised 68 confirmed PD patients (adults >=18 years) with comprehensive clinical documentation.

Patient Data and Clinical Assessment Clinical data collected included age, sex, disease duration, levodopa responsiveness, and motor severity. Motor severity was quantified using the Movement Disorder Society – Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale Part III (MDS-UPDRS Part III). Daily dopaminergic medication burden was calculated as the Levodopa Equivalent Daily Dose (LEDD). DBS eligibility and surgical targeting (STN vs. GPi) followed established institutional and published protocols. Postoperative motor severity was assessed at the patient’s optimal clinical state, occurring between 3 and 12 months post-surgery. Symptom laterality analysis focused on total appendicular MDS-UPDRS and tremor subscores contralateral to the DBS implantation side.

MRI Acquisition and Nigrosome Identification MRI was performed using a 3T Siemens Vida scanner. Nigrosome-1 imaging was acquired using an axial 3D multi-echo spoiled gradient echo sequence (voxel size 0.5 x 0.5 x 1 mm; TR = 48.0 ms, TE = 39.00 ms, Flip Angle = 20 degrees). Post-processing involved the creation of a Susceptibility Map-Weighted Image (SMWI) via Quantitative Susceptibility Mapping (QSM) reconstruction, which enhances contrast based on magnetic susceptibility differences.

All MRI scans were independently reviewed by three blinded readers (a board-certified neuroradiologist, a fellow, and a resident). Interrater reliability was calculated between two readers (kappa = 0.88 in an institutional validation cohort). The Swallow-Tail Sign (STS) integrity was assessed using a modified four-point ordinal scale derived from Shams et al. (Grades 1 to 4, excluding Grade 0), where Grade 4 represented fully abnormal findings. Patients were categorized into fully abnormal (Grade = 4) or non-fully abnormal (Grades = 1-3).

Unexpectedly, nine patients exhibited non-fully abnormal nigrosome findings. To investigate this subgroup, an age- and sex-matched control group (n=18) was identified from the fully abnormal cohort. Three patients from the non-fully abnormal group were excluded from the postoperative analysis due to lack of surgery or follow-up data.

Statistical Analysis The Shapiro–Wilk test was used for normality assessment. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test, and categorical variables using the chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test. Descriptive statistics are reported as means +/- standard deviations and counts/percentages.

The primary outcome measure was the difference in scores (Delta), calculated as: Delta = Preoperative Score - Postoperative Score. Positive Delta values indicate clinical improvement. Effect sizes were calculated using Hedges’ g (with 95% CIs) for group and side-specific comparisons. Associations were assessed using Spearman’s correlation coefficients (r) (with 95% CIs). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 (two-tailed), with Benjamini–Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) adjustment applied for multiple testing.

Results

A total of 27 Parkinson's disease (PD) patients eligible for Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) were included in the analysis: 9 in the non-fully abnormal nigrosome group (NFAG: 6 males, 3 females) and 18 age- and sex-matched controls in the fully abnormal nigrosome group (FAG: 12 males, 6 females). The NFAG included one patient with bilaterally normal nigrosomes, three with symmetric Grade 3 findings (probably abnormal), and five with asymmetric abnormalities. All patients in the FAG showed bilaterally abnormal nigrosomes (Grade 4).Preoperative Nigrosome and Baseline Clinical FeaturesWhen comparing the 9 NFAG patients (including a surgical subgroup of 6: 5 STN-DBS, 1 GPi-DBS) to the 18 FAG patients (10 STN-DBS, 8 GPi-DBS), baseline clinical characteristics—including age, sex, disease duration, levodopa responsiveness, and motor severity (MDS-UPDRS Part III)—did not differ significantly between the two groups. Further side-specific motor severity comparisons (contralateral UPDRS and tremor scores) also showed no significant differences, suggesting no apparent moderate-to-large association between preoperative nigrosome integrity and baseline motor symptom severity.Exploring the relationship using the full four-point nigrosome integrity scale (Grades 1 to 4) for each hemisphere also yielded no consistent trends linking greater structural degeneration to more severe contralateral motor symptoms. After statistical correction, only the categorical group difference in total nigrosome grading remained significant. All other clinical comparisons and correlations did not survive correction, underscoring the limited precision and small effect sizes found in relation to baseline impairment. This motivated the primary investigation into postoperative outcomes.Preoperative Nigrosome and Postoperative OutcomesThe 6 NFAG patients who underwent DBS were compared to all 18 FAG patients. The distribution of DBS targets was comparable, as were baseline clinical features and levodopa challenge responses.At the optimal postoperative status, the NFAG demonstrated significantly lower total UPDRS III scores and gait scores compared to the FAG (P < 0.01 for both). Right- and left-sided postoperative UPDRS scores were also significantly lower in the NFAG surgical subgroup compared to the FAG.However, when measuring the relative improvement ($\Delta$ = preoperative score – postoperative score), no significant differences were observed for total UPDRS III or gait. Other clinical parameters, including freezing of gait, speech, tremor severity, and LEDD reduction, also showed no significant group differences. Side-specific improvement on both the right and left sides remained statistically comparable between groups.A detailed analysis using the four-point graded scale revealed a correlation between left-sided nigrosome grade and right-sided postoperative UPDRS scores (Spearman r = 0.50), but the explained variance was low ($R^2 = 0.183$), suggesting minimal predictive value. Critically, this association, along with all other improvement score comparisons and correlations, did not survive statistical correction.Collectively, these postoperative analyses indicated that preoperative nigrosome integrity—whether classified categorically or on a graded scale—did not demonstrate a moderate-to-large effect in predicting side-specific motor improvements or overall DBS benefit. The therapeutic gain of DBS was not significantly influenced by the degree of preoperative nigrosome integrity. The minimal clinical utility of nigrosome grading as a predictive biomarker in this context is highlighted by the negligible effect sizes and limited explanatory power observed.

Conclusion

While nigrosome-1 integrity assessed via 3T SMWI MRI remains diagnostically informative for PD, no moderate-to-large associations were observed with baseline clinical features or postoperative DBS outcomes in this small and heterogeneous cohort. Although small effects cannot be excluded, such effects are generally difficult to translate into meaningful clinical benefits. These findings suggest that nigral imaging may have limited predictive value as a standalone biomarker and should instead be interpreted within the context of broader clinical and imaging measures. Future efforts should focus on validating these observations in larger, prospective cohorts and integrating nigrosome assessment into multimodal biomarker frameworks to support individualized DBS planning.
