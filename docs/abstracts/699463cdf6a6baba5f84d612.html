Introduction

Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB) is the second most common neurodegenerative disorder in adults aged over 65 years. It is associated with poor disease outcomes and substantial economic burden. Diagnostic accuracy for DLB is low due to disease heterogeneity and overlap with other dementias despite the well-developed diagnostic criteria. This requires accurate diagnostic tools that are non-invasive, accessible and cost-effective. While structural MRI might not show a signature pattern for DLB, incorporating structural MRI features in machine learning (ML) based models could improve the diagnostic accuracy and assist clinicians. We develop ML-based classifiers to differentiate DLB from healthy controls (HC) and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) using features extracted from structural MRI in a large, multi-centre dataset.

Methods

We included T1-weighted MRI scans of  201 DLB, 120 AD, and 244 age- and sex-matched HC from the European DLB consortium (E-DLB), National Alzheimer’s Coordinating Center (NACC), Neuroimaging of Inflammation in Memory and Related Other Disorders (NIMROD), Multimodal Imaging in Lewy Body Disorders (MILOS), and AMyloid imaging for Phenotyping LEwy body dementia (AMPLE) cohorts. We segmented the cortical gray matter and white matter using Freesurfer version 7.3.4 and computed FD from the Freesurfer outputs using the fractalbrain toolkit. These structural MRI features were corrected for age and sex, rescaled, and split as 80:20 for training and test datasets. For ML modelling, we employed Logistic regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), and Support Vector Machine (SVM) with Lasso regularization for feature selection to differentiate DLB from HC and AD, and report their evaluation metrics on test datasets.

Results

For DLB vs HC classifiers, we achieved the highest accuracy of 83% using Random Forest, followed by 82% accuracy with Logistic regression, and 79% accuracy with Support Vector Machine. For DLB vs AD classifiers, we achieved the highest accuracy of 77% using Logistic regression, followed by 75% using Random Forest, and 74% accuracy with Support Vector Machine. Conclusion

ML-based models using T1 MRI features show reasonable performance in diagnosing DLB. Incorporation of multimodal MRI features and applying deep learning may further enhance these classifiers for implementation in clinical settings.
