Introduction

Laminar fMRI enables investigation of cortical computation at the circuit and layer level in both health and disease. Reliable layer-specific activation requires sub-millimetre resolution, achievable at 7T using 3D Echo Planar Imaging (3D-EPI). However, EPI is highly susceptible to geometric distortions, which are exacerbated at ultra-high field strengths, complicating accurate layer definition.

Distortion correction approaches fall into two broad categories: phase-based methods that compute field maps from MRI phase to derive Voxel Displacement Maps (VDMs)1, and magnitude-based reversed-polarity approaches such as FSL’s TOPUP and SPM’s SCOPE2. In high-resolution 7T fMRI reversed-polarity methods have been found to outperform field maps when benchmarked against distortion-free MP2RAGE structural scans3. However, these comparisons used 3D EPI with reduced distortions achieved via segmented readouts, which reduce temporal resolution and limit experimental paradigms.

Here we show that, for highly distorted non-segmented 3D-EPI, both TOPUP and SCOPE can leave residual distortions, and in some regions exacerbate them. We therefore introduce SCOPE-PI, a hybrid strategy that combines the complementary strengths of the phase and magnitude components available directly from the EPI data. Phase-based unwarping corrects large distortions, while subsequent reversed-polarity correction eliminates the residual smaller-scale errors arising from phase offsets or phase unwrapping inaccuracies. Additional dynamic changes in distortions across an fMRI time series can then be modelled using SPM’s realign & unwarp framework4.

Methods

A single volunteer was scanned at 7T (Siemens Healthineers) using an 8Tx/32Rx head coil (Nova Medical) under local ethics approval. High-resolution whole-brain magnetisation-transfer–prepared 3D-EPI3, which provides good grey-white matter contrast, was acquired to evaluate distortion-correction accuracy for TOPUP, SCOPE, and SCOPE-PI. Additionally, slab-selective high-resolution BOLD-weighted fMRI time series were acquired under: i) no motion, ii) breath-hold / free breathing, and iii) slow head drift conditions. The efficacy of SCOPE and SCOPE-PI were assessed for each dataset. A structural MP2RAGE served as the distortion-free reference.

Acquisition parameters:

Whole-brain 3D-EPI:

0.8mm isotropic; off-resonant magnetization transfer pre-pulse preceded each excitation, effective echo spacing 0.25ms; TE=26ms; FoV=192×192×128mm³; volume TR=100ms; volume acquisition time=1min 20s. No in-plane acceleration, but 4xin-plane segmentation to match geometric distortions to the subsequent fMRI runs. One posterior-anterior (PA) phase-encoded and one reversed-polarity (AP) volume were acquired.

Slab-selective 3D-EPI fMRI: 

0.8mm isotropic; effective echo spacing 0.25ms; TE=26ms; FoV=192×192×76.8mm³; TR=68ms; volume acquisition time = 3.264s; 4×in-plane and 2×through-plane acceleration. Four PA volumes were acquired followed by eighty AP volumes.

MP2RAGE: 

0.65m isotropic; FoV=220×220×156mm³; TR=5000ms; inversion times 900/2750ms; 3×in-plane acceleration.

All EPI data (whole-brain and fMRI runs) were reconstructed using MORSE-PI, an open-source image reconstruction yielding high-quality magnitude and phase images from accelerated 3D EPI5. 

Distortion-correction pipelines:

TOPUP was called via FSL commands. The remaining post-processing steps were implemented in MATLAB using the SPM 25 release. Whole-brain EPI data were processed using pipelines 2-4; fMRI time series were analysed using pipelines 1, 3, and 4.

  1. Control (motion correction only): SPM’s realign (estimate&reslice) was run on fMRI time series with default parameters except for smoothing, which was adapted to FWHM = 5 voxels given the sub-millimetre resolution.
  2. TOPUP: Applied to the PA-AP whole brain EPI volume pair using the standard topup + applytopup FSL routine.
  3. SCOPE: VDM estimated using SPM SCOPE, motion and distortion jointly corrected with realign&unwarp using the SCOPE-derived VDM for unwarping, again with smoothing FWHM=5 voxels.
  4. SCOPE-PI: EPI phase from whole brain PA-AP pair or eight fMRI volumes (4 PA and 4 AP) was unwrapped using ROMEO, masked, and extrapolated outside the brain. A phase-based VDM for each volume was computed as:

VDM(r,volume) = φ(r, volume)×total_EPI_readout_time / (2×π×TE)

Where φ is the volume-specific EPI phase at voxel r. Gross distortions in the EPI magnitude were corrected by applying these VDMs using the spm_bsplins.m function with 4th-order B-spline interpolation.

Residual displacements were estimated by applying SCOPE to the phase-corrected EPI magnitude data. A final hybrid VDM was formed by combining the SCOPE-PI and SCOPE VDMs. For fMRI, the average of four AP phase-based VDMs was used in the summation. This hybrid VDM was applied to the original EPI time series using SPM realign&unwarp, as for pipeline #3.

Evaluation metrics.

Distortion correction accuracy was assessed by overlaying MP2RAGE-derived grey matter boundaries on the corrected EPIs after coregistration of the MP2RAGE to the EPI space – see Figure 1. To further quantify anatomical correspondence, the Correlation Ratio (CR) between either uncorrected, SCOPE or SCOPE-PI corrected EPIs and the MP2RAGE UNI was computed. For fMRI, CR was computed using the mean EPI after realign&unwarp. Temporal SNR (tSNR) was also calculated, voxel-wise, in grey matter for each fMRI run. Both tSNR and CR were calculated in two regions of interest: frontal cortex and deep grey matter (where prominent discrepancies in distortion correction were observed) and in the whole brain/slab.

Results

Figure 1 shows qualitative comparisons of different distortion correction methods for the whole-brain 3D-EPI. All methods improved alignment relative to the uncorrected data. TOPUP and SCOPE performed well for moderate distortions (i.e. regions with 4–6 mm VDM shifts) but showed clear inaccuracies in regions with severe distortions (i.e. regions with 8–10 mm VDM shifts). TOPUP introduced cortical blurring (see yellow circle) and “ventricle shadowing” (see yellow arrows in TOPUP column) consistent with previous reports1. SCOPE partially corrected ventricle displacement but in several places over-corrected frontal cortex boundaries (see red arrows in SCOPE column). These SCOPE-specific artefacts were also observed in all fMRI time series. SCOPE-PI produced the closest correspondence to the structural anatomy, without noticeable blurring, for both whole-brain and fMRI data.

Figure 2 summarises CR and tSNR metrics. Across all datasets and regions of interest, SCOPE-PI consistently achieved higher CR values than both motion-correction-only and SCOPE cases, indicating more accurate anatomical alignment. Improvements in tSNR were modest and less consistent, though both SCOPE and SCOPE-PI slightly increased mean whole-brain/slab grey-matter tSNR relative to the motion-correction-only case. This was particularly the case for the intentional head drift fMRI time series, where slow rotation about the left–right axis progressed from 0 to −6° and then reversed to +4°.

Conclusion

We demonstrated that SCOPE-PI, which combines phase-based removal of gross distortions with magnitude-based correction of residual errors, provides accurate and robust unwarping for highly distorted high-resolution 7T 3D-EPI. In scenarios where TOPUP and SCOPE were unable to fully remove distortions—and in some cases introduced artefacts such as ventricle shadowing or overstretched cortical boundaries—SCOPE-PI produced anatomically faithful results.

In SCOPE-PI, phase-based unwarping captures large susceptibility-induced shifts, while the subsequent reversed-polarity step corrects smaller-scale residual distortions arising from phase offsets or unwrapping imperfections. Although this second step could in principle be performed with TOPUP, using SCOPE allows direct integration with SPM’s realign&unwarp framework to model dynamic field changes while minimising software dependencies.

Like TOPUP and SCOPE, SCOPE-PI relies on blip-reversed EPI volumes but additionally requires high-quality EPI phase, which is now increasingly accessible with modern reconstruction techniques. In this study we used MORSE-PI, which provides high-quality magnitude and phase images even for highly accelerated 3D-EPI acquisitions. Importantly, SCOPE-PI does not require the EPI phase to contain only the echo-time-dependent field inhomogeneity (i.e. ΔB0) term; instead, it assumes that residual TE-independent phase offsets—arising from transmit/receive field modulations, gradient delays, or eddy currents—remain small relative to the ΔB0-induced phase evolution, which holds valid at the long echo times typically used for BOLD fMRI. This assumption was validated here: the phase-based VDM correction reduced distortions relative to the uncorrected images. Moreover, because the residual phase offsets introduce distortions in opposite direction in AP and PA acquisitions, they are effectively eliminated by the SCOPE correction. This distinguishes SCOPE-PI from previously published distortion-correction methods using EPI phase1, where additional pre-scans are involved to remove residual phase offsets. Additionally, SCOPE-PI builds on the robustness of the reversed-polarity approach, as previously demonstrated in high-resolution fMRI3.

SCOPE-PI leverages the same phase information used in functional quantitative susceptibility mapping (fQSM) and in thermal denoising approaches such as NORDIC, and can be applied alongside these techniques. It can be particularly beneficial for high-resolution fMRI studies that demand precise correspondence between functional and structural data, including cortical layer analyses and the localisation of small brainstem nuclei (e.g., the locus coeruleus). Future work will focus on integrating SCOPE-PI into the SPM graphical interface to provide a streamlined and user-friendly workflow.
