Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) profoundly alters how functional dynamics are constrained by the brain’s anatomy. However, little is known about how this structure-function coupling evolves during the preclinical stage. Although abnormalities in delta have been reported in early stages of AD and fMRI studies have revealed early DMN disconnection, the relationship between delta functional dynamics, as measured by EEG, and their integration with the structural connectivity underlying white matter architecture in the preclinical phase, remains poorly understood1. 

A multilayer modelling offers a powerful framework to characterize how distinct modalities interact within a unified connectome2. By applying a two-layer multiplex network model integrating white matter tract integrity (DWI) and electroencephalography (EEG), we examined the structure-function coupling in preclinical AD. The multiplex participation coefficient (PC) quantifies the similarity between structural and functional connectivity and reflect reduced network flexibility or pathology over alignment. 

In this study, we specifically investigated whether multilayer structure-function coupling in DMN could distinguish amyloid-positive progressors from non-progressors and if it predicts long-term cognitive trajectories over 5 years. We hypothesized that pathological aging would manifest as abnormally increased structure-function similarity in delta frequency band within the DMN, reflecting early loss of network flexibility.

Methods

We analysed 66 cognitively unimpaired older adults aged 70-85 years with subjective cognitive complaint for at least six months from the INSIGHT-preAD. The participants were assigned to three groups according to their amyloid status and clinical progression over time: Aβ-/controls (participants without amyloid deposition), Aβ+/stable (participants with amyloid burden who remained cognitively stable) and Aβ+/progressors (participants with amyloid burden who later progressed to AD)3.

In this study, the MMSE for global cognition and the FCSRT for episodic memory were the main focus of the cognitive abilities assessed as well as the EEG and the DTI for neuroimaging data. 

The resting state EEG (rsEEG) was acquired with a 256-channel system. The pre-processing steps included filtering, bad-channel rejection, ICA artifact removal and average referencing. Source reconstruction was performed using BEM forward model and sLORETA inverse solution. Functional connectivity was computed using the weighted Phase Lag Index (wPLI) across canonical frequency bands and 148 regions matrices was obtained using the Destrieux atlas brain parcellation through Brainstorm4. The DWI pre-processing included participants’ motion correction, tensor estimation and deterministic DWI tractography using ExploreDTI. Structural connectivity matrices were defined as fractional anisotropy-weighted matrices using the same parcellation (148 regions).

Afterwards, a two-layer multiplex (PLI functional layer + FA structural layer) was constructed and the multilayer participation coefficient (PC) indexing similarity between layers per region was extracted. The DMN nodes were identified using the Destrieux (Figure 1).

Finally, variation rate or TVr was calculated to assess the participants’ longitudinal cognitive trajectory for the global cognition (MMSE) and the episodic memory (FCSRT). The TVr is the percentage difference between the score at the participant’s last follow-up session (e.g., follow up to 48 months (M42) or 60 months (M60)) and their baseline (i.e M0). This difference was then divided by the score at M0 and multiplied by 100. (e.g., (MMSE M60 – MMSE M0) / MMSE M0) * 100). Group comparisons to assess the level of PC within the DMN among the participants used ANCOVA models adjusting for age, sex and education level. Pearson’s correlation was used to assess the link between the PC within the DMN and the cognition. All statistical tests were conducted using JASP software and R-studio. Results were FDR corrected for multiple comparisons.

Results

Group analyses revealed a significant effect of amyloid status and clinical progression on multilayer PC. Specifically, Aβ+/progressors displayed an increase of structure-function similarity within the DMN and for the delta frequency band only (p=0.005) compared to the other two groups (Figure 2). This pattern was absent in Aβ-/controls and Aβ+/stable. At a network level, only the DMN exhibited the strongest deviation from normal structure-function organisation, showing reduced differentiation between modalities. That is consistent with reduced dynamic flexibility and impaired network segregation.

When correlated with the cognition especially for the episodic memory, higher whole brain PC within the delta band frequency is negatively correlated to cognition (r=-0.371, p=0.002). A similar negative association was observed when restricting the PC correlation to the DMN network (r=-0.358, p=0.003). Notably, among individual DMN areas, only the left dorsal PCC showed a significant correlation with the FCSRT_TR (Figure 2). The correlation also highlighted that this relationship was mainly driven by the stronger decline in Aβ+/progressors participants relative to the other groups. Within group correlations were non-significant, indicating that the relationship between the PC and cognition reflects a pathological mechanism rather that interindividual variability. 

To test the specificity of our results, other frequency band and resting state network such as the Salience Network (SN) were analysed. They yielded no significant results, thus confirming the specificity of our results in the DMN for the delta frequency band.

Conclusion

Our main finding was that individuals with high amyloid burden who progressed to AD exhibited significantly higher multiplex connectivity PC in the delta frequency band within the DMN compared to controls and stable amyloid positive individuals over the follow-up period. This increase in structure-function similarity in the DMN was associated with subsequent memory decline, as measured by the FCSRT performance, particularly with regard to consolidation which occurs when structure-function similarity is high in both the DMN and the global brain. Notably, this effect within the DMN was driven primarily by the left dorsal posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), which emerged as the only DMN regions with a significant association with episodic memory decline5. 

Our study highlights the importance of a multimodal approach to distinguishing between healthy and pathological aging. Combining EEG and DWI to examine multilayer brain networks provide valuable insight into early network level changes that precede clinical symptoms and can distinguish amyloid positive progressors several years before clinical conversion. The specificity of this approach to the DMN, which is associated with cognition, may point at a core vulnerability pathway and could even hold a prognostic value for individuals at risk of AD, potentially serving as a biomarker on its own. 

Beyond its neuroscientific relevance, multilayer modelling provides a promising biomarker framework for large scale screening, risk stratification and personalised monitoring in aging populations.

By integrating fast neural dynamics structural architecture, multilayer connectome offers new avenues for understanding pathological aging and may serve as a tool for identifying individuals most likely to benefit from early intervention.
