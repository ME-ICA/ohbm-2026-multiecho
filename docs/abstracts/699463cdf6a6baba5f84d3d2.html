Introduction

Fetal diffusion MRI (dMRI) is a powerful non-invasive technique for characterizing white matter maturation and cortical development in utero. However, clinical fetal dMRI acquisition and processing remain highly challenging due to unpredictable motion, low signal-to-noise ratio, and severe artifacts.¹ While recent state-of-the-art pipelines have advanced fetal dMRI reconstruction, they are optimized for research-grade data acquired with specialized protocols (e.g., multi-shell, multi-phase-encoding) that are not feasible in clinical routine. Clinical datasets are inherently heterogeneous, typically limited to a single phase-encoding direction and short acquisition times, creating a gap between advanced research tools and their translational applicability. To address this, we introduce an automated, open-source pipeline specifically designed to robustly process heterogeneous clinical fetal dMRI, complemented by an extended set of anatomical parcellations enabling detailed regional analysis.

Methods

We analyzed data from the MarsFet cohort, a retrospective collection of 314 clinical fetal dMRI sessions acquired between 2008 and 2021 on 1.5T and 3T Siemens systems at La Timone University Hospital (Marseille, France). Ethical approval was obtained from the local committee (N°2022-04-14-003). Diffusion protocols varied substantially, with 6, 12, or 30 directions (b=1000 s/mm²) at various resolutions and without reverse phase encoding. The proposed framework integrates established tools from FSL, MRtrix3, and ANTs as illustrated in Figure 1. Preprocessing included MP-PCA denoising and a deep learning–based fetal brain extraction network.² Motion and eddy-current artifacts were corrected using FSL Eddy with slice-to-volume correction tuned for fetal data.³ To address arbitrary fetal head orientation, b-vectors were reoriented via registration to the spatiotemporal dHCP fetal atlas.⁴ Diffusion tensors were fitted in the subject’s native space using MRtrix3 to extract standard microstructural metrics, including Fractional Anisotropy (FA) and Mean Diffusivity (MD). To enable fine-grained anatomical analysis, we generated an extended parcellation of the dHCP atlas by propagating cortical and subcortical regions from multiple fetal and neonatal templates. These labels were warped to individual subjects for region-wise quantification.

Results

The automated pipeline successfully processed all 314 datasets without manual intervention. After visual quality control to assess the goodness of reconstruction, 152 sessions were retained for analysis. The resulting tensor-derived metrics exhibited developmental trajectories consistent with established literature.⁵ Age-related FA and MD changes in the cortical plate and corpus callosum isthmus (Figure 2) matched expected developmental patterns. While absolute metric values differed systematically between 1.5T and 3T acquisitions, developmental slopes were highly consistent across scanners, demonstrating the pipeline’s robustness to heterogeneous acquisition protocols.

Conclusion

We present a robust, automated processing pipeline tailored to the specific challenges of clinically acquired fetal dMRI. By integrating motion correction, anatomical standardization, and diffusion modeling within a unified framework, the method handles heterogeneous datasets that lack the redundancy of research-grade protocols. The incorporation of an extended parcellation scheme for the dHCP fetal atlas further enhances its utility for region-specific developmental analyses. The successful application to a large retrospective cohort demonstrates the pipeline's potential to exploit currently underutilized clinical datasets for investigating neurodevelopmental trajectories and disorders. The code is available at https://github.com/MecaLab/Fetal_DWI.
