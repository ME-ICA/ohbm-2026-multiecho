Introduction

The last trimester of pregnancy is critical for cerebral growth and maturation. When birth occurs prematurely, crucial stages take place in the intensive care environment, characterized by high sensory and noxious stimulations, leading to altered brain development. Consequently, prematurity is a major cause of neurodevelopmental disorders, and it has been associated with impairments  or delays in the acquisition of wide-ranging functions including sensorimotor skills, academic abilities or language 1. These deficiencies are paralleled with alterations of functional cortical networks, including weaker functional connectivity (FC) between the sensorimotor network and the visual and dorsal attention networks, within the sensorimotor network and within the central executive network, but stronger FC between the sensorimotor network and parts of the salience and the central executive network 2. 

While behavioral testing revealed impaired learning abilities in PT children, the neural correlates sustaining the acquisition of new behaviors has only been weakly studied in this population. In the context of motor learning, PT children tend to show slower rates or to make more errors than FT counterparts. Other results reveal that PT adults reach performance levels comparable to FT controls, but display greater activity in the right cerebellum and the lingual, parahippocampal and middle temporal gyri3. This suggests that premature birth does not necessarily lead to permanent deficits, but rather to altered and potentially less efficient cortical developmental routes.

In this work, we propose to leverage a framework for dynamic FC and to apply it to continuous data acquired during the practice of a visuomotor task to identify large-scale brain networks and their differential contribution to behavioral adaptations in school-aged children born either at term or prematurely. 

Methods

12 preterm children born before 32 weeks of gestational age (PT;  mean age (SD) 9.56 (1.94),  5 girls, 7 boys) and 17 children born full-term (FT; mean age (SD) 9.00 (1.54), 9 girls, 8 boys) practiced a center-out reaching task with a rotation of the visual feedback (96 trials) while lying in a Siemens 3T Magnetom Prisma scanner. Structural T1-weighted images were acquired with the following MPRAGE sequence: voxel size = 1 mm3; repetition time (TR) = 2,300 ms; echo time (TE) = 2.25 ms; inversion time (TI) = 900 ms; flip angle (FA) = 8°; and  field of view (Fov) = 256 mm. T2*-weighted functional images were acquired with the following EPI sequence: 48 slices; voxel size = 3 x 3 x 2.5 mm3; TR = 800 ms; TE = 30 ms; FA = 52°, Fov = 192 mm. 

The functional data were realigned, corrected for distortions and coregistered to the structural image using fmriprep version 24.0.0. Motion parameters, average grey matter and cerebrospinal fluid signals were regressed out and the images were warped to template space using a study-specific SHOOT template. Lastly, ICA-AROMA (ICA-based Automatic Removal Of Motion Artifacts) was applied to remove remaining motion-related components, and the data was smoothed with a 6mm isotropic kernel.

Large-scale brain networks were extracted using the innovation-driven coactivation pattern analysis (iCAPs) framework 4 to the aggregated data from all participants. This framework extracts spatial components (iCAPs) constituted of regions with similar (de)activation patterns, and reconstructs the possibly temporally overlapping time courses of these components. iCAPs were considered (de)active when their z-scored time course exceeded a threshold of |1|.

Each trial was characterized by four metrics: initial angular error, movement time, path length to target (tortuosity) and movement smoothness computed the spectral arc length (SAL). Learning curves were determined for each metric through power law fitting for each metric, yielding a learning rate (LR) and an estimated final score at the end of task practice (yEnd).  Multivariate correlation between the iCAPs’ temporal characteristics and the multidimensional learning rate and final performance was assessed using partial least squares correlation (PLSC). Demographic characteristics including gender, video game habits, and general visuospatial abilities (VSI) were also included as covariates. 

Results

The clustering analysis revealed three large-scale networks: iCAP1-visual comprising occipital and cerebellar regions, iCAP2-somatomotor including left-lateralized centro-temporal areas and iCAP3-prefrontal covering the prefrontal cortex.

PLSC identified one significant latent component (p=0.0002) with a behavioral component generally signaling better learning abilities, encompassing lower final angular error, tortuosity, movement time and movement smoothness, along with faster angular error LR, but lower movement time LR. Age, average time of action game playing and general visuospatial abilities positively related with the behavioral scores. Associations with underlying network dynamics differed between groups. FT showed significant negative contributions for the overall occurrence of iCAP, both in active (iCAP1+) and deactive (iCAP1-) states, iCAP2+, and iCAP3-, as well as the coupling of iCAP1- with iCAP2- and iCAP3-. For PT, in turn, network temporal metrics negatively affecting learning included overall occurrence of iCAP2+/-, iCAP3+/-, coupling of iCAP1+ and iCAP3+, and anticoupling of iCAP3 with iCAPs 1 and 2. 

No between group behavioral differences were significant after correction for multiple comparisons. 

Conclusion

Using a dynamic connectivity and multivariate statistical frameworks to evaluate associations between brain activity and visuomotor learning abilities in FT and PT children, we identified three major networks, associated with visual, sensorimotor and higher cognitive functions respectively, in line with previous studies on visuomotor learning 5.  The dynamic nature of the iCAPs framework and the retrieved temporal characteristics of the iCAPs highlight the importance of the time-varying interplay of these networks for efficient learning and reveal that a common behavioral latent component is sustained by group-specific brain activity patterns. Improved learning in FT children was driven by the dynamics of the visual network. The negative effect of iCAP1 occurrence suggests a lower reliance on visual feedback and therefore indicates overall faster integration of the visuomotor mapping. Instead, its joint deactivation with any of the other two networks might prevent integration of the visual transformation into appropriate motor output. In contrast, in PT participants, occurrence and coupling of the frontal network, particularly with the visual network appear to play a central role in visuomotor learning, complemented by the occurrence of the sensorimotor network. Previous works demonstrated a learning-induced release of cognitive control centers, yet without assessing directional causality 5. In these participants, the deleterious effect of the iCAP1+ - iCAP3+ coupling suggests a stronger reliance on higher cognitive processes to manipulate the visual input and decide on the appropriate motor response, while the disadvantageous anticorrelation of iCAP3 with the sensorimotor and visual networks could prevent integration of the visuomotor mapping, particularly in the early trials. 

These results indicate differential information processing in PT children, with a stronger contribution of frontal network temporal characteristics to learning outcomes than for their FT counterparts. This variation could be imputed to the perinatal hardships experienced by very preterm newborns and their effect on brain development. Regions with later maturation, such as the higher-order frontal areas are particularly vulnerable to the early exposure to extra-uterine environment and consequently to display modified integration in the global cerebral functional network.
