Introduction

Altered auditory processing in tinnitus has been linked to changes in auditory cortical excitability and deviance processing ^1. In prior work, we showed that bilateral HD-tRNS over the auditory cortex combined with acoustic stimulation (AS) can modulate auditory evoked responses and reduce objective tinnitus loudness in a controlled within-subject crossover design ^2.

Here, we extend this approach to a multi-session, double-blind, sham-controlled crossover protocol targeting bilateral primary auditory cortex (Heschl’s gyrus) with HD-tRNS, with or without acoustic stimulation. We test whether repeated bimodal stimulation produces measurable changes in tinnitus loudness and whether these changes are accompanied by modulation of ERP components in an auditory oddball paradigm. As hypothesized, the bimodal condition appears to produce larger reductions in tinnitus loudness than in unimodal HD-tRNS. Across both study arms, neurophysiological changes were found in the active compared with the sham conditions. 

This suggests that neuromodulation can modify auditory cortical excitability and influence tinnitus loudness. 

Methods

This study uses a double-blind, randomized, sham-controlled crossover design with two study arms. 35 Participants with chronic tinnitus (18–75 years) complete 20 stimulation visits plus additional assessment visits. Participants receive 10 sham and 10 active stimulation sessions (every 1- 3 days) with 4-5 weeks of no stimulation in between. 

The control study arm receives only tRNS with no acoustic stimulation whereas the experimental study arm receives the bimodal stimulation protocol with tRNS and AS.

HD-tRNS

High-definition tRNS is applied using the Soterix Medical MxN-33 device (Soterix Medical, New York, US) delivered bilaterally over Heschl’s gyrus using a 17-electrode montage. Stimulation is applied for 20 minutes with 30 s ramp-up and ramp-down. The random-noise waveform is band-limited to 0.1–100 Hz, with a maximal intensity of 0.78 mA per single electrode. The sham condition consists of a 30s ramp-up and ramp-down with no current flow in between. 

Auditory stimulation (AS)

In the bimodal study arm, participants receive continuous white noise presented at 10 dB above the individual minimal masking level (MML) via in-ear headphones combined with the tRNS stimulation.  

The sham condition applies deep pure tones (100–200 Hz) which are presented with randomized timing. 

EEG/ERP recording and preprocessing

EEG is acquired with a 64-channel system (10–20 layout), sampled at 2500 Hz with online reference at FCz and impedances kept below 20 kΩ. Data are low-pass filtered and then bandpass filtered (0.1–30 Hz). Ocular artifacts are removed using ICA. Epochs are extracted from −200 to 500 ms relative to stimulus onset with baseline correction (−200 to 0 ms). Trials with excessive peak-to-peak amplitude are rejected, followed by manual quality control.

Outcomes

Behavioral outcomes include tinnitus loudness and distress ratings (VAS) and MML. Neurophysiological outcomes include ERP component measures derived from the auditory oddball paradigm.

Results

MML & VAS

Participants who received bimodal stimulation (Group E; tRNS + AS) showed a significant reduction in tinnitus loudness after 10 stimulation sessions , both subjectively (VAS Loudness: paired-samples t-test, two-tailed, t(17)=2.96, p=.008; N=18) and psychoacoustically (MML: paired-samples t-test, two-tailed, t(17)=3.151, p=.006; N=18), indicating changes in auditory perception. 

The control/unimodal group (Group C; tRNS only) did not show any improvements in the active condition.

Smaller improvements were observed in some sham conditions: sham unimodal (Group C) MML (paired-samples t-test, t(16)=2.35, p=.031; N=17) and sham bimodal (Group E) VAS Loudness (paired-samples t-test, t(17)=2.46, p=.02; N=18). Tinnitus distress remained unchanged.

ERP

Using an auditory oddball paradigm, we observed significant pre–post changes at Cz for the loudness-up deviant in both Group C (unimodal tRNS) and Group E (bimodal tRNS+AS). Specifically, the N200 amplitude shifted after 10 sessions in Group C (N=17; mean −0.79 → −0.27; t=−2.97, p=.009) and in Group E (N=18; mean −0.47 → −0.18; t=−2.64, p=.017). No such effects have been detected in the sham stimulation. 

This suggests that neuromodulation altered auditory cortical excitability and stimulus evaluation processes. 

These findings indicate that the stimulation protocol influenced neuroplastic processes within auditory and fronto-parietal networks, improving the efficiency of auditory processing rather than mere sensory gain.

All results above are from a preliminary/interim analysis and should be interpreted as exploratory until the full dataset and corrected models are completed.

Conclusion

In this preliminary multi-session, double-blind crossover study, HD-tRNS targeting bilateral Heschl’s gyrus produces measurable changes in tinnitus-related outcomes and auditory ERP markers. Consistent with our prior single-session work, pairing HD-tRNS with concurrent acoustic stimulation is associated with a stronger behavioral effect: the bimodal group shows significant reductions in tinnitus loudness after 10 sessions on both VAS and MML, while the unimodal control arm does not improve in the active condition. At the neurophysiological level, both stimulation arms show N200 modulation to a loudness-up deviant at Cz, with no comparable effects in sham, supporting stimulation-related changes in auditory deviance processing rather than nonspecific effects. 

Future analyses will expand the EEG outcomes to include Mismatched Negativity (MMN), additional deviant types and a broader set of channels/regions. Ongoing analyses across the full crossover dataset will test the specificity and durability of bimodal vs unimodal effects and clarify how ERP changes relate to behavioral loudness measures. The completed analysis will be thoroughly presented at the conference.
