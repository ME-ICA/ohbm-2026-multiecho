Introduction

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a prevalent neurodevelopmental disorder. While brain network abnormalities are implicated, findings regarding structural connectivity (SC) from diffusion MRI remain inconsistent. White matter development extends well into adulthood, coinciding with the peak onset and dynamic symptomatic changes of ADHD. However, how SC trajectories differ between ADHD and typically developing (TD) children, and whether these deviations evolve with age, remains unclear. We leveraged the large-scale longitudinal Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) dataset to investigate spatiotemporal SC development patterns in ADHD, focusing on trajectory shapes and the timing of deviations.

Methods

As shown in Figure 1a, we utilized the longitudinal ABCD study, including 1,114 children with ADHD and 6,689 TD controls. Structural connectomes were reconstructed using dMRI and T1-weighted data (Figure 1b). Nodes were defined by the Schaefer-400 atlas, mapped to 15 functional networks and sorted by the Sensorimotor-Association (S-A) cortical axis rank. Edge strength in the connectivity matrix (Figure 1c) was defined as streamline counts weighted by SIFT2 coefficients and normalized by volume. Site effects were corrected using ComBat. We employed Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMM) to model developmental trajectories and test for age-by-diagnosis interactions (Figure 1d), identifying altered maturation patterns. To characterize the spatial pattern of these differences, we correlated the interaction effect sizes with the S-A axis rank. Finally, we performed a finer age-resolved analysis using sliding windows and tertile splits to pinpoint the specific developmental windows where diagnostic differences are most profound.

Results

Significant age-by-diagnosis interactions were identified in 8 structural edges (Figure 2a; FDR corrected, P < 0.05). In these connections, children with ADHD exhibited a "delayed onset" of connectivity strength increase compared to TD peers (e.g., onset at ~10.7y vs. earlier in TD). Trajectories in ADHD tended to be flatter or show a downward trend in early childhood compared to rising trends in TD. The magnitude of developmental deviations followed a hierarchical pattern. Interaction effect sizes significantly correlated with the S-A cortical axis rank (Figure 2b; rho = 0.28, P = 0.002), indicating larger deviations in higher-order association networks. Diagnostic group differences were dynamic. The largest effect sizes (Cohen’s d) occurred in the youngest tertile (8.9–10.3y, Figure 2c). In this group, differences aligned strongly with the S-A axis (rho = 0.41, P < 0.001). These differences, and their spatial organization, diminished in the middle and oldest tertiles. Fine-grained analysis confirmed that significant diagnostic effects were concentrated in late childhood and faded during adolescence (Figure 2d).

Conclusion

Structural connectivity deviations in ADHD are developmentally dynamic rather than static. Children with ADHD exhibit a delayed maturation of white matter networks, particularly in edges connecting high-order association areas. These deviations are most profound in late childhood (approx. 9-10 years) and normalize during adolescence. This age-dependent pattern highlights the importance of considering developmental stages in ADHD neuroimaging research.
