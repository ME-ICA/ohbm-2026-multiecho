Introduction

Fetal diffusion MRI (dMRI) provides unique insight into the development of neural connectivity, but acquisition and processing remain highly challenging due to motion, low signal-to-noise ratio, and severe geometric distortions.¹ While Non-Human Primate (NHP) models enable controlled longitudinal studies, processing tools remain fragmented. Existing human fetal pipelines often lack support for super-resolution from orthogonal stacks, while specialized tools for motion correction or super-resolution are rarely integrated into unified end-to-end frameworks. To address this gap, we introduce a comprehensive, open-source pipeline that combines preprocessing, motion correction, and super-resolution to reconstruct high-resolution motion corrected fetal diffusion volumes. We validated the framework on a unique longitudinal dataset of fetal baboons, achieving the first in vivo reconstruction of higher-order diffusion models in fetal NHPs.

Methods

We processed 63 scanning sessions from the BaboFet project, comprising seven pregnant baboons scanned weekly between gestational weeks 13–23 on a 3T Siemens Prisma. Acquisitions included orthogonal stacks of T2-weighted HASTE images and diffusion-weighted SE-EPI scans (1.1×1.1×3 mm³, b=1000 s/mm², 30 directions). The pipeline integrates tools from FSL, MRtrix3, ANTs, and SVRTK. Preprocessing included MP-PCA denoising and Gibbs ringing removal. Brain extraction was performed with a custom 3D nnU-Net trained on 359 manually segmented b=0 volumes to ensure robustness across gestational ages.² Following distortion and motion correction via FSL Eddy, the Slice-to-Volume Reconstruction toolkit (SVRTK) was used to generate 0.5mm isotropic super-resolved 4D diffusion volumes, modeling the diffusion signal with Spherical Harmonics basis.³ Diffusion tensor and Fiber Orientation Distributions (FODs)⁴ were then estimated, and longitudinal trajectories of Fractional Anisotropy (FA) and Mean Diffusivity (MD) were quantified within cortical and white matter segmentations propagated from the reconstructed anatomical volumes (Figure 1).

Results

The pipeline consistently produced high-quality, motion-corrected 4D volumes across all sessions, with the custom nnU-Net achieving a mean Dice score of 0.959 ± 0.017. Super-resolution reconstruction significantly improved anatomical details and diffusion contrast compared to raw stacks. The resulting tensor maps and FODs exhibited coherent fiber architecture consistent with known anatomical pathways. Quantitative longitudinal analysis demonstrated smooth developmental trajectories: cortical FA decreased monotonically, reflecting the transition from radial microstructure to increasing cellular density, while white matter FA followed a characteristic U-shaped pattern, capturing early cell migration followed by progressive tract organization (Figure 2). These trends closely align with established observations from human fetal development.⁵

Conclusion

We present an open-source, end-to-end framework specifically designed for longitudinal fetal NHP dMRI. By integrating motion correction and super-resolution within a unified reconstruction pipeline, the method enables robust in-vivo estimation of higher-order diffusion models. This framework offers a consistent methodological basis for characterizing microstructural maturation and facilitates comparative studies of primate brain development. Code is available at https://github.com/MecaLab/Babofet_DW.
