Introduction

Social dominance and sex influence behavior, but their combined effects on brain structure remain unclear. Male rodents often show higher dominance than females，however, the latter is under researched. The aim of this study is to evaluate how social dominance status and sex modulate longitudinal brain volumetric changes in Wistar rats.

Methods

Male and female Wistar rats (n= 10; postnatal day 35) were assessed for anxiety, locomotion, and social hierarchy using the open field and dominance tube tests, while a separate group of female and male naive (n=5) rats served as imaging controls. Structural MRI was acquired for all animals at three time points (adolescence, early adulthood and adulthood) using a 3D FLASH T2-weighted sequence with TR = 30.76 ms, TE = 5 ms, flip angle = 10°, FOV = 25.6 × 19.1 × 25.6 mm, and isotropic resolution of 160 μm and analyzed by using deformation-based morphometry (DBM) to assess for local volumetric changes.

Results

65% of the cohort were Dominant rats , and 80% of males were dominant, but only 50% of females were dominant. Dominant rats of both sexes exhibited significant volume increases in the piriform cortex and lateral substantia nigra, accompanied by reductions in the posterior hypothalamus compared to non-dominant. Within the dominant group, males showed smaller volume of striatum and entorhinal cortex, alongside increased orbitofrontal cortex volume compared to dominant females.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that social dominance and sex differentially modulate structural adaptations linked to hierarchical and social behaviors. Consequently, this sex-dominance interaction serves as a relevant framework for studying the neuroanatomy of disorders associated to social hierarchy.
