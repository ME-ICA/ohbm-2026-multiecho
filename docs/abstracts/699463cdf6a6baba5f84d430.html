Introduction

Sustained attention is critical for stable, goal-directed behavior. Fluctuations in sustained attention are commonly indexed using continuous performance tasks, where trial-wise reaction time variability (RTV) tracks moment-to-moment attentional stability.1 Neuroimaging studies have linked these fluctuations to the dorsal attention network (DAN), which supports externally directed attention, and the default mode network (DMN), which is associated with internally oriented cognition.1,2 Typically, the DMN is active during rest and self-referential thought, while the DAN is engaged during goal-directed behaviors that demand sustained attention to external environments.3 However, in relation to RTV, research has described a stable “in the zone” state characterized by increased DMN activation and a more effortful “out of the zone” state that relies on activation of DAN regions.1

Crucially, DMN–DAN relationships have largely been examined at the whole network level as monolithic systems. Precision neuroimaging now demonstrates that both systems comprise functionally and anatomically distinct networks with dissociable roles: DMN-A (midline/internal cognition), DMN-B (lateral/semantic processing), DAN-A (goal maintenance), and DAN-B (attentional reorienting).4 Despite growing evidence for such network specialization, these distinctions have primarily been examined in memory and social cognition, not sustained attention. Moreover, most prior work relies on group-averaged network parcellations, which obscure meaningful inter-individual variability in functional topography. Multi-Session Hierarchical Bayesian Modelling (MS-HBM), an individualized parcellation approache, preserves subject-specific network organization, improving behavioral relevance.5 We leverage individualized MS-HBM networks to test whether RTV preferentially relates to specific networks within canonical DAN and DMN boundaries (DAN-A, DAN-B, DMN-A, DMN-B), rather than unitary systems.

Methods

We analyzed data from 20 healthy adults (18–35 years) who each completed two fMRI sessions. Neuroimaging data were acquired using a 64-channel head coil on a 3-Tesla Siemens Prisma scanner with a multiband sequence (TR = 2.0 sec, voxel size = 2.0x2.0x2.0 mm). All procedures were approved by the local institutional review board, and participants provided informed consent. During each session, participants completed one 8-minute run of gradual-onset Continuous Performance Task (gradCPT), yielding two task runs per subject. The task was designed to engage sustained attention by requiring participants to monitor a rapid stream of grayscale images and respond continuously with a button press. Visual stimuli consisted of a pseudorandom sequence of city and mountain scenes, with city scenes comprising 90% of trials and mountain scenes comprising 10%. Each image gradually transitioned into the next every 800ms, producing a continuous perceptual flow without abrupt stimulus onsets. Participants were instructed to press a button for every frequent city scene and withhold response to infrequent mountain scenes. 

Individualized cortical network parcellations were generated for all 20 participants using Multi-Session Hierarchical Bayesian Modeling (MS-HBM) to estimate subject-specific functional topography while accounting for within- and between-subject variability. MS-HBM was applied to two-session rest-based fMRI data with intermittent experience sampling using a Yeo-17–based prior, focusing on DAN-A, DAN-B, DMN-A, and DMN-B. For visualization, a group-level network representation was derived using a majority-vote consensus across individual parcellations, in which each cortical vertex was assigned to the subnetwork most frequently identified across subjects (Figure 1A).

RTV was modeled as a continuous parametric regressor indexing moment-to-moment fluctuations in sustained attention. For each participant, first-level general linear models (GLMs) were computed separately for each gradCPT run, with RTV convolved with the canonical hemodynamic response function. Run-level estimates were combined within subjects using fixed-effects modeling, followed by group-level mixed-effects analysis. To quantify subnetwork-specific RTV–BOLD associations, mean Z-scores were extracted for each subject by averaging within their individualized subnetwork masks, yielding four values per subject (Figure 1B) that were entered into a repeated-measures ANOVA. In addition, one-sample t-tests against zero were performed for each subnetwork to assess significance of RTV–BOLD associations.

Results

Descriptive statistics revealed that at a group level, the DAN-A network showed the highest mean RTV-BOLD correlation (mZ = +1.118, s = 1.588), followed by DAN-B (mZ = +0.393, s = 1.871). The DMN networks showed near-zero or negative correlations (DMN-A: mZ = -0.190, s = 1.407; DMN-B: mZ = -0.173, s  = 1.600). A one-way repeated measures ANOVA on the mean Z-scores demonstrated a significant main effect of networks (F(3, 57) = 6.53, pHF = 0.007). One-sample t-tests against zero revealed a significant positive RTV–BOLD association in DAN-A (t (19) = 3.15, p = 0.005), whereas DAN-B and both DMN networks did not differ from zero (all p > 0.35).

Conclusion

By utilizing individualized functional network parcellation, we found that changes in RTV that signify attentional fluctuations are differentially related to specific networks within canonical DMN and DAN systems. We confirm the expected positive correlation between RTV and DAN BOLD signal, but show that this effect is highly specialized, being localized specifically to the DAN-A network. This finding suggests that RTV fluctuations may not be a result of uniform DAN overactivity but rather reflect an inefficient or maladaptive hyper-engagement of the core sustained goal maintenance component (DAN-A). These results underscore the necessity of using individualized network topography to resolve functional ambiguities in the domain of sustained attention.
