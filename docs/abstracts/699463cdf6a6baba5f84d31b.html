Introduction

Cannabis Use Disorder (CUD) affects approximately 50 million individuals globally and is linked to negative outcomes, which have been ascribed to hyperactivity in the craving neurocircuitry including the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC). How craving-related function changes in real-time remains unexamined, leaving neuroscientific theories of addiction untested in CUD. We investigated whether real-time functional MRI neurofeedback (fMRI-neurofeedback) can be used to volitionally engage the craving neurocircuitry in CUD, with a focus on the ACC as region-of-interest (ROI).

Methods

Ten individuals with moderate-to-severe CUD were tested using7 Tesla MRI. Participants completed: (i) a “localiser” fMRI task to identify the most consistently engaged parts of the ROI at the individual level; (ii) two fMRI-neurofeedback runs to upregulate ROI activity via increasing a “craving bar” displayed in the scanner, showing the % signal change in the ROI. Offline group-level data was analysed using hypothesis-driven ROI-based and exploratory whole-brain general linear models contrasting Upregulation versus Neutral blocks.

Results

ROI analysis showed lower ACC activity (t = -6.57, p < .05), as well as whole-brain analysis, which also demonstrated significant deactivation in frontal (t = -9.76, p < .05) and parietal regions (t = -11.50, p < .05) during fMRI-neurofeedback. 

Conclusion

The direction of the results was contrary to our hypotheses. The ACC may be implicated in down-regulation of craving, and perhaps a ceiling effect was reached, suggesting that the brain activity during cravings differ from prominent neuroscientific theories of addiction and needs to be validated in larger samples.
