Introduction

The accurate delineation of cortical areas is critical to developing an understanding of the brain networks that underlie behaviours and identifying patterns of atypical brain activity. Non-invasive parcellation of cortex is generally reliant on functional localizers, which can impede the study of atypical cortex (e.g., deaf auditory cortex). Conversely, structural parcellation often depends on invasive approaches (e.g., quantifying biomarkers in histological sections). However, neuroimaging sequences have been developed that provide non-invasive estimates of anatomical features, including myelin density, which may allow us to perform “virtual histology”. Here, we generate a stereological myeloarchitectonic map of the feline auditory cortex, and compare to maps generated within the same animals (3 hearing, 3 deaf) using myelin–sensitive neuroimaging sequences. Histological estimates in hearing animals suggest that unique patterns of myelination exist across the 13 feline auditory cortical areas. Moreover, myelination follows a hierarchical gradient, such that core areas show greater myelin density than higher-order auditory and multimodal areas. Moreover, histological myelin density estimates correlated significantly with non-invasive estimates of myelin derived from magnetization transfer (MT) imaging, but not diffusion-weighted images. These findings validate the myelin-sensitivity of MT sequences, and highlight their potential to create in vivo anatomical maps of cortex that are not dependent on functional responses. We further discuss the results of diffusion-weighted imaging in the context of other microstructural features that may be of interest, and offer preliminary insights into MT-derived myelin maps of deaf auditory cortex and their comparisons with hearing animals.

Methods

Subjects

The current study collected MR images and histological myelin estimates from a total of six adult domestic short-haired cats (3 hearing, 3 deaf).

Histology and Stereology

The feline auditory cortex comprises 13 distinct subregions. The borders between adjacent subregions were drawn according to SMI-32 neurofilament staining patterns1 and were used to generate regions of interest from which myelin density estimates could be generated using adjacent sections stained for myelin using the modified Gallyas silver protocol2. Specifically, bias-free myelin fiber length density (MFLD) was calculated stereologically within each subregion using the Spaceballs probe workflow 3 in MBF Stereoinvestigator. 

Imaging

In the interval between scanning the hearing and deaf animals, the imaging facility upgraded their hardware; accordingly, the sequence parameters differed across groups. Hearing brains were imaged using a 9.4T ultra-high field MRI scanner using an xm coil (400mT/m). Deaf cats were imaged in a 15.2T Bruker scanner (1000mT/m). 

In magnetization transfer imaging, an off-resonance RF pulse is applied that selectively excites the bound proton pool, which then cross-relaxes with surrounding free protons and partially saturates them. The result is signal attenuation after a second RF pulse is applied in the frequency domain of the free protons. As myelin is the predominant macromolecule in the brain, MT-related measures should be closely related to cortical myelin content.

The MT protocol used to image hearing animals comprised three FLASH-3D scans. Reference T1w, PDw, and MTw images were acquired via the following repetition times (TR) and the flip angles (α): TR/α = 8.5 ms/20 ms for the T1w scan and 25 ms/9 ms for the PDw and the MTw scans. MT-weighting was achieved by applying an off-resonance Gaussian-shaped RF pulse (12-ms duration, 385-degree nominal flip angle, 3.5 kHz frequency offset from water resonance, 5 μT RF peak amplitude) prior to the excitation. Other acquisition parameters were: TE = 2.75ms; resolution: 400 um isotropic. The MT used to image deaf animals included T1w images acquired with TR/α = 12 ms/20 degrees and Mtw/PDw scans with TR/α = 30 ms/9 degrees. An off-resonance RF pulse was applied (12-ms duration, 500 degree nominal flip angle, 5.5 kHz frequency offset from water resonance, 6 μT RF peak amplitude) prior to the excitation. Other acquisition parameters were: TE = 3 ms; resolution: 300 um isotropic.

Diffusion MRI-based metrics (dMRI) are often used to represent changes in myelination. However, how well diffusion metrics correlate with myelin content in grey matter remains to be seen. Here, microscopic anisotropy (uA) imaging and oscillating gradient spin-echo (OGSE) imaging were combined to evaluate how diffusion-weighted sequences correlated with histological myelin content.

Hearing dMRI images were acquired with single-shot spin echo echo-planar-imaging (EPI). The OGSE protocol included a PGSE sequence and four OGSE sequences with oscillating gradient frequencies (50HZ, 80HZ, 115HZ, 150 HZ) at b = 1000s/mm^2 (10 directions for each ƒ). TR/TE were 20s and 37.7ms respectively. The uA sequence was implemented with LTE and STE encodings, at b = 1320 s/mm^2 (6 directions each), b = 2640 s/mm^2 (9 directions), and  b = 4000 s/mm^2 (15 directions). TR/TE were 20s and 39ms respectively. 400μm isotropic resolution. The deaf dMRI images were acquired using multi-shot spin echo EPI. The OGSE protocol included PGSE and OGSE (120 Hz) sequences at b = 1500 s/mm^2 (10 directions for each shape), with TE/TR = 30/42000 ms, 6 repetitions. The uA sequence was implemented with linear and spherical tensor encoding (LTE/STE) acquisitions at b = 1333 s/mm^2 (6 directions each), b = 2666 s/mm^2 (9 directions), and b = 4000 s/mm^2 (15 directions), with TE/TR = 35/31000 ms, 3 repetitions,. 400μm isotropic resolution.

Results

A stereological analysis of myelin density revealed a gradient across auditory cortical subregions that corresponds to their hierarchical arrangement. Core auditory areas were significantly more densely myelinated than higher-order regions, with decreasing myelination observed with ascending hierarchical position (Figure 1). Furthermore, an anterodorsal to posteroventral gradient of myelination was observed.

Comparisons between histology and MRI-derived estimates revealed a strong correlation between MT-sequences and histological estimates of MFLD (r = 0.69, MTR;  r = 0.63, MTsat). This suggests that MT-based MRI sequences are highly sensitive to grey matter myelination. Conversely, no diffusion-related metrics correlated significantly with histological myelin estimates.

Lastly, we compared MT estimates derived from both hearing and deaf cats, and preliminary analyses implicate multisensory auditory areas have an increase in myelin-related metrics when compared to primary and secondary auditory regions in deaf cats.

Conclusion

The potential of non-invasive structural biomarkers for use in research and clinical settings is enormous. They would provide an effective way of localizing aberrant or non-functional cortex, and would provide a wealth of information regarding microstructural changes that follow functional changes. However, validating these biomarkers by co-registering histological quantifications with purported feature-sensitive neuroimaging sequences remains a critical issue to be addressed.

In this work, we provide detailed quantitative estimates of myelin content throughout the 13 subregions of feline auditory cortex. We demonstrate that myelin differs across cortical subregions in a predictable way. Specifically, core primary sensory areas are densely myelinated, with progressively less myelination in areas with increasingly complex function. This demonstrates that quantifiable differences in myelin density exist between subregions that might be observed non-invasively using myelin-sensitive neuroimaging. 

In addition to histology, we acquired magnetization transfer and diffusion-MRI images of the auditory cortex in these same animals. We co-registered our MRI-derived and histological estimates to determine the sensitivity of these sequences for grey matter myelination. We found strong correlations between MT-derived estimates and histological MFLD. This signals the promise of MT-MRI as an effective tool for performing virtual histology to map different cortical areas based on myelin content. No diffusion-based metrics correlated significantly with histological myelin content. Accordingly, we suggest that that these diffusion metrics - particularly microscopic fractional anisotropy - may capture other important microstructural features (unmyelinated axons, dendritic arborization, etc.), though further work is needed to clarify these relationships and uncover a role for dMRI in non-invasive cortical mapping.

After confirming the sensitivity of these MT-MRI estimates of cortical myelination, we acquired similar images from auditory cortex in 3 deaf animals to examine how patterns of auditory myelination are modified in response to early-onset hearing loss. Preliminary analyses suggest that myelin density may be decreased in core auditory cortical subregions, while remaining stable or even increasing across regions known to undergo crossmodal reorganization following deafness.
