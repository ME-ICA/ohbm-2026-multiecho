Introduction

Focal cortical dysplasia (FCD), characterized by abnormalities in normal cortical lamination and neuronal morphology, is a common underlying pathology in patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy who are candidates for surgical resection. It usually necessitates a multimodal evaluation to ensure optimal surgical treatment. This study aims to evaluate the localization value of electric source imaging (ESI) concordance with resection margin and to assess whether this prognostic value in predicting 1-year seizure freedom differs based on MRI lesion status (lesional vs. non-lesional) or lobar location (temporal vs. extratemporal).

Methods

We performed a retrospective analysis of 97 patients with drug-resistant epilepsy who underwent presurgical long-term video EEG monitoring and epilepsy surgery in the Seoul National University Hospital. ESI was performed using BESA software (Figure 1), and its localization concordance with the surgically resected area was determined. Postoperative seizure outcome was classified using Engel criteria, with Engel I and II considered seizure-free. The primary outcome was seizure freedom (SF) at 1-year post-surgery. Patients were categorized into subgroups based on MRI findings (MRI-positive vs. MRI-negative) and resection location (Temporal vs. Extratemporal). We calculated the odds ratio (OR), sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) of ESI concordance for predicting a seizure-free outcome. To ensure methodological consistency across the cohort and subgroups, all statistical associations were analyzed using Fisher's exact test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The study cohort included 97 patients (MRI-positive: n=44; MRI-negative: n=53; Temporal: n=60; Extratemporal: n=37). Overall, 57.7% (56/97) of patients achieved seizure freedom at 1 year. ESI concordance was present in 70.1% (68/97) of cases. In the overall analysis, patients with ESI concordance demonstrated a significantly higher likelihood of achieving seizure freedom compared to the discordant group (OR 2.60; 95% CI 1.07–6.33; p=0.044). The diagnostic performance of ESI concordance yielded a Sensitivity of 78.6%, Specificity of 41.5%, Positive Predictive Value (PPV) of 64.7%, and Negative Predictive Value (NPV) of 58.6%. Subgroup analyses revealed that the prognostic utility was most pronounced in the temporal lobe cohort, where concordance was significantly associated with favorable outcomes (OR 4.86; 95% CI 1.26–18.77; p=0.022) and showed high Sensitivity (89.5%). In contrast, no prognostic association was found in the Extratemporal group (OR 1.12; p=1.000). Regarding MRI status, while positive trends were observed in both groups, neither reached statistical significance. The MRI-negative group showed an OR of 2.75 (95% CI 0.81–9.38; p=0.145), and the MRI-positive group showed an OR of 2.12 (95% CI 0.48–9.37; p=0.425) (Figure 2).

Conclusion

In this cohort, ESI concordance was a significant predictor of surgical success, conferring a 2.6-fold increase in the odds of 1-year seizure freedom. This prognostic value was largely driven by patients with temporal lobe epilepsy, where concordance was significantly associated with a nearly five-fold increase in the odds of a favorable outcome (OR 4.86). While a favorable trend was observed in MRI-negative patients (OR 2.75), it did not reach statistical significance, and ESI concordance showed no predictive value in extratemporal cases. These findings suggest that ESI is a highly sensitive supportive tool in the presurgical evaluation, as an adjunctive tool to aid in surgical planning, particularly for temporal lobe epilepsy.
