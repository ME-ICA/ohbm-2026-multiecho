Introduction

Depression is a heterogeneous disorder with wide variability in symptoms, disease trajectories, and treatment response, suggesting the existence of multiple underlying biological forms. Current diagnostic frameworks however focus only on clinical presentation and fail to capture this molecular diversity. Proteomic profiling enables large-scale assessment of protein-level variation, providing a functional understanding of biological processes influenced by both genetic and environmental factors.

Critically, network-based analysis of proteomic data further allows the detection of coordinated molecular changes, which is particularly relevant to depression given its involvement of inflammatory, metabolic, and circadian pathways. Population-scale cohorts, such as the UK Biobank, integrate proteomic, clinical, and imaging-derived phenotypes, enabling the construction of normative molecular networks and the quantification of individual deviations from these networks . 

Such patient-specific perturbation profiles are invaluable; they may reveal biologically grounded subtypes and clarify their links to systemic disease. To address this critical gap, we employed a network perturbation framework on plasma proteomic data from 43,822 UK Biobank participants, including 6,768 individuals with depression and 37,054 controls . We constructed a covariate-adjusted protein co-expression reference network, quantified patient-specific deviations, and identified recurrent molecular signatures defining robust patient subgroups. These subtypes were then characterized with respect to clinical, cardiovascular, and imaging-derived phenotypes, ultimately providing a data-driven framework for understanding biological heterogeneity in depression.

Methods

Cohort and proteomic data : 

We obtained data from the UK Biobank, a large population-based cohort with linked health records. We then identified major depressive disorder (MDD) cases using ICD-10 codes F32–F33, yielding 6,768 cases and 37,054 controls among participants with available plasma proteomics.Protein abundances were measured using the Olink Explore 3072 platform (NPX, log2 scale)and proteins with more than 30% missing values were excluded and imputed remaining missing data using k-nearest neighbors (k = 5). We identified sample outliers using robust Mahalanobis distance and removed them prior to analysis. Finally ,we adjusted protein values for technical covariates, including batch effects, storage duration, and hemolysis indicators. All downstream analyses additionally adjusted for age, sex, body mass index, smoking status, alcohol intake frequency, and blood pressure

Reference network construction : 

Protein co-expression network was constructed using the healthy control group. We estimated direct protein-protein relationships using Pearson partial correlation while conditioning on all proteins and covariates simultaneously1. Since the number of observations exceeded the number of variables, the empirical covariance matrix was full rank and could be stably inverted to obtain the precision matrix. From this matrix, we derived partial correlation coefficients. The resulting reference network represents covariate-adjusted conditional protein associations in the non-depressed population.

Patient-specific network perturbation : 

For each MDD participant, we computed an individualized perturbed network by temporarily adding that individual to the control cohort and recomputing the covariate-adjusted partial correlation network.Patient-specific network perturbations (ΔPCC) is defined as the difference between the perturbed and reference networks. Consistent with prior theoretical and empirical work, ΔPCC values followed a characteristic “volcano” distribution2, which enable the edge-level inference using Z-scores. We calculated Z-scores for each protein pair as a function of ΔPCC magnitude and the control sample size.

Statistical testing : 

P-values were derived from the Z-scores and corrected on a per-patient basis using the Benjamini Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR q < 0.05). The edges passing this threshold are considered significantly perturbed and together constituted an individualized network perturbation signature for each patient.

Subtype identification and validation : 

In order to identify shared molecular structure across patients, we ranked significant edges by their recurrence across individuals.Top 20 recurrent edges were selected as features for clustering then grouped the patients using agglomerative hierarchical clustering with 1 − Pearson correlation as the distance metric. We evaluated cluster solutions from k = 2 to 5 and observed the highest silhouette score at k = 2. stability through repeated random initializations, variation in feature-set size, and split-half replication. Finally ,to exclude methodological artifacts, a full analysis pipeline was applied to randomly selected healthy controls treated as pseudo-patients, which did not produce stable clustering.

Results

Proteomic Heterogeneity and subtype structure : 

Individualized network perturbation analysis revealed molecular heterogeneity among individuals with MDD . Clustering based on recurrent partial-correlation deviations identified two reproducible proteomic subgroups, each characterized by an opposing co-expression patterns across a recurrent protein-protein interactions. The overall structure of these patterns is consistent with organized molecular variation rather than random noise. The largest between-subgroup difference was observed for the interaction between CCER2 and PER3, which was therefore used as a representative marker of a cardiovascular-immune-circadian axis distinguishing the two subgroups.

Pathway enrichment analysis of subtype-associated proteins highlighted several biological processes that differed between the subgroups. The most dominant enrichments involved GPCR and adenylate cyclase signaling, immune and inflammatory pathways, extracellular-region processes, and pathways related to cardiac muscle contraction. Additional enrichment was observed for metabolic processes, such as pancreatic secretion and carbohydrate metabolism, suggesting coordinated differences across cardiovascular, immune, and metabolic systems.

Phenome wide associations : 

One subgroup, referred to as the CMI subtype, showed consistent associations with a range of cardiometabolic traits after multiple correction testing. The most notable associations involved vascular measures and lipid-related biomarkers. Individuals in this subgroup tended to receive a diagnosis of angina pectoris at an earlier age and showed greater carotid intima-media thickness across several measures. Additional differences were observed in systolic blood pressure and circulating lipid profiles, with lower HDL cholesterol in the CMI subtype and higher triglyceride levels in the opposing subgroup.

Neuroimaging phenotypes : 

After multiple correction testing, associations were observed across several imaging measures. The most dominant differences involved brainstem intensity measures. Additional differences were observed in white-matter microstructural metrics, including mean diffusivity and L3 in major tracts such as the superior corona radiata3. Regional volumetric differences were also present, with the most notable variation observed in the choroid plexus. These neuroimaging patterns occurred alongside the cardiometabolic differences identified between the proteomic subgroups.

Cardiovascular diagnosis prevalence : 

To further characterize cardiovascular involvement, we compared the prevalence of ICD-10 circulatory system diagnoses between the proteomic subgroups. The highest relative prevalence was observed for angina pectoris in the CMI subtype, with trends toward increased prevalence of myocardial infarction. These findings are consistent with the cardiometabolic associations identified in the phenome-wide analyses, while remaining descriptive and not implying causality.

Conclusion

Our proposed study successfully utilized the network perturbation framework across a large, covariate-adjusted cohort to demonstrate that Major Depressive Disorder is defined by at least two biologically distinct proteomic subtypes , moving beyond conventional symptom-based diagnostics. The most critical finding is the identification of CMI Subtype, characterized by a reciprocal molecular signature anchored by the ACE2-GGT1 and CCER2-PER3 co-expression edges. This divergence is rooted in a coordinated, multi-systemic disruption centered on the Cardiovascular-Immune-Circadian axis. The clinical relevance is profound: CMI Subtype exhibits a multi-systemic clinical profile characterized by significantly accelerated cardiovascular risk (earlier onset of Ischemic Heart Disease), alongside a distinct neurological signature (structural changes in the brainstem). This molecular biotype was demonstrated to be independent of residual confounding by BMI, establishing it as a high-risk subtype of depression. In establishing this systems-level framework, our findings transition the concept of depressive heterogeneity from descriptive classification to mechanistic stratification. This integrated molecular and clinical profile offers a valuable and immediate target for personalized medicine. Future efforts should focus on validating these proteomic signatures as clinical biomarkers to enable proactive cardiovascular risk screening and the development of targeted therapies for CMI Subtype that intervene directly on the convergence of vascular, inflammatory, and circadian dysregulation.
