Introduction

Schizophrenia is a severe psychiatric disorder characterized by positive, negative and cognitive symptoms. Aberrant dopaminergic function plays a key role in the pathophysiology of schizophrenia. While many studies focus on aberrant dopamine transmission of dopaminergic midbrain neurons in the striatum 1,2, further structural abnormalities from dopaminergic midbrain to the striatum via white matter pathways in schizophrenia is yet to elucidate 3. Thus, the first goal of the current study is to investigate connections between dopaminergic ventral tegmental area, substantia nigra, and the striatum in the disorder. Furthermore, dopaminergic midbrain neurons are controlled by connections from deep cerebellar nuclei 4,5. For example in mice, using optogenetics and electrophysiology, recent studies demonstrated that deep cerebellar nuclei modulate the activity of neurons in dopaminergic midbrain via monosynaptic and excitatory projections, and this control contributes to reward processing and social behavior 4,5. Thus, our second goal was to investigate these connections and associate connection metrics with those of connections between midbrain and the striatum in patients. To do this, we performed diffusion MRI, tractography, and clinical assessment in patients with schizophrenia and healthy controls and compared tract metrics across groups or related them among each other.

Methods

We performed probabilistic tractography based on diffusion- and T1- weighted images of 105 healthy controls and 108 patients with schizophrenia by MRtrix3 (https://www.mrtrix.org/). Participants were recruited from the Wuxi Mental Health Center, Jiangsu, China. Patients were diagnosed with the DSM-IV, with symptom severity assessed by PANSS. The written informed consent was obtained from subjects, and this study was approved by the local ethics committee. To reconstruct streamlines of ipsilateral dopaminergic midbrain – striatum pathways individually, we performed tractography from dopaminergic midbrain unidirectionally to the nucleus accumbens, caudate nucleus and putamen, respectively. The dopaminergic midbrain is composed of the substantia nigra pars compacta and ventral tegmental area. The streamlines traverse hemisphere were not allowed. For each hemisphere, three bundles connecting ipsilateral dopaminergic midbrain and subregions of striatum would be reconstructed for all subjects. To reconstruct ipsilateral monosynaptic streamlines of deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain pathways individually, we performed tractography unidirectionally from the deep cerebellar nuclei to dopaminergic midbrain target and stopped once traversed dopaminergic midbrain targets. The deep cerebellar nuclei are composed of the fastigial, interposed, and dentate nuclei, and the dopaminergic midbrain is the same as described above. Streamlines were not allowed to traverse cerebellar cortex, medulla, red nucleus, substantia nigra pars reticulata, subthalamic nucleus, parabrachial pigmented nucleus and the hemisphere. For each hemisphere, one bundle connecting ipsilateral deep cerebellar nuclei and dopaminergic midbrain would be reconstructed for all subjects. To characterize the structural features of tracts of deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain – striatum pathways, we extracted the normalized streamline number, fractional anisotropy and fixel-wise fiber density of these tracts. Further, we tested the correlation between tract measurements for the ipsilateral tracts for both controls and patients. And we tested the group difference and clinical relevance for quantitative measurements of tracts in this study.

Results

To test for altered structural features of deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain – striatum pathways in patients with schizophrenia, we compared normalized streamline number, fractional anisotropy and fixel-wise fiber density of these tracts, respectively, for tracts of each pathway by using a linear model approach with structural features as dependent variables, respectively, and group, sex, and age as the independent variables. We observed lower normalized streamline number of left dopaminergic midbrain – caudate pathway (T = - 2.777, P =0.006), and higher normalized streamline number of left dopaminergic midbrain – putamen pathway (T = 2.145, P =0.033) (Figure 1A). No group differences for normalized streamline number of deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain were found. And we observed lower fractional anisotropy of right dopaminergic midbrain – caudate pathway (T = - 3.265, P =0.001), and of right deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain pathway (T = - 2.248, P =0.026) (Figure 1B). Furthermore, we observed lower fixel-wise fiber density for all tracts we investigated in this study, including tracts of bilateral deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain and bilateral dopaminergic midbrain – striatum (caudate, nucleus accumbens and putamen) pathways (Figure 1C).

To test for the correlation between tract measurements of the ipsilateral tracts for both controls and patients, we performed Pearson’s correlation in both controls and patients. We observed that, for fiber density, all six pairs the correlation were significant for both patients and controls, namely the correlation between fiber density of deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain pathway and fiber density of dopaminergic midbrain – striatal subregions pathway (Figure 1D). 

To test for the clinical relevance of altered structural features of deep cerebellar nuclei – dopaminergic midbrain – striatum pathways in patients with schizophrenia, we used Pearson’s correlation. We observed lower fractional anisotropy of right ipsilateral deep cerebellar nuclei - dopaminergic midbrain was negatively correlated with patients’ positive symptoms severity (R = -0.162, P = 0.047), and lower fiber density of left ipsilateral dopaminergic midbrain – nucleus accumbens was negatively correlated with patients’ negative symptoms severity (R = -0.157, P = 0.053). Further, we observed lower fiber density of right ipsilateral dopaminergic midbrain – nucleus accumbens (R = -0.194, P = 0.023) and putamen (R = -0.169, P = 0.040) were negatively correlated with patients’ total symptoms severity.

Conclusion

Our results demonstrate that tracts between the dopaminergic midbrain and the striatum are widely and consistently altered in schizophrenia. Due to the structural nature of our finding, the result suggests a stable alteration of the dopaminergic midbrain in the disorder.

Furthermore, tracts between the deep cerebellar nuclei and the dopaminergic midbrain are impaired in patients. Due to the control function of cerebellar nuclei over dopaminergic neurons, this result suggests impaired cerebellar-midbrain connectivity as a factor of impaired dopaminergic function in schizophrenia. This suggestion is confirmed by the correlation between fiber densities of both midbrain-cerebellum and midbrain-striatum connectivity in patients and controls.

Overall, we provide evidence for impaired dopaminergic midbrain connectivity in schizophrenia, likely modulated by impaired cerebellar nuclei-to-midbrain connectivity in patients.
