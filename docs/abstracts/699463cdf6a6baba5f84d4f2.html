Introduction

The dysconnectivity hypothesis proposes that widespread disturbances in coordinated information flow across large-scale brain networks underlie psychiatric symptoms and cognitive impairments of schizophrenia (SCZ). While machine-learning (ML) approaches using resting-state functional connectivity (FC) have shown moderate success in distinguishing SCZ from healthy controls (HC), FC reflects only statistical associations and does not capture directional interactions within neural systems. Effective connectivity (EC), by modeling directional and causal influences between brain regions, may offer improved sensitivity to disease-related alterations. Recent advances in regression dynamic causal modeling (rDCM) now enable whole-brain EC estimation across hundreds of regions, overcoming computational limitations of traditional DCM frameworks. Although proof-of-concept studies have demonstrated the promise of rDCM-derived EC features in predicting clinical states, no prior study has applied whole-brain EC features to ML classification in SCZ. This study aimed to evaluate whether EC-based ML can improve generalizable SCZ classification across independent cohorts and to identify causal pathways that contribute most strongly to disease expression.

Methods

Resting-state fMRI data were obtained from 814 participants across multiple sites, including the SRPBS database (431 HC, 145 SCZ), Wakayama Medical University (52 HC, 40 SCZ), and the COBRE database (74 HC, 72 SCZ). After rigorous motion and quality control, 625 datasets (457 HC, 168 SCZ) were included. EC was estimated using rDCM applied to 100 cortical parcels defined by the Schaefer atlas. Fully connected rDCM models (10,000 parameters per subject) were computed for each participant. Site-related variance was harmonized with ComBat including age, sex, and diagnosis as covariates.

For classifier development, undersampling was used to balance discovery datasets, and nested cross-validation with Optuna-based hyperparameter tuning generated 100 independent classifiers for each ML algorithm (SVM, LightGBM, Random Forest [RF], and MLP). Generalizability was assessed using the independent WMU and COBRE datasets. Performance metrics included AUC, balanced accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and MCC (Figure.1). SHAP values were computed for RF, which consistently achieved the best or near-best performance, to identify influential EC features. Associations between RF-derived class probabilities and PANSS symptom dimensions were examined in the WMU dataset.

Results

In the WMU independent dataset, RF achieved the highest performance (AUC = 0.692, balanced accuracy = 0.645, MCC = 0.287), while in the COBRE dataset, SVM, RF, and MLP performed comparably, with MLP yielding the best AUC (0.763) and MCC (0.339). Despite moderate classification accuracy, EC-based models showed stable generalizability across datasets. 

SHAP analysis revealed that the most discriminative EC features were widely distributed across networks and demonstrated characteristic asymmetries: (1) higher-order versus primary networks asymmetries, including stronger outgoing EC from default-mode network (DMN) regions to salience (SAN), dorsal attention (DAN), and primary sensorimotor networks; (2) right- versus left- hemisphere asymmetries, with left frontoparietal networks (FPN) showing broader cross-network influence than s right FPN. These patterns were not captured by conventional FC analyses and may reflect altered propagation of information between higher-order and primary networks in SCZ (Figure.2).

Classifier output showed a significant positive correlation with PANSS negative symptom severity (ρ = 0.44, p = 0.0495), indicating that the model was particularly sensitive to EC abnormalities associated with motivational and affective deficits. No associations were observed with positive or general symptoms, nor with antipsychotic medication dosage.

Conclusion

Whole-brain effective connectivity estimated with rDCM provides informative features for distinguishing individuals with SCZ from HC and for capturing clinically meaningful variation in symptom severity. Although classification performance was moderate, EC-based ML generalized well across independent datasets and revealed biologically interpretable patterns, including asymmetric and hierarchical disruptions linking DMN, SAN, DAN, and sensorimotor networks. These findings support the potential of EC as a biomarker reflecting both disease presence and negative-symptom burden. Future research using higher-temporal-resolution fMRI and multimodal EC-FC integration may further enhance classification performance and mechanistic interpretability.
