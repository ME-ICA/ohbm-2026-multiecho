Introduction

Schizophrenia is characterized by widespread executive function deficits, notably in cognitive flexibility and abstract concept formation, as measured by the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test (WCST). Structural brain abnormalities, often manifesting as overall or regional volume reduction, are common in individuals with schizophrenia compared to healthy controls. In particular, subcortical structures are critically involved in cognitive functioning and executive control, and their structural alterations may substantially contribute to cognitive deficits observed in schizophrenia. Brain volume-based predicted age deviation could serve as a robust biomarker quantifying structural brain abnormalities. This study aimed to elucidate the relationship between cognitive flexibility and cortical structural features in individuals with schizophrenia. We investigated the association between Volume Deviation and core WCST performance indices in individuals with schizophrenia, specifically perseverative responses and categories completed.

Methods

Participants 

Preprocessed structural magnetic resonance images from 702 individuals with schizophrenia and healthy controls, aged 6 to 85 years, from the TAMI dataset (Taiwan Aging and Mental Illness Cohort, https://data.dmc.nycu.edu.tw/zh_Hans_CN/dataset/taiwan-aging-and-mental illness-cohort-tami ). In this study, the schizophrenia group included individuals who were diagnosed with schizophrenia according to DSM-5. Individuals with schizophrenia lacked complete demographic data, high-quality structural magnetic resonance imaging data, or valid scores for either Categories Completed or Perseverative Responses on the WCST were excluded. A total of 259 schizophrenia individuals were screened, and 44 were excluded, leaving a final sample of 215. 

Statistical analysis 

This study utilized MATLAB 2025a (The MathWorks, Inc.) to perform the whole study. We firstly used the regression learner app to train the machine learning model; calculating Volume Deviation using a Gaussian Process Regression model trained on healthy controls, which utilized 16 bilateral subcortical structure volumes and Intracranial Volume extracted by Freesurfer v7.4.0 as features. Secondly, we applied a polyfit model to the raw brain age prediction to correct for regression bias, regression to the mean effect. By using the corrected Predicted Age minus the Actual Age, we computed the final individual metric, termed Volume Deviation. Then we investigated the association between individual brain aging deviations and cognitive performance in the schizophrenia cohort. We adopted Pearson correlation analysis to quantify the linear association between Volume Deviation and the two WCST scores, and further assessed the independent effect of Volume Deviation on these two WCST indices by fitting separate robust linear regression models. 

Results

The Gaussian Process Regression model demonstrated acceptable performance (RMSE: 9.78 years; R2: 0.34; MAE: 8.17 years). Volume Deviation showed no statistically significant linear association with Perseverative Responses. The Pearson correlation coefficient was 0.050, with a corresponding p = 0.463. The robust linear regression model estimated the effect of Volume Deviation on Perseverative Responses to be a beta value of 0.538 (Standard Error, SE = 0.192). While the coefficient's p-value in the regression table was 0.005, the weak correlation (r = 0.050) suggests that the overall association is not robust, failing to reach the significance threshold typically expected for a simple linear relationship. The model accounted for approximately 15.1% of the variance in Perseverative Responses (Adjusted R2: 0.151).   

Similarly, Volume Deviation showed no statistically significant linear association with the number of Categories Completed on the WCST. The Pearson correlation coefficient r was 0.041, with a corresponding p-value of 0.552. The robust linear regression estimated the effect of Volume Deviation on Categories Completed to be a beta value of -0.024 (Standard Error, SE = 0.026). This negative association was not statistically significant (p = 0.342), indicating that Volume Deviation was not a reliable predictor of the number of categories completed. The model accounted for 4.06% of the variance in Perseverative Responses (Adjusted R2: 0.0406).  

Conclusion

In this study, we examined whether brain volume–based predicted age deviation derived from subcortical structures is associated with executive dysfunction, specifically abstract concept formation and cognitive flexibility, in individuals with schizophrenia. Although the Gaussian Process Regression model demonstrated acceptable performance in estimating brain age from subcortical volumes, Volume Deviation was not robustly associated with core WCST performance indices, including perseverative responses and categories completed. The lack of significant linear associations suggests that global subcortical volume–based brain aging deviations may not directly capture individual differences in executive functioning, as assessed by the WCST, in schizophrenia.

These findings indicate that executive deficits in schizophrenia may arise from more complex neural mechanisms that are not fully reflected by aggregate subcortical volume–derived brain age metrics. Potential contributors include region-specific cortical–subcortical interactions, microstructural alterations, or network-level dysconnectivity underlying executive control processes. Future studies integrating multimodal neuroimaging measures, finer-grained regional or circuit-level analyses, and longitudinal designs may be necessary to clarify how brain aging–related structural alterations relate to cognitive flexibility and abstract concept formation in schizophrenia.
