Introduction

Behavioral variant frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) is primarily characterized by frontal cortex degeneration. 1,2 However, mounting evidence indicates that striatal degeneration represents a critical component of disease pathophysiology with significant implications for clinical presentation and patient prognosis. 3 TDP-43 and tau pathologies are known to spread transneuronally along connected neural circuits. 4 Given the robust anatomical fronto-striatal projections, pathological proteins may propagate from the frontal cortex to the striatum, resulting in striatal neurodegeneration. Here, we investigated the relationship between fronto-striatal disconnection and striatal degeneration in patients with mild bvFTD.

Methods

Twenty-nine patients with mild bvFTD and 29 age- and sex-matched healthy subjects (HS) underwent multimodal neuroimaging on a 3-Tesla hybrid PET/MR scanner, including high-resolution T1-weighted MRI, diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), and positron emission tomography with [¹⁸F]fluorodeoxyglucose  ([¹⁸F]FDG-PET).

T1-weighted images were segmented into gray matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid using SPM12, and the Automated Anatomical Labeling 2 (AAL2) atlas was nonlinearly transformed into individual T1 space using ANTs. FDG-PET images were co-registered to T1-weighted images, partial volume effects were corrected using the Müller-Gärtner method, and regional FDG uptake was quantified within AAL2 ROIs and normalized to cerebellar uptake (SUVR).

Structural connectivity was reconstructed using FSL probabilistic tractography, with DWI data co-registered to T1 space. The AAL2 atlas was transformed into diffusion space as seed and target masks. Streamline counts between ROI pairs were normalized by the gray–white matter interface surface area, with connections retained in at least 75% of subjects. Frontal-striatal and occipital-striatal connectivity (FS and OS) were extracted.

Group differences in SUVR and connectivity metrics were assessed using Mann–Whitney U tests. Linear mixed-effects models tested associations between striatal SUVR and FS or OS connectivity, including diagnostic group and their interaction, with random intercepts for participant and striatal ROI.

Results

Frontal and striatal SUVR were markedly reduced in bvFTD relative to HS, by 28% and 20%, respectively (both p<0.001; Figure 1A), whereas occipital SUVR as reference did not differ between groups (p=0.957). ROI-level analyses confirmed significant hypometabolism in the caudate and putamen bilaterally (all p≤ 0.001).

FS integrity was 34% lower in bvFTD than in HS (p<0.001; Figure 1B), while OS integrity as reference did not differ (p=0.592). ROI-wise SC reductions were restricted to FS-SC, showing significantly reduced connectivity from frontal lobe to the left caudate (p<0.001), right caudate (p=0.004), left putamen (p=0.015), and right putamen (p=0.011). No significant group differences emerged for any ROI in OS-SC.

Linear mixed-effects modelling revealed a significant FS-SC × group interaction (p=0.002), indicating group-specific coupling between FS integrity and striatal hypometabolism. In bvFTD, higher FS integrity was associated with higher striatal SUVR (p=0.023; Figure 1C). In contrast, no association was found in HS (p=0.132). No significant effects were observed in the OS model: the OS-SC × group interaction was not significant (p=0.835), and no within-group associations were observed for either bvFTD or HS.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that striatal degeneration in mild bvFTD is not an isolated event but is significantly associated with the loss of structural connectivity from the frontal cortex. The specificity of this relationship—absent in healthy controls and control tracts—supports the hypothesis of anterograde transneuronal spreading of pathology as a key mechanism in bvFTD pathophysiology and suggests that interventions targeting fronto-striatal circuit integrity may help preserve striatal function and modify disease progression.
