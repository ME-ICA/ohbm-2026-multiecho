Introduction

Schizophrenia (SZ) is a complex psychiatric disorder accompanying positive, negative, and cognitive symptoms. It is recognized as a brain network disorder characterized by dysfunction across distributed brain regions and demonstrates degenerative changes as the disease progresses. The "disconnection hypothesis" has been proposed to explain SZ’s multidimensional pathological mechanisms, supported by abnormally increases or decreases in both structural and functional connectivity1. Recently, the analysis of traveling waves has emerged as a method to interpret brain activity through dynamic propagation rather than static states. It shows that the phase of infra-slow BOLD activity is correlated to functional hierarchical organization. Also, the peak amplitude of infra-slow BOLD activity is propagating from unimodal to transmodal networks. A study suggests that the propagation patterns observed in resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) offer a substantially broadened framework for investigating neural communications in human pathology2. However, most traveling wave studies in fMRI have investigated brain-wide global propagation patterns. Understanding not only global propagation traits but also local distortions in the flow of waves will be crucial for elucidating the altered integration and segregation of brain connectivity in SZ. This study aims to quantify global and local propagation properties and investigate their alterations in SZ.

Methods

We analyzed an open dataset consisting of rs-fMRI data in Healthy Controls (HC, n = 74, age 35.43 ± 11.14) and patients with Schizophrenia (SZ, n = 73, age 38.92 ± 14.05) 3. Clinical measures included PANSS scores and illness duration. Participants underwent approximately 5 minutes of rs-fMRI scanning (eye-opened). Preprocessing included the removal of the first 5 volumes, motion rejection (excluding 4 HC and 20 SZ participants due to excessive head motion), slice timing correction, and registration to the Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) template. Mean BOLD time series were extracted in individual parcels determined by the Schaefer 400-parcel atlas and band-pass filtered to infra-slow frequency band (0.01–0.1 Hz). To quantify traveling waves in each parcel, we calculated the local phase gradient (LPG)4. The LPG is a vector quantifying spatial propagation of traveling waves by estimating phase differences between adjacent neighbors across three orthogonal axes (left-right, anterior-posterior, superior-inferior). The vector length indicates the strength of local propagation, while the direction represents the wave propagation direction. For a global analysis, representative LPG patterns (cluster centroids) were identified via k-means clustering on the HC group, with the optimal number of clusters (k) determined by the silhouette criterion. Then, SZ group data were assigned to these clusters. We calculated dwell time, occurrence, and coverage of each cluster and compared them between groups using rank-sum tests. For a local analysis, we determined the distribution of LPG was significant different between groups at each parcel. Because LPGs are 3-dimension vector-valued, we first transformed them into scalar quantities to allow nonparametric statistical test by projecting each individual LPG vector onto group-level reference vectors: HC median LPG and SZ median LPG. We computed the projection of HC LPG and SZ LPG onto the HC median LPG and compared the two projected LPG distribution between group using statistical test (yielding p1). Similarly, we repeated the same procedure using the SZ median LPG (yielding p2). To verify the test, we selected the smaller p-value between p1 and p2. To decouple the effects of magnitude and orientation, we analyzed the normalized LPG vector (direction only) and the vector length separately. After every statistical test at the parcel level, a False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction was applied to correct p-values for multiple comparisons. Associations between significant LPG measures and clinical variables were assessed using Spearman’s correlation.

Results

There was no significant difference in age between the HC and SZ groups (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p = 0.2188). In the global analysis, two clusters were identified. Visual inspection indicated distinct propagation directions within the parietal regions between the clusters. However, no significant group differences were observed in dwell time, occurrence, or coverage of each cluster.

In contrast, the local analysis revealed significant group differences in local propagation. Specifically, the Left Occipital Superior Gyrus, Right Frontal Inferior Gyrus, and Left Fusiform Gyrus showed significant differences in SZ. The length and direction of the LPG vectors were analyzed separately to assess which component contributed more substantially to the observed effects. It revealed no significant differences in vector length; rather, the differences were driven by the LPG direction. Significant directional deviations between groups were found in the three aforementioned regions, as well as the Left Temporal Inferior Gyrus. Finally, we examined the relationship between LPG directionality and clinical measures. A significant correlation was observed between the propagation direction in the Left Fusiform Gyrus and the negative symptom score (Emotional Withdrawal) (Spearman’s correlation, p = 0.0056).

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that local propagation direction, rather than global patterns captures schizophrenia-specific dynamics in rs-fMRI. The observation of altered directionality despite preserved LPG length suggests that a reorganization of neural information propagation trajectories rather than a loss of local coherence may be implicated in SZ. We identified altered wave propagation directions in temporal, occipital, and frontal regions in patients with SZ. Crucially, the correlation between fusiform gyrus directionality and emotional withdrawal offers a mechanistic link between aberrant local wave dynamics and negative symptoms, potentially reflecting deficits in social emotional perceptual processing5. These findings highlight the utility of local phase gradient analysis in uncovering the distorted spatiotemporal neural information dynamics in schizophrenia.
