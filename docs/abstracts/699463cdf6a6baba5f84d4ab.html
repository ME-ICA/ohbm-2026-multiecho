Introduction

The brain's functional organization dynamically reconfigures to adapt to environmental demands, shifting from intrinsic resting-state patterns to task-evoked configurations. While resting-state dynamics are well-characterized, how these dynamics transition during naturalistic stimuli—specifically food-related cues—remains less understood. This is particularly relevant given the complex interplay between sensory processing, reward evaluation, and cognitive control involved in appetite regulation. Previous studies often analyze resting-state and task data separately, limiting our understanding of the shared and distinct neural mechanisms across these modalities.

In this study, we aimed to bridge this gap by employing a unified analytical framework to integrate resting-state and naturalistic viewing fMRI data. We utilized Hidden Markov Models (HMM) to identify discrete brain states and map their dynamic transitions within a functional connectivity gradient space. Specifically, we investigated how brain state fractional occupancy (FO) and transition probabilities (TP) differ between resting state, low-appetite, and high-appetite naturalistic viewing conditions. We hypothesized that high-appetite stimuli would trigger specific state reconfigurations characterized by increased engagement of reward and control networks, distinct from the visual-dominant patterns observed in low-appetite conditions.

Methods

Participants and Data Acquisition:

We analyzed data from 58 healthy young female participants. MRI data were acquired using a 3T scanner. Each participant underwent an 8-minute resting-state scan (eyes open, fixation) and a 12-minute naturalistic viewing task (watching food-related videos). T1-weighted structural images and T2*-weighted EPI functional images (TR=1000ms, multi-band factor=4) were collected.

Preprocessing:

Data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep, including motion correction, co-registration, normalization to MNI space, and denoising via aCompCor. Linear detrending and band-pass filtering (0.008-0.09Hz) were applied.

HMM Analysis:

To create a unified state space, resting-state and task fMRI time series were concatenated. An HMM was trained on this aggregated dataset to identify discrete brain states. We evaluated model stability using split-half reliability and information criteria (zRand, Activity CI, NMI), determining K=7 as the optimal number of states.

Gradient Mapping:

We computed functional connectivity gradients using the BrainSpace toolbox. Brain states were projected onto the first two principal gradients: the primary gradient (unimodal-to-transmodal) and the secondary gradient (sensorimotor-to-visual).

Statistical Analysis:

We calculated the Fractional Occupancy (FO) and Transition Probability (TP) for each state. Paired sample t-tests were used to compare dynamic features across three conditions: Resting State, Low-Appetite viewing, and High-Appetite viewing. Results were corrected for multiple comparisons using FDR (p<0.05).

Results

The HMM analysis identified seven distinct brain states that mapped systematically onto the functional gradient space, comprising transmodal (State 1-DMN, State 2-FPN), attentional (State 3-DAN), and unimodal (State 4-Visual, State 7-Sensorimotor) networks, alongside global synchronization states (States 5/6). Significant dynamic reconfigurations were observed across conditions (p<0.05, FDR corrected). While resting-state dynamics exhibited a relatively balanced distribution across the gradient space, naturalistic viewing (both Low-Appetite [LA] and High-Appetite [HA]) shifted neural activity toward external processing, characterized by increased fractional occupancy (FO) of attentional and visual states (States 3 and 4) and suppression of the DMN. Crucially, stimulus content further modulated these dynamics: compared to the LA condition, which was dominated by basic visual state occupancy and transitions toward the visual network, the HA condition elicited a shift toward higher-order cognitive processing. Specifically, HA viewing significantly increased the FO of the DMN, FPN, and DAN. Temporal trajectory analysis revealed that HA viewing engaged a specific cognitive loop (DMN → FPN → DAN) and induced higher persistence (self-transition) of the DMN, suggesting sustained engagement of reward and self-referential processing during exposure to high-calorie food cues.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that naturalistic food viewing induces a dynamic reorganization of brain states that can be effectively mapped within a unified gradient framework. We found that while visual stimulation generally shifts dynamics toward sensory networks, high-appetite cues specifically recruit a "Reward-Control-Attention" loop. The increased persistence of the DMN and the specific transition trajectory from DMN to executive and attention networks during high-appetite viewing suggest a shift from bottom-up sensory processing to top-down cognitive evaluation and regulation. These findings provide new insights into the neural mechanisms underlying naturalistic appetite processing and highlight the utility of multimodal HMM in characterizing context-dependent brain dynamics.
