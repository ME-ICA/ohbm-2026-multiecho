Introduction

Temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE), the most common pharmaco-resistant epilepsy in adults, is associated with structural alterations beyond the primary mesiotemporal substrate. Although TLE is traditionally not considered a neurodegenerative disorder, emerging evidence from ex vivo specimens has shown elevated levels of misfolded tau protein (1), a hallmark of neurodegeneration (2). However, whole-brain patterns of increased phosphorylated tau in epilepsy remain unknown due to the limited scope of surgical resections. Moreover, while ex vivo tau increases in TLE have been linked to postoperative cognitive decline (3), their relationship with preoperative cognitive function remains incompletely understood. Together, these observations underscore the need for in vivo analyses of tau in TLE to evaluate its potential role as both a modulator of cognitive function and a predictor of clinical outcomes. In this study we used the [18F]-MK-6240 tracer (hereafter MK-6240), a second-generation PET tracers sensitive and specific to pathological tau deposits,  with high affinity and selectivity for intracellular neurofibrillary tangles (4). Our objective was to assess the in-vivo distribution and prevalence of tau aggregates in TLE using MK-6240 PET. Tau mapping was complemented with network decoding models informed by structural and functional MRI connectomes in the same participants. These approaches were used to relate the regional Tau uptake to brain network organization and cognitive performance.

Methods

Participants. 
We recruited 28 patients with TLE (14 females; mean ± SD age, 33 ± 8 years) and 28 healthy volunteers with similar age and sex distributions (14 females; mean ± SD age, 37 ± 12 years), of whom 7 patients and 13 controls had longitudinal follow-up.The study was approved by the Montreal Neurological Institute and Hospital Ethics Committee, and all participants provided written informed consent.

Cognitive assessments.
Evaluation included the EpiTrack, a brief 15-minute screening tool designed to detect and monitor cognitive side effects of antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) and the adverse effects of seizures in patients with epilepsy as well as the Episodic and semantic memory using standardized tasks.

PET processing.
PET data were co-registered via edge registration to a T1-weighted 3T MRI, where they were partial-volume corrected, normalized for cerebellar grey matter uptake to obtain a standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR), and mapped to cortical surfaces (Figure 1). 

MRI processing.
MRI data were acquired on a 3T Siemens Prisma scanner using T1-weighted structural imaging, multi-shell diffusion-weighted imaging, and resting-state fMRI. Multimodal MRI processing and feature integration were performed using a unified pipeline (micapipe v0.2.3) to derive fsLR-5k subject-level structural and functional connectivity matrices. In summary, structural images were processed for tissue segmentation and cortical surface reconstruction with Fresurfer and manual quality control. Diffusion MRI data underwent motion and distortion correction and were used to generate whole-brain structural connectomes via anatomically constrained tractography, incorporating both streamline counts and edge lengths to derive weighted structural connectivity measures. Resting-state fMRI data were preprocessed to correct for motion and distortions, denoised, and mapped to the cortical surface. Functional connectomes were constructed by computing pairwise Pearson correlations between vertex-wise time series (fsLR-5k), followed by Fisher r-to-z transformation.

Analyses. 

Global and regional SUVR distributions were examined at the individual level, and abnormal uptake was defined as values exceeding the 97.5th percentile of the control group. Group differences between patients and controls were assessed using mixed-effects models adjusting for age and sex, with correction for multiple comparisons using random field theory (pval=0.025). Group-by-sex interactions were also tested. Regional SUVR values from significant clusters were summarized and compared between groups using Welch’s t-tests. Longitudinal changes in tau uptake were evaluated in participants with repeated PET scans. Associations between tau uptake and clinical or behavioral measures were assessed using Pearson correlations. Spatial relationships between group-level tau differences and brain connectivity were examined by correlating tau T-maps with neighborhood-weighted functional and structural connectivity, the latter also referred to as Neighborhood Deformation Estimates. These metrics were used as dependent variables in group-level correlation analyses, contextualizing regional differences in MK-6240 uptake (T-statistical map) within the broader network architecture. Finally, structural equation modeling was used to examine relationships between tau burden, structural network measures, and behavioral outcomes, with indirect effects tested using bootstrap procedures.

Results

In both the individualized MK-6240 SUVR histogram analysis across the cortical surface (Figure 2A) and the surface-wide MK-6240 SUVR analysis (Figure 2B), we found that, compared with controls, patients exhibited increased MK-6240 uptake in bilateral superior and medial temporal as well as parietal regions. We next evaluated the effects of sex on MK-6240 uptake to investigate potential sex-related differences in tau pathology. Females with TLE showed a higher tau burden than their male counterparts (Figure 2C). This effect was similar between groups when examining mean MK-6240 SUVR within regions of significance from the main analysis (Figure 2D).

Longitudinal data were available for a subset of the cohort (10 patients and 7 controls) with a minimum follow-up of 12 months (median = 15 months, range = 12–36 months). Although patients with TLE showed a numerically greater annual increase in MK-6240 SUVR (0.0184 SUVR/year) compared with controls (0.0161 SUVR/year), this difference did not reach statistical significance, and no significant longitudinal changes were observed over the evaluation period (Figure 2E).

No significant correlations were observed between mean MK-6240 SUVR and age or disease duration (r = 0.03/0.07, p = 0.80/0.72, respectively; Figure 2F). In contrast, smaller hippocampal volume was significantly associated with higher MK-6240 SUVR in patients (r = −0.29, p = 0.03).

Cognitive performance measures revealed distinct associations with mean significant MK-6240 SUVR. Lower EpiTrack scores, reflecting impaired executive function, were associated with higher MK-6240 SUVR (r = −0.30, p = 0.03). Similarly, poorer episodic memory performance was associated with higher MK-6240 SUVR (r = −0.31, p = 0.03), whereas semantic memory performance was not associated with MK-6240 SUVR (r = −0.08, p = 0.61).

Association with network architecture. We assessed the susceptibility of highly interconnected brain regions to tau accumulation using network decoding models (Figure 2G). This analysis revealed a modest association between MK-6240 SUVR and neighborhood-weighted functional connectivity (r = 0.51, p_spin = 0.02), and a stronger association with neighborhood-weighted structural connectivity (r = 0.52, p_spin < 0.001).

Structural equation modeling. In the SEM analysis (Figure 2H), mean significant MK-6240 was significantly associated with semantic neighbors (β = 0.38, p = 0.003), but not with semantic efficiency. Higher MK-6240 was directly associated with lower EpiTrack performance (β = −0.49, p = 0.009) and lower episodic memory performance (β = −0.52, p = 0.011), but not semantic memory. Paths from semantic network measures to memory outcomes were not significant, and none of the indirect (mediation) effects reached significance. The total effect of MK-6240 on episodic memory was significant, indicating a predominantly direct relationship rather than mediation.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates increased in vivo tau accumulation in patients with TLE, predominantly affecting bilateral temporo-posterior regions, and links this pathology to brain network architecture and cognitive dysfunction. Elevated MK-6240 uptake was associated with altered functional and structural connectivity, indicating that highly interconnected network hubs and their neighboring regions are particularly vulnerable to tau deposition. Notably, females with TLE exhibited a higher tau burden than their male counterparts, suggesting potential sex-related differences in tau pathology. Cognitive analyses showed that higher tau burden was directly associated with poorer episodic memory performance. Mediation analyses did not support indirect effects of structural network measures on cognitive outcomes, indicating that the relationship between tau burden and memory impairment was predominantly direct rather than mediated by network properties. Although longitudinal changes were modest, these findings suggest that tau pathology may contribute to disease-related network disruption and cognitive impairment in drug-resistant TLE. Larger studies with extended follow-up are needed to clarify the temporal dynamics of tau accumulation and its potential role in neurodegenerative cascades associated with epilepsy.
