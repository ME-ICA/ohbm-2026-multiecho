Introduction

Schizophrenia (SCZ), a chronic and disabling psychiatric disorder, is characterized by profound disturbances in thought, perception, emotion, and behavior (Thaker & Carpenter, 2001). Treatment response to antipsychotic drugs (APDs) in schizophrenia is highly variable, necessitating predictive biomarkers for personalized treatment (Tarcijonas & Sarpal, 2019). While neuroimaging-based predictive models (NPMs) offer promise, their reliance on correlation-based methods renders them vulnerable to influence of confounders (Feuerriegel et al., 2024; Krishnadas, Leighton, & Jones, 2025). Advancing beyond simple correlational approaches to establish a biologically causality-informed predictive framework is imperative for precision psychiatry of schizophrenia treatment.

Methods

Here we propose CINP, a causality-informed neuroimaging prediction framework that incorporates Mendelian randomization (MR)-derived causal effects as biological priors into model inference (Figure 1). This framework aims to advance beyond simple correlational approaches to establish a biologically constrained neuroimaging prediction. To test feasibility, we recruited patients with schizophrenia and collected multimodal magnetic resonance imaging data from two independent longitudinal datasets (PKU6: n = 37; ZMD: n = 58). Antipsychotic treatment responses were quantified using the percentage reduction of positive and negative syndrome scale (PANSS) at follow-up relative to baseline. CINP were trained to predict individualized PANSS reduction using neuroimaging data. Model accuracy was evaluated using internal cross-validation, and generalizability by inter-site cross-validation.

Results

The results showed that the CINP achieved statistically significant predictions in all datasets (Figure 2a), with significant prediction accuracies (PKU6: r = 0.600 [0.342 to 0.774], pperm < 0.001; ZMD: r = 0.692 [0.528 to 0.806], pperm < 0.001) and cross-site generalizability (PKU6: r = 0.575 [0.308 to 0.758], pperm < 0.001; ZMD: r = 0.402 [0.160 to 0.598], pperm = 0.002). In addition, permutation tests with 10,000 randomized penalty factors confirmed that the improvement from informed causality was significantly above chance (Predictive accuracy: PKU6: pcausality < 0.001; ZMD: pcausality < 0.001; Predictive generalizability: PKU6: pcausality = 0.001; ZMD: pcausality = 0.011). We further benchmarked the CINP against conventional neuroimaging models for antipsychotic treatment response, namely NPMs without MR-derived causal priors and connectome predictive model (CPMs), across multiple neuroimaging modalities. As shown in Figure 2b, the CINP consistently achieved higher predictive accuracy in the training set in both PKU6 and ZMD datasets, with accuracies (correlation coefficients) ranging from approximately 0.5 to 0.7 (all pperm < 0.05). Notably, the white matter-based CINP achieved the highest performance across both datasets, with cross-site mean accuracy approaching 0.704. In contrast, conventional NPMs and CPMs demonstrated markedly lower accuracy, with functional CPMs showing the weakest results. CINP also demonstrated superior generalizability in hold-out validation, whereas most conventional models either failed to generalize or produced near-zero to negative correlations. Furthermore, the predictive features’ weight was highly consistent across datasets (similarity = 0.397 to 0.510), indicating replicable patterns.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we proposed a CINP to integrate mechanistically biological process into neuroimaging-based prediction model. Beyond the feasibility and validity in antipsychotic response prediction, CINP provides a flexible computational framework to incorporate diverse forms of causal constraints enable robust neuroimaging-based predictions in multiple clinical contexts.
