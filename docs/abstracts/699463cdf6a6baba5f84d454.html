Introduction

Systematic literature reviews and meta-analyses are foundational to cumulative science in neuroimaging, yet they remain labor-intensive, slow, and difficult to reproduce. Core steps such as literature search and abstract and full-text screening often require months of expert effort and are increasingly difficult to scale as the neuroimaging literature continues to grow. Our team previously introduced Neurosynth Compose, a researcher-in-the-loop platform that supports PRISMA-guided study screening and semi-automated data extraction (Kent et al., 2025). While Neurosynth Compose streamlines several downstream steps through a user-friendly web-based interface designed for researcher-in-the-loop curation, it still relies on careful manual selection of studies and expert judgment throughout the PRISMA workflow. Moreover, although heuristic-based extraction techniques reduce the burden of manual data entry, substantial effort remains to parse reported activation coordinates into independent analytic units (e.g., distinct contrasts) and to select those most relevant to a given research question.

Recent advances in large language model (LLM) agentic systems enable automated reasoning over unstructured scientific text, offering the opportunity to substantially reduce manual effort across the entire evidence-synthesis lifecycle. Here, we introduce AutoNIMA (Automated Neuroimaging Meta-Analysis), an LLM-powered framework that implements a complete, end-to-end systematic review and meta-analysis pipeline for neuroimaging studies in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. We also introduce neurometabench, a structured benchmarking dataset of curated neuroimaging meta-analyses designed to evaluate automated meta-analyses against expert-derived reference standards. Using neurometabench, we evaluate AutoNIMA’s ability to reproduce both study inclusion decisions and downstream meta-analytic brain maps from a published social processing meta-analysis.

Methods

AutoNIMA implements a complete evidence-synthesis pipeline, beginning with automated literature search and ending with a finalized meta-analytic dataset compatible with NiMARE. The framework employs specialized large language model (LLM) agents to reason and make structured decisions at each stage of the workflow. Users specify a machine-readable review protocol (e.g., study objective, search parameters, inclusion and exclusion criteria), which AutoNIMA uses to query PubMed, retrieve abstracts, and perform LLM-based abstract screening. Candidate studies are then retrieved at the full-text level using multiple sources—including PubMed Central and user-defined HTML sources—and subjected to full-text screening against user-defined criteria. AutoNIMA is LLM-agnostic by design and supports a wide range of models through both the OpenAI API and OpenRouter, allowing the same workflow to be executed with alternative commercial or open-weight LLMs without changes to the pipeline logic. While results are reported using GPT-5-mini, AutoNIMA is LLM-agnostic by design, enabling systematic benchmarking of alternative language models within a fixed and reproducible workflow.

From the final set of included studies, AutoNIMA applies LLM-based data extraction to identify and parse neuroimaging activation coordinates, organizing them into distinct contrasts or analyses. Users can further define annotation rules to select analyses that meet specific inclusion criteria, yielding a StudySet which can be executed using automated meta-analytic pipelines using a standard algorithms (e.g., MKDA, ALE) and automated result reporting with interactive HTML outputs.

To benchmark performance, we developed neurometabench, a curated dataset of neuroimaging meta-analyses containing bibliographic metadata and original coordinate inputs for evaluating automated workflows. In the present study, we focused on a single social processing meta-analysis comprising five domain-specific analyses aligned with RDoC constructs (Lobo et al., 2023). We replicated these analyses by submitting the original coordinate data to NiMARE using MKDA, to serve as a gold-standard benchmark for evaluating AutoNIMA’s performance. Nurometabench is publicly available on GitHub as an open-access resource and is designed to support community contributions, with the goal of serving as a shared benchmark for evaluating the accuracy of automated neuroimaging meta-analysis pipelines.

Results

At the full-text screening stage, AutoNIMA achieved high recall in reproducing the original inclusion decisions (recall = 0.99), correctly identifying 307 included studies with only two false negatives. Precision was high (precision = 0.89), indicating strong agreement with expert inclusion decisions while maintaining a conservative bias toward recall. When propagated through the full pipeline, AutoNIMA-generated datasets produced meta-analytic brain maps that closely resembled gold-standard results. For the primary social processing analysis, the Dice coefficient between AutoNIMA-derived and original MKDA maps was 0.72, with domain-specific sub-analyses yielding Dice coefficients ranging from 0.40 to 0.66. These findings indicate that AutoNIMA recovers spatially meaningful and interpretable meta-analytic patterns that closely approximate expert-derived results, while also highlighting opportunities for further improvement in replicating nuanced, expert-driven selection of analyses for more specialized or niche research questions.

Conclusion

AutoNIMA demonstrates that general-purpose LLMs can automate key stages of systematic neuroimaging meta-analysis with high recall and strong correspondence to expert-derived results. By integrating automated search, screening, full-text eligibility assessment, and standardized data outputs within a single framework, AutoNIMA substantially reduces the manual burden of evidence synthesis while preserving transparency and reproducibility. Ongoing expansion of neurometabench will enable more comprehensive evaluation across diverse meta-analytic domains. AutoNIMA represents a step toward scalable, transparent, and continuously updatable neuroimaging meta-analysis within the Neurosynth and NiMARE ecosystems.
