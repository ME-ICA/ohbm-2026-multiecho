Introduction

Depression affects an estimated one in five pregnant individuals and is associated with changes in offspring neurodevelopment. Previous studies have found links between exposure to prenatal maternal depressive symptoms and offspring brain development across different magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) modalities, including brain morphology1 and white matter integrity2. Multimodal imaging approaches refer to the combination of different types of imaging (such as diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) and structural MRI scans) to investigate physiological processes. Integration of multiple modalities could detect differences in brain functioning that might be overlooked when relying on single imaging modality approaches.3 Multiset canonical correlation analysis with joint independent component analysis (mCCA + jICA) is a method for obtaining multimodal imaging components that allows flexible modality linkage based on different mixing matrices for each modality (mCCA) while maximising spatial independence of joint components (jICA).4 To our knowledge, no study has investigated the associations between exposure to prenatal maternal depressive symptoms and later offspring brain development using multimodal MRI data fusion approaches.

Methods

The study population consisted of mothers and children from the Generation R Study, a population-based cohort in Rotterdam, with a total of 9778 enrolled women at baseline. Prenatal maternal depressive symptoms were measured at 20 weeks’ gestation using the Brief Symptom Inventory, a validated self-report questionnaire. Children were scanned for structural MRI (sMRI) and diffusion MRI (dMRI) scans at the age of 8 to 11 years (mean = 10.16, SD = 0.60). sMRI data were pre-processed using the SMRI-Prep software, which leverages ANTs for nonlinear registration (MNI152NLin2009cAsym), and FSL FAST for tissue segmentation. dMRI data were processed with a standard FSL pipeline which included brain extraction, “eddy”, and a weighted least squares tensor fit, followed by nonlinear registration (FA map to T1-weighted sMRI with ANTS, and concatenation with the sMRI-Prep nonlinear warps to MNI Space). MNI-Space grey matter (GM) probability maps (modulated by the Jacobian determinant) and fractional anisotropy (FA) maps were obtained. All scans were visually inspected for registration accuracy. A total of 2,663 participants were included with sufficient scan quality for both modalities. Covariates included maternal national origin, maternal age at intake, maternal education, marital status, maternal pre-pregnancy body mass index, prenatal maternal smoking and alcohol use, child’s assigned sex at birth, and child age at neuroimaging assessment. Missing data on exposure and covariates were imputed using expectation maximization. Unimodal associations of prenatal maternal depressive symptoms with offspring GM and FA individually were assessed in voxel-wise analyses using FSL’s randomise tool, adjusting for covariates. For the multimodal analyses, images were first residualised for the child’s assigned sex at birth, child age at scanning, and maternal national origin. Following this, a MATLAB-based pipeline for mCCA + jICA (Fusion ICA Toolbox) was performed to obtain joint multimodal components, using the minimum description length (MDL) criterion to set the optimal number of components. Component stability was measured by repeating the infomax algorithm 10 times in ICASSO. Linear regressions were then performed to test associations between prenatal depressive symptoms and the components. In order to ascertain the impact of important confounding factors, three levels of covariate adjustment were implemented. This included a basic model with no additional covariate adjustment (I), a model additionally correcting for maternal education level (II), and a model correcting for the full list of covariates (III). Visualization of these three levels of confounders allows for understanding to what extent observed correlation between multimodal components and exposure to prenatal maternal depressive symptoms is explained by external factors, such as maternal socioeconomic status and lifestyle. Lastly, moderation analyses were performed to test whether associations between prenatal depressive symptoms and multimodal components differed between children scoring below or above a clinical threshold of total problems from the child behaviour checklist (CBCL). Multiple testing correction (false discovery rate, FDR) was performed per modality, across all voxels and components. See Figure 1 for an overview of the pipeline.

Results

In the unimodal analyses, prenatal depressive symptoms were associated with increased FA in the right hemisphere, including the superior longitudinal fasciculus, inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, and corticospinal tracts (threshold‐free cluster enhancement-corrected p < .05, see Figure 2), but not with any GM differences after adjusting for covariates. In the multimodal analyses, the MCCA + jICA pipeline on the (child age- and sex-) residualised images resulted in 42 joint components. However, none of these joint multimodal components were associated with prenatal maternal depressive symptoms in any of the regression models (I, II, or III). In the moderation analyses, we found no significant interaction between prenatal depressive symptoms and CBCL group (i.e. children scoring above versus children scoring below the clinical threshold). 

Conclusion

This study employed both voxel-wise permutation testing and data-driven multimodal fusion. To our knowledge, this work presents the largest multimodal neuroimaging study using the MCCA + jICA pipeline to date. Findings show unimodal associations between prenatal maternal depressive symptoms and higher FA in the right hemisphere in offspring. Furthermore, we report 42 joint multimodal brain signatures in a population-based sample in middle childhood, but no evidence for associations of these components with exposure to maternal depressive symptoms. Future studies should replicate these findings in population-based samples as well as explore additional modalities in the multimodal fusion, such as functional MRI.
