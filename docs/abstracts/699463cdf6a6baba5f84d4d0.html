Introduction

Gliomas are the most prevalent and aggressive primary tumours of the central nervous system, and diagnosis, treatment planning, and long-term monitoring all depend on the precise identification of tumour subregions.^1 For glioma evaluation, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) offers rich multimodal information; yet, manual segmentation is still laborious, subjective, and heavily reliant on skilled neuroradiologists. These difficulties are made worse in low-resource environments, especially in Sub-Saharan Africa, where scanner heterogeneity, inconsistent protocols, and a shortage of specialists lead to delayed diagnosis and less than ideal clinical results. On carefully chosen international benchmarks, deep learning-based segmentation frameworks, most notably nnU-Net, have shown state-of-the-art performance. However, when used in actual African clinical settings, the majority of models' robustness and generalizability are limited since they are trained and assessed on comparatively homogeneous datasets obtained in high-income settings. Models that can maintain high segmentation fidelity under heterogeneous imaging settings without incurring unaffordable processing costs are needed to close this gap. ^2 An evaluation of an attention-enhanced nnU-Net architecture for multimodal glioma segmentation utilizing heterogeneous African MRI data of 58 scans from clinics within sub-Saharan Africa is presented and tested using BraTS-GLI 2024/2025 dataset in this study. The goal of the work is to determine if, when trained on a variety of real-world African neuroimaging data, attention-guided feature recalibration inside a self-configuring nnU-Net pipeline can achieve steady convergence and dependable performance.

Methods

Using the collected dataset, which includes clinically diagnosed low- and high-grade glioma cases obtained from African medical facilities, we performed a retrospective deep learning analysis. T1-weighted, contrast-enhanced T1-weighted (T1ce), T2-weighted, and FLAIR are the four MRI contrasts per subject in the dataset, which reflects significant inter-scanner and inter-site heterogeneity characteristic of standard clinical practice. The automated 2D planning technique was used to configure the nnU-Net v2 framework, which served as the foundation for model creation. LeakyReLU activations, instance normalization, and a six-stage encoder-decoder PlainConvUNet with feature widths ranging from 32 to 512 comprised the network architecture. Z-score normalization was applied separately to each modality, and the input patch size was set to 192 X 160 with a batch size of 105.  For model selection, the optimization pipeline used a combination of the Adam optimizer, Dice-based loss, and exponential moving average (EMA) weighting. Attention methods were incorporated into the nnU-Net architecture to improve robustness under heterogeneous imaging settings. This allowed for better spatial contextualization and channel-wise feature recalibration.  Five-fold cross-validation was used for training. Using cosine scheduling across epochs, learning rates were annealed from a starting value of 0.01. The self-configuring concept of nnU-Net was maintained by not adding any extra human preprocessing or post hoc architectural tuning. Validation loss and pseudo-Dice similarity coefficients, presented per tumour location, were used to track the model's performance during training and validation. Every experiment was conducted on CPU/GPU hardware that the nnU-Net framework supported, representing computational parameters that might be deployed.

Results

Across training epochs, the model showed rapid and steady convergence. From an initial mean of 0.72 at epoch 0 to 0.90 by epoch 18, validation pseudo-Dice scores steadily increased, demonstrating robust learning dynamics and successful generalization on unseen validation folds. In line with this, validation loss reduced monotonically during the course of training, indicating increased segmentation accuracy devoid of overfitting. Across tumour subregions, class-wise pseudo-Dice coefficients demonstrated strong performance, which supports previous findings. ^3 Later epochs saw more difficult subregions, like the tumour core and low-contrast areas, regularly reach about 0.94, whereas dominant tumour regions got pseudo-Dice scores above 0.95. These findings show consistent border delineation even in scans impacted by anatomical heterogeneity, noise, and contrast variations. Best-performing checkpoints improved gradually as a result of the exponential moving average (EMA) model selection technique, with EMA pseudo-Dice values rising progressively from 0.72 to over 0.90 across epochs. Training times per epoch showed computational efficiency comparable with near-real-time inference pipelines, ranging from approximately 515 to 540 seconds. Overall, despite greater variability in acquisition conditions, the results show that the attention-enhanced nnU-Net framework maintains high segmentation fidelity on heterogeneous African MRI data, achieving performance levels comparable to those reported on more homogeneous international benchmarks.

Conclusion

This study shows that when trained on diverse African MRI data, an attention-enhanced nnU-Net architecture can produce reliable, high-quality multimodal glioma segmentation. Despite significant inter-site and inter-scanner heterogeneity, the completed experiments demonstrate good generalization, stable convergence, and consistently high pseudo-Dice scores across cancer subregions. The suggested method strikes a balance between segmentation accuracy and computing efficiency by maintaining nnU-Net's self-configuring design while enhancing feature representation through attention mechanisms, enabling viability for deployment in clinical settings with limited resources. The results emphasize African neuroimaging data as a rigorous testbed for robustness and generalizability and stress the significance of testing neuroAI models on a variety of real-world datasets. By developing scalable, deployable AI methods for brain tumour mapping in situations with limited resources, this study advances equitable neuroimaging research. Future research will examine downstream clinical integration, such as radiation planning and long-term disease monitoring, and expand evaluation to additional test sets.
