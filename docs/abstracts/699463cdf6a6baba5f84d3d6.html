Introduction

Bipolar disorder (BD) is a severe and chronic psychiatric condition whose diagnosis currently relies on clinical criteria. However, the limited specificity and fluctuating nature of symptoms result in a high risk of misdiagnosis, often delaying appropriate treatment. To address these limitations, recent research has focused on identifying neurobiological biomarkers of BD. MRI modalities provide non-invasive access to brain structure and function, capturing patterns that may distinguish BD patients from healthy individuals. Machine learning approaches are well suited to detect such complex, multivariate signatures and may enhance diagnostic accuracy.

Here, we evaluated whether integrating multimodal MRI data with machine-learning methods improves the classification of BD versus healthy control (HC).

Methods

A total of 263 patients with bipolar disorder (BD) and 227 healthy controls (HC) from seven sites underwent T1-weighted structural MRI and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI). Gray-matter volumes (141 GMV ROIs) were extracted using the CAT12 toolbox, and fractional anisotropy values (48 FA ROIs) were derived using FSL-TBSS. Quality-control procedures were applied to exclude noisy or low-quality data.

To reduce inter-site variability, we evaluated two harmonization methods MULM and NeuroCombat separately for each modality. Both approaches effectively corrected site effects, and MULM was retained for subsequent modeling due to its lower computational cost.

Unimodal datasets (GMV-only, FA-only) and multimodal datasets (early fusion; late naïve Bayesian fusion) were used to classify BD versus HC participants. Two machine-learning algorithms, ElasticNet and Linear SVM, were trained using nested cross-validation with both 10-fold stratified splits and Leave-One-Site-Out (LOSO) evaluation. Model performance was assessed using Area Under the Curve (AUC) and balanced accuracy (B.Acc).

Results

The ElasticNet logistic regression yielded results comparable to those obtained with the Linear SVM. Unimodal models showed similar performance under stratified 10-fold validation (FA: AUC = 0.677, B.Acc = 0.634; GMV: AUC = 0.680, B.Acc = 0.631). However, differences between modalities became more pronounced under LOSO validation, with FA outperforming GMV (FA: AUC = 0.652, B.Acc = 0.573; GMV: AUC = 0.596, B.Acc = 0.573), suggesting higher robustness of diffusion-based features to site variability.

Combining GMV and FA significantly improved classification performance regardless of the fusion strategy. Late naïve Bayesian fusion achieved the best results in stratified 10-fold validation (Early fusion: AUC = 0.723, B.Acc = 0.660; Late fusion: AUC = 0.736, B.Acc = 0.658). This advantage remained consistent in the LOSO framework (Early fusion: AUC = 0.629, B.Acc = 0.550; Late fusion: AUC = 0.671, B.Acc = 0.602), indicating better cross-site generalization.

Overall, late Bayesian fusion appeared the most robust approach, demonstrating improved generalizability and reduced sensitivity to site effects compared with unimodal and early-fusion strategies.

Conclusion

Combining structural MRI (sMRI) and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) substantially improved

bipolar-versus-control classification compared with single-modality models. These results

support the value of multimodal MRI and appropriate fusion strategies for enhancing

machine-learning performance in imaging-based psychiatric classification. Future work will

extend this approach by integrating fMRI measures, which may further improve accuracy

and robustness.
