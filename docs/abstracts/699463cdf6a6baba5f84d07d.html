Introduction

Studies in the last decade have revealed a lymphatic system of the brain, the so-called glymphatic system where the movement of the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is critical for removing metabolic waste and maintaining the water homeostasis of the brain.1,2 A wealth of evidence has documented a decline in glymphatic flux and drainage in the aged brain, associating the onset of these diseases with aging-related impairments in glymphatic function.4 However, the developmental trajectory of glymphatic function across the human lifespan remains unknown. Furthermore, while resting-state BOLD-CSF coupling5 is increasingly used as a non-invasive proxy for glymphatic function, its reported associations with cognitive performance may be confounded by the global, non-linear effects of aging. We aimed to map the normative trajectories of glymphatic function and examine whether BOLD-CSF coupling predicts cognition independent of age.

Methods

Resting-state fMRI data in 2066 healthy individuals (ages 8–90) from the Human Connectome Project (Development, Young Adults, and Aging datasets) underwent a standard preprocessing pipeline for motion correction, skull stripping, spatial smoothing, temporal filtering and removal of linear and quadratic trends as well as frames with excessive movements. T1 structural images were segmented into different types, which were then used to create the global gray matter mask and bottom-slice CSF mask. Both regions of interest (ROIs) were delineated in the native functional space where average time series were extracted within each ROI. We then calculated BOLD-CSF coupling as the cross-correlation coefficients between mean ROI signals. Pearson’s r at the time lag where the two signals exhibit the strongest negative correlations was used to quantify BOLD-CSF coupling for each participant, with a more negative r indicating better clearance function. 

We then leveraged a data-driven GAMLSS (Generalized Additive Models for Location, Scale, and Shape)6 modelling approach to map the trajectories of BOLD-CSF coupling as a function of age, which allowed us to generate age- and sex-normalized Quantile Residuals (Z-scores) for every subject. To test the specificity of cognitive associations, we performed a two-stage correlation analysis across five domains (episodic memory (Picture Sequence Memory), executive function (Dimensional Change Card Sort, Flanker), processing speed (Pattern Comparison), working memory (List Sorting), and general cognitive composites (NIH Total Cognition)), comparing: (1) Raw coupling vs. Unadjusted cognitive scores, and (2) GAMLSS Z-scores vs. Age-adjusted cognitive scores.

Results

We revealed different glymphatic growth trajectories between early and late life stages and between males and females. There were no sex differences in signal variability or shape (homoscedasticity) but significant sex differences in life-course trajectories (Age×Sex interaction t = 3.08, p = 0.002). While males exhibited a stable trajectory across the lifespan with a later peak function (Nadir: 44.4 years), females showed an earlier peak (Nadir: 38.7 years) followed by a rapid, significant decline, diminishing the advantage they possessed in young adulthood (Figure 1).

Consistent with prior reports, we observed highly significant correlations between raw BOLD-CSF coupling and cognitive performance (unadjusted for age) across all domains. Stronger negative coupling (better clearance) was associated with better cognitive scores, with Pearson correlation coefficients (r) ranging from -0.11 to -0.14 (all uncorrected p < 1.0 × 10-6). However, associations became insignificant when correlating Glymphatic Z-score with age-adjusted cognitive scores (uncorrected p > 0.05 for all domains).

Conclusion

We provide the first normative reference charts for BOLD-CSF coupling, revealing distinct sexual dimorphism: females exhibit a rapid peak and decline pattern, whereas males show a stable maintenance pattern. Crucially, the complete dissociation of cognitive associations after age-normalization suggests that previously reported links between BOLD-CSF coupling and cognition in healthy cohorts are likely spurious correlations driven by chronological aging, rather than reflecting a specific mechanistic link between fluid dynamics and cognitive performance.
