Introduction

Resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) is an important imaging technique in the investigation of preclinical models of traumatic brain injury (TBI), as it can provide insights into injury-induced changes in functional brain networks. In preclinical studies, it is paramount to achieve high statistical power to minimize the number of subjects sacrificed while maintaining the ability to detect subtle neuroanatomical and functional changes. In this paper, we evaluate the efficacy of the Brainsync transform1 at enhancing sensitivity and effect size in a rodent TBI experiment using rs-fMRI.

Methods

We used previously collected rodent rs-fMRI data2 for analysis in the present study. In the previous study, 18 male Sprague-Dawley rats were randomized into controlled cortical impact CCI (N=9) and sham (N=9) groups. In the CCI group, a moderate TBI was induced using a pneumatic impactor (20 psi, 2 mm depth) over the left cortex. Longitudinal rs-fMRI scans were acquired at 7- and 28-days post-injury using a 7 Tesla Bruker scanner. Anatomical T2-weighted RARE and EPI rs-fMRI sequences (TR/TE=2000/30ms, 450 volumes) were collected. Data were processed using a Python-based pipeline integrating FSL, ANTs, and RATS. Images were registered to an 83-ROI atlas. 

We performed subsequent rs-fMRI analysis using three different methods to compare their efficacy in characterizing network disruptions. Specifically, we compared the proposed BrainSync-based technique against conventional Node Degree and Functional Connectivity metrics. The features were compared using the rank_sum test to identify regions of interest (ROIs) that were affected in the CCI cohort relative to the sham cohort at 7 days post-injury. Tests were conducted to identify longitudinal changes within the groups: identifying ROIs where the functional distance to the atlas decreased over time (distance at 7 days > distance at 28 days). FDR correction was then applied to find significant regions.

Results

Our analysis showed that the Brainsync-based technique yielded improved effect size and statistical power compared to both node degree and functional connectivity metrics. The Brainsync-based technique identified a greater number of statistically significant regions of interest (ROIs) in group studies compared to alternate methods.

Conclusion

These results suggest that applying the Brainsync transform during the analysis of rs-fMRI data can significantly improve the detection of trauma-related changes in brain connectivity compared to conventional analysis methods. By increasing sensitivity, this approach may allow for more robust findings in preclinical TBI research, potentially reducing the sample sizes required for future studies.
