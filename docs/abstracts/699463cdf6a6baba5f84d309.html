Introduction

Quantifying variation in brain organization and identifying individual-level deviations from typical age- and sex-related patterns across the human lifespan is fundamental to translational neuroscience. Accordingly, there is a need for robust normative models encompassing a range of measures that capture different aspects of brain structure, connectivity, and function.

Methods

We introduce the CentileBrain platform, an open-access resource (https://centilebrain.org/) developed by the ENIGMA Lifespan Working Group. The CentileBrain platform provides normative models of brain morphometry, functional connectivity, and white-matter integrity derived from over 60,000 healthy individuals across diverse geographical and ethnoracial groups. We will present details of the model development which incorporated benchmarking of analytical approaches, adjustment for relevant covariates, and evaluation of site effects, with external validation performed across multiple independent and demographically diverse cohorts to ensure robustness and generalizability.

Results

Sex-specific and race neutral normative models were generated for brain morphometry, functional connectivity, and white-matter integrity across more than 60,000 healthy individuals aged 3–90 years. Centile curves captured established developmental and aging trajectories for cortical thickness, subcortical volumes, network connectivity, and fractional anisotropy. Model performance was high and external validation in independent cohorts (n = 8,000) demonstrated consistent centile assignments and robust individual deviation metrics across geographically and ethnoracially diverse samples.

Conclusion

The CentileBrain platform provides a robust framework for quantifying individual differences in brain structure, connectivity, and white-matter integrity across the lifespan. By integrating data from tens of thousands of healthy individuals and rigorously validating models across diverse cohorts, it offers reliable normative benchmarks for brain health. These resources provided through this platform support cross-cohort comparisons, align with the principles of open and reproducible science, and facilitate the identification of biologically and clinically meaningful deviations from typical brain patterns.
