Introduction

Electroencephalography (EEG) is a valuable non-invasive technique for capturing neural activity, deriving processes underlying cognition and neurodevelopment. Spontaneous (i.e., resting-state) signals in the EEG power spectrum are composed of neural oscillations and a broadband non-oscillatory component, commonly referred to as aperiodic activity. Aperiodic activity has been characterized as a 1/f distribution, reflecting decreased power with increased frequency, and consists of two components: an exponent and offset. The aperiodic exponent models the inverse relationship between power and frequency as the slope of the linear fit in log-log space and has been found as a proxy for the excitation-inhibition (E/I) ratio, where increased slopes are associated with greater inhibition1. The aperiodic offset represents the broadband power across frequencies and is indicative of increased single-neuron spiking2.

Both aperiodic components decrease across the lifespan, but infancy represents a unique window: this is when E/I balance undergoes its most rapid reorganization, with changes occurring heterogeneously across regions as different functional systems come online. Crucially, atypical E/I trajectories during this period have been implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders, including autism and epilepsy, making early characterization of regional aperiodic patterns essential for identifying at-risk infants. Yet spatial organization of aperiodic activity during early infancy remains poorly characterized due to the scarcity of large, high-quality datasets. The Healthy Brain and Child Development (HBCD) study addresses this gap, offering EEG recordings from a large, diverse infant cohort. Here, we introduce a framework for mapping the spatial topography of aperiodic components and their regional trajectories. Applying this approach to HBCD data, we characterize the aperiodic exponent and offset across cortical regions in infants aged 3-9 months.

Methods

Data. We analyzed resting-state EEG (rsEEG) from 81 healthy infants aged 3–9 months from the HBCD study. Data were acquired using a 128-channel Geodesic Sensor Net (GSN-128) and preprocessed with the Maryland Analysis of Developmental EEG (MADE) pipeline, which removed the outermost electrode ring, yielding 105 channels for analysis.

Aperiodic Parameterization. Electrode-wise absolute power spectral density (PSD) was computed using HBCD EEG Utilities v1.0.2. Aperiodic activity was extracted by fitting oscillations and one-over-f (FOOOF) across 2–40 Hz. Due to reported instability in infants under 12 months3, we modified FOOOF parameters: enforcing a linear slope, expanding peak width limits, and constraining maximum peak number. This yielded aperiodic exponent (slope) and offset (intercept) coefficients per electrode. Mean and variance were computed across subjects for each electrode and visualized via topographic maps (Figure 1).

Regional Parcellation. To identify regions of interest (ROIs), 105-channel coordinates were scaled to MNI space and centered at Cz = (0,0,0). Each channel was mapped to the nearest 10-10 system electrode using 3D Euclidean distance. ROIs were defined following standard 10-10 nomenclature, organized by cortical lobes (frontal, central, temporal, parietal, occipital) and more precisely by the coronal lines they fall on4. Aperiodic exponent and offset were averaged across channels within each ROI to examine within- and between-region patterns.

Comparison with Cortical Structure. To relate EEG measures to underlying anatomy, we compared aperiodic parameters with cortical thickness (derived from T2-weighted MRI) and T2 relaxometry values (from quantitative MRI) acquired from the same subjects at an earlier timepoint (0–6 months).

Results

Spatial Topography. Topographic maps (Figure 1) revealed distinct spatial organization of aperiodic components, with posterior regions showing heterogeneous patterns compared to more uniform central and temporal areas. The aperiodic exponent and offset were strongly correlated (r = 0.834), indicating coordinated spatial variation. We observed significant associations between components within all non-lateral regions (r > 0.85), demonstrating heightened similarity within cortices, whereas the temporal lobe had a remarkably low correlation (r = −0.109).

Regional Differences. Regional analysis (Figure 2) revealed significant lobe-level differences. Frontal and temporal regions exhibited lower mean aperiodic exponent and offset compared to central, parietal, and occipital regions (p < 0.05), indicating reduced inhibition and lower neuronal spiking in anterior and lateral cortices. Occipital regions showed the highest aperiodic offset, consistent with greater neuronal activity in the early-maturing visual cortex. Parietal and occipital ROIs displayed the widest spread in mean values, reflecting within-lobe heterogeneity.

Regional Variability. Aperiodic exponent variance differed significantly across lobes, with frontal and temporal regions showing greater inter-subject variability than posterior regions (p < 0.05). Offset variance showed no significant regional differences. This pattern suggests that E/I balance is more variable in higher-order regions during this developmental window.

EEG-MRI Correspondence. Aperiodic exponent correlated strongly with T2 relaxometry values (r = −0.846), linking electrophysiological measures of E/I balance to underlying tissue properties related to myelination and water content5. The strongest associations resided in the parietal (r = −0.926) and occipital (r = −0.967) lobes, consistent with more rapid E/I stability in lower-order regions. Aperiodic offset correlated moderately with T2 relaxometry values (r = −0.635), with the most marked association in the occipital lobe (r = −0.873), revealing a notable relationship between neuronal-spiking and myelination. Additionally, the temporal lobe had a significant positive correlation (r = 0.526), contrary to the negative relationship in all other regions. Global correlations between aperiodic components and cortical thickness were less pronounced, with coefficients of 0.231 and −0.102 for the exponent and offset, respectively. However, both analyses demonstrated strong correlations among the frontal (exponent: r = 0.675, offset: r = 0.611) and central (exponent: r = 0.846, offset: r = 0.865) lobes.

Conclusion

We demonstrate regionally organized aperiodic activity in infant EEG, with spatial patterns reflecting the hierarchical structure of cortical maturation. Frontal and temporal regions, associated with higher-order functions, exhibited lower aperiodic exponent and offset values, indicating reduced inhibition and neuronal spiking compared to sensory regions. Conversely, the occipital cortex showed elevated aperiodic offset, consistent with early maturation of visual systems. The greater inter-subject variability in frontal and temporal exponent values may reflect the rapid, heterogeneous development of higher-order circuits during this period. The strong correspondence between aperiodic exponent and T2 values provides multimodal validation, linking scalp-level E/I dynamics to underlying tissue microstructure. Our framework enables non-invasive mapping of regional E/I organization in infants, establishing normative patterns from the HBCD cohort that may facilitate early identification of atypical trajectories associated with neurodevelopmental disorders.
