Introduction

Imbalance between excitatory (primarily glutamatergic) and inhibitory (primarily GABAergic) neural transmission is commonly implicated in the aetiology of psychiatric and cognitive dysfunction. 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (22q11DS) is a genetic disorder associated with an increased risk of psychosis and cognitive impairments, alongside a variety of other psychiatric disorders. Genetically mediated disruption of neural excitation-inhibition balance is thought to contribute to the psychiatric profile of 22q11DS 1. In healthy populations, excitation-inhibition imbalance appears to correlate with functional connectivity 2, including within the Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC). The ACC is at the heart of the Salience Network and is often included in the wider Default Mode Network (DMN). Dysconnectivity in both the DMN and ACC is a consistent feature of 22q11DS 3. Riluzole, an FDA-approved drug, appears to modulate glutamate and GABA levels in the ACC in 22q11DS 4, and has the potential to lessen functional connectivity abnormalities and psychiatric symptoms 5. This proof-of-concept study examined the relationship between excitation-inhibition ratio and functional connectivity in 22q11DS for the first time and explored how it may be modified with riluzole.

Methods

Eight participants were included in the study as part of a wider placebo-controlled, fixed-order crossover trial of the clinical efficacy of riluzole. Participants were aged between 16-44 years (mean = 27.62, SD = 9.88) with a molecular confirmed diagnosis of 22q11DS, and psychotic and/or cognitive symptoms. During the intervention periods, participants received 100mg/day of riluzole or a matched placebo capsule. 7-Tesla proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (1H-MRS) and resting-state functional MRI data were acquired after the placebo and riluzole intervention periods.

1H-MRS spectra were acquired from a manually placed voxel in the ACC using a STimulated Echo Acquisition Mode (STEAM) sequence with water suppression, optimised for quantification of glutamate. Figure 1A-D depicts 1H-MRS voxel placement and example spectra. Multi-band gradient-echo echo planar imaging (EPI) resting-state fMRI data were acquired at 1.5mm isotropic with a TR of 1700ms and 200 total volumes. Five volumes with opposite phase encoding (posterior > anterior) were acquired immediately after the initial anterior > posterior run.

STEAM data were analysed in LCModel, with water referencing and eddy current correction, using a GAMMA-simulated basis set, and corrected for cerebrospinal fluid fraction. Resting-state fMRI data were pre-processed using tools from the FMRIB Software Library (FSL). The denoised, distortion- and motion corrected, smoothed data were then transformed to 2mm MNI152 standard space, using tools in FSL and the Advanced Normalisation Toolkit (ANTs).

A functional seed within the boundaries of the ACC spectroscopy voxel was derived on a per-scan basis using single-subject FSL MELODIC decompositions in functional space. These were then transformed into MNI space and merged across the placebo and riluzole scans for each subject. A seed-based correlation analysis was performed in FEAT for all subjects using the mean timeseries of the composite ACC functional seed. A group seed-based correlation map was created from the contrast parameter estimates (COPEs) of each subject, separately for placebo and riluzole acquisitions, and significantly correlated regions were identified using FSL randomise with 2mm variance smoothing and threshold free cluster enhancement. The difference in contrast parameter estimates between the placebo and riluzole scans was compared against zero in a t-test, also in randomise.

Glutamate and GABA z-scores and the excitation-inhibition ratio in the placebo and riluzole phases were compared using paired-samples Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Analyses of the correlation of glutamate and GABA concentration with functional connectivity between the ACC seed and spherical regions of interest (Figure 1 E-F) within the salience and default mode networks were performed using a Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

Results

Riluzole administration was associated with a non-significant reduction in glutamate concentration and increase in GABA concentration, with no overall effect on excitation-inhibition imbalance (Figure 2A-C). Seed-based correlation analysis revealed an expected network of connected brain regions. Activity in the ACC seed region was significantly correlated with clusters that included the left insula (Placebo: peak t = 3.222, p = 0.012, MNI coordinates = -34 10 4; Riluzole: peak t = 2.797, p = 0.008, MNI coordinates = -36 16 -8) and right insula (Placebo: peak t = 3.487, p = 0.012, MNI coordinates = 36 6 6; Riluzole: peak t = 1.626, p = 0.008, MNI coordinates = 38 16 -8)  – the other primary nodes of the salience network – and areas considered to be part of the cortico-striatal-thalamic loop: the putamen, caudate, and thalamus. The seed region often encompassed a portion of the dorsomedial prefrontal cortex, due to the size of the 1H-MRS voxel, individual differences in brain anatomy, and variability in voxel placement. There was therefore significant functional connectivity with other nodes of the default mode network – the posterior cingulate cortex (Placebo: peak t = 4.042, p = 0.008, MNI coordinates = 0 -44 26; Riluzole: peak t = 4.107, p = 0.004, MNI coordinates = 0 -36 26) and precuneus (Placebo: peak t = 4.389, p = 0.008, MNI coordinates = 3 -62 40; Riluzole: peak t = 3.550, p = 0.004, MNI coordinates = 2 -60 38) – with activity in the left angular gyrus only significantly correlated in the riluzole phase (Riluzole: peak t = 1.958, p = 0.035, MNI coordinates = -58 -54 32). Significant clusters were also observed in the bilateral superior, middle and inferior frontal gyri and frontal pole. There was no significant change in connectivity between the ACC seed and any voxel after riluzole administration. There were no significant correlations between mean functional connectivity within the ROIs and 1H-MRS measurements in the placebo phase. In the riluzole phase, mean connectivity between the right insula and the ACC was significantly negatively correlated with glutamate (r = -0.833, p = 0.015) and GABA (r = -0.786, p = 0.048) z-scores (Figure 2D-E).

Conclusion

This study investigated the relationship between excitation-inhibition ratio and functional connectivity for the first time in 22q11DS. Although there was no significant effect of riluzole on the concentrations of glutamate and GABA, nor on the overall functional connectivity from the ACC, glutamate and GABA concentrations within the ACC after riluzole administration do appear to affect the functional connectivity of the salience network, particularly between the ACC and right insula. Given the crucial role of this network in models of psychosis, this suggests that there may be a connection between genetically-mediated glutamate and GABA imbalance in 22q11DS and predisposition to psychosis, potentially moderated by abnormalities in the salience network. Further, this relationship may be modifiable with riluzole. However, given the limited sample size and high levels of individual variability, these findings must be taken with caution. Future studies should aim to replicate these findings in a larger sample and attempt to stratify participants by baseline excitation-inhibition imbalance.
