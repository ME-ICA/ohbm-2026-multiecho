Introduction

Transcranial pulse stimulation (TPS) is a novel non-invasive brain stimulation technique that has shown promising neuromodulation and therapeutic effects in neurological and psychiatric disorders. Although it can non-invasively stimulate deeper brain regions (up to 80 cm in depth), no TPS studies have evaluated the feasibility and effectiveness of target-specific deep brain stimulation. Given the importance of the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) and anterior insula cortex (AIC) in various mental disorders, this study aims to investigate whether TPS of dACC and AIC can modulate target-related performance and related brain circuits in healthy participants.

Methods

In this randomized, single-blind, three-arm, parallel, sham-controlled trial, 60 healthy participants were randomly assigned to receive either active TPS targeting dACC, active TPS targeting AIC, or sham TPS at the vertex for 10 daily sessions over 2 weeks. Each session consisted of 1000 TPS pulses delivered at 4 Hz with an energy flux density of 0.2 mJ/mm2. Behavioral outcomes were changes in task performance after the 2-week stimulation period, measured using the counting Stroop task, the Go/No-go task (GNGT), the balloon analog risk task (BART), and the reading the mind in the eyes test (RMET). Neuroimaging outcomes were the TPS-induced changes in resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) of the stimulation targets, measured using resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI).

Results

A generalized linear mixed model revealed a significant interaction effect (AIC vs. sham x pre-TPS vs. post-TPS) on the adjusted average number of pumps (p = 0.026) in the BART following AIC stimulation. Post-hoc analysis indicated that the AIC group demonstrated a higher adjusted average number of pumps at Week 2 (pcorrected = 0.006), but no changes were observed in the dACC or sham groups. No other significant interaction effects were found. Within-group comparisons showed that the number of exploded balloons decreased significantly following dACC stimulation (pcorrected = 0.019), but not after AIC or sham stimulation. Target-specific TPS-induced rsFC changes were observed following both dACC and AIC stimulation compared to sham. dACC stimulation enhanced the rsFC of dACC with the bilateral ventrolateral prefrontal, right dorsolateral prefrontal, and right dorsomedial prefrontal cortices. In contrast, AIC stimulation resulted in decreased rsFC of AIC with the right middle and inferior frontal (opercular and triangular parts), right precentral gyrus, right supramarginal, right posterior superior temporal, the left posterior middle temporal, left superior and middle occipital cortices, left middle cingulate cortex, and left cuneus.

Conclusion

TPS application to dACC and AIC over two weeks is feasible and safe. Preliminary results suggest that dACC-TPS decreases impulsivity, whereas AIC-TPS increases impulsivity. Target-specific TPS-induced changes in rsFC were observed following stimulation of both the dACC and AIC. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to confirm the short- and long-term efficacy of dACC-TPS and AIC-TPS, and to elucidate the underlying neural mechanisms using online neuroimaging or neurophysiological investigations.
