Introduction

Diverse datasets sampled from multiple imaging sites contribute to the validity and reproducibility of Neuroimaging studies. The integration of data from varied sources necessitates site harmonisation as an additional preprocessing step to avoid imaging artefacts from scanners, protocols and sampling bias. Functional Connectivity (FC) – derived from resting state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) – compresses the time dimension of brain signals in selected ROIs into a connectivity matrix with neat algebraic properties. Manifold geometry, especially the Riemannian affine-invariant metric of Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD) matrices, offers a rich toolset for the analysis of relationships between FC matrices. Furthermore, interpreting FC as an adjacency matrix of dense graph-like structures, allows us to apply methods from signal theory, such as the Graph Fourier Transform (GFT). GFT factorises the graph Laplacian to obtain orthonormal modes, ranging from localised neural activity to brain-wide patterns. GFT and SPD geometry reveal latent clustering among the multi-site datasets, but also form a mathematical foundation for the subsequent harmonisation of FC from different datasets into a coherent whole.

Methods

The data used include 805 participants from the SRPBS dataset and 3157 participants from the BMB datasets. Both datasets contain multi-site, multi-disorder data gathered nationwide in Japan. Additionally. Some participants from the original datasets were excluded due to missing data. The fMRIPrep pipeline was used to preprocess the functional scans. For computing Functional Connectivities, 446 ROIs were selected, including 54 subcortical ROIs form the Tian parcellation, 32 cerebellar ROIs from the Nettekoven parcelaltion and 360 cortical ROIs from the Glasser parcellation. Connectivity was computed as the correlation of brain signals in subject-native space after removing confounding regressors such as motion, white matter, CSF and their derivatives. Normalised graph Laplacians were computed by summing the absolute values of connections in order to avoid creating graph-nodes with negative degree. Cross-site harmonisation was performed by applying parallel transport along the Riemannian geodesic pointing from a local group-wise barycentres towards a "global" barycentre, which was computed as the mean of local means on the SPD manifold. The absolute value of the cosine was used to compare orthonormal modes across subjects.

Results

Spectral modes were computed for the FC matrices and their corresponding Laplacians, both before and after harmonisation. Unsupervised , nonparametric algorithms, such as tSNE and UMAP, revealed a natural clustering of un-harmonised spectral modes according to the category triplet: i) scanning site, ii) scanner model, iii) scanner protocol. These clusterings persist in the spectral domain even after linear harmonisation methods were applied. However, harmonisation on the Riemannian manifold alleviates this effect.

Conclusion

The spectral decomposition of FC is dominated by modes linked to confounding factors such as site, scanner model and scanning protocol. Furthermore, spectral modes of Laplacians mirror structural information, e.g. by grouping together sub-cortical ROIs. Harmonisation should not only respect the original manifold topology of the underlying data, but also take into account latent signals hidden in the eigenspace of matrices.
