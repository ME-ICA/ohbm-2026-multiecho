Introduction

Prenatal alcohol exposure (PAE) is associated with facial dysmorphology, altered brain development, and behavioural and mental health challenges, and can lead to a diagnosis of fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD). Diffusion studies suggest that white matter is altered in individuals with PAE, with widespread differences in white matter properties (lower fractional anisotropy, higher mean and radial diffusivities) compared with unexposed individuals across commissural, association and projection tracts1 that are linked to cognitive and behavioural outcomes.2,3  

Although diffusion tensor-based models have provided valuable insights into white matter development in individuals with PAE, measures derived from these models lack specificity.4 Here we applied a multimodal approach combining inhomogeneous magnetization transfer imaging (ihMT) and fixel-based analysis to assess white matter myelin, fibre density (FD), and morphology (fibre cross-section; FC) in youth with PAE. 

Methods

This study used 200 diffusion MRI datasets from 139 participants (43 with PAE, 96 unexposed controls; aged 7–18 years, mean age = 12.16 ± 2.74 years) collected with four slightly different acquisitions (Fig 1, panel A) across two Canadian sites (Calgary and Edmonton) as part of the PEACH (Prenatal Exposure And Child brain and mental Health) longitudinal study. 133 ihMT datasets from a subset of 100 participants were also analyzed (Fig 1, panel A). FASD diagnosis or confirmed heavy PAE was established via maternal self-report and/or documentation from medical, legal, or child protection sources. Follow-up MRI data were acquiews ~2 years after the initial scan.

Diffusion MRI data were preprocessed (Gibbs-ringing, motion, susceptibility- and eddy current-induced distortion corrections) with MRtrix3 and FSL software packages (Fig 1, panel B). Fibre orientation distributions (FODs) were estimated via multi-shell, multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution, and participants’ white matter FODs were registered and warped to a study-specific template (generated from 40 randomly selected participants; 20 FASD, 20 controls; 50% female). FOD segmentation to identify fixels, fixel reorientation and assignment were performed and fixel metrics (FD, FC) were estimated5 in 60 white matter tracts segmented using TractSeg.  

ihMT data were preprocessed (denoised, Gibbs-ringing and motion corrected) and ihMT ratio (ihMTR) maps were derived using a custom-built ihMT processing script (https://github.com/lsoustelle/ihmt_proc) (Fig 1, panel B). Maps were linearly registered to template space and ihMTR values were extracted from the same 60 tracts.  

Data were harmonized across sites using ComBat while retaining variance associated with age, sex, and exposure group. Linear mixed effects models assessed developmental trajectories of FD, log-transformed FC, and ihMTR metrics across exposure group and sex, with a random effect of participant and average relative motion (FD, FC) and ICV (FC) included as covariates. FDR correction was applied across tracts.

Results

FD showed no age-related changes (q >0.05) across all tracts of interest. There was a 3-way interaction (exposure, sex, age) across many commissural, association, projection, limbic and temporal tracts (partial R2 = 0.04-0.15; q =0.002-0.048; Fig 2a-c) indicating that males with PAE exhibited FD decreases with age, a pattern not observed in females with PAE or unexposed youth of either sex (Fig 2a-c).  

FC increased with age in 80% of white matter tracts (q<0.05). A 3-way interaction (exposure, sex, age) was observed within a subset of thalamocortical and corticospinal tracts (partial R2 = 0.05; q = 0.022-0.029; Fig 2d-f)). Females with PAE displayed distinct trajectories compared with males with PAE and unexposed groups (Fig 2d-f)  

There were no overall age-related changes in ihMTR (q>0.05). A 2-way interaction (exposure, age) was observed across most white matter tracts assessed, indicating that PAE group had lower ihMTR than the unexposed group in early childhood but higher ratios in adolescence (partial R2 = 0.03-0.08, q = 0.001-0.047; Fig 2g-i), suggesting altered myelin maturation trajectories. 

Conclusion

PAE appears to impact white matter development in both region- and sex-specific ways. Fixel-based analysis revealed subtle fibre density and widespread tract morphological changes, while ihMT revealed distinct alterations in myelin trajectories. This work suggests that males with PAE show greater disruptions in fibre density, while females with PAE exhibit changes in fibre bundle morphology. Interestingly, sex did not appear influence myelin trajectories in either exposure group, potentially pointing to distinct underlying mechanisms of change. Given that these altered trajectories occur in pathways important for executive function and mental health, further work is needed to understand how these brain changes may contribute to cognitive and mental health challenges observed in youth with PAE. Together, these complementary imaging modalities provide a nuanced insight into how PAE may alter white matter maturation, highlighting the value of multimodal, longitudinal approaches for characterizing brain development.
