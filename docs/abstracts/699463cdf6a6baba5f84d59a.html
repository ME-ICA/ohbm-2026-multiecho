Introduction

Reinforcement Sensitivity Theory (RST) proposes biologically grounded motivational systems that regulate approach, avoidance, and constraint-related behavior. Recent work identified four neuroanatomical dimensions consistent with RST using structural covariance of gray matter volumes, corresponding to the Behavioral Inhibition System / Fight–Freeze–Flight System (BIS/FFFS), the Behavioral Approach System (BAS), and two Constraint streams, the Constraint Dorsal Stream (CDS) and the Constraint Ventral Stream (CVS).^1 However, this initial model relied on limited subcortical parcellation and was restricted to structural data. Whether the proposed dimensional organization is preserved across structural and functional brain covariance remains unresolved. The present study aimed to characterize the correspondence and dimensional consistency of RST-related brain systems across structural and functional MRI using Exploratory Graph Analysis (EGA), a network psychometrics approach designed to identify latent dimensions in complex multivariate data.

Methods

Structural MRI data was obtained from 1,113 participants of the Human Connectome Project (HCP). Functional data was drawn from the HCP 7T movie-watching dataset, comprising four naturalistic fMRI runs (182, 181, 177, and 177 participants), with 176 common subjects across runs. Gray matter volumes were extracted from T1-weighted images preprocessed with CAT12 and corrected for total intracranial volume (TIV) using the residual method.^2 Functional time series were extracted from minimally preprocessed fMRI data using FSL^3, and averaged across the 4 runs for ROI, obtaining a single average value of functional activation per subject and each ROI combination. Seventy-seven cortical and subcortical indicators were selected from the HCPex atlas based on prior RST definitions. Homotopic brain regions were averaged to reduce redundancy across hemispheres; this decision was supported by Unique Variable Analysis. Dimensionality was estimated using EGA^4 and bootstrap EGA^5 (500 samples) for both structural (with and without TIV correction) and functional data. Indicators with item stability below 0.75 were removed, except for theoretically central regions.

Results

Structural EGA identified nine dimensions in both TIV-corrected (TEFI = -81,26) and TIV-uncorrected gray matter data (TEFI = 18.87), showing high consistency across datasets. BIS/FFFS and BAS indicators formed clearly distinct dimensions, while Constraint-related regions subdivided into multiple dorsal and ventral streams. Since the corrected data presented better TEFI, bootstrap analysis was carried out and removed unstable indicators, retaining the nine-dimensional solution with high item and dimension stability (range 0.80–1.00). 

Functional EGA of the averaged movie-watching data initially yielded eight dimensions and converged to six dimensions after bootstrap-based refinement. Functional analyses consistently identified independent BIS/FFFS and BAS dimensions, alongside four Constraint-related dimensions. The highest variability in dimensional assignment was observed in anterior cingulate, frontal, subcortical, and posterior cingulate regions. Conclusion

Across structural and functional MRI, the core RST systems—BIS/FFFS and BAS—emerged as robust and independent dimensions. Compared to previous four-factor models^1, the present findings reveal a finer-grained organization, particularly within Constraint-related systems and subcortical components of BIS/FFFS and BAS. Variability in cortical assignments may reflect differential nodal centrality within RST-related networks. Overall, these results support the integration of psychological constructs with biologically grounded brain network phenotypes.
