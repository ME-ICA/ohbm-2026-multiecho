Introduction

Degenerative cervical myelopathy (DCM) is the most common nontraumatic spinal cord disorder in adults and a leading cause of age-related spinal dysfunction. Traditionally viewed as a spinal pathology, DCM is now recognized as a central nervous system (CNS) disorder involving supraspinal changes. Neuroimaging studies have revealed that patients with DCM exhibit structural and functional alterations in cortical and subcortical brain regions, particularly within sensorimotor and associative networks. However, existing morphometric approaches focus primarily on group-level differences in cortical thickness or volume, which may obscure individual variability and correspondingly complicate interpretability.

Morphometric similarity networks (MSNs) offer a powerful framework for mapping brain-wide cytoarchitectural organization by integrating multiple morphometric features (e.g., thickness, surface area, curvature, and volume) into individualized structural networks. MSNs reflect inter-regional similarity in cortical architecture and can capture disease-specific reorganization at the individual level. Recent work in neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disorders suggests that MSN alterations correspond with both clinical symptoms and gene-expression patterns.

In this study, we investigated cortical MSN differences between patients with DCM and healthy controls (HC), the association between MSN strength and motor function (dexterity and balance), and transcriptional correlates by using the Allen Human Brain Atlas. We hypothesized that MSN alterations in DCM would be spatially specific, clinically relevant, and biologically interpretable via their association with the expression of DCM risk genes.

Methods

We enrolled 52 patients with DCM and age- and sex-matched 33 HC; all patients were right-handed. Clinical assessments included the NIH Toolbox 9-Hole Pegboard Dexterity Test (average score per participant) and the Standing Balance Test.

FreeSurfer v8.0 was used to process all structural T1-weighted MRI data for cortical reconstruction and segmentation. From each of the 68 cortical regions, we extracted five morphometric features: cortical thickness, surface area, gray matter volume, mean curvature, and Gaussian curvature. We then constructed MSNs by computing pairwise Peason correlations between cortical regions sharing similar morphometric features. Regional MSN strength was defined as the mean similarity of each region with all others.

We used t-tests with FDR correction to compare MSN strength between groups (DCM vs HC) and Pearson correlation to assess for correlations between regional MSN strength and clinical scores.

We then explored the molecular correlates of MSN alterations by using gene-expression data from the Allen Human Brain Atlas. Using the abagen toolbox, we matched cortical regions to expression levels of 40 DCM-relevant genes, selected on the basis of their use in prior studies in neuroinflammation, ECM remodeling, and neuroplasticity. For each gene, we computed the Pearson correlation between regional MSN t-values (DCM–HC) and gene-expression levels. Results were FDR-corrected (q < 0.05).

Results

Patients with DCM exhibited significantly worse motor function than HC (dexterity [mean ± SD]: 37.75 ± 10.87 vs 51.38 ± 8.17, p < 0.0001; balance: 32.91 ± 13.46 vs 42.31 ± 7.84, p = 0.001). Our analysis revealed that patients with DCM had comparatively lower MSN strength in the   caudal/middle frontal gyri, superior temporal cortex, frontal pole, rostral anterior cingulate, paracentral lobule, inferior temporal gyrus, temporal pole, and isthmus cingulate cortex; however, MSN strength was comparatively higher in the right inferior parietal cortex, right fusiform gyrus, right precuneus, and left lateral occipital cortex.

Regional MSN strength in the bilateral frontal poles correlated positively with dexterity scores (left: r = 0.35, p = 0.014; right: r = 0.31, p = 0.034), indicating that structural coherence in these executive-motor regions is linked to the preservation of fine motor function. MSN in the superior temporal cortex showed positive but nonsignificant associations with balance performance.

Gene-expression analysis showed that regional MSN changes significantly correlated with cortical RUNX2 expression (r = 0.31, q = 0.011). Other genes, such as USP43, FGF2, and CALM1, showed subthreshold associations with MSN reductions. These genes are involved in synaptic plasticity, stress response, and proteostasis—suggesting potential molecular pathways for regional MSN alterations.

Conclusion

Our study provides novel evidence of structural network reorganization in DCM beyond spinal cord pathology, highlighting supraspinal cortical involvement. We demonstrate that MSN strength is reduced in key frontal and temporal areas but increased in posterior associative regions, suggesting a complex interplay of degeneration and compensation. MSN alterations in frontal poles were linked to dexterity performance, reinforcing the clinical relevance of supraspinal changes in DCM.

Importantly, the regional pattern of MSN changes correlated with RUNX2 expression, a transcription factor implicated in neuroplasticity. This spatial correspondence suggests that molecular factors may predispose certain cortical regions to degeneration or adaptive reorganization in DCM.

By integrating MRI-based MSNs with transcriptional data, this work advances our understanding of how structural brain networks reflect and shape clinical deficits in DCM. These findings lay a foundation for future studies aimed at identifying biologically informed imaging biomarkers and therapeutic targets.
