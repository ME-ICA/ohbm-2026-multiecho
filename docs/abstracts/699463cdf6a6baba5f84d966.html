Introduction

Facial expression recognition is a fundamental component of social communication that develops rapidly during infancy and continues to improve throughout childhood and into adulthood. Although prior work has characterized behavioral improvements in emotion recognition, the neural mechanisms underlying its developmental maturation are far from fully understood, largely due to the difficulty of acquiring neural data with both high spatial and temporal resolution from young children.

Methods

Here, we analyzed intracranial EEG (iEEG) data recorded from children (5–10 years old) and a post-childhood group (13–55 years old) while participants watched video clips. AI models were used to extract 48 dynamic facial emotions and encoding models were used to evaluate the neural representations of facial emotion.

Results

We found that in the post-childhood group, both the posterior superior temporal cortex (pSTC) and the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) encode facial emotion features from a high-dimensional space. However, in children, the facial emotion information is only significantly represented in the pSTC, not in the DLPFC. To further characterize developmental changes in network dynamics, we examined directional functional connectivity between DLPFC and pSTC using Granger Causality analyses. The example child data showed less sustained top-down influence from DLPFC to pSTC, implying immature prefrontal modulation of sensory processing during facial emotion perception.

Conclusion

Taken together, our results suggested that young children rely more on low-level sensory processing in pSTC than on the prefrontal cortex for facial emotion processing, implying that the prefrontal cortex matures with development to enable a full understanding of facial emotions.
