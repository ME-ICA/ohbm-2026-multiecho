Introduction

The hippocampus is central to episodic memory, spatial navigation, and flexible cognition, with its moment-to-moment activity strongly shaped by neuromodulatory systems that regulate excitability, plasticity, and large-scale network coordination. Dopaminergic signals engaged by novelty and motivational salience stabilize hippocampal representations via D1/D5-dependent plasticity1.. Cholinergic input from the basal forebrain regulates hippocampal gain and theta rhythmicity, biasing circuits toward encoding by enhancing entorhinal drive and suppressing recurrent CA3 transmission2. Noradrenergic output from the locus coeruleus, particularly during phasic responses to salient events, modulates hippocampal dynamics under arousal, increases signal-to-noise, and prioritizes behaviorally relevant information, with known interactions across systems3,4.A critical gap remains as dopaminergic, cholinergic, and noradrenergic systems have largely been examined in isolation, often using distinct experimental paradigms or neuromodulatory proxies. 

Addressing this gap is essential for advancing a systems-level understanding of hippocampal function in humans. A coordinated neuromodulatory perspective offers a unifying framework for interpreting how dopamine, acetylcholine, and noradrenaline jointly regulate hippocampal dynamics, providing new insight into the neural mechanisms supporting cognition in the human brain. We hypothesised that dopaminergic, cholinergic, and noradrenergic systems act in a coordinated and asymmetric manner to shape moment-to-moment hippocampal network topology and attractor-state transitions, biasing the hippocampus between integrated, flexible states and stabilized, segregated states in humans.

Methods

We analysed preprocessed resting-state and movie-watching fMRI data from the 7T Human Connectome Project (HCP) dataset (n = 176; TR=1sec; 1.6mm3 voxels). Time series were extracted from 400 cortical regions using the Schaefer 17-network parcellation, hippocampal subregions and neuromodulatory nuclei including the cholinergic nucleus basalis of Meynert (nbM), noradrenergic locus coeruleus (LC), and dopaminergic ventral tegmental area (VTA) using high-resolution probabilistic atlases (Figure 1 A). All timeseries were z-scored and temporally aligned across runs. To characterize hippocampal network dynamics, we estimated time-resolved functional connectivity using the multiplication of temporal derivatives (MTD), yielding dynamic connectivity matrices for the whole brain across all runs. Network topological measures were computed for hippocampal nodes at each time point, including within-module connectivity and participation coefficient, to index shifts between functionally integrated and segregated states. To identify discrete hippocampal states based upon network topology, k-means clustering was applied to the above-mentioned metrics5.  

Leveraging previous approaches to capture neuromodulatory related brain state transitions3, we quantified the relationship between phasic neuromodulatory bursts and hippocampal state transitions. Phasic neuromodulatory bursts were identified based upon the difference between each respective nuclei signals and peaks in BOLD signal were identified by two-times the standard deviation of the BOLD signals second derivative, which resulted in sustained epochs of noradrenergic, cholinergic and dopaminergic modulation. Hippocampal state transitions were defined by a low-dimensional energy attractor analysis that defines the attractor landscape based upon the mean change in hippocampal BOLD activity (mean-squared displacement, MSD) at a given temporal displacement (TR). Operationalising the energy (E) required to transition to a new state by taking the natural logarithm of the inverse probability of observing a change in BOLD activity (mean-squared displacement, MSDt) over a set temporal window (TR=8), P(MSD,t), E=ln(). The probability of a hippocampal state transition P(MSDt) was calculated using a Gaussian kernel density estimation. As such, a highly probable state transition (unsteady-attractor) was indicated by a lower-energy transition compared to infrequent state transitions (steady-attractors) that require more-energy3 (Figure 1 B-C). This approach enabled assessment of coordinated neuromodulatory influence on low-dimensional hippocampal state fluctuations. All significant tests included FDR-Bonferroni corrected p-values and non-parametric randomised permutations (1000 iterations).

Results

We found integrated topology was characterized by greater overall coupling across distributed networks which is supported by previous literature. Upon focusing on hippocampal dynamic topology, we found a greater coupling between both left & right hemispheres of the anterior hippocampus with the dopaminergic system during integrated states compared to segregated states (L VTA pFDR=6.9x10-8, R VTA pFDR =8.791x10-12). 

The hippocampal attractor landscape analysis revealed coordinated phasic bursts across the distinct neuromodulatory nuclei drove fluctuating hippocampal states. We found that both the noradrenergic and dopaminergic bursts drove low-energy thresholds relative to baseline (i.e., higher probability) for hippocampal state transitions; ergo making previously unlikely state transitions more probable. The greatest relative change occurred around 5TRs post phasic activity (p<0.05) for both nuclei. Whereas the cholinergic bursts drove a relatively higher energy barrier for hippocampal state transitions – this is similar to shifting the hippocampus into a steady-state attractor (depicting a “well”). In addition, the conjoint effect of the noradrenergic and cholinergic phasic bursts drove a lower energy threshold for hippocampal state transitions – which is a similar finding to previous work suggesting that the noradrenergic system may have stronger driving effects on brain state dynamics compared to the cholinergic system4.  The joint effect of cholinergic and dopaminergic bursts was driving a higher energy threshold making hippocampal state transitions less likely, suggesting that the cholinergic phasic bursts have a dominant effect on hippocampal BOLD dynamics than the dopaminergic system. Lastly, we observed a lower energy threshold for state transitions for the conjoint noradrenergic and dopaminergic phasic bursts suggesting that these two neuromodulatory systems have similar effects on hippocampal state transitions – in enabling unsteady state attractors that can facilitate hippocampal state shifts. 

Conclusion

Our results provide evidence for distinct modes of hippocampal dynamics in resting-state and movie-watching 7T data. Empirically, we demonstrated that integrated hippocampal topology selectively couples the anterior hippocampus with dopaminergic VTA signals which supports previous findings that the dopaminergic system coordinates hippocampal activity by strengthening hippocampal-cortical coupling and supporting the processing of salient events; which we will conduct further follow-up analyses with the cognitive task fMRI data within the same cohort to determine whether there is distinct signatures of dopaminergic driven hippocampal dynamics in memory reinforcement learning. 

We also found that by tracking fluctuations in BOLD signal within the noradrenergic, cholinergic and dopaminergic nuclei, that there are distinct hippocampal attractor state transitions. Critically, the joint effects of the neuromodulatory nuclei are asymmetric; as the noradrenergic activity overrides cholinergic stabilization to facilitate state transitions, cholinergic-dopaminergic co-bursts bias stabilization and noradrenergic-dopaminergic co-bursts facilitate state transitions. These findings provide convergent evidence that the neuromodulatory systems act in concert to gate hippocampal BOLD state transitions; which converge with previous literature suggesting that the noradrenergic systems drive state transitions and cholinergic system drives steady states. These findings reveal how neuromodulatory interactions may bias the hippocampus toward flexible exploration versus stabilized maintenance, offering testable predictions for memory formation, consolidation, and retrieval under varying arousal and motivational contexts. In follow-up work, we will apply similar methodological approaches to a 7T overnight memory-consolidation dataset to elucidate the neuromodulatory effects on memory function within the hippocampus. It further suggests neuromodulatory influences on hippocampal dynamics could inform mechanistic interpretations of task-evoked states, bridge human neuroimaging with circuit-level models, and guide translational work on disorders of memory and arousal where these systems are disrupted.
