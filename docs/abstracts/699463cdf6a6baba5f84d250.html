Introduction

Neuroaesthetics plays an important role in decoding multiple behavioral dimensions of human information processing, including art appreciation, consumer behavior, social interaction, and decision making. Previous studies have shown that the brain regions involved in reward learning are also engaged during aesthetic judgments. However, the specific neural substrates of aesthetic evaluation remain inconclusive, and the mechanisms underlying their contribution to aesthetic processing are still not well understood. In the current study, we used independent component analysis (ICA) to investigate the dynamic neural circuits associated with aesthetic experience during subjective valuation.

Methods

We recruited twelve healthy volunteers who participated in a calligraphy appreciation task during fMRI scanning. The scanning was performed on a 3T MR scanner (GE Discovery 750, Milwaukee, MI, USA) equipped with a 48-channel phase-array head coil at the Radiology Department of China Medical University Hospital, Taichung, Taiwan. Functional images were acquired with a resolution of 128 x 128 x 64 and a repetition time (TR) of 2 seconds using an acceleration factor of 3. During the experiment, participants viewed a 6-second calligraphy video that varied in aesthetic level and direction (beautiful-to-ugly, B2U, or ugly-to-beautiful, U2B) in each trial. After each video clip, participants had 2 seconds to answer via button press whether the sequence of Chinese calligraphy characters became more beautiful or uglier. A total of 126 trials were administered. 

To analyze the fMRI data, we applied an ICA-based approach using FMRLAB7. Independent blood-oxygenation-level-dependent (BOLD) time-series components obtained from ICA decomposition were identified and clustered into the primary visual cortex (V1), default mode network (DMN), and orbital frontal cortex (OFC). For event-related BOLD (er-BOLD) analyses, we extracted a stimulus-locked epoch extending from 4 seconds before to 10 seconds after stimulus onset. Baseline correction was performed by computing the mean signal in the pre-stimulus interval (prior to 0 seconds) and subtracting it from each trial’s time series. Behavioral responses were used to classify trials into subjective U2B or B2U conditions. To compare the er-BOLD time series between conditions, we averaged the er-BOLD responses across trials and computed the area under the curve (AUC), thus the total BOLD changes, for each condition. Statistical comparisons were performed using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Results

The Wilcoxon signed-rank test revealed a significant difference in total BOLD changes between the U2B and B2U conditions in the DMN (p = 0.0398), reflecting differences in subjective valuation during aesthetic experience. In contrast, no significant differences were observed in V1 (p = 0.6772) or the OFC (p = 0.9453).

Conclusion

Our findings show that subjective ratings with aesthetic reference in aesthetic perception are associated with distinct patterns of network-level BOLD dynamics. Notably, DMN activity increased when stimuli were perceived as becoming less aesthetically pleasing, suggesting greater engagement of self-referential and evaluative processes during negative aesthetic transitions. No significant differences were observed in V1, suggesting that early visual processing was not differentially modulated by the aesthetic transitions. The absence of significant OFC differences may reflect the relatively low reward salience of aesthetic transitions, given that the dopaminergic system is evolutionarily tuned to more biologically relevant rewards than subtle aesthetic changes. Together, these results highlight the involvement of both perceptual and introspective networks in shaping subjective aesthetic experience.
