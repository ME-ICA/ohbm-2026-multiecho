Introduction

Parkinson’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder mainly characterized by motor symptoms such as rigidity, bradykinesia, and tremor.1 In recent years, magnetic resonance–guided high intensity focused ultrasound (MRgFUS) - an intervention that allows for the ablation of deep-brain structures without craniotomy or electrode penetration - has been proved to be an effective treatment for this disease when the subthalamic nucleus (STN) is targeted unilaterally, improving motor scores in several pilot trials with a total joint sample of 200 patients.2 Nevertheless, evidences of the neural basis of this improvement are scarce. To the best of our knowledge, only a previous study addressed the relationship between lesion location in MRgFUS subthalamotomy and motor improvement in Parkinson patients, by using a voxel-based lesion-symptom mapping approach, and finding specific brain voxels within the STN with an optimal effect against each motor symptom.3 However, this focuses on the location of the MRgFUS lesion, not on its structural or functional consequences on the brain. Due to the scarcity of evidence, the aim of our study was to contribute to the slowly growing literature by investigating the relationship between white matter (WM) disintegration and motor improvement after a unilateral MRgFUS subthalamotomy intervention in Parkinson’s disease patients.

Methods

Participants and procedure

We included 45 participants in this study (mean age = 60 ± 6’84, ~80% males), obtained from Hospital La Fe of València, Spain. All participants were diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease, following the criteria of the Movement Disorder Society 1. A total of 30 participants were treated with MRgFUS lesions in the left STN and 15 participants were treated with MRgFUS lesions in the right STN. 

Motor examination and change in motor functioning

We measured motor functioning of our participants with the Movement Disorder Society-Sponsored Revision of the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS), in its Spanish version. We focused our analyses on the scores obtained from Part III (“motor examination”, which measures rigidity, bradykinesia and tremor) of the side of body treated (right in left STN and left in right STN) when patients were off medication. We examined our participants before and after the MRgFUS intervention, with a mean time between measurements of 2.4 months (SD = 2.08). We obtained follow-up motor scores of 20 participants with left STN lesion and 10 with right STN lesion. 

We analysed longitudinal changes between baseline and post-MRgFUS motor scores by running General Linear Models of repeated measures with IBM SPSS Statistics, including time as the within-subject factor. We also explored if the longitudinal change in motor signs was influenced by the hemisphere where the intervention was carried out (left STN or right STN) by including intervention side as a between-subject factor in the model.

Image acquisition and analysis

We also obtained diffusion tensor images (DTI) before and after the MRgFUS intervention. 

All DTI were preprocessed using the FMRIB Software Library (FSL) before extracting mean diffusivity (MD) maps for each participant. 

We then prepared group data for statistical comparisons using a Modified Tract-Based Spatial Statistics (TBSS) protocol for the analysis of longitudinal Diffusion-weighted MRI data (https://github.com/btaraku/Longitudinal_TBSS). This resulted in a 4D skeletonised MD image that was used in the next steps of voxelwise statistical analyses. 

DTI longitudinal analysis

We ran longitudinal whole-brain voxelwise statistical analysis of MD using TBSS in left STN and right STN groups separately. We used the procedure recommended in the FSL documentation for a single group paired difference, manually computing the difference between the images of the two time-points (post- minus pre-intervention) for each subject and then running a 1-sample t-test on the subtracted images using randomise. We used the mean FA skeleton mask computed in the previous preprocessing steps as the fibres of interest for our analysis, as recommended in the TBSS documentation. The threshold-free cluster enhancement (TFCE) statistic was estimated, and statistical significance was determined using permutation-based nonparametric inference (5000 permutations contrast) at a threshold of p < 0.05, family-wise error (FWE) corrected. Finally, we identified the designation of the tracts that showed significant DTI changes by using the JHU ICBM-DTI-81 white-matter label atlas.

Relationship between WM disintegration and change in motor functioning

We subtracted post minus pre-intervention MDS-UPDRS Part III scores. Positive results would indicate motor worsening after intervention (post > pre), while negative values indicated motor function improvement (pre > post). We used this differences (demeaned) as a covariate of interest in our 1-sample t-test FSL model, including post-intervention DTI of participants as the inputs of the model. We restricted our analyses to a mask of the tracts where WM integrity was affected by the left STN MRgFUS intervention, i.e., the ones that showed longitudinal differences in MD after left STN MRgFUS. We determined statistical significance using permutation-based nonparametric inference (5000 permutations), TFCE and p < 0.05 FWE corrected. 

Results

Effect of intervention on motor functioning

The mean score of MDS-UPDRS Part III (treated side, off medication) for the whole sample was 18.87 (SD = 5.56) before the MRgFUS intervention, and 7.16 (SD = 4.39) post-intervention. We found significant differences between pre- and post-intervention scores (F = 44.546, p < 0.001), revealing a significant decrease in motor signs of Parkinson’s disease on the treated side after the STN MRgFUS intervention. We found no significant interaction between time of measurement and intervention side (F = 1.195, p = 0.283), indicating that changes in motor functioning after the MRgFUS intervention did not differ between left STN and right STN groups. 

Effect of intervention on WM integrity

We found significant differences between WM integrity before and after the MRgFUS intervention both in left STN and right STN groups. Participants with a left STN MRgFUS intervention showed a statistically significant increase in MD (lower WM integrity) in fibres emerging from bilateral STN, corresponding to cerebral peduncle and internal capsule, extending dorsolaterally through the corpus callosum to superior corona radiata and superior longitudinal fasciculus, reaching inferior frontal fibers in the right hemisphere, and extending posteriorly to posterior corona radiata and posterior thalamic radiation (p < 0.05, FWE corrected; Figure 1). Participants with a right STN MRgFUS intervention showed significantly more MD in the same fibres, but more widely extended bilaterally, with relatively symmetric distal extensions in both hemispheres (p < 0.05, FWE corrected).

Relationship between WM disintegration and change in motor functioning

We investigated if WM disintegration after the MRgFUS intervention was related to change in motor functioning for the left STN group. Motor functioning change was calculated by subtracting post minus pre-intervention scores - positive results of this difference would indicate motor worsening, and negative values indicated motor function improvement. We found a statistically significant negative relationship between MD values after the MRgFUS intervention and motor function change (MDS-UPDRS Part III, treated side, off medication) in a cluster comprising internal capsule fibres adjacent to left STN (p < 0.005, FWE corrected). This negative relationship indicates that the higher the WM disintegration adjacent to left STN (i.e., higher MD value), the more motor function improvement (i.e., more negative value of motor change). 

Conclusion

Our study unveils the specific effects of unilateral STN MRgFUS intervention on WM fibre disintegration and its associated improvements in motor functioning. 

We found that unilateral MRgFUS subthalamotomy produced a statistically significant decrease in motor signs of Parkinson’s disease in both left STN and right STN groups. The intervention also produced a significant decrease in WM integrity in fibres emerging from bilateral STN, extending dorsolaterally through the corpus callosum to superior corona radiata and superior longitudinal fasciculus, also reaching inferior frontal fibers, and extending posteriorly to posterior corona radiata and posterior thalamic radiation, a pattern of WM disintegration which turned out to be more bilateral in the right STN group. Finally, we found a significant relationship between WM disintegration produced by the MRgFUS intervention and motor functioning change in the left STN group, in a cluster comprising internal capsule fibres adjacent to left STN. The relationship indicated that the higher the WM disintegration in these fibres, the more motor function improvement. 

To the best of our knowledge, our investigation is the first one to show a direct relationship between WM disintegration produced by a unilateral MRgFUS STN intervention and motor improvement in a sample of Parkinson’s disease patients. Thus, we provide novel evidence that contributes to the still scarce literature on the neural basis of motor improvements after MRgFUS subthalamotomy in Parkinson’s disease.
