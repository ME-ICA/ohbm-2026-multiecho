Introduction

Brain connectivity supports cognitive and motor functions, with white matter tracts acting as the highways linking cortical and subcortical regions. In infants, these pathways undergo rapid maturation that lays the foundation for later abilities. Understanding tract-level organization is therefore essential for characterizing both typical development and early deviations associated with neurodevelopmental disorders. Importantly, white matter tracts are not homogeneous: different segments exhibit distinct microarchitecture that reflects their functional roles. Conventional bundle-averaged analyses collapse this spatial information into a single value, obscuring regional variations that may hold developmental or clinical significance.

Here, we introduce an along-tract profiling framework for the infant brain that reconstructs white matter pathways and extracts microstructural measures at anatomically corresponding positions. Applying this pipeline to data from the Healthy Brain and Child Development (HBCD) Study, we reconstruct 51 major bundles and characterize DTI and NODDI metrics along each pathway in infants aged 0–3 months, revealing pronounced spatial heterogeneity across commissural, association, and projection bundles.

Methods

We analyzed cross-sectional diffusion MRI (dMRI) and T2-weighted (T2w) scans from the first HBCD data release, comprising 481 infants aged 5–268 days (523 sessions; mean age 50 days) [1]. Data were processed using our infant-centric pipeline [2], which includes tissue segmentation and surface reconstruction for T2w images, as well as motion, eddy current, and susceptibility distortion correction for dMRI. White matter pathways were reconstructed using Precision Cortex Tractography [3], yielding 51 bundles per subject following the definitions in [4].

To enable along-tract profiling across individuals, we established streamline-to-streamline and point-to-point correspondence by transforming each subject’s bundles into a common space and aligning them to reference tract skeletons, ensuring that microstructural measurements at each position reflect anatomically consistent locations (Fig. 1).

Microstructure was quantified using DTI metrics (FA, MD) and NODDI metrics (ICVF, ODI, ISO), and population-level profiles were computed as the mean and standard deviation at each along-tract position. Bundle shape descriptors (length, span, and diameter) were also derived [5].

Generalized additive mixed model (GAMM) was used to derive population bundles and growth curves of shape descriptors and microstructure profiles.

Results

Bundle Reconstruction. Our pipeline achieved robust reconstruction at individual (Fig. 1, shown for infants aged 37 days) and population levels (Fig. 1, shown for newborn and 3 months), yielding anatomically coherent bundles, even in challenging bundles like the anterior commissure.

Spatial Heterogeneity. Along-tract profiles (Fig. 2) revealed spatial heterogeneity with low inter-subject variance, confirming reliable correspondence. Commissural pathways (anterior commissure, AC) showed FA peaking centrally (~0.30 at 3 months) with lower values at lateral terminations (~0.15). Association bundles (inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, IFOF) exhibited inverted-U profiles: FA rose from posterior (~0.10) to central segments (~0.40) before declining anteriorly (~0.20). Projection bundles (pyramidal tract, PYT) showed a mid-tract FA peak of ~0.55, with lower values toward both ends (~0.18). ICVF profiles mirrored the FA pattern in the AC but were largely stable in the IFOF and showed a decline from inferior to posterior in the PYT.

Age-Related Changes. Comparing newborn and 3-month profiles revealed consistent maturation patterns. FA and ICVF increased globally across all bundles while spatial profile shapes were preserved. Shape descriptors captured corresponding geometric growth: AC increased from 67.46 mm to 75.25 mm in length; IFOF grew from 107.73 mm to 121.16 mm; PYT extended from 87.7 mm to 92.09 mm. Diameter and span showed proportional increases across all bundles.

Developmental Gradients. Two spatial gradients emerged across bundles: (1) a core-to-periphery gradient, with central segments showing more mature microstructure than terminations, and (2) a posterior-inferior to anterior-superior gradient, with caudal regions exhibiting higher FA and ICVF than rostral regions. In the pyramidal tract, ICVF declined from inferior (~0.69) to superior (~0.39), reflecting more advanced maturation near the brainstem compared to cortical terminations.

Conclusion

Along-tract profiling in HBCD infants reveals spatially organized microstructural development governed by two principles: a core-to-periphery gradient indicating earlier maturation of central tract segments, and a posterior-inferior to anterior-superior gradient consistent with established caudal-to-rostral developmental sequences. These patterns are present at birth and maintained through 3 months, with global microstructural increases preserving the underlying spatial organization. Our findings suggest that white matter maturation follows intrinsic spatial axes that may reflect the temporal sequence of axonal growth and myelination during fetal and early postnatal life. The low inter-subject variance demonstrates that our infant-centric pipeline enables reliable population-level tract characterization in the challenging pediatric imaging environment. This framework opens avenues for detecting localized abnormalities as early biomarkers of neurodevelopmental disorders and, as longitudinal HBCD data expand, for charting regional maturation trajectories along white matter pathways throughout infancy.
