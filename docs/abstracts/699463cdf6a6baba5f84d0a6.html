Introduction

Human brain functional connectivity (FC) develops dramatically in the first few years after birth. However, there exists huge research gap on how such functional organization shifted from perinatal prototype to adult-like school-age architecture. This is mainly due to inevitable transition of functional MRI (fMRI) scanning protocol from natural sleep (for 0-3 years old) to wakefulness (for 3-6 years old). Such vigilance state discontinuity causes dramatic changes in brain FC that cannot be simply compensated via standard methods (e.g., ComBat).1

While generative model could potentially predict FC from one to another state, it faces great challenges due to lack of paired data (i.e., FC in both wakefulness and sleeping states) because of the rocket-high demanding on infants. This is even more difficult when whole-brain FC between every pair of hundreds of brain regions is considered; such high dimensionality and scarce sample size impedes usage of deep learning when charting brain functional development for the full preschool development period. 

To solve the challenges, we propose a novel structural connectivity information-guided latent space mapping algorithm for bi-directional infant brain FC translations across vigilance states. Our generative model does not require paired data at all, as it leverages diffusion MRI (dMRI)-derived structural connectivity (SC) as a state-invariant anatomical “bridge”, which ensures a better FC representation mapping across states through a shared SC latent space. The merit of unpaired data learning makes our method more scalable with significantly improved generation fidelity and better-preserved individual-specific topological features, even under the circumstance of high-dimensionality of whole-brain FC. 

Methods

Datasets: We used the largest datasets to date that comprising both fMRI and dMRI from two large cohorts: Baby Connectome Project and Chinese Baby Connectome Project,2, 3 resulting in 891 scans from 630 subjects (age: 0-75 month) after thorough quality control (e.g., head motion-based data censoring). Infant-dedicated preprocessing was conducted based on the established pipelines. For each subject at each scan, whole-brain FC and SC were calculated using Pearson’s correlation and tractography streamline density, respectively, across 100 regions of interest (ROIs) from seven subnetworks defined by the Schaefer's atlas.4 

Algorithm: We propose a novel cycle-consistent, structure-bridged latent space mapping framework that leverages SC as a state-invariant anatomical “bridge” for unpaired awake-sleep FC translation, while preserving individual specific topology and cross-modal consistency. By mapping all FC into a state-invariant SC latent space, our approach enables cross-state FC harmonization without relying on paired FC data (thus can be trained based on more data) while remaining fully compatible with paired-data supervision when available; such framework also provides a more flexible framework for connectome transformation.

Fig. 1a shows main flowchart of our proposed model. Our model consists of three main novel modules: 1) Modality-Specific Encoder-Decoders (Fig. 1b): it passes FC and SC separately through modality-specific encoder-decoder based on multi-graph convolutional networks (MGCN) and differentiable pooling to learning modality-specific latent features space. Please note that the differentiable pooling and its inverse serve as graph information bottleneck (GIB) for better topological feature extraction. 2) Latent Space Mapping (Fig. 1c): Inspired by latent space translation,5 we invent a cross-attention-based latent space mapping module that integrates target-domain priors during latent space translation by features fusion. 3) Modality-Specific Conditional Discriminators: The translated features are decoded to reconstruct the target-domain SC or FC, followed by conditional discriminator to enforce domain-specific fidelity.

We propose three different constraints working integrally: 1) Subject-Matching Constraint (SMC), which maximizes mutual information between generated FC and real SC pairs, as well as between generated SC and real FC pairs, to strengthen cross-modal correspondence and enhance our model’s ability of preserving subject-specific topology during FC translations; 2) Latent Space Constraint (LSC), which explicitly aligns mapped latent features across vigilance states; 3) Core-Connectivity Regularization (CCR), which prioritizes reconstruction of strong, dominant functional connectivity.

Experiments: We benchmark our framework against prior SC-FC transformation models as well as representative multimodal translation architectures to comprehensively evaluate performance. To further assess the contributions of the proposed modules and the integrated constraints, we conduct a series of ablation experiments. We applied the model to all 891 scans, using SC as primary input when available (n=786), otherwise using FC from either vigilance state. We further analyzed the developmental pattern of both inter-network and intra-network FC using Generalized Additive Mixed Models (GAMMs) from 0 to 6 years old to chart brain functional development in a full period without interrupting by changed vigilance state. The GAMMs modeled subjects as a random effect variable and included gender and site as fixed effect variables.

Results

Using our model, we generated whole-brain FC matrices from one to another vigilance states (i.e., sleeping to wakefulness, and vice versa). As shown in Fig. 2d, the FCs under different states generated by our model exhibit more resembled patterns to the real FCs under that states than those of other competing models. Quantitative analysis shows (Fig. 2b) that the generated FC in wakefulness and sleeping states based on SC achieved mean Pearson's r of 0.635±0.070 for wakefulness, and 0.680±0.111 for sleeping state, compared to the real data, higher than the performance of other methods. Moreover, the yielded developmental FC trajectories (Fig. 2a) using generated data statistically resemble to those generated by real data across all seven subnetworks (bootstrap comparison, with overlapped 95% confidence interval). We further conducted ablation studies to systematically evaluate the contribution of each of our invented modules. Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of each constraint and architectural component, that is, removing Subject-Matching Constraint, Latent Space Constraint, Graph Information Bottleneck or Core-Connectivity Regularization significantly degraded reconstruction quality (Fig. 2c). 

To demonstrate the benefit to downstream studies, we analyzed uninterrupted developmental trajectories from 0 to 6 years combining real FC with those predicted by our model, meaning we can now chart the entire early development period of 0-6 years of age, rather than having to be interrupted by different vigilance states at 3 years old. As shown in Fig. 2a, the visual, somatomotor, and dorsal attention networks that are associated with sensory processing, motor readiness, vigilance, and goal-directed attentional control exhibited notable differences between wakefulness and sleeping states. The developmental trajectories of inter-network connections (Fig. 2e) exhibit greater differences across states compared with those of intra-network connections. This could be primarily because inter-network connections are more sensitive to vigilance states and mature later compared to intra-network connections, and wakefulness versus sleep significantly reconfigures the coupling patterns of the large-scale networks.

We also plotted age-dependency matrices of inter-network connectivity under different vigilance states. As shown by the two F-statistics matrices in Fig. 2e, the overall age dependency exhibits profound differences between different vigilance states. These results indicate that the developmental trajectories of intra- and inter-network functional connectivity could have unneglectable state-dependent differences.

Conclusion

We propose a flexible cross-state FC transformation framework for infant fMRI studies. Thanks to the anatomical bridge and latent space mapping, our model does not require paired data, thus can be trained based on more dataset, resulting in a scalable cross-state FC translation with higher fidelity and better-preserved individual variability. With generated FC in either vigilance state, we re-chart the brain FC development trajectories in both sleeping and awake states for the entire early development period (0-6 years old). Such a more complete FC chart may serve as fundamental normative for detecting and monitoring developmental disorders.
