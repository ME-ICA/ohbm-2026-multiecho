Introduction

We recently developed a method for simultaneous wide-field optical imaging (WOI) and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) in mice, opening new possibilities for multimodal characterization of intrinsic brain activity. Because WOI and rs-fMRI are acquired at different spatial and temporal scales, comparison across modalities is not trivial. We register both set of images to the Allen brain atlas of the cortical surface1 and adopt an approach based on co-activation patterns (CAPs)2,3 to define brain states from each modality at their intrinsic sampling rates. We then examine the spatial similarity of CAPs from WOI and rs-fMRI, and determine whether particular WOI CAPs are more likely to occur during particular rs-fMRI CAPs.

Methods

Simultaneous WOI and rs-fMRI (14 scans) were obtained from 9 mice expressing GCaMP6f in excitatory neurons and anesthetized with 1% isoflurane on a 9.4 T/20 cm Bruker preclinical MRI using a method reported previously (Pan et al., ISMRM 2024). Light was delivered at 466 nm and 525 nm for excitation of the fluorophor (Fluor) and reflectance imaging of total hemoglobin (HBT), respectively. The emitted and reflected 525 nm light were detected in interleaved fashion at a rate of 50 Hz for each signal. After positioning and shimming on the region of interest, rs-fMRI (GE-EPI; TR 1 s, TE 15 ms, 400 micron isotropic resolution) and WOI were acquired for 20 minutes. Temperature, heart rate, and respiration were maintained within normal physiological ranges.
 WOI and rs-fMRI data were aligned to the Allen mouse brain atlas. Because WOI is confined to the cortical surface, only the cortical parcels were considered in further analysis for both modalities. Preprocessing for rs-fMRI included motion correction, regression of tissue signal from outside the brain, and bandpass filtering (0.01-0.1 Hz). The fluorescence WOI images were corrected for hemodynamic contamination using regression of the HBT images (both filtered to 0.01-0.1 Hz and z-scored).  For this initial analysis, 6 CAPs were obtained for Fluor, HBT, and rs-fMRI using k-means clustering on the 30% of the data with the highest amplitudes.  The remaining data were assigned to the most similar cluster and all data in each cluster were averaged to obtain the final CAPs.  CAPs were obtained for the concatenated rs-fMRI data, but at the individual level for the WOI data due to the large amount of memory needed. Spatial similarity was assessed as the correlation between the pattern of activity in each pair of CAPs, and the relative incidence of each WOI CAP (50 per second) was calculated for each rs-fMRI CAP (1 per second), incorporating a hemodynamic delay estimated using the time of maximum cross-correlation between the Fluor and HBT signals. Group average functional connectivity matrices of pairwise correlation between cortical parcels were created for each modality for comparison.

Results

The average correlation for functional connectivity matrices from rs-fMRI and Fluor was 0.54 +/- 0.1; for rs-fMRI and HBT, 0.54 +/- 0.08; and for Fluor and HBT, 0.83 +/- 0.08, confirming that the spatial structure of the signal is relatively conserved across modalities. A depiction of activity in each of the six CAPs for each modality is shown in the first row of Figure 1, along with the correlation between CAPs obtained from different modalities in the second row. As expected, most CAPs for each modality exhibit areas of stronger-than-average and weaker-than-average signal, and many occur in anticorrelated pairs (e.g., rows 1 and 3 for HBT). Several of the MRI CAPs are relatively flat, suggesting that fewer CAPs may be appropriate for this data or that the inclusion of all time points in the final CAPs is diluting the original pattern. The choice of six CAPs for this preliminary analysis was motivated by the desire to obtain an equal number of patterns across modalities to facilitate spatial comparisons, but in the future, we plan to allow the number of CAPs to vary across modalities. Nevertheless, some Fluor and HBT CAPs are highly similar to those obtained with rs-fMRI (correlation of ~ 0.8). Notably, the Fluor and HBT CAPs are roughly equally similar to those from rs-fMRI, though rs-fMRI is inherently based on hemodynamics and thus might be expected to be more similar to HBT. Correlation between HBT and Fluor CAPs is generally high and roughly one-to-one, indicative of consistent neurovascular coupling across the cortical surface.

As a preliminary investigation of the relationship between the timing of the CAPs, incidence of HBT and Fluor as a function of the rs-fMRI CAP present were calculated (not shown). Certain HBT and Fluor CAPs were more likely to occur during a subset of the MRI CAPs, evidence of coupling across temporal scales.

Conclusion

Coactivation patterns can be obtained at native time scales for rs-fMRI, Fluor and HBT.  Spatial structure was similar across modalities for some but not all CAPs, with fewer rs-fMRI CAPs exhibiting distinct spatial structure than Fluor or HBT CAPs.  Preliminary analysis suggests that the presence of particular rs-fMRI CAPs makes the occurrence of certain Fluor/HBT CAPs more likely. Further work is needed to determine whether all data or only thresholded data should be used for each modality; whether CAPs should be obtained at the group or individual level; the ideal way to set the number of CAPs for each modality; whether subcortical information should be included in the calculation of rs-fMRI CAPs; and the range of frequencies that should be included for WOI analysis.
