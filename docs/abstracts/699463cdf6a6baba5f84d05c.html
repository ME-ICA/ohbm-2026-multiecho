Introduction

Physical exercise in older age can improve cardiorespiratory fitness, a biomarker of health and longevity, and has benefits for brain and cognitive function1. Previous cross-sectional findings have implied relationships between physical exercise and white matter diffusion metrics. Overall, older adults with higher cardiorespiratory fitness also have more preserved white matter in brain regions vulnerable to aging. However, causal evidence from randomized controlled trials is scarce. Meta-analytical evidence from existing studies has demonstrated a positive association between physical exercise and increased fractional anisotropy2. The neurite density index (NDI) and the orientation dispersion index (ODI) can provide more specific information on white matter microstructure than fractional anisotropy alone. However, it remains unclear whether potential benefits from increasing cardiovascular fitness through physical exercise impact both the density and orientation of axons and dendrites (i.e., neurites) of major white matter tracts throughout the brain. In this secondary analysis of a randomized controlled trial (RCT)3, we investigated whether physical exercise improves the neurite density (increase) and orientation dispersion (decrease) indices in healthy older adults.

Methods

We selected 66 healthy older participants (age: 66.4±3.6 y; 43 females) from a larger RCT3 based on the availability of diffusion MRI. Participants were randomly assigned at enrollment to either a multicomponent intervention including aerobic, balance, and coordination exercises (SPO, n=34) or a control intervention consisting of progressive muscle relaxation and listening to podcasts (CON, n=32). Both interventions were online-guided, performed from participants’ homes, and required ~3h/week. Diffusion-weighted MRI was acquired with 2D spin-echo EPI at a Siemens 3T scanner. A total of 104 volumes were acquired with anterior-to-posterior phase encoding (TR=3313 ms, TE=87.2 ms, FA=78°; voxel size = 1.5 mm isotropic). This primary dataset consisted of 16 volumes with b=0 s/mm2 and 88 diffusion-weighted volumes acquired across 30 unique, non-collinear directions at three different b-shells (800, 1600, and 2500 s/mm2). Additionally, 104 posterior-to-anterior phase-encoding volumes (same parameters) and a high-resolution anatomical volume (MPRAGE; voxel size = 0.8 mm isotropic) were acquired. White-matter data were preprocessed and tracts were reconstructed using TRACULA4, and NDI and ODI were obtained5. Right and left tract values were averaged, yielding 26 white-matter tracts (Fig. 1). We tested the global effect of intervention on NDI and ODI using mixed-effects linear models (fixed factors: Timepoint and Group; covariates: Age and Sex; random factors: Subjects and Tracts). To test the intervention effect on each NDI/ODI of each tract separately, we used a Bayesian multilevel modeling framework. To assess the potential implications of intervention-induced changes in white matter, we examined the Pearson correlations between cognitive scores previously reported3 and the diffusion metrics.

Results

We found a significant Timepoint × Group interaction (95% Confidence Interval, CI [-0.01, -0.002]) on NDI but not on ODI (95% CI [-0.004, 0.003]). Follow-up contrasts to interpret the interaction revealed a significant NDI increase from pre- to post-intervention in SPO (95% CI [-0.002, -0.0001]) but not in CON (95% CI [-0.0003, 0.002]). The Bayesian multilevel model revealed that all but four major white-matter tracts (middle cerebellar peduncle, corticospinal tract, corpus callosum body-central, and corpus callosum splenium) showed strong evidence for a Timepoint × Group interaction (Bayes Factor = 21.11) on NDI only (Fig. 2). Cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2max) increased for SPO post-intervention (difference = -0.09).  However, the Timepoint × Group interaction was not significant (95% CI [-0.20, 0.01]). Relatively high baseline VO2max across groups might have limited the magnitude of observable fitness gains. Increased NDI correlated with improved cardiorespiratory fitness post-intervention in SPO in the inferior longitudinal fasciculus, the corpus callosum body-central, and the anterior commissure (all p-values<0.040). Increase in the Rey-Osterrieth memory score post-intervention in SPO correlated with the anterior commissure’s increase in NDI (r=0.40, p=0.019). This correlation coefficient, however, did not differ from that of CON (p=0.138). No other significant correlations with cognition were found.

Conclusion

Eight weeks of online-guided physical exercise likely and unspecifically increases neurite density across major white-matter brain pathways in older adults. This increase, especially in association and commissural tracts, correlated with improved cardiorespiratory fitness. No clear decrease in orientation dispersion or association between white matter and cognitive changes could be observed. As our sample consisted of healthy older adults with relatively high fitness, future studies should confirm changes in neurite density resulting from physical exercise and investigate whether an association with cognitive changes is found in samples with lower baseline cardiorespiratory fitness or among individuals at higher risk of cognitive decline.
