Introduction

Normative modeling is a promising tool to parse biological heterogeneity by leveraging existing large datasets1. This approach is designed to chart the trajectory of biological variables across the lifespan in aggregated large-scale samples, and enables the calculation of deviations from that population reference norm (z-scores) at the individual level. Importantly, it enables the calculation of z-scores in unseen samples, provided a calibration set is used to account for new site effects. While some previous whole-brain voxel-level normative models of structural MRI have been published, those were bespoke to some degree, designed to examine deviations in childhood adversity or copy number variant carriers, respectively2,3.

Preterm birth occurs in 10% of births worldwide and is associated with an elevated risk of long-term neurological and cognitive impairments. However, previous studies have demonstrated substantial heterogeneity among individuals born preterm, with neurodevelopmental trajectories ranging from significant impairments to outcomes within the typical range4.

Here, we thus present newly estimated large-scale voxelwise normative models using a state-of-the-art MRI preprocessing pipeline shown to be superior to the one used in previous voxelwise models, with a clinical application to long-term brain development after preterm birth. 

Methods

Voxelwise normative models were estimated using available large-scale cohorts, including UKBiobank, ABCD, CMI-HBN CamCAN, HCP-1200, HCP-Dev, HCP-Aging, IXI, PING, and PNC. All T1-weighted anatomical 3T MRI scans were preprocessed with the ANTs toolbox. For every voxel, log jacobian determinants (JD) were extracted as volumetric measures of expansion or contraction, alongside the more commonly used grey and white matter volumes (GMV and WMV). Final sample sizes included N=58,597 observations for log(JD) and N=53,711 observations for GMV and WMV, from 53 scan sites. 

For each volumetric measure (log(JD), GMV or WMV), normative models were estimated for each separate voxel with the warped Bayesian Linear Regression approach from the PCNtoolkit (https://github.com/amarquand/PCNtoolkit/ , version 1.1.0) using age as a covariate, and binary sex and site as batch effects, with a train-test split of 50-50. The estimated models thus produced site-corrected and sex-specific z-scores across the lifespan, reflecting the degree to which individual brain anatomy deviates from the biological norm at voxel-level. 

Estimated models were then transferred to an independent longitudinal cohort, generationR, including N=8,172 total observations, with five percent (N=493) used as calibration set to account for new site effects. Finally, in both ABCD and genR, the proportion of extreme z-scores (|z-scores| ≥ 2) was compared at each timepoint between subjects with preterm birth (N=63 extremely preterm subjects with  gestational age ≤ 28 weeks in ABCD, N=284 preterm subjects with gestational age ≤ 37 weeks in genR) and a same-sized, randomly selected group of subjects born at term. 

Results

Voxelwise models accounted for as much as 69%, 79% and 78% of the variance in log(JD), GMV and WMV respectively. Explained variance was generally higher in the ventricles and in the surrounding white matter. Across all three modalities, the shape of the modelled distribution was appropriate for the data in that the z-score distributions derived from the voxel-wise models generally showed acceptably low skewness (|skew| < 1) and excess kurtosis (|kurtosis|<5). 

A higher proportion of subjects with preterm birth showed extreme z-scores relative to controls born at-term in widespread voxels across the brain. This difference in proportion of extreme deviations, almost exclusively negative deviations, was found in both genR and ABCD, and persisted across timepoints (ages 9-10 and 11-12 in ABCD, ages 10-11, 13-15, 17-19 in genR).

Conclusion

The present work demonstrates that the normative modeling framework is scalable to whole-brain voxel-level precision using structural MRI, while still remaining transferable to unseen datasets in separate computing environments. The latter is of particularly interest for clinical datasets that cannot easily be shared with external institutions. 

Herein, whole-brain normative models based on large-scale reference samples were used to parse out the heterogeneity of brain morphometry after preterm birth at the voxel level. A larger proportion of subjects with preterm birth were found to have extreme deviations compared to controls born at-term in voxels widespread across the brain, and this persisted across childhood and adolescence. This aligns with the heterogeneity of brain development after preterm birth reported in the literature4, while demonstrating that normative approaches are well-equipped to investigate more subtle, variance-based patterns in brain morphometry at the individual level.
