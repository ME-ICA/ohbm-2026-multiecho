Introduction

Magnetoencephalography (MEG) and electroencephalography (EEG) provide unique, non-invasive access to human brain dynamics with millisecond temporal resolution and are widely used to study perception, cognition, and neurological disorders. However, MEG and EEG analyses rely on complex, multi-stage workflows encompassing artifact correction, event handling, spectral and evoked analyses, and source reconstruction, each of which can substantially influence final results. Although mature and widely adopted analysis toolkits are available, neurophysiology studies frequently employ custom, dataset-specific pipelines that vary across laboratories, software versions, and computing environments. This heterogeneity introduces analytical variability, limits reproducibility, and hampers direct comparison and integration of results across studies.

In contrast to other neuroimaging modalities, such as functional MRI, MEG/EEG currently lacks standardized, scalable, and cloud-executable workflows that support both preprocessing and downstream analyses across heterogeneous datasets and experimental paradigms. This limitation is particularly restrictive given the growing availability of large, open neurophysiology datasets and the increasing emphasis on FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) data practices. Addressing this gap is therefore critical for enabling reproducible, large-scale, and collaborative neurophysiology research, as well as for facilitating the reuse and integration of MEG/EEG data across studies, sites, and populations.


Methods

We developed a cloud-native framework providing fully containerized preprocessing and analysis pipelines for MEG and EEG data using MNE-Python¹, deployed on the brainlife.io platform². Pipelines are executed through containerized applications in Docker/Singularity that encapsulate all software dependencies and parameters, ensuring portable and reproducible execution across computing environments. The framework supports BIDS-compliant datasets as a preferred standard, while also accommodating non-BIDS data through explicit metadata specification and standardized data conversion steps. Dedicated pipelines are provided for resting-state and task-based recordings, with analyses performed in both sensor and source space.

Preprocessing pipeline

A common preprocessing pipeline is applied to both resting-state and task-based data. This includes automated detection of bad channels and time segments, temporal filtering, artifact attenuation using signal space projection (SSP) and/or independent component analysis (ICA), channel interpolation, and normalization of sensor signals. Modality-specific procedures are explicitly incorporated, including Maxwell filtering and the use of head-position information for MEG data, and electrode montage handling and re-referencing for EEG data. All preprocessing steps are implemented within version-controlled containers with explicit parameterization and automated provenance tracking, enabling full reproducibility and transparent inspection of processing choices.

Resting-state pipeline

For resting-state recordings, continuous data are segmented into equal-length epochs to enable standardized spectral analysis. Power spectral density is estimated using Welch’s method, from which peak frequency, absolute power, and relative power within canonical frequency bands are derived. Region-specific summaries are computed by aggregating sensor-level measures over predefined channel groups, enabling reproducible comparison across subjects and datasets. This pipeline has been applied to large-scale resting-state MEG and EEG datasets to support consistent spectral characterization across heterogeneous recordings²,³.

Task-based pipeline

The task-based pipeline supports a wide range of EEG and MEG experimental paradigms and incorporates structured event handling based on dataset metadata. Continuous or concatenated recordings are segmented into epochs, with optional generation of trial-level metadata to support downstream statistical analyses. Baseline correction and evoked-response computation are performed following artifact correction using SSP and/or ICA. Additional modality-specific steps include re-referencing for EEG and interpolation of bad channels. Source-space analyses shared with the resting-state pipeline include estimation of noise covariance, forward modeling using subject-specific or template head models, and source reconstruction of task-related cortical activity.

Results

The framework was validated on a diverse set of open and in-house MEG and EEG datasets, including the CamCAN resting-state MEG dataset (N > 652), the Brainstorm auditory oddball tutorial dataset (ds000246, N = 1), the MNE checkerboard sample dataset (ds000248, N = 1), the Wakeman and Henson multimodal MEG/EEG dataset (ds00117, N = 19), and an in-lab gaze task EEG dataset (N = 22)⁴. Collectively, these datasets span multiple acquisition systems (CTF, Elekta/MEGIN, EGI), sensor layouts, sampling rates, experimental paradigms, and noise environments, providing a rigorous test of the pipelines’ generalizability.

Across all datasets, the preprocessing pipelines executed successfully for all subjects, producing consistent outputs. On average, 1–2 channels per dataset were flagged as bad, and 1–3% of epochs were excluded due to artifacts. Artifact attenuation using SSP and ICA consistently reduced ocular and cardiac artifacts across MEG and EEG recordings. Modality-specific preprocessing steps, including Maxwell filtering for MEG and average re-referencing for EEG, were applied reliably, and containerized execution ensured full computational reproducibility across repeated runs and computing environments.

For resting-state data, the pipelines generated reproducible power spectral density (PSD) estimates, with stable peak frequency, absolute power, and relative power measures across subjects. Region-specific summaries allowed cross-subject and cross-dataset comparison. Using the CamCAN dataset, we analyzed the relationship between individual alpha peak frequency and age across the adult lifespan (18–88 years). Results replicated expected trends, showing a gradual decline in peak frequency with increasing age, demonstrating the pipeline’s ability to support biologically meaningful analyses at scale.

For task-based data, the pipelines successfully generated epoched data and evoked responses aligned with task events. Source-space analyses produced spatially consistent cortical activation patterns across subjects and experimental paradigms. Cloud-based execution enabled parallel processing of all >600 CamCAN subjects in <48 hours, demonstrating the framework’s scalability and suitability for large datasets.

Overall, these results demonstrate that the pipelines reliably process heterogeneous MEG and EEG data, produce reproducible and high-quality outputs, and enable large-scale analyses across both resting-state and task paradigms.

Conclusion

We present a fully containerized, cloud-executable framework that integrates standardized preprocessing and analysis pipelines for both resting-state and task-based MEG and EEG data, operating in sensor and source space. By combining MNE-Python based workflows with cloud execution, automated provenance tracking, integrated visual quality-assessment checkpoints, and FAIR-aligned data management, the framework minimizes analytical variability, reduces manual intervention, and ensures fully reproducible processing across heterogeneous datasets. Containerization and explicit parameter tracking provide portable, version-controlled execution that can be seamlessly shared and re-executed across laboratories and computing environments.

Validation across multiple open and in-house datasets, including large-scale resting-state cohorts and diverse task paradigms, demonstrates the framework’s robustness, scalability, and adaptability to different acquisition systems, sensor layouts, and noise conditions. The platform supports efficient, parallelized cloud-based processing, enabling large-scale analyses that would be impractical using conventional local infrastructure.

By providing standardized, reproducible, and scalable pipelines, this framework lays the groundwork for rigorous cross-dataset comparisons, large-scale meta-analyses, and collaborative multi-site neurophysiology research. It facilitates the application of FAIR principles to MEG and EEG, supports transparent evaluation of intermediate and final outputs, and accelerates the adoption of robust, shareable workflows across the neuroscience community. Ultimately, this resource addresses a critical gap in neurophysiology research infrastructure, promoting reproducibility, interoperability, and broad accessibility of MEG/EEG data processing.
