Introduction

Myelination is a critical aspect of brain development in which layers of myelin form around axons, improving neuronal signal transmission speed and efficiency.1 Williams syndrome (WS) is a rare neurodevelopmental disorder that results from a hemideletion of ~26 genes at chromosomal locus 7q11.23, including general transcription factor II-I (GTF2I), which has been linked to alterations in myelin thickness and integrity in rodent models and human post-mortem tissue2. Regional distributions of myelination in the brain can be quantified in vivo by using the MRI sequence “multicomponent driven equilibrium single pulse observation of T1 and T2” (mcDESPOT)3 to generate maps of myelin water fraction (MWF), allowing analysis of group differences and age-based trends. We acquired mcDESPOT longitudinally in order to model developmental trajectories of white-matter myelination and test for alterations in children and adolescents with WS compared to typically developing (TD) individuals matched for age and sex.

Methods

We acquired mcDESPOT data longitudinally from 20 individuals with WS (15 female) who participated in 48 visits (age range 7-24, mean=15.3±4.7 years) and 72 TD individuals (52 female) who participated in 189 visits (age range 8-22, mean=13.9±2.6 years). We computed voxelwise MWF for each mcDESPOT session using a three-pool model and performed analyses in native space as well as in standard space, where images were anatomically normalized to a study-specific template using a longitudinally aware approach incorporating SPM and ANTS tools. In native space, we performed ROI analyses of MWF at a macroscale level (white matter in whole cerebrum and in whole cerebellum as identified by Freesurfer) and at a tract-specific level using automated tractography methods4 that defined up to 42 tracts based on contemporaneously acquired diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) on these same participants. Spatiotemporal patterns of myelin were analyzed using a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) approach as implemented in R’s gamm package (for native-space ROI analyses) and with AFNI’s 3dMSS5 (for voxelwise analyses).

Results

We found whole cerebrum-level white matter myelin deficits of 4-7% in WS (p<0.004, Cohen’s D ≈ 0.6), but did not find alterations in the cerebellum. There was a main effect of age (p=0.04) in both groups, but no group-by-age interaction. We also found a main effect of hemisphere (p<0.001), with higher MWF in the right cerebral hemisphere, but we did not detect a group-by-hemisphere interaction. At the level of individual tracts, we identified deficits in the WS group (pFDR<0.05) in 20 of the 42 identified tracts, including forceps minor, bilateral superior and inferior longitudinal fasciculi, bilateral cingulum, anterior thalamic radiation, and frontal aslant tract. There were no findings in the opposite direction. Effect sizes (Cohen’s D) ranged from 0.4 to 0.7. At a voxelwise level, we found widespread clusters of deficits (pFDR < 0.01) in regional patterns that generally reflected the locations of the standard-space-averaged versions of the myelin-deficient tracts identified in native space of all participants. We did not find either group-by-age interactions or an effect of sex in any of the modeled tract trajectories.

Conclusion

We found widespread myelin deficits within the cerebrum in a group of individuals with Williams syndrome relative to typically developing controls at both macroscale and fine levels, with finer-grained approaches revealing that roughly half of the studied tracts showed deficient myelination following an overall pattern in which altered tracts tended to be frontal or longitudinal. Developmental trajectories within the cerebrum showed relatively parallel increases in myelination of the two groups throughout childhood and adolescence, such that the Williams syndrome group continuously trailed the typically developing group. Myelination within the cerebellum was not found to be altered in Williams syndrome. This approach broadens our understanding of the role of genetically driven myelination in this neurodevelopmental disorder, and potentially offers quantitative outcome measures and treatment target for future myelin-focused treatment strategies in WS.
