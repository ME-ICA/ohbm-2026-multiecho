Introduction

Current diffusion MRI (dMRI) tractography methods enable to map the white matter long range connectivity. However, due to challenges inherent to data acquisition and modeling, including limited resolution and reconstruction uncertainty, many of the reconstructed pathways are anatomically implausible. Implausible streamlines adopt trajectories potentially violating the known anatomical constraints, yield unreliable tractograms, and undermine conclusions drawn from structural connectivity.^1 Multiple works have proposed methods for outlier rejection and streamline bundling; however, most processing methods consider streamlines as indisputable entities that can only be kept, rejected or assigned to a group (e.g.,  plausibles/implausibles, or bundles). However, none of these methods allows to model streamline circuits as multi-step (multi-synaptic) instances. In this work, we address the limitations of existing tractography analysis frameworks by modeling streamlines as point-separable entities, one point at a time, based on prior knowledge and a set of operators employing natural language syntax. We demonstrate the proposed operator on the extraction of cerebellar pathways.

Methods

One participant from the minimally-processed Human Connectome Project-Young Adult (HCP-YA) S1200 release dataset is selected for demonstration purposes.^2 We perform brain tissue parcellation and segmentation following the Desikan-Killiany atlas using FreeSurfer on the T1w volume. We delineate manually the cerebellar dentate nuclei on the spherical mean data computed on the b =1000 s mm−2 dMRI volume.^3 We then combine the thalamic nuclei segmentation obtained from FreeSurfer and the cerebellar dentate segmentations into a single labelmap for our participant. Whole-brain streamline tractography is reconstructed applying Kalman filter tractography on the dMRI data.^4

We adopt the White Matter Query Language (WMQL) to implement a set of operators that enable tractography streamlines to be bounded by the particular regions of interest (ROIs) considered.^5 Specifically, we extend the WMQL to support multi-synaptic trajectories: shown in Figure 1, we introduce a new between operator that allows streamlines to be split in a set of trajectories defined by gray matter nuclei acting as relay nodes. We consider that a streamlines satisfies a connectivity criterion between a pair ROIs when at least one point along the streamline traverses them.

In this work, we investigate the pathways that constitute the superior cerebellar peduncle (SCP). The SCP pathways are known to stem from the dentate nuclei, decussate and reach the contralateral thalamic nuclei. However, unless the appropriate seeding mechanisms and exclusion criteria are used when propagating streamlines, tractography algorithms also reconstruct ipsilateral pathways. We apply our post hoc point-based streamline modeling framework to disentangle these two fiber populations.

Conclusion

Careful tractography streamline post-processing is required to elucidate the complex wiring pattern of the brain white matter. In this work, we demonstrate that point-wise streamline post-processing can be a useful tool to model multi-synaptic axonal pathway trajectories. Results in Figure 2 show that the natural language-based WMQL tool can be harnessed to implement an intuitive point-based streamline processing framework towards that goal.

We introduce a simple yet effective point-based approach into the popular WMQL framework that harnesses the expressive power of logical operators to define and explore complex anatomical trajectories. This approach addresses key methodological limitations of current tractography analysis tools through a post hoc mechanism to interpret and refine streamlines —particularly to model connectivity across multiple gray matter nuclei.

Our point-based modeling framework, in conjunction with finely parcellated regions of interest, enables precise spatial reasoning over the trajectory of individual streamline points. This granularity allows for the incorporation of detailed anatomical knowledge. Rather than treating streamlines as monolithic entities, our point-based modeling framework allows to encode a priori knowledge that reflects true neuroanatomical transitions —such as entering and exiting specific gray matter regions, including intermediate nuclei, or conforming to specific topological organizations.

Our results constitute a step towards enabling more reliable axonal pathway identification. As higher resolution diffusion and structural MRI data are acquired, and finer parcellations become available, a richer set of logical and spatial operators can be used to encode anatomical priors with fine granularity. This may include modeling distinct connectivity patterns according to the organization of cortical layers, or functional regions on nuclei, among others. Thus, by leveraging anatomical constraints, an effective natural language tractography querying framework, and an underlying point-based modeling approach, increasingly accurate white matter connectivity patterns may be uncovered. Future work includes extending our results to other deep white matter bundles, including cortical axonal projections.

In summary, we propose a principled point-based modeling framework implemented as a set of operators employing a natural modeling language to provide increased control and expressiveness in defining and characterizing tractography streamline results. We provide evidence that modeling streamlines as point-separable entities within a natural language query framework can help processing increasingly complex anatomical constraints and axonal pathways in the white matter. This work lays the foundation towards delivering higher fidelity structural brain connectivity patterns and maps through post hoc tractography analysis. Ultimately, this fine-grained and anatomically informed modeling strategy can contribute to bridge the gap between tractography outputs and the actual wiring architecture of the brain.
