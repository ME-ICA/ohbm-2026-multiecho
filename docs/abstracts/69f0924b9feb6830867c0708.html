Introduction

The hippocampus is a critical structure for neurodevelopment, yet segmenting its subfields in neonatal MRI remains a persistent challenge. Neonatal neuroimaging is made challenging due to inverted contrasts, partial myelination, and motion artifacts, which blur the gray-white matter interface and complicate the separation of hippocampal gray matter from surrounding structures. While adult-centric tools like HippUnfold1 provide a robust framework, they exhibit elevated failure rates (~15%) even on good-quality neonatal research datasets (dHCP2). These failures increase in noisy scans where severe motion or inverse myelination obscures boundaries. Furthermore, manual segmentation, currently a reliable reference standard, is labor-intensive and limited by MRI resolution compared to the histological gold standard.

While infant-specific deep learning models exist3, they frequently rely on volumetric approaches that struggle to capture the hippocampus’s intrinsic morphology as a contiguous, folded sheet. Although synthetic data training (e.g., SynthSeg4) improves robustness to contrast variability, standard implementations often fail to enforce these topological constraints. Consequently, when exposed to out-of-distribution data with high noise-to-signal ratios, false positives/negatives increase, leading to topological breaks that violate anatomical reality.

To address this, here we introduce SynthTopo, a framework integrating domain-randomized synthetic training with a Persistent Homology (PH) topological loss5. By enforcing topology that is based on anatomy, specifically treating the hippocampus as a continuous topological feature rather than independent voxels, our approach mitigates human-rater errors and ensures robust, topology-preserving segmentation even in highly noisy data. Ultimately, this model will be integrated into HippUnfold to enable robust unfolding and morphometry in neonatal cohorts.

Methods

Dataset & Task: We utilized data from the Developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP), comprising 783 neonates scanned across 887 sessions. A reliable reference standard subset of 169 unilateral segmentations was generated via an iterative semi-automated framework: the adult HippUnfold model was fine-tuned on neonatal data through successive cycles of prediction, expert quality control by M.G., B.K., and E.S., and retraining to ensure anatomical fidelity. We stratified this data into a training set of 135 term-born neonates and a distinct test set of 34 subjects (Term born n=7, Term Equivalent age n=4, Preterm born n=23) to evaluate generalizability. To test generalization, we utilized a local dataset of extremely preterm neonates of 9 subjects (<28 weeks’ gestation) characterized by severe motion and low SNR (Figure 2).

Segmentation Target: Our objective is to train a deep learning model to segment 8 distinct Hippocampal Gray Matter (GM), Stratum Radiatum, Lacunosum, and Moleculare (SRLM), Medial Temporal Lobe cortex (MTLc), Pial surface, Hippocampal-Amygdaloid Transition Area (HATA), Indusium Griseum (IndGris), Cysts, and the Dentate Gyrus (DG). These tissue segmentations are the prerequisite input for HippUnfold, which uses them as anatomical endpoints to solve Laplace’s equation and to computationally "unfold" the hippocampus. Subfield labels are subsequently propagated onto this unfolded space from a high-resolution atlas.

Segmentation Backbone: We employed SynthSeg (domain randomization) as the segmentation backbone. This framework generates training data with randomized contrast and resolution on-the-fly, eliminating the need for extensive real-world annotations and ensuring robustness to the variable contrast seen in neonatal MRI.

SynthTopo (Proposed Method): To ensure the hippocampus is segmented as a continuous folded sheet even in highly noisy data, we integrated a Persistent Homology (PH) topological loss. Unlike pixel-wise objectives that ignore global shape, PH analyzes probability maps across filtration thresholds to distinguish persistent anatomical features from noise. Leveraging anatomical knowledge, we defined topological constraints using Betti numbers: enforcing single connected components (B0=1) for individual structures (e.g., DG) and constraining the union of adjacent laminations (e.g., GM, DG, and SRLM) to form a single contiguous complex. To resolve the optimization conflict between topological and pixel-wise gradients, we employed a two-stage training strategy. The model is first pretrained with Dice/Cross-Entropy for localization, then fine-tuned with the combined objective LTotal = LDice + wtopo * LTopo. This end-to-end method learns topology directly from data, avoiding iterative post-processing bottlenecks during inference, as was the case in the original paper5.

Results

Model Benchmarking: On the high-quality dHCP test set (in-distribution), nnUNetv2 (Old Hippunfold) yielded the highest overlap (Dice 0.714). However, SynthSeg (Dice 0.686) and the proposed SynthTopo (Dice 0.689) maintained competitive accuracy while leveraging synthetic data for domain independence. A hyper-parameter tuning study identified a topological weight of wtopo=0.001 as the optimal balance between pixel-wise accuracy and structural connectivity.

Qualitative & Topological Impact: Despite similar Dice scores, performance diverged significantly on highly noisy, out-of-distribution scans. nnUNetv2 (Old Hippunfold) failed to generalize, collapsing into fragmented predictions incompatible with unfolding (Figure 2B). While the SynthSeg baseline was more robust, it often produced topological breaks, such as discontinuous components (Figure 2C). SynthTopo corrected these deficiencies (Figure 2D), successfully recovering the hippocampus's continuous "folded sheet" morphology. By combining topological validity with raw pixel overlap, our method ensured the anatomical correctness required for downstream unfolding, even in scans with severe motion artifacts.

Conclusion

We present SynthTopo, a novel framework that integrates synthetic learning with topological data analysis. By benchmarking against nnUNetv2 and standard SynthSeg, here we demonstrate that a synthetic backbone fine-tuned with topological loss offers the best trade-off between quantitative accuracy and generalizability to highly noisy data. Crucially, by segmenting the hippocampus' continuous "folded sheet" morphology, our method enables robust downstream unfolding (using HippUnfold) even in scans with severe artifacts, a capability previously limited by topological segmentation failures and required postprocessing that degrades the level of detail. Future work will extend this label-efficient framework to other small datasets, leveraging the unsupervised topological signal to mitigate structural breaks in data-scarce environments.
