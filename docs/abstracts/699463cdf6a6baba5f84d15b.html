Introduction

Suicidal ideation (SI) has been known to be a critical predictor of suicide attempts and death by suicide. The prevalence (12.1%) of SI is high during adolescence because this developmental period is characterized by dramatic biological, emotional, and social changes1. SI is even more prevalent (27.3%) in adolescents with major depressive disorders (MDD)2. For these reasons, a deeper understanding of SI may contribute to prevention of suicide attempts and death in adolescents with MDD. Of suicide theories, the three-step theory of suicide postulated that pain and hopelessness are contributing factors in SI3. However, little evidence exists how the brain regions involved in pain and hopelessness are related to SI in adolescents with MDD. This study aimed to examine clinical characteristics and resting-state functional connectivity in key regions of pain and hopelessness in depressed adolescents with SI compared to those without SI. 

Methods

This study included 73 adolescents with MDD (age M (SD) = 15.0 (1.5) years, 51 girls) and 45 healthy adolescents (age M (SD) = 14.2 (1.4) years, 25 girls). Adolescents with MDD were classified into two groups based on SI-related items from the Kiddie-Schedule for Affective Disorders and Schizophrenia for School-Age Children-Present and Lifetime Version (MDDSI+: MDD with SI and MDDSI-: MDD without SI). The MDDSI+ and MDDSI- groups included 30 (age M (SD) = 15.0 (1.6) years, 22 girls) and 23 adolescents (age M (SD) = 14.8 (1.4) years,12 girls), respectively. Adolescents with MDD completed the Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) to measure their suicidal ideation severity and intensity. All participants’ clinical and behavioral features were assessed using the Children’s Depression Rating Scale-revised (CDRS-R), Screening for Childhood Anxiety Related Emotional Disorders (SCARED), Child Behavior Checklist and ADHD Rating Scale. Early adversity and social functions were also assessed using the Early Trauma Inventory, Peer Victimization Scale, and Family Adaptability and Cohesion Evaluation Scale. Two-sample t-tests were conducted to find differences in the clinical, behavioral, and social measures between the MDDSI+ and MDDSI- groups. Resting-state functional images were processed using the AFNI pipeline. The dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (dACC) and anterior insula, key areas of pain and the salience network4 were used as seed regions. The amygdala, one of core regions associated with negative bias in depression5 was also employed as a seed region. Resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) of the seed regions were analyzed to identify neural correlates of suicidal ideation in adolescents with MDD by comparing rsFC between the MDDSI+ and MDDSI- groups. Cluster-wise corrections were applied to correct multiple comparisons and Bonferroni correction was also applied to correct for multiple seed regions. Furthermore, altered rsFC was correlated with suicidal ideation severity and intensity assessed by the C-SSRS. 

Results

Adolescents in the MDDSI+ group reported higher suicidal ideation severity (Mann-Whitney = 114, p < 0.001) and intensity (Mann-Whitney = 151, p < 0.001) compared to those in the MDDSI-group. They also higher CDRS-R (t = 3.08, p = 0.003) and SCARED scores (t = 2.72, p  = 0.009) than those in MDDSI-. There were no significant group differences in age, sex, and other behavioral and social measures. MDDSI+ adolescents showed increased rsFC with the right and left amygdala with prefrontal cortices, including the inferior frontal gyri (IFG) extending to precentral gyri (PCG) and dorsomedial prefrontal cortex (dMPFC), compared to adolescents in the MDDSI- group (Bonferroni-corrected p < 0.01) (Figure 1). These increased rsFC in the MDDSI+ group were also significantly different from those in healthy controls. Altered rsFC between the right amygdala and left IFG/PCG was positively correlated with suicidal ideation severity (partial r = 0.41, p = 0.003), controlling for age, sex, CDRS-R, and SCARED scores. However, rsFC in the dACC and AI seeds was not significantly different between MDDSI+ and MDDSI- groups. 

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that MDD adolescents were more severely depressed and anxious, and showed increased rsFC between the amygdala and prefrontal regions. The alteration in the amygdala rsFC may be a particularly important neural marker of suicidal ideation in adolescents with MDD. Such clinical and neural characteristics of suicidal ideation may be targeted for developing and optimizing inventions in MDD adolescents with suicidal ideation.
