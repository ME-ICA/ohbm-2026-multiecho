Introduction

The insular cortex acts as a central hub of the allostatic-interoceptive system, integrating visceral, affective and cognitive inputs across widely distributed cortical and subcortical networks. Recent, high field 7T fMRI data1 has demonstrated reproducible group-level functional connectivity profiles of insular subregions that support distinct functionality within interoception and allostatic control. However, recent high field structural profiling2 demonstrated that atlas choice based on region-of-interest (ROI) specification- whether derived from microarchitecture or macroscopic anatomy- strongly influences the presence of insula clusters that are observed, raising the possibility that functional inferences may be partly driven by atlas choice. Therefore, fine grained characterisation of these connectivity patterns can be limited by heterogenous definitions of insula subregions across anatomical atlases. The insula ROI’s of the original 7T data set1 were selected based on previous fMRI studies of interoception and allostasis3, but such task-driven definitions may not generalise across different parcellation schemes. To reduce ROI selection bias and test the replicability of connectivity patterns independent of the original atlas, we will re-parcellate the insula using the Human Brainnetome atlas4, which provides high-resolution, connectivity-defined subdivisions derived from multimodal MRI. Two analyses will be conducted: (1) identification of seed-to-voxel functional connectivity patterns across three concatenated runs, and (2) assessment of connectivity reliability across individual 10-minute runs. We hypothesise that insular re-parcellation will preserve functionally distinct connectivity patterns of insular subregions comparable to those previously reported1 and that connectivity profiles will demonstrate consistent reliability across individual runs, providing a direct evaluation of run-wise insular subregion functional connectivity reliability at 7T.

Methods

Pre-processed resting-state fMRI data from 84 participants (N = 6 excluded due to incomplete functional data) in the Zhang et al. (2025) OpenNeuro dataset were analysed in MNI152 space. Each participant completed one anatomical scan and three consecutive 10-minute resting-state fMRI runs. The analysis was conducted on the pre-processed data provided by the original authors in the OpenNeuro dataset. Resting-state data underwent a rigorous preprocessing pipeline, including reorientation, slice-timing correction, concatenation of the three runs, coregistration to each subject’s T1w-EPI image using boundary-based registration, and motion correction. Nuisance regression removed motion-related and physiological noise using six motion parameters and mean time courses extracted from white matter and multiple cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) compartments, including a manually defined cerebral aqueduct mask to account for brainstem-related pulsatility. Data were then temporally filtered (0.01–0.08 Hz), normalized to MNI152 space using ANTs affine and symmetric diffeomorphic transformations, detrended, minimally smoothed (1.25 mm FWHM), and resampled to cortical surfaces. All registrations were visually inspected for quality.

A total of 210 cortical and 36 subcortical seed regions were defined using the maximum probability map from the Human Brainnetome Atlas4. Seed-based functional connectivity analyses (Fisher Z-transformed correlation) were run from the left and right hypergranular insula (G), left and right ventral agranular insula (vIa), left and right dorsal agranular insula (dIa), left and right ventral dysgranular and granular insula (vId/vIg), left and right dorsal granular insula (dIg) and the left and right dorsal dysgranular insula (dId) to whole-brain separately using data from all three runs in CONN, MATLAB5. Seed-to-voxel connectivity was thresholded at p < .001 (voxel-wise, uncorrected) and q < .01 (cluster-level, FDR-corrected). Test-retest reliability of insular connectivity profiles was assessed using a two-way mixed-effects intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC (3,1)) in MATLAB to quantify the consistency of single run fMRI measurements across repeated resting-state fMRI acquisitions. The dataset’s pre-processed 4D files were split into their constituent volumes (256 volumes per run; TR = 2340 ms; TE = 28 ms) to reconstruct and inspect individual 10-minute resting-state runs.

Results

Significant (p <. 001, uncorrected; q-FDR <.01) seed-based functional connectivity profiles of all three runs revealed increased connectivity between the bilateral G-pre/postcentral gyrus and the precuneus and decreased bilateral G- frontal pole and lateral occipital cortex (LOC) connectivity. The bilateral vIa demonstrated increased connectivity with the pre/postcentral gyrus and precuneus, and decreased connectivity with the LOC and frontal pole. The bilateral dIa showed increased connectivity with the frontal pole and decreased connectivity with the LOC, precuneus, middle temporal gyrus, and superior parietal lobule. The bilateral vId/vIg exhibited increased connectivity with the frontal pole and postcentral gyrus, and decreased connectivity with the LOC, cerebellum, and precuneus. The bilateral dIg demonstrated increased connectivity with the pre/postcentral gyrus and precuneus, and decreased connectivity with the frontal pole. Finally, the bilateral dId showed increased connectivity with the pre/postcentral gyrus and anterior cingulate gyrus, along with decreased connectivity involving the frontal pole and posterior cingulate gyrus. ICC estimates across the 12 seed regions (left and right) were stable (ICC = 0.524 - 0.673), with overlapping 95% confidence intervals (lower bounds 0.349 - 0.535; upper bounds 0.664 - 0.775), indicating moderate and consistent reliability. 

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate that insular subregions exhibit distinct and functionally meaningful connectivity patterns, a comparable observation to that of the original authors1. Additionally, these complementary analyses highlight that the connectivity patterns are preserved across repeated resting-state fMRI runs. The moderate and consistent reliability estimates indicate that Brainnetome defined insular parcellations provide stable functional profiles, reducing sensitivity to anatomical atlas choice. Together, these findings support the efficacy of connectivity-based parcellations for robust analyses of interoceptive and allostatic network organisation and underscore the structural and functional diversity of the insular cortex.
