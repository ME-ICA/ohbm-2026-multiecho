Introduction

Maternal diabetes (MD) and maternal obesity (MO) are linked to adverse physical, mental, and neurodevelopmental outcomes in offspring. Although fetal-programming effects of MD and MO often differ by sex, it remains unclear whether these exposures independently or jointly shape early functional brain development.

Methods

This was a cross-sectional study of 1,113 infants (age [Mean (SD) = 25.7 (18.2) days, 48.8% female, 82.9% full-term, 8.1% exposed to MD, 19.8% exposed to MO, 3.7% exposed to both) from 7 international cohorts. Resting state fMRI data were processed (Figure 1) and networks of interest were derived in three clusters (Primary, Cognitive, and Socioemotional), and 9 summary measures were created quantifying 1) within-cluster within-network, 2) within-cluster between-network, and 3) between-cluster between-network functional components. ComBat was implemented to harmonize across sites, and linear regression models were used to examine associations of functional connectivity components with independent and interaction effects of MD, MO, and sex, adjusting for covariates. FDR corrections were applied. Pairwise comparisons were conducted to explore differences in connectivity between exposures. Sensitivity analysis was conducted excluding infants born pre-term.

Results

A significant MD × MO interaction was observed for within-network connectivity in the cognitive cluster (standardized β = –0.660, 95% CI: –1.124 to –0.196, FDR-corrected P = 0.048; Figure 2A). Infants with combined exposure showed reduced connectivity compared with those exposed only to MD (standardized β = –0.617, 95% CI: -1.055, -0.180, P = 0.006) and only to MO (standardized β = –0.354, 95% CI: -0.703, -0.006, P= 0.046). MD-by-MO interaction was also marginally significant for between-network connectivity in the cognitive cluster (standardized =-0.591, 95% CI: -1.063, -0.120, FDR-corrected P = 0.063]. A significant MD × MO × sex interaction emerged for within-network connectivity in the primary cluster (β = –1.396, 95% CI: –2.333 to –0.460, FDR-corrected P = 0.031). Among males, MD × MO interactions were significant (β = –0.887, 95% CI: -1.536, -0.237, P = 0.011; Figure 2B), but not among females (β = 0.510, 95% CI: -0.165, 1.184, P = 0.139; Figure 2B). Male infants with combined exposure exhibited lower primary-cluster connectivity than those exposed to MD alone (β = –0.693, 95% CI: -1.302, -0.085, P = 0.032) or MO alone (β = –0.615, 95% CI: -1.092, -0.137, P = 0.012). The observed effects were amplified in analyses restricted to full-term infants. 

Conclusion

Combined maternal diabetes and obesity is associated with lower infant connectivity in cognitive and primary sensorimotor networks, with some effects disproportionately impacting males. Findings highlight the need to consider maternal metabolic comorbidities and infant sex in early brain development and risk assessment.
