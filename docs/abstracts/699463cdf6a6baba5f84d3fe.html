Introduction

Psilocybin, a classic psychedelic, profoundly alters perception and brain function, and is increasingly studied in therapeutic contexts.1 However, the neural mechanisms underlying its effects – particularly during dynamic, real-world experiences – remain poorly understood. To address this, we analysed data from a rare, placebo-controlled fMRI dataset acquired during movie-watching – a naturalistic paradigm that is highly sensitive to individual differences and balances ecological validity with experimental control.2 We quantified stimulus-evoked neural synchrony using inter-subject correlation (ISC) to compare psilocybin and placebo.3 This well-established measure reflects the shared temporal patterns of brain activity across individuals during shared experience. We hypothesize that psilocybin reduces ISC, reflecting increased idiosyncratic and decreased stimulus-driven brain activity.

Methods

Using a Siemens Prisma 3T MRI scanner, we measured the brain activity of 25 healthy subjects (15M, 10F, mean age ± SD: 37.84 ± 7.90 years) while they watched an uninterrupted segment from the feature film 'The Good, the Bad and the Ugly' (17:25–26:48; 9 min 23s).

The study employed a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design, with subjects undergoing two scanning sessions separated by 28–135 days. During each session, either psilocybin (THC Pharm GmbH, >98% purity) or a placebo was administered orally with 200 ml of water. The dosage was adjusted based on each participant's body weight, targeting an approximate dose of 0.26 mg/kg.

The movie-watching fMRI was recorded approximately 180–210 min after drug administration. Functional T2*-weighted images with BOLD contrast (GE-EPIs; TR/TE = 2500/30 ms, flip angle = 52°, voxel size = 3 × 3 × 3 mm3, FOV = 210 mm, 225 volumes, 52 slices per volume), as well as high-resolution 3D T1-weighted images (for anatomical reference), were acquired. Data preprocessing was performed using fMRIPrep 23.0.0, based on Nipype 1.8.5.

Mean BOLD time series of 90 ROIs of the AAL atlas were extracted. Leave-one-out ISC was computed for each ROI and condition. Fisher r-to-Z transformation was applied before statistical testing. The two conditions were compared using paired t-tests both on ISC values averaged across ROIs and on ISC values per ROI. Effect sizes (Cohen’s d) were computed in Z-space, and regional comparisons were corrected for multiple comparisons using FDR (q < 0.05).

Results

Mean ISC was significantly lower under psilocybin than placebo (Placebo: 0.261 ± 0.121; Psilocybin: 0.163 ± 0.125; t(24) = 3.71, p = 0.0011; Cohen’s d = 0.74, Z-space).

Regional analysis revealed that ISC was significantly lower under psilocybin (Placebo > Psilocybin) in 31 of the 90 AAL regions (FDR-corrected, q < 0.05), with no regions showing the opposite effect (Psilocybin > Placebo). The effect sizes for these regional reductions ranged from Cohen’s d = 0.53 to d = 1.33 in Z-space. These regions were primarily located within the visual, parietal, and medial temporal cortices (Figure 1).

Conclusion

Psilocybin reduces neural synchrony during movie-watching, both globally and in specific brain regions. This suggests that brain responses become more idiosyncratic under psilocybin. Future work will relate these changes to subjective experience and explore time-resolved dynamics.
