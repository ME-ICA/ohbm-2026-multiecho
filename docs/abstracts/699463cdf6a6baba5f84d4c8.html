Introduction

Topographic organization is a unifying principle of sensory representations in human cortex. In visual cortex, graded changes in ventral visual cortex traverse sulci and gyri such that their retinotopic representations and polar angle boundaries follow cortical folding1,5. In contrast, no comparable structure-function relationships have been established in the dorsal stream. This gap likely arises because dorsal stream maps are buried within a continuous intraparietal sulcus (IPS) and its paroccipital branch (IPS-PO). Because these representations follow a single, primary sulcal fold rather than traversing gyri and sulci, they fall outside the assumptions of current structure-function models.

Given the dorsal visual stream’s central role in visuospatial processing and attention, its delineation currently relies on a probabilistic model defined in adults4 or fMRI paradigms requiring sustained attention and population receptive field (pRF) mapping, which demand substantial expertise to interpret. These requirements impose steep technical barriers and limit accessibility, particularly in clinical and developmental contexts. As a result, the dorsal stream’s structural-functional organization and developmental trajectory remain poorly understood. 

We address this gap by focusing on plis de passage (PdPs), consistent folds that line sulcal walls2. We hypothesize PdPs exist within the human IPS-PO and align with boundaries between dorsal stream visual field maps. In defining the PdPs of the human dorsal stream, we also ask if their localization corresponds to cytoarchitectonic organization and whether variability in parietal cortex folding can be used to predict behavior known to depend on dorsal stream function. Using contemporary surface reconstruction software capable of rendering hidden cortex visible, we integrate structural, functional, and behavioral data to test whether mesoscale folding provides a framework for dorsal stream organization.

Methods

Our dataset included an observation group (adults, n = 33) used to develop the anatomical model and two independent replication groups (adults, n = 20; children, n = 22). Structural (T1/T2) and functional (retinotopic mapping) MRI were collected on a 3 Tesla Siemens Prisma MRI scanner, alongside behavioral measures of visuospatial bias (line bisection) and reading fluency (RAN, TOWRE), both linked to dorsal stream function. Retinotopic mapping was done using a sweeping bar filled with cartoon images which updated at a rate of 7Hz while participants maintained central fixation and used peripheral attention to monitor the bar for the occasional appearance of a target pattern. Visual field map boundaries were defined on each participant’s native cortical surface at polar angle transitions consistent with prior work4. Cortical surface reconstruction was performed in FreeSurfer (v7.4.1). Each PdP was identified manually within IPS-PO. PdPs were used as landmarks to delineate anatomically-predicted dorsal stream regions to compare against functionally defined dorsal stream visual field maps which served as ground truth. To compare against existing probabilistic definitions of the dorsal stream, we also applied the Wang visual atlas in each participant’s native cortical space. Cytoarchitectonic maps from the Julich-Brain Atlas were transformed into individual subject space to assess correspondence with anatomically predicted boundaries. Structural measures (i.e., surface area, thickness, folding index, depth) were extracted from the cortical sheet underlying anatomically defined dorsal stream regions. Hemispheric asymmetries, computed as the normalized difference (LH - RH) / (LH + RH), were tested as predictors of visuospatial bias and reading fluency, with functionally defined dorsal stream regions used for validation.

Results

Across 54 adults (mean age 26 ± 11 years), four PdPs were consistently observed within the IPS-PO complex, including one that bisected the IPS from PO and frequently interrupted sulcal continuity (RH: 94%, LH: 85%). An average of 3.13 (± 0.56) PdPs were identified within the left PO and 3.13 (± 0.49) within the right PO. These hidden gyri were consistent to the extent that they remain visible in cortical surface averages from both FreeSurfer and Human Connectome Project datasets. 

These four PdPs reliably overlapped with retinotopically defined dorsal stream map boundaries for V3ab/IPS0 (78%), IPS0/IPS1 (84%), IPS1/IPS2 (82%), and IPS2/IPS3 (88%). Using functional maps as ground truth, anatomically defined regions achieved equal or better accuracy than the field’s leading probabilistic atlas of the dorsal visual stream, matching atlas-level performance in both adult and pediatric replication cohorts. In the observation group, the field map predictions generated from PdPs had significantly higher d-prime scores (more hits, fewer false alarms) in predicting which cortical vertices belonged to which visual field map when compared to the probabilistic functional atlas (LH: p = 3.27 x 10-6, RH: p = 0.0011). For the replication groups, the PdP anatomical predicts performed numerically better than probabilistic functional predictions but this difference was not significant.  

To get a deeper understanding of how retinotopic representations of visual cortex relate to PdPs, we ask whether anatomical strips defined at the center of each PdP spanning the IPS can recapitulate known changes in pRF tuning properties as once ascends the dorsal stream. That is, given that boundaries between visual field maps alternate in their preference for foveal of peripheral visual space, we predict that PdP’s should show changes in their representation of central and peripheral space. Within each PdP ROI, we extracted the mean pRF eccentricity values within each participant. In an analysis of variance, we find that PdPs overlapping field map boundaries V3AB/IPS-0 and IPS-1/2 show significantly more peripherally-tuned pRF fits in each hemisphere (RH: 9.6°, LH: 9.4°) compared to PdPs which overlapped map boundaries IPS-0/1 and IPS-2/3 (RH: 8.2°, LH: 8.3°), p<0.05.

If PdPs relate to the functional differentiation of cortex, then they likely also correspond to changes in the cytoarchitecture of the underlying cortical sheet. To address this, we again defined anatomical strips centered at each PdP spanning the IPS and ask whether these anatomical regions defined in each participant’s native cortical space show preferential overlap with cytoarchitectonic regions defined in postmortem human participants3, testing against the null hypothesis that if there is no consistent relationship between PdPs and cytoarchitecture then they should not show any consistent or preferential overlap. We employ 7 cytoarchitectonic regions from the Julich Brain Atlas which span the IPS-PO complex. What we find is that each PdP preferentially overlapped two cytoarchitectonic regions organized into pairs which span a medial-lateral section of the IPS-PO, and that this overlap pattern between PdPs and cytoarchitectonic regions was significantly correlated across hemispheres (Pearson’s r = 0.63, p = 3.4 × 10⁻⁴). Lastly, structural variability within anatomically defined dorsal stream regions predicted behavior. Lasso-selected features of the cortical sheet (surface area, folding index, thickness) within IPS regions defined using PdPs significantly predicted visuospatial bias (r = 0.61, p = 3.1×10⁻⁴) and reading fluency (r = 0.67, p = 7.9×10⁻⁵), demonstrating that these mesoscale folding patterns can be used to probe individual differences in dorsal stream-dependent behavior.

Conclusion

By demonstrating that intrasulcal plis de passage (PdPs) can delineate the visual field maps of the dorsal stream with accuracy matching or exceeding current probabilistic atlases, we identify a previously overlooked form of structure-function coupling in human visual cortex. The convergence of folding-defined boundaries with functional maps, cytoarchitectonic organization, and behaviorally predictive structural variability highlights a direct link between cortical structure, function, and behavior. The PdPs of the human IPS appear positioned at locations that denote either foveal representations shared by a given pair of visual field maps (IPS-0/1 and IPS-2/3) or regions representing the visual periphery (V3AB/IPS-0 and IPS-1/2). This work also suggests that pleating within large human sulci are not random but consistent across individuals and linked to the functional differentiation of cortex. Because the dorsal stream is largely buried within the paraoccipital-intraparietal sulcal complex, its delineation has traditionally required attention-demanding fMRI paradigms and expert interpretation, limiting accessibility in developmental and clinical populations. Our anatomy-based framework overcomes these constraints and performs robustly in both adults and children. Together, these findings establish PdPs as conserved anatomical anchors linking cortical folding, function, and behavior in the dorsal visual stream, and provide a novel framework for studying individual differences in dorsal stream organization and structure-function relationships across the brain.
