Introduction

Schizophrenia is commonly associated with deficits in brain circuit function induced by learning and memory tasks1. Changes in learning proficiency typically induce network plasticity in healthy controls (i.e., changes in statistically estimated functional connectivity between brain regions), but circuit function in schizophrenia is presumed to be relatively dysplastic (i.e., functional connectivity is less sensitivity to task-driven changes)2. Here we studied brain network plasticity using a specifically curated learning paradigm1 that required learning associations across eight task iterations. Network interactions were estimated across a 246-region functionally derived atlas3, during each task iteration. Plasticity of pairwise network interactions was investigated over the course of the eight task iterations.

Methods

45 stable schizophrenia patients (SCZ) and 33 healthy controls (HC) gave informed consent to participate (all procedures were approved by the WSU Institutional Review Board). The task (previously described4) required participants to learn nine object-location associations over eight successive iterations (each iteration had conditions for encoding, post-encoding consolidation, cued-retrieval, and post-retrieval consolidation)(see Figure 1). From the co-acquired fMRI data [3.0T Siemens Verio scanner, 32-channel volume head coil), multiband gradient EPI sequence (TR = 3 s, TE = 24.6 s, multiband factor = 3, FOV = 192 × 192 mm, matrix = 96 × 96, 64 axial slices, resolution = 2 mm3); T1-weighted MRI images collected for normalization and co-registration with the EPI scan (3D Magnetization Prepared Rapid Gradient Echo sequence, TR = 2150 ms, TE = 3.5 ms, TI = 1100 ms, flip angle = 8°, FOV = 256 × 256 × 160 mm3, 160 axial slices, resolution = 1 mm3], time series were extracted from 246 cerebral regions defined in the Brainnetome atlas3. For each of the eight task iterations we estimated the full functional connectivity matrix (30,135 unique pairs) in each of the 78 participants. Next, we mined the data for significant linear relationships between time (epoch) and FC for each of the 30,135 FC values, separately in each group (Spearman’s Rho). Edges with significant (pFDR<.05) positive or negative correlations were identified. A significant positive correlation suggests plastic increases in functional connectivity over the course of the task, whereas a significant negative correlation indicated the converse.

Results

Over the course of eight experimental iterations, HC showed substantial network plasticity. Increases were particularly prominent between region pairs within the frontal cortex, and between region pairs across the frontal and temporal cortices (Figure 2). By comparison, effects were sparse in schizophrenia patients and mostly confined to one region in the inferior partial lobe, being the left angular gyrus. Conversely, over the course of the eight experimental epochs, schizophrenia patients exhibited widespread decrease of FC, notably between regions of the frontal lobe and basal ganglia, while no loss of FC was observed in HC (effects not shown on account of space limitations).

Conclusion

Our results suggest that in the healthy brain, learning-induced network plasticity is largely cooperative (increase in functional connectivity over the course of learning). Conversely, learning-induced plasticity in schizophrenia is largely antagonistic (decrease in functional connectivity over the course of learning). Normative learning appears to be associated with increasingly cooperative processing between regions that are associated with higher order function (frontal lobe) and/or memory consolidation (temporal lobe) and these may constitute early signatures of normal long-term memory consolidation5. Conversely, pathological memory consolidation in schizophrenia may result from a loss of cooperative interactions over the course of learnings. These opposing patterns highlight the nuanced nature of disordered network plasticity in schizophrenia and motivate the search for such evidence in other task-based contexts.
