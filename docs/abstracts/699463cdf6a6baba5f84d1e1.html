Introduction

Anxiety disorder is the most prevalent psychiatric condition and a major contributor to disability in daily life. Although pharmacological and psychological treatments are widely used as first-line therapies, up to 50% of patients do not achieve adequate symptom improvement, underscoring the need for accessible and effective non-pharmacological strategies. Peripheral neuromodulation techniques—such as transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS)—have recently emerged as promising approaches capable of modulating central autonomic and affective regulatory circuits through bottom-up pathways. However, the neural mechanisms underlying these effects remain insufficiently understood, particularly regarding how peripheral nerve stimulation influences neurotransmitter dynamics and functional connectivity within key emotion-regulatory regions such as the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC).

Methods

In this study, we investigated the neuromodulatory effects of peripheral nerve stimulation targeting the auricular branch of the vagus nerve (VNS) and the median nerve (MNS) in patients with anxiety disorders. Twenty-two patients with clinically confirmed anxiety disorders participated and were randomized to receive either VNS or MNS. All participants underwent MRI scanning on a 7T system (Philips Healthcare) equipped with a 32-channel receive head coil. Neurochemical profiles were acquired using MR spectroscopy (MRS), and resting-state functional MRI data were collected to evaluate large-scale functional connectivity.

For electrical stimulation, acupoints associated with relaxation were selected for both MNS and VNS. MNS was applied at the PC6 acupoint on the wrist using transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation, whereas auricular vagus nerve stimulation was delivered via electrodes placed on the cymba conchae (TF4 acupoint). Peripheral nerve stimulation was administered during MRS acquisition using a block design consisting of alternating 1-minute periods of stimulation and rest over an 11-minute run. Stimulation intensity was individually adjusted to evoke a clear yet comfortable sensory perception for each participant.

MRS data were acquired from the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC, 25 × 25 × 25 mm³) using a semi-LASER sequence (repetition time = 5000 ms, echo time = 32 ms). Spectral quantification was performed with LCModel, and the unsuppressed water signal from the same voxel was used as an internal reference for metabolite quantification as well as for phase and eddy current correction. Metabolite concentrations, including phosphocreatine (PCr), were corrected for tissue composition based on voxel-wise segmentation of gray matter, white matter, and cerebrospinal fluid. Resting-state fMRI data were preprocessed using standard procedures, including motion correction, temporal filtering, spatial normalization, and nuisance regression. Seed-based functional connectivity analyses were conducted using the ACC as the region of interest to evaluate its connectivity with subcortical and cortical regions. 

To assess stimulation-induced changes in neurotransmitter levels, LCModel analysis was applied to spectral data acquired during the block-design paradigm. Metabolite concentrations were estimated separately for the 1-minute stimulation or rest blocks. Linear regression analysis was then performed to evaluate the relationship between peripheral nerve stimulation and changes in metabolite concentrations.

Results

Before electrical stimulation, phosphocreatine (PCr) concentrations in the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) were negatively correlated with clinical anxiety and depression scores, indicating lower PCr levels in patients with more severe symptoms (HAM-A: r = −0.40, p < 0.05; BDI-II: r = −0.49, p < 0.05). Resting-state functional connectivity analysis further revealed that ACC–thalamus connectivity was strongly and inversely associated with anxiety severity (HAM-A: r = −0.78; BAI: r = −0.73), suggesting reduced engagement of this regulatory circuit in individuals with higher anxiety.

To evaluate neuromodulatory effects, stimulation-induced changes in metabolite concentrations were examined. Linear regression analysis demonstrated a significant increase in ACC PCr concentrations following vagus nerve stimulation (p < 0.02), whereas median nerve stimulation did not produce significant changes. These findings indicate that vagus nerve stimulation may modulate neural energy metabolism within the ACC more effectively than median nerve stimulation.

In addition, stimulation-induced changes in ACC–thalamus functional connectivity showed a trend toward association with changes in anxiety symptoms, although these associations did not reach statistical significance (VNS: r = −0.44, p = 0.17; MNS: r = −0.54, p = 0.11). This pattern is consistent with the possibility that peripheral nerve stimulation may influence ACC–thalamus coupling in relation to symptom change. 

Conclusion

These findings suggest that peripheral nerve stimulation—particularly taVNS—may modulate both neurochemical and functional connectivity of the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), a core region implicated in anxiety regulation. The stimulation-induced increase in PCr concentrations is consistent with altered ACC energy metabolism, while the trend-level modulation of ACC–thalamus connectivity may reflect effects on broader circuit dynamics supporting affective regulation. Collectively, these results provide preliminary support for a metabolic and circuit-based mechanism that could contribute to the anxiolytic potential of peripheral neuromodulation.
