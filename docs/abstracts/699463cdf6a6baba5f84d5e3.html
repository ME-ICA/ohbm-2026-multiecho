Introduction

Schizophrenia is a severe, debilitating disorder that profoundly affects perception, emotion, and cognition, yet its neurobiological correlates remain poorly understood. Emerging evidence from diffusion imaging shows that schizophrenia is associated with white matter alterations.1 However, these findings are widespread and non-specific, particularly early in psychosis onset (EP), a critical period to determine prognosis and disorder trajectory.2 While cannabis use is associated with a heightened risk of developing schizophrenia,3 it has been often overlooked as a confounding factor when investigating structural connectivity in EP. 

Methods

We used Fixel-Based Analysis (FBA), a state-of-the-art framework to examine white matter microstructural fibre density and cross-sectional effects using diffusion-weighted imaging data.4,5 We compare white matter microstructure in EP and controls, and investigate the distinct and combined effect of comorbid Cannabis Use Disorder (CUD). Data from the Human Connectome Project for Early Psychosis were obtained, including 116 EP participants (25 of which met criteria for comorbid CUD, and 91 did not), and 50 controls. Diffusion-weighted image (DWI) data were acquired using 3T Siemens MAGNETOM Prisma scanners across four scanning sites in the US. Data were analysed according to the recommended pipeline of FBA,5 with Figure 1 showing a schematic summary of preprocessing and FBA workflow. 

Results

Individuals with EP demonstrated significantly lower fibre density (% effect: mean = 1.64, SD = 10.56) and fibre density-cross section (% effect: mean = 2.53%, SD = 11.34%) compared to controls, adjusting for age, sex, and intracranial volume (FWE-corrected p<0.05), as shown in Figure 2. Individuals with EP also demonstrated a subtle yet significant negative correlation between fibre bundle cross section and psychotic symptom severity (% effect: mean = 0.004%, SD = 0.013%). There was no significant difference in FBA metrics between EP with comorbid CUD, and those without (FWE-corrected p>0.05). 

Conclusion

White matter alterations early in psychosis onset seem to be consistent with schizophrenia and the role of CUD, alcohol and nicotine use is unclear. While CUD increases psychosis risk, its impact on white matter in EP needs to be further elucidated in longitudinal studies that follow the trajectory of illness progression.
