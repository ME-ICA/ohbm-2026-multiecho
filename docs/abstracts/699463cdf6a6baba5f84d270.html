Introduction

Early identification of individuals at risk of developing Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) is crucial, especially as new disease-modifying therapies are most effective in the earliest disease stages. In this context, plasma biomarkers are proving to be valuable triage tools for detecting preclinical AD. Among them, phosphorylated tau 217 (pTau217) has emerged as the most sensitive marker of amyloid beta (Aβ) and tau brain deposition and shows strong associations with domain-specific cognitive alterations across the AD spectrum1. Both tau and Aβ spreading have also been recently linked to functional connectivity, such that greater protein deposition is seen in strongly connected brain regions, supporting the hypothesis of transneuronal propagation2. Conversely, greater functional segregation between epicenters of protein accumulation may serve as a protective factor by slowing the accumulation process3. A novel metric of functional segregation, known as dispersion, has recently been introduced that builds upon gradient connectivity analysis. Gradients characterize large-scale brain organization by describing gradual transitions between distinct functional profiles across anatomical space4.

We propose that blood-derived and neuroimaging biomarkers are critical to forming an unbiased assessment of an individual’s clinical status—one that may otherwise be obscured by compensatory mechanisms or biological variability. In line with this view, the present study aims to evaluate how brain functional dispersion patterns relate to cognitive performance as a function of plasma pTau217 levels, while controlling for the effects of cognitive reserve and renal function. Specifically, we focus on older cognitively unimpaired individuals and individuals at risk of developing AD, assessing whether meaningful predictive interactions between blood biomarkers and neuroimaging alterations are already detectable at the earliest stages of disease.

Methods

Overall, 79 participants (mean age: 73 士 5.5 years; mean education: 13.1 士 4.18 years) were recruited from the University Hospital of Padua following written informed consent and accordingly with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants underwent an extensive neuropsychological assessment covering six cognitive domains—memory, executive functions, attention, visuospatial abilities, language, and social cognition. Furthermore, the Cognitive Reserve Index questionnaire (CRIq) was administered to assess cognitive reserve and quantify the individual resilience to the pathology. PTau217 was tested in K-2 ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid plasma samples with a research-use-only Lumipulse G1200 assay (Fujirebio, Japan; lot 4,129). To identify the presence of AD–related pathology using plasma pTau217 as a biomarker, we applied a previously validated cutoff (established against Aβ-PET using the same assay) that accounts for up to 10% analytical variability5. Accordingly, individuals with pTau217 concentrations exceeding 0.22 ng/l were classified as positive for AD pathology. Based on the neuropsychological evaluation and the assessment of pTau217 levels, 28 participants were classified as having mild cognitive impairment (MCI), while the remaining 51 were considered cognitively unimpaired. Among the MCI group, 8 individuals exhibited elevated plasma pTau217 concentrations (>0.22 ng/l) and were categorized as high-risk (HR) for AD pathology. In the cognitively unimpaired group, 5 individuals with similarly elevated pTau217 levels were excluded, resulting in a final sample of 46 participants defined as unimpaired controls (UC). To determine if HR individuals could already present functional patterns similar to those of AD patients, we retrieved 66 AD patients (mean age: 74.7 士 7.85; mean education: 15.7 士 2.4 years) from the 3rd Cohort of the Alzheimer’s Disease NeuroImaging Initiative (ADNI). Structural and functional magnetic resonance imaging data of both the Padua and ADNI cohorts underwent preprocessing, which included brain extraction, bias field correction and tissue segmentation. Preprocessing of the functional images also involved slice timing, motion and distortion correction based on field maps acquisition and coregistraion to the anatomical scan. Schaefer’s Atlas was applied to parcellate the cortex into 400 regions of interest (ROIs). The BrainSpace Toolbox was then used to extract gradients from the individual functional connectivity matrix. We utilized a normalized angle kernel and a principal component analysis (PCA) approach to identify the first 3 gradients that explained most of the data variance. To enable direct comparisons among individuals, gradients were aligned by means of Procrustes analysis. A measure of gradients’ dispersion was then extracted as the Euclidean distance of each ROI from its resting state network’s center of gravity, namely, its median gradients’ value. To better understand dispersion patterns outside the networks’ boundaries, we conducted a second PCA on the dispersion values for each participant and ROI. Multiple linear regression models were then tested for their ability to predict cognitive performance using individuals’ loadings onto the first three components as predictors, together with their interaction with pTau217 values and CRIq scores. Creatinine concentrations were included as a covariate, given that renal dysfunction—reflected by elevated creatinine levels—can influence pTau217 concentrations. Finally, we directly tested if HR individuals might already present AD-like functional alteration patterns. To this aim, analysis of covariance was run to compare the dispersion values between UC, HR and AD participants. To control for age-related dispersion differences between groups, we kept age as a covariate in the models. False Discovery Rate correction was applied to all statistics. Results

We explored the association between functional dispersion and cognitive function, specifically looking at the interaction between dispersion measures, plasma pTau217 levels and cognitive reserve. We did so by computing a PCA on the dispersion measures in order to obtain secondary maps (i.e. the components) representing the regions of increased functional differentiation. When put in relation to the individual cognitive performance, we observed that in individuals with high pTau217 levels, the greater the load on the second component (representing higher dispersion especially in the left superior temporal sulcus), the worse their performance in social cognition tasks, with an opposite interaction in healthy controls (F(74,6)= 2.78, p = 0.018, R2= 0.20; β=-0.92, p=0.022). The superior temporal sulcus plays a central role in social cognition as a major hub for crossmodal visuo-auditory integration. In AD, however, it is among the most affected regions. The positive association between dispersion in this region and social cognition in healthy individuals suggests that variability in its functional profile supports efficient crossmodal processing. By contrast, in the presence of pathology, greater dispersion may reflect progressive disconnectivity driven by Aβ buildup, which is a strong risk factor for future decline. Altered connectivity of the lateral temporal cortices has indeed been reported as a distinguishing feature between AD converters and non-converters, and more broadly as part of the AD biomarker profile.

Interestingly, the reversed pattern was observed for the association between the third component and memory performance as a function of pTau217 levels. In this case, the third component (which shows higher dispersion in the bilateral superior temporal gyrus, precuneus, anterior and posterior cingulate cortex, primary sensorimotor and supplementary motor areas) showed a positive association with performance on memory tasks in individuals with high pTau217 levels (F(75,6)= 3.68, p = 0.003, R2= 0.24; β = 1.94, p = 0.009). Considering that most of the regions grouped by the third component represent known areas of abnormal protein accumulation, and given that proteinopathy spread appears mediated by functional connectivity, we argue that greater decoupling of those regions from their own networks might act as a protective mechanism, thereby contributing to maintaining more efficient memory functions.

This posits the question of whether functional dispersion patterns might hence have a role in the early identification of individuals at risk of developing AD. To address this, we compared our UC and HR participants to an AD sample taken from the ADNI cohort. At the network level, we observed that only the dispersion within the DMN significantly differentiated the 3 groups, especially UC and AD, with the HR group locating halfway between the extremes (F(2,116)= 7.6, p = 0.0008). Remarkably, the DMN is indeed among the earliest and most affected networks by the pathology, with its progressive disaggregation recognized as an AD hallmark and here captured by the progressive increase in its dispersion values. When we examined this difference in single regions, HR individuals had significantly different dispersion values in 12 brain regions compared to the UC group. Most interesting, however, was the observation that 6 of those regions had dispersion values significantly different between the HR and the UC group, but equal between HR and AD individuals. All these regions have been reported to show early functional alterations in the AD continuum, as well as Aβ accumulation, and might hence represent vulnerable loci which alteration is detectable even in HR individuals. Conclusion

The present study aimed at addressing if functional connectivity alterations could be predictive of cognitive impairment as a function of the plasma levels of pTau217, a recently introduced biomarker of AD-related pathology in the brain useful for the early triage of older individuals at risk of developing AD. In addition, we monitored if this association could be influenced by additional confounding factors, such as the individual level of cognitive reserve and a measure of renal (dys)functioning. The first represents a favorable factor in increasing the resilience and coping capacity against the pathology, whereas the latter can be both a risk factor of developing dementia, as well as a confounder for blood biomarkers of proteinopathy given the role of the kidneys in blood filtration. Taken together, our study supports the hypothesized role of functional dispersion measures as significant predictors of cognitive performance in a sample of older adults. Importantly, dispersion measures showed significant interactions with pTau217 levels. In more detail, in cognitively unimpaired controls, greater functional dispersion in the superior temporal sulcus was positively linked to social cognition skills, meaning that this region possibly benefits from a more diverse functional profile to favor multisensory integration processes. Conversely, dispersion in a more distributed set of regions in the temporal, parietal, cingulate and sensorimotor cortices seemed to benefit the memory performance of individuals with high pTau217 levels. As some of those regions are known epicenters of Aβ and Tau accumulation, we hypothesize that their functional dispersion might help hinder the proteinopathy spreading with a consequent positive impact on cognitive functioning3. These results hence suggest that functional dispersion positively impacts cognitive performance in healthy participants, but only in crossmodal integration hubs and in tasks requiring multimodal processing. In HR individuals, instead, dispersion within a wider profile of regions appeared to favour memory performance, potentially due to functional disconnectivity hindering the proteinopathy spread.

Furthermore, dispersion values within several cortical regions were observed to significantly differ between UC and HR individuals, with a subset of those showing similar values between HR and AD participants. Although preliminary, these data support the use of functional alteration patterns, in conjunction with other cognitive and clinical measures, as an early biomarker for the risk of converting to AD.

Few limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the HR sample size was limited and derived from the use of a hard cut-off value in plasma pTau217 levels (>0.22 ng/l). However, it is worth noting that this Lumipulse pTau217 threshold was validated against Aβ-PET in a previous work of our group, conducted in a cohort of cognitively impaired subjects and AD patients, using the same methodology5. Furthermore, the assessment of a region's dispersion relative to its resting state network is impacted by the choice on the parcel and on the reference network maps. Of note, we used Schaefer's parcellation and Yeo's labels, which are among the most commonly used in the literature. We also acknowledge how critical the harmonization of preprocessing is in studies including scans from multiple sites: here, the same preprocessing pipeline was applied to datasets acquired with different voxel size and TR. Nonetheless, the use of smoothing and averaging within the same parcellation should mitigate differences.
