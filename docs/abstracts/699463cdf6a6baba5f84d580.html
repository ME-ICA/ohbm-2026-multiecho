Introduction

Introduction: Nonlinear developmental changes are a defining characteristic of early brain maturation, with different functional networks exhibiting distinct rates and patterns of growth during the first months of life 1. However, most infant neuroimaging studies examine these systems independently 2, leaving open the question of how network-specific developmental trajectories differ in shape and timing across brain networks. By comparing developmental trajectories, we can observe clear divergences in the maturational pathways of sensorimotor, visual, subcortical, and association networks—differences that emerge not just in the magnitude of age-related change but also in the form of their nonlinear trajectories. Some networks show rapid early increases followed by plateaus, whereas others exhibit multi-phasic or U-shaped patterns, reflecting heterogeneous underlying neurodevelopmental processes 3. In this study, we model these age-related changes using a cubic polynomial framework, which provides an effective balance between interpretability and flexibility. Cubic models are capable of capturing several key developmental motifs—early acceleration, mid-infancy deceleration, and later re-acceleration—without overfitting the data or introducing excessive model complexity. By characterizing and comparing cubic coefficients across networks, we aim to quantify systematic differences in their developmental shapes and identify which networks undergo the most rapid or complex maturation during the first six months of life. This framework allows us to move beyond isolated trajectories and instead map how functional networks mature relative to one another, offering a richer understanding of early brain organization.

Methods

Methods: This study analyzed 137 longitudinal resting-state fMRI scans from 74 neurotypically developing infants between 0 and 6 months corrected age. Infants were scanned up to three times, with age corrected by gestational age used to capture developmental timing more accurately. Preprocessing included discarding the first 16 volumes, head-motion correction, distortion correction using AP/PA field maps, slice-timing correction, and a two-stage normalization procedure in which each scan was first aligned to an infant anatomical template and then transformed into standard space using an age-appropriate functional template. Large-scale functional networks were identified using group independent component analysis (ICA) with model order as 20, repeated ICA runs for stability, and individualized back-reconstruction 4. Thirteen networks were extracted, including primary and secondary visual and motor systems, auditory, temporal, cerebellar, subcortical, attention, default mode, and three prefrontal networks 5.

 

To quantify developmental differences in spatial organization, five complementary voxelwise metrics were computed from each network’s z-map, as conceptually illustrated in Figure. 1. Network-averaged spatial similarity (NASS) assessed how closely each infant’s network resembled the group-level template, indexing the degree of topological alignment (Figure. 1A). Network engagement range (NER) measured the spread of voxel involvement by capturing the difference between the highest and lowest voxel intensities, reflecting how broadly or narrowly the network engaged its spatial territory (Figure. 1B). Network strength represented the mean contribution of suprathreshold voxels, providing an estimate of the overall robustness of network activation (Figure. 1C). Network size quantified the spatial extent of activation by counting suprathreshold voxels, tracking expansion or contraction over age (Figure. 1D). Network center of mass (NCM) characterized the spatial configuration by evaluating how intensity values were distributed relative to the network’s weighted center, capturing shifts or dispersions in topography 5. To examine developmental change, each metric was modeled using a shared cubic polynomial design matrix that captured both linear and nonlinear age-related effects. The resulting linear, quadratic, and cubic coefficients were extracted for each network and compared across networks using pooled standard errors and corrected statistical testing. This cross-network nonlinear trajectory characterization (CNTC) framework enabled direct comparison of how the shape, timing, and magnitude of developmental trajectories differed across all 13 networks, revealing relative acceleration, deceleration, and multi-phasic patterns in network maturation.

Results

Results: Cubic models captured pronounced age-related changes characterized by early increases, mid-infancy decelerations, and later re-acceleration patterns. These trajectories differed substantially across networks: primary visual, secondary visual, and primary motor networks showed rapid early refinement, whereas association networks—including default mode, medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC), and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex (vlPFC)—displayed slower, more gradual, and more multi-phasic developmental profiles. Subcortical and cerebellar systems showed distinct U-shaped or plateau-like curves, highlighting their unique maturational timing. 

Figure 2 presents cross-network contrasts of the cubic, quadratic, and linear slope parameters derived from the cubic developmental models for all five spatial metrics. Cross-network comparisons of trajectory coefficients showed that these nonlinear patterns differed significantly between networks. For NASS, slope and quadratic terms—but not cubic terms—distinguished several networks, particularly separating primary motor and subcortical systems from prefrontal and temporal regions. NER showed comparatively fewer differences, indicating that variability in voxel intensity ranges follows similar developmental timing across networks. In contrast, network strength revealed widespread differences, with primary motor, visual, and prefrontal regions exhibiting distinct quadratic and slope coefficients, suggesting heterogeneous maturation of voxel-level engagement. Network size showed strong nonlinear divergence driven largely by the subcortical and secondary motor networks, reflecting differences in spatial expansion and contraction across systems. NCM displayed robust cross-network differentiation: default mode, secondary motor, and secondary visual networks showed significantly different cubic, quadratic, and slope parameters compared with many other systems. Overall, the results indicate that infant brain networks do not follow a uniform developmental timeline. Instead, each network exhibits a unique nonlinear trajectory, with sensory and motor networks maturing earlier and more rapidly, and higher-order associative networks undergoing slower, more complex, and multi-stage refinement.

Conclusion

Conclusion: This study demonstrated that infant functional networks follow distinct and highly nonlinear developmental trajectories during the first six months of life. Using a unified cubic modeling framework across five spatial metrics, we showed that networks differ not only in the rate of maturation but also in the shape and timing of their developmental profiles. Early maturing systems, including primary and secondary visual, motor, cerebellar, and subcortical networks, exhibited rapid increases and early inflection points, consistent with accelerated consolidation of perceptual and sensorimotor circuitry. In contrast, higher-order associative networks such as the default mode (DMN), mPFC, and vlPFC showed slower, multi-phasic, and more complex trajectories, reflecting a more extended period of refinement.

Cross-network comparisons further revealed systematic differences in cubic, quadratic, and slope parameters, indicating that networks diverge in both the magnitude and curvature of their developmental changes. Steeper slopes in subcortical, cerebellar, and vlPFC networks point to rapid early gains, whereas significant cubic trends in systems like the DMN and secondary motor network capture multi-stage restructuring rather than monotonic growth. These patterns align with known biological principles: sensory and motor circuits mature early to support foundational behaviors, whereas associative systems undergo prolonged reorganization to support later-emerging cognitive functions.

Together, these findings underscore that early brain development is not uniform but instead unfolds through asynchronous, network-specific trajectories. Mapping these nonlinear patterns offers a more complete understanding of how large-scale systems establish during infancy and provides a normative framework for identifying atypical developmental pathways in future studies. Given the rapid neuroplasticity of this early period, characterizing these trajectories may help inform early detection of neurodevelopmental risk and guide future work aimed at understanding the emergence of functional specialization in the human brain.
