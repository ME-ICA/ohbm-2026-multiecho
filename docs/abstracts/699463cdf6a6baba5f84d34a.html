Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a multisystem neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the accumulation of misfolded alpha-synuclein and the resulting degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra. Other neurotransmitter systems, such as the noradrenergic and serotonergic system, also degenerate, contributing to both motor and non-motor symptoms. Depletion of dopamine and noradrenaline, together with the accumulation of Lewy-bodies in the hypothalamus and adrenal glands, is thought to impair effective coping with stress and dysregulate the stress-response in PD.1,2 This dysregulation may increase stress sensitivity, leading to chronic stress and related symptoms, such as depression and anxiety.3 Chronic stress may further accelerate PD progression, as suggested by evidence from rodent PD models.4 However, data supporting this hypothesis in humans are limited, and the cerebral mechanisms underlying stress sensitivity in PD remain largely unexplored. 

The aim of this study is to determine the neurobiological processes at play during acute stress in PD and to identify whether the pathophysiology of PD is associated with an increased sensitivity to stress. Specifically, we address three questions: (1) How does acute stress affect the organization of large scale resting-state networks relevant for the stress-response – the salience network (SN), default mode network (DMN), and executive control network (ECN)? (2) Which clinical characteristics predict functional connectivity (FC) changes in response to acute stress? and (3) Which molecular systems are associated with stress-related FC changes in PD? 

Ultimately, our goal is to provide new insights into the mechanisms underlying stress and related symptoms in PD and to facilitate the development of treatment strategies to alleviate stress.

Methods

Data of 115 people with mild-moderate PD and 49 age- and gender matched healthy controls (HCs) will be included in the final analyses (data acquisition is ongoing at the time of abstract submission; expected to be finalized by January 2026). Data acquisition for this study is embedded in the MIND-PD trial,5 a randomized controlled trial investigating the short- and long-term effects of mindfulness-based cognitive therapy on PD symptoms and MRI markers of disease progression. 

For the current study, we used the socially evaluated cold pressor test (SECPT) combined with a cognitive flexibility task to induce acute stress. Resting-state BOLD EPI images (400 volumes, 2.5 mm isotropic resolution) were acquired on a 3T MRI scanner before and after stress induction. Heart rate, pupil size, and tremor amplitude (in the PD group) were measured during functional MRI (fMRI) and stress induction. Salivary cortisol was measured before stress induction, directly after, and 30 and 60 minutes after stress induction, respectively. Before and after stress induction, subjective stress was measured on a 10-point scale (1=not stressed at all, 10=very stressed). People with PD were assessed OFF dopaminergic medication. Data collection took place at the Donders center for cognitive neuroimaging in Nijmegen, the Netherlands.

Preprocessed and standardized (to MNI152NLin6Asym) fMRI images and theory-based templates of the DMN, SN and ECN will be used in a dual regression approach to identify individual and network specific FC before and after stress induction in people with PD compared to HCs (research question 1.1). Voxel-wise stress- and group effects will be estimated using permutation testing (research question 1.2). We will use linear mixed models to identify potential predictors of the cerebral stress response (e.g., symptoms of depression and anxiety, disease severity, locus coeruleus integrity; research question 2). Lastly, to identify which molecular systems are involved in the acute stress response (research question 3), we will use receptor enriched analysis of functional connectivity by targets (REACT), a state-of-the-art fMRI analysis technique using positron emission tomography templates as spatial priors to estimate receptor-driven FC networks from fMRI data.

Results

If accepted, results from all described analyses using the full dataset will be presented at the conference. We refrain from reporting statistics for preliminary findings.

Preliminary data (PD: N=92, HC: N=35) suggest that the SECPT effectively induced stress in both groups. Heart rate and tremor amplitude (in PD) increased during stress induction and decreased afterward. Salivary cortisol increased 30 minutes after stress induction. People with PD showed a steeper increase in subjective stress, compared to HCs. Also, the magnitude of subjective stress increase was positively associated with levels of self-reported stress-symptoms (Hospital Anxiety and Depression scale). 

Preliminary analyses of FC within the DMN, SN and ECN revealed no effects of acute stress on average within-network connectivity and no group differences in FC changes. Self-reported symptoms of anxiety and depression did not account for FC changes in either group. Baseline (pre-stress) connectivity was higher in HCs than in people with PD. 

REACT, voxel-wise analyses, and additional tests to explore potential predictors of FC changes will be conducted on the full dataset and presented at the conference.

Conclusion

Preliminary findings show that people with PD and age-matched HCs respond to a laboratory stressor. In the PD group, tremor amplitude increased during acute stress, which confirms previous findings and illustrates one aspect of stress sensitivity in PD. Furthermore, people with PD reported a stronger increase in subjective stress than HCs and the increase in subjective stress across groups was larger in people with more stress symptoms. This supports the idea that an increased sensitivity to stressful circumstances may contribute to the development of stress-related symptoms. In contrast, early analyses indicate that within network connectivity remains stable after stress exposure. Given the evident presence of physiological stress in our data (e.g., heartrate and salivary cortisol increased in response to the stressor), it is unlikely that this pattern reflects an insufficiently strong stressor. Instead, a lack of connectivity alterations may point towards a blunted response to stress, which has been suggested before in older adults. At the same time, average network connectivity alone may not be sufficient to capture nuanced interactions within and between networks occurring during stress. Ongoing analyses focusing on voxel-wise changes within and between networks will provide a more sensitive perspective on the mechanisms at work during acute stress in elderly people with and without PD.
