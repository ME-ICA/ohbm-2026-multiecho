Introduction

The substantia nigra (SN) is a core of dopaminergic circuitry whose degeneration underlies the motor and non-motor manifestations of Parkinson’s disease (PD). Accumulating histopathological and connectivity studies indicate that the SN is not a unitary structure but comprises multiple subregions with distinct cytoarchitecture and projection targets. In primate and human studies, converging evidence from calbindin-based histology, tract-tracing, and connectivity MRI supports a tripartite organization of the SN, broadly corresponding to limbic, sensorimotor, and associative territories.^1,2

Despite this well-established anatomical and network-level heterogeneity, in vivo imaging studies of PD have largely treated the SN as a single structure or focused primarily on its diagnostic utility. Such approaches overlook the marked heterogeneity of PD symptoms, which span motor, autonomic, and cognitive domains and likely reflect differential vulnerability of distinct nigral circuits rather than uniform degeneration. As a result, it remains unclear whether degeneration of specific SN subregions preferentially maps onto particular symptom domains in early PD.

Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI (NM-MRI) provides a unique opportunity to address this gap by enabling in vivo visualization and quantification of dopaminergic neuronal loss within the SN. When combined with anatomically and functionally informed subregional parcellation, NM-MRI offers a framework for testing domain-specific structure–symptom relationships.

In this study, we leveraged NM-MRI–derived volumetric measures of functionally defined SN subregions to examine whether degeneration of SN subregional territories differentially relates to motor, autonomic, and cognitive symptoms in early-stage PD. We hypothesized that dorsolateral (sensorimotor) SN volume would primarily relate to motor impairment, medial (limbic) SN volume to autonomic dysfunction, and ventral (associative) SN volume to cognitive performance.

Methods

Participants

We retrospectively included 105 patients with early-stage Parkinson’s disease (Hoehn & Yahr ≤2.5; disease duration ≤60 months) who underwent neuromelanin-sensitive MRI at a single tertiary center. All patients met the Movement Disorder Society clinical diagnostic criteria for PD and underwent clinical assessments in the levodopa off-state. Motor (MDS-UPDRS Part III), cognitive (Mini-Mental State Examination), and autonomic evaluations (Composite Autonomic Scoring Scale, including cardiovagal and adrenergic subscores, and tilt-table testing for orthostatic hypotension) were performed within 12 months of MRI acquisition.^3

MRI Acquisition

All scans were performed on a 3T MRI system using a standardized protocol. Neuromelanin-sensitive images were acquired using a multi-echo gradient-echo–based SandwichNM sequence.^4 Multiple echo times were acquired to enhance neuromelanin-related contrast while mitigating susceptibility-related artifacts in the midbrain. High-resolution 3D T1-weighted images were obtained for anatomical reference and normalization.

NM-MRI Preprocessing and Contrast Construction

To enhance substantia nigra contrast, low-echo images were combined voxel-wise, followed by denoising and bias-field correction. Skull stripping and tissue segmentation were performed to generate brain masks. A deep-learning–based multi-label brain segmentation model was used to identify midbrain structures on T1-weighted images, which were subsequently registered to the NM-MRI space. This approach ensured robust localization of the SN while minimizing contamination from adjacent structures.

Subregional SN Parcellation and Volume Quantification

A study-specific NM-MRI template was constructed and used for region-of-interest propagation. The SN pars compacta was segmented into three functionally defined subregions—ventral (associative), medial (limbic), and dorsolateral (sensorimotor)—based on prior histological and connectivity frameworks. Template ROIs were transferred to individual subject space using affine followed by nonlinear registration (ANTs SyN), with midbrain-focused masking to improve registration accuracy.

Neuromelanin-positive voxels within each subregion were identified using a periaqueductal gray–referenced intensity threshold, providing biologically grounded normalization across subjects. Subregional volumes were calculated and normalized by intracranial volume to account for inter-individual differences in head size.

Statistical Analysis

Pearson correlation analyses were performed to assess associations between subregional SN volumes and clinical measures, including total and subscale scores of the MDS-UPDRS III, MMSE, and autonomic outcomes. For binary outcomes such as orthostatic hypotension, point-biserial correlations were applied. To account for potential confounding effects, partial correlations controlling for age and sex were computed. All analyses were two-tailed, with statistical significance set at p<0.05.

Results

Participant Characteristics

A total of 105 patients with early-stage Parkinson’s disease were included in the analysis. The cohort had a median age of 67.7 years and a median disease duration of 14 months, with all participants classified as Hoehn & Yahr stage ≤2.5. Motor symptom severity was generally mild to moderate, and global cognitive performance was largely preserved, with MMSE scores clustered toward the upper range. Autonomic dysfunction, when present, was predominantly mild, consistent with early disease status.

Subregional SN Volumes and Demographic Effects

Across the cohort, neuromelanin-derived volumes differed across substantia nigra subregions, with the medial (limbic) and dorsolateral (sensorimotor) territories constituting the largest portions of total SN volume. Increasing age was associated with reduced neuromelanin volume, most prominently in the medial SN and in total SN volume. Sex-related differences were also observed, with female patients showing relatively larger medial and ventral SN volumes. Disease duration and Hoehn & Yahr stage did not show significant associations with subregional SN volumes, indicating that neuromelanin loss in these territories was not simply a function of clinical stage within this early PD cohort.

Motor Symptom Associations

Motor severity, quantified by the MDS-UPDRS Part III, demonstrated domain-specific associations with SN subregions. Lower dorsolateral SN volume was associated with greater overall motor impairment, as reflected by higher MDS-UPDRS III total scores. Among motor subscores, rigidity showed the strongest association, with smaller dorsolateral SN volumes corresponding to greater rigidity severity. Axial motor symptoms were more closely related to medial SN volume, whereas tremor and bradykinesia did not exhibit robust associations with any SN subregion.

These relationships largely persisted after adjustment for age and sex, supporting a preferential link between degeneration of the sensorimotor SN territory and motor dysfunction in early PD rather than a global effect of nigral volume loss.

Cognitive Associations

Global cognitive performance, assessed using the MMSE, showed a selective association with dorsolateral SN volume. Larger dorsolateral SN volumes were associated with higher MMSE scores, and this relationship remained significant after controlling for age and sex. No significant associations were observed between MMSE and medial or ventral SN volumes. Given the relatively preserved cognitive performance and limited score variability in this cohort, no additional subregional associations with cognition were identified.

Autonomic Symptom Associations

In contrast to motor and cognitive findings, autonomic dysfunction exhibited its strongest associations with the medial (limbic) SN subregion. Smaller medial SN volumes were consistently associated with higher cardiovagal and adrenergic Composite Autonomic Scoring Scale subscores. These associations were among the strongest observed across all symptom domains and remained significant after adjustment for age and sex.

Orthostatic hypotension, assessed as a binary outcome, was also associated with reduced medial SN volume, with a similar but weaker association observed for total SN volume. No significant autonomic associations were identified for the dorsolateral or ventral SN subregions.

Summary of Domain-Specific Mapping

Taken together, these findings demonstrate a dissociation in symptom–anatomy relationships across SN subregions in early Parkinson’s disease. Degeneration of the dorsolateral (sensorimotor) SN preferentially mapped onto motor impairment and rigidity, whereas medial (limbic) SN involvement was selectively associated with autonomic dysfunction. Ventral (associative) SN volume did not show reliable associations with motor, cognitive, or autonomic measures within this cohort.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that neuromelanin MRI–derived subregional analysis of the substantia nigra reveals domain-specific structure–symptom relationships in early Parkinson’s disease. Degeneration of the dorsolateral, sensorimotor-related SN preferentially mapped onto motor impairment, particularly rigidity, whereas reduced volume of the medial, limbic-related SN was selectively associated with autonomic dysfunction across cardiovagal, adrenergic, and orthostatic domains. In contrast, the ventral, associative SN subregion did not show robust associations with motor, cognitive, or autonomic measures in this early-stage cohort.

These findings support an in vivo functional organization of the human substantia nigra that aligns with prior histological and connectivity-based models and highlight that nigral degeneration in PD is not spatially uniform but follows domain-specific vulnerability patterns.^5 By integrating neuromelanin-sensitive MRI with functionally informed subregional parcellation, our results illustrate the utility of NM-MRI as a tool for mapping symptom-specific neural substrates and for probing structure–function relationships in neurodegenerative disease.
