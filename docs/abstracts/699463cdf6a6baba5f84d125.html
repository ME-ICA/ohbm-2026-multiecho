Introduction

Cerebellar dysconnectivity is strongly implicated in cognitive impairments, contributing to the pathogenesis of severe psychiatric disorders such as psychosis. Our prior work has shown that cerebellar dysconnectivity is a robust neural trait for psychosis and predictive of psychosis onset and prognosis [1,2,3]. However, how cerebellar connectivity evolves across the lifespan and how deviations from the normative connectivity relate to psychosis remain unclear. Clarifying these trajectories is essential for evaluating age-standardized cerebellar “normality” at the individual level and its clinical relevance.

Methods

We analyzed resting-state fMRI data from 2330 healthy individuals (aged 6-100 years) across three large-scale neuroimaging cohorts – the HCP Development (aged 6-21 years), HCP Young adult (aged 22-35 years), and HCP Aging (aged 36-100 years). Cerebellar connectivity was calculated as the mean functional connectivity between 10 cerebellar systems defined by the CAB-NP atlas and the whole brain. Generalized Additive Model for Location, Scale and Shape (GAMLSS, [4]) was employed to estimate connectivity changes for 10 cerebellar systems across the lifespan, separately by sex. A total of 50+ distributions and 10+ smoothers was fitted to each cerebellar system, and the best model for each cerebellar system was selected based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). Z scores quantifying connectivity deviations from the age-normative models were subsequently calculated for each individual. Associations between the z scores and cognitive functions were examined by canonical correlation analysis. The resulting age-normative models were further applied in the HCP Early Psychosis dataset (115 patients and 49 controls, aged 16-36 years).

Results

Overall, progressively declined cerebellar connectivity was observed across the lifespan in females, while males showed relatively stable connectivity in most cerebellar systems after early adulthood (p < 0.001). Lower z scores (larger negative deviations from the normative models) were associated with worse fluid and crystalized cognitions in healthy individuals (p < 0.007). In patients, significantly lower z scores were observed in 8 out of 10 systems (p < 0.017), as well as a larger proportion of extremely negative z scores (z < -1.96) compared with controls (p = 0.02).

Conclusion

This is the first study to build age-normative models of cerebellar connectivity across the lifespan. Greater negative deviations were related to both cognitive dysfunction and psychosis, aligning with the “synaptic over-pruning” and “accelerated aging” hypotheses of schizophrenia.
