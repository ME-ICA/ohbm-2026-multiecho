Introduction

Suicide is a leading cause of premature mortality in major depressive disorder (MDD), yet the neurobiological substrates that differentiate suicidal from non-suicidal depression remain incompletely defined.^1,2 Clinical and behavioral evidence suggests that suicide attempters represent a distinct depressive phenotype characterized by impaired emotion regulation, heightened stress reactivity, and altered inhibitory control beyond overall depression severity.^2 Neuroimaging studies implicate fronto–limbic systems in affective regulation and impulse control; however, structural findings specific to suicide attempt history have shown limited reproducibility, partly due to heterogeneity in clinical phenotyping and methodological variability.^3,4 Early-life adversity is a major developmental risk factor that may sensitize stress-related neural systems and amplify vulnerability to suicidal behavior, potentially shaping neuroanatomical profiles within stress-regulation circuits.^5 Against this background, we investigated gray matter volume (GMV) alterations associated with suicide attempts in MDD and tested their associations with childhood trauma exposure and current suicidal ideation.

Methods

T1-weighted MRI data were obtained from 204 adults: 61 patients with MDD with a lifetime history of suicide attempts (suicidal depression; SD), 62 patients with MDD without suicide attempts (non-suicidal depression; NSD), and 81 healthy controls (HC). Whole-brain voxel-based morphometry (VBM) tested SD–NSD GMV differences controlling for age, sex, education, and total intracranial volume (TICV) (voxel-wise FWE-corrected). The significant SD–NSD cluster was defined as a region of interest (ROI) for covariate-adjusted group comparisons and partial correlations with suicidal ideation (Beck Scale for Suicide Ideation; SSI) and childhood trauma (Childhood Trauma Questionnaire; CTQ total and subscales). False discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied across CTQ total and subscale tests. Exploratory voxel-wise regression examined CTQ–GMV associations within groups.

Results

SSI differed across groups (SD>NSD>HC; p<0.001). CTQ total and subscale scores were higher in SD and NSD than in HC, with no SD–NSD differences. Whole-brain VBM identified a focal GMV reduction in SD versus NSD in the septal nucleus (Z=4.73, pFWE=0.011). In contrasts versus HC, SD additionally showed GMV reductions involving the septal nucleus–hypothalamus and centromedial amygdala, whereas NSD–HC differences were centered on the sgACC. Septal ROI extraction confirmed a robust group effect (F(2,197)=19.95, p<0.001; partial η²=0.17), driven by lower septal GMV in SD than NSD and HC. Across patients with MDD, septal GMV was inversely associated with SSI (r=−0.209, p=0.023), while septal ROI associations with CTQ scores were not significant. In exploratory voxel-wise regression within SD, higher CTQ total and CTQ emotional abuse scores were associated with lower GMV in the cerebellar vermis/tonsil (TFCE-based voxel-wise FWE p<0.05).

Conclusion

Distinct gray matter alterations were observed in patients with MDD, with a focal septal GMV reduction associated with a lifetime history of suicide attempts and lower septal GMV tracking greater suicidal ideation. In parallel, childhood trauma severity showed negative voxel-wise associations with GMV in the cerebellar vermis/tonsil within SD. Together, these findings may implicate partially dissociable septal and cerebellar structural correlates of suicidality and early adversity in depression, warranting longitudinal and multimodal replication to clarify generalizability and temporality.
