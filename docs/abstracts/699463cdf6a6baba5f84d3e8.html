Introduction

Quantitative, temporal and spatial processes have been proposed to be part of a generalized magnitude system sharing the right intraparietal sulcus (r-IPS) as a common neural substrate [1]. Specificities for time and space have been also reported, identifying a role of the right Precuneus (r-Precuneus) in temporal estimations [3]. Translating this evidence to the language domain, and in line with the extended embodied knowledge framework, some studies associated the IPS with the processing of quantity-related concepts [2]. No evidence is available for concepts of time and space.

Here we investigated the causal role of r-IPS and r-Precuneus in representing time- and space-related concepts using TMS.

Methods

Fifty-six time- and space-related nouns, matched for frequency, length, age of acquisition, emotional valence, and mean priming effect were selected. Priming to a category label (TIME or SPACE) modulated the neural activation prior to TMS stimulation and target presentation. Subjects had to indicate the target’s category. Fifty-six congruent and 56 incongruent trials were presented for r-IPS, r-Precuneus, and sham-baseline (Vertex) sites. Twenty-two healthy subjects (12 females) participated. We performed a repeated measure ANOVA on the priming effect with TMS site as within-subject factor.

Results

For both time- and space-related concepts the r-IPS stimulation abolished the priming effect reported for the Vertex (p = .016), whereas r-Precuneus (p < 0.006) stimulation was effective only for time-related concepts.

Conclusion

In line with the Magnitude theory [1], results highlight the r-IPS as the shared substrate of the processing of linguistic stimuli connected to space and time. Instead, the r-Precuneus was tuned only for time concepts, which supports its role in temporal judgements [3].
