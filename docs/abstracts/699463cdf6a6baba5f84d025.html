Introduction

Auditory verbal hallucinations (AVH) in schizophrenia are hypothesized to arise from disruptions in cortical hierarchy and multisensory integration. However, the structural substrates linking cortical reorganization to AVH-related mechanisms remain poorly defined. Cortical gradient analysis is grounded in the macroscale organizational axis described by Margulies et al., who demonstrated that brain connectivity can be compressed into low-dimensional gradients capturing hierarchical transitions from sensory to transmodal systems.1 Leveraging the OpenNeuro ds004302 dataset (2022),2,3 we aimed to characterize multiscale cortical alterations and identify interpretable structural biomarkers distinguishing hallucinating from non-hallucinating patients.

Methods

T1-weighted MRI scans from 71 participants (25 HC, 23 AVH−, 23 AVH+) were preprocessed using SPM12 and subsequently parcellated with the Brainnetome Atlas (BNA, 3 mm). Cortical thickness across 122 ROIs was extracted to construct structural covariance matrices and diffusion-based cortical gradients (Fig. 1(a1–b3)). Low-dimensional gradient embeddings (Fig. 1(c1–c3)) characterized macroscale cortical organization. Gradient-derived features were used for multiclass classification (XGBoost, Logistic Regression, SVM), with performance metrics summarized in Fig. 2(a–d). XGBoost feature-importance rankings were used to derive ROI-level biomarkers and relate them to AVH-associated mechanisms.

Results

AVH+ patients showed reduced modularity of structural covariance and compressed cortical gradients (Fig. 1(a1–b3)), reflecting diminished sensory–association differentiation. Embedding manifolds displayed blurred topography unique to AVH+ (Fig. 1(c1–c3)).

Machine-learning models reliably separated AVH+, AVH−, and HC, with XGBoost achieving the best performance (micro-AUC = 0.707; Fig. 2(a)). Confusion matrices and bar-chart comparisons (Fig. 2(b–c)) confirmed group discriminability. XGBoost identified a stable set of AVH-related structural biomarkers (Fig. 2(d)), including:

Semantic–auditory integration areas: A20r (rostral area 20), A21r (rostral area 21), OPC (occipital polar cortex)

Self-monitoring & affective valuation regions: A12/47l (lateral area 12/47), A12/47o (orbital area 12/47), dld (dorsal dysgranular insula)

Executive-control cortex: A46 (area 46), A9/46d (dorsal area 9/46), A10l (lateral area10)

Affect–memory coupling regions: TH (medial PPHC), A32p (pregenual area 32), GP (globus pallidus)

Multisensory integration areas: V5/MT+ (area V5/MT+), A4ul (area 4(upper limb region))

These regions correspond to mechanisms such as impaired recognition of internally generated speech, misattribution of inner speech, weakened top-down control, and cross-modal propagation of hallucinatory content, consistent with mechanistic models of AVH involving auditory-language-memory interactions and impaired self-monitoring.4

Conclusion

Our multiscale analysis reveals that AVH is associated with structural covariance disruption, gradient compression, and altered embedding topology (Fig. 1). Gradient-derived ROI features not only enable accurate classification (Fig. 2) but converge onto mechanistically interpretable cortical systems involved in semantic processing, self-monitoring, executive regulation, and multisensory integration. These findings highlight a coherent structural basis for hallucinations and nominate a set of cortical biomarkers with translational relevance for symptom stratification in schizophrenia.
