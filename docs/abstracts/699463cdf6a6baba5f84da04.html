Introduction

Episodic migraine is characterized by heightened pain sensitivity, dysregulated stress reactivity, and altered interoceptive processing, implicating central nervous system mechanisms that integrate bodily signals with affective and autonomic regulation. The insula plays a key role in interoceptive awareness and is consistently implicated in migraine pathophysiology.1 Interventions that combine top-down interoception-focused behavioral training with bottom-up autonomic neuromodulation may yield synergistic clinical improvements by better targeting migraine neurocircuitry beyond either modality alone. Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) trains sustained attention to internal bodily signals,2 including respiratory drive, while Respiratory-gated Auricular Vagal Afferent Nerve Stimulation (RAVANS) delivers vagal afference synchronized with the respiratory rhythm and engages brainstem-autonomic circuits.3 We tested whether combining these interventions (Mindful-RAVANS) produces synergistic improvements in migraine-related symptoms, potentially via insula-based interoceptive neural processing.

Methods

We randomized n=106 individuals with episodic migraine to one of four 8-week therapies: MBSR paired with RAVANS (i.e. synergy), MBSR with sham stimulation, Nature-education control with RAVANS, or Nature-education control with sham stimulation.4 Post-therapy changes in headache-related disability and pain-related negative affect were assessed using the Headache Impact Test (HIT-6) in n=76 participants. Post-therapy changes in interoceptive processing were assessed in n=57 participants using a validated interoceptive attention fMRI task known to elicit robust bilateral anterior and mid-insula activation. The block-design task required sustained attention to cardiac and respiratory sensations, compared with control blocks of exteroceptive attention cues. Whole-brain and a priori insula region-of-interest analyses quantified task-evoked activation at pre- and post-therapy timepoints. Linear models with Holm-adjusted estimated marginal means contrasts tested whether the synergy group exhibited greater clinical improvement relative to combined monotherapy groups (i.e. MBSR-Sham and Nature-RAVANS). Steiger tests were used to compare group differences in the strength of the association between changes in HIT-6 scores and changes in insula activation.

Results

The Mindful-RAVANS (synergy) intervention reduced headache-related disability (HIT-6, p=0.024) more significantly than the monotherapy conditions. For the interoceptive fMRI task, reduced anterior insula response to interoception cues following therapy was linked to improvements in HIT-6, only for the combined therapy group (MBSR+RAVANS). This association differed significantly from the monotherapy groups (Steiger p=0.006; Zou 95% confidence interval for Δr [0.245, 1.245]), suggesting that alterations in interoceptive processing may underlie the augmented migraine relief following our novel synergistic therapy.

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate that combining top-down mindfulness-based interoceptive training with bottom-up vagal afferent neuromodulation produces synergistic improvements in migraine clinical outcomes, which may be supported by altered interoceptive processing in the insula. Our results highlight a mechanistically informed, brain–body integrative treatment approach for migraine and provide direct neuroimaging evidence of synergistic modulation of interoceptive circuits.
