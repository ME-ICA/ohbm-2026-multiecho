Introduction

Early life represents a critical window for the dynamic remodeling of white matter (WM) microstructure, with rapid axonal growth and myelination that lay the structural foundation for higher cognitive functions. ^1 Previous studies have indicated that differences in intrinsic biological factors as well as postnatal cultural environments may jointly shape axonal growth and myelination. However, most existing neurodevelopmental imaging studies have been limited to single population cohorts, primarily Western populations. The absence of direct cross-population comparisons within a unified analytical and modeling framework has limited the quantitative characterization of population-related differences in WM developmental patterns. Such cross-population studies are essential for establishing brain developmental references while addressing population-dependent bias, providing a more comprehensive picture of early brain development, and offering insights into the joint influence of genetic and environmental factors on early brain maturation.

To address this gap, the present study employed a combined diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) and neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI) approach to comprehensively capture axonal and neurite density characteristics beyond the limited sensitivity of single-tensor models. ^2 By combining data from the U.S. Baby Connectome Project ^5 (BCP) and the Chinese Baby Connectome Project ^4 (CBCP), we aggregated diffusion MRI datasets from two major infant populations. This enabled a systematical comparison of WM microstructural developmental trajectories between Chinese and Western infants aged 0-65 months, with the goal of identifying both unviersal and population-specific features of early brain structural maturation. Such cross-population insights may support more accurate and equitable clinical assessments of developmental disorders in diverse populations.

Methods

Age- and sex-matched (two-sample t test/χ² test, P > 0.05) infant participants were drawn from two multi-center MRI cohorts: CBCP  (n=140) and BCP (n =118). Diffusion MRI data from the two cohorts have the same spatial resolution (1.5 mm isotropic), similar imaging parameters (e.g., multiband factor of 4, multi-shell with b values ranging from 500 to 3000 s/mm2) were preprocessed with the same infant-dedicated pipeline ^3 consisting of denoising, Gibbs ringing artifact removal, eddy-current and motion correction. White-matter microstructure was quantified using two complementary models: DTI, fitted to low-b shells (b < 1000 s/mm²) to derive  fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusitivity (MD), axial diffusitivity (AD), and radial diffusivity (RD); and NODDI, fitted to the full multi-shell data using AMICO to estimate neurite density index (NDI), orientation dispersion index (ODI), and isotropic volume fraction (ISOVF).

Given the rapid morphological changes in infant during the first five years, an individualized white matter parcellation pipeline was performed. First, nonlinear deformation fields were estimated from the JHU-ICBM-DTI-81 white matter atlas to age-specific BCP templates. Second, subject-specific deformation matrices were computed from each participant’s T2-weighted image to the nearest age-matched template. Finally, the inverse of these transformations was concatenated to accurately project 48 major white matter ROI labels from the JHU atlas into each subject’s native diffusion space using nearest-neighbor interpolation. This approach minimized potential spatial distortions introduced by resampling rapidly developing infant images into a standard space. All individualized ROI masks underwent rigorous visual quality control

Statistical analyses were performed in R (v4.3.2) using generalized additive mixed models (GAMMs). Age effects were evaluated to determin whether metrics changed significantly with age, and age × cohort interaction terms were assessed to identify significant differences in developmental trajectories of white matter microstructure between Chinese and Western infants. Multiple comparisons were corrected using false discovery rate (FDR, p < 0.05)

Results

At the whole-brain WM and major fiber type levels, developmental trajectories exhibited highly similar nonlinear patterns in both CBCP and BCP. For diffusivity metrics (MD, AD, RD), ISOVF and ODI, both cohorts showed rapid age-related decreases followed by plateaus, with overall higher values in CBCP than in BCP; FA and NDI increased rapidly with age and gradually leveled off, showing highly overlapping trajectories and slightly higher values in BCP. At the whole-brain level, no significant age × cohort interaction effects were detected for any metric after FDR correction (Fig. 1a), indicating shared global developmental patterns. 

At the fiber type level, significant age × cohort interactions were observed for AD in association (p = 0.006) and commissural fibers (p = 0.013), for NDI (p = 0.021) and ODI (p = 0.017) in projection fibers, and for ISOVF in association fibers (p = 0.032), indicating fiber-system-specific group differences in developmental patterns. For fibers with significant age × cohort interactions on AD and ISOVF, CBCP showed overall higher values with more gradual decreases, in comparison to steeper declines in BCP. In projection fibers, NDI increased rapidly in both cohorts but plateaued earlier in CBCP, whereas ODI was initially higher in BCP at early months and declined more over time, resuting in reduced group differences later in development. No significant interaction effects were observed for FA, MD, or RD across fiber types.

ROI-level analyses revealed metric-specific group differences in developmental patterns (Fig.1b). Among DTI metrics, RD showed the highest between-cohort consistency, with only 2.7% of ROIs exhibiting significant interaction effects, indicating highly similar radial diffusivity trajectories and myelination patterns across most WM fibers. FA differences were observed in 9.5% of ROIs, primarily in the corpus callosum (CC, p = 0.042) and anterior limb of the internal capsule (ALIC, p = 0.042), suggesting group differences in fiber organization. MD (20.3%) differences were mainly observed in the corticospinal tract (CST, p = 0.0002) and superior longitudinal fasciculus (SLF, p = 0.032), indicating divergent developmental patterns of overall diffusion properties. In contrast, AD showed the most widespread differences (52.7%), encompassing nearly all major WM tracts, including the CST (p = 0.008) and SLF (p = 0.014), reflecting population-specific developmental trajectories of axial diffusivity. 

For NODDI metrics, NDI (16.2%) differences were concentrated in the posterior limb of the internal capsule (PLIC, p = 0.004) and cerebellar peduncles (CP, p = 0.004), indicating distinct neurite density developmental patterns. ODI differences (13.5%) involved the CST (p = 0.021) and PTR (p = 0.018), reflecting group differences in fiber orientation dispersion. ISOVF showed the most pronounced differences (29.7%), predominantly in the CC (p = 4.1×10⁻⁵) and SLF (p = 0.016), indicating divergent developmental trajectories of free water content.

To better reveal the overall patterns of significant effects, metrics were classified into three categories (Fig. 1b). The first category was dominated by age effects: RD showed age-only effects in 73.0% of ROIs, followed by MD (58.1%), NDI (56.8%), and FA (52.7%), indicating similar age-related changes across most fibers. The second category showed both age and interaction effects: AD showed such effects in 45.9% of ROIs, followed by MD (18.9%), NDI and ISOVF (both 16.2%), ODI (10.8%), and FA (8.1%), indicating age-related developmental changes that varied by cohort in these regions. The third category included metrics with distinct cohort-specific interaction effects in the absence of main age patterns: ODI was relatively stable during infancy, with age effects appearing in only 29.7% of ROIs, while ISOVF showed only interaction effects in 13.5% of ROIs, indicating cohort-specific developmental trajectories despite the absence of a strong overall age trend.

Conclusion

This study compared WM developmental trajectories between Chinese and Western infants. Our findings demonstrate that both cohorts exhibit highly similar nonlinear developmental patterns at the whole-brain WM level, with no significant age × cohort interaction effects detected for any diffusion or microstructural metrics. However, at the level of specific fiber systems and regional ROIs, AD and ISOVF exhibited relatively widespread group differences, while FA, MD, RD, NDI, and ODI showed consistent developmental patterns across most regions. These findings indicate that the overall patterns of early WM maturation are similar between Chinese and Western infants, while axial diffusivity and free water fraction display distinct developmental patterns in certain fiber tracts. This indicates that these two metrics may be particularly sensitive to early growth environments and population-specific developmental influences.
