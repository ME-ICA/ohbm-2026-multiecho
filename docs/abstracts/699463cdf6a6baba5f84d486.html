Introduction

Multisensory decision-making relies on integrating information from different sensory modalities, thereby guiding coherent and adaptive behavior. The visual uncertainty has been shown to significantly affect multisensory decision-making.1,2,3 Previous research has indicated the existence of different neural pathways for audiovisual integration4,5: Specifically, (1) the sensory area model posits that the direct interaction between the visual and auditory cortices forms the basis of audiovisual integration; (2) the higher association area model suggests that single-modal signals are first processed independently within their respective sensory cortices, and then integrated in areas such as the superior temporal sulcus and prefrontal cortex; and (3) the multi-pathway model posits that all pathways coexist and play different roles depending on task requirements and cognitive context. Despite the existence of these models for many years, it remains unclear which neural pathway predominates under conditions of increased visual uncertainty. In the present study, we used fMRI to examine how different levels of visual uncertainty modulate the cortical mechanisms supporting audiovisual decision-making.

Methods

Fifteen participants completed an audiovisual classification task in which stimuli were presented randomly under seven conditions (Figure A). These seven conditions included: high, medium, and low visual uncertainty (HV, MV, LV); one medium auditory uncertainty (MA); and high, medium, and low audiovisual uncertainty (HAV = HV + MA; MAV = MV + MA; LAV = LV + MA). Under all audiovisual conditions, the spatial frequency of Gabor patches was paired with corresponding auditory tones. When a high spatial frequency Gabor patch or a high-frequency tone was perceived, the left green button was pressed; when a low spatial frequency Gabor patch or a low-frequency tone was perceived, the right blue button was pressed. Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) data were acquired using a high temporal resolution T2*-weighted multiband accelerated echo-planar imaging (EPI) sequence. Acquisition parameters were as follows: repetition time (TR) = 800 ms, echo time (TE) = 30 ms, flip angle = 55°, matrix size = 104 × 104, voxel size = 2 × 2 × 2 mm³, field of view (FOV) = 208 × 208 mm, and 72 consecutive slices (slice thickness = 2 mm, no interslice spacing). fMRI preprocessing and general linear model (GLM) analysis were performed using FreeSurfer and AFNI software. This research protocol was approved by the ethics committees of Okayama University, Japan.

Conclusion

In the present study, we used fMRI to explore the neural mechanisms by which visual uncertainty affects multisensory integration. Our results indicate that the integration network exhibits a hierarchical structure and condition-dependent remodeling. The sensory cortex provides the necessary foundation for multisensory integration. In contrast, the brain increasingly relies on the higher association area model (STS/PFC) rather than the sensory area model with decreasing visual uncertainty. In summary, these findings support a multi-pathway model of audiovisual integration, the primary sensory cortex provides stable, modality-specific input, while higher association areas flexibly adjust according to the sensory uncertainty. Our study highlights the flexible weight redistribution mechanisms during multisensory decision-making and underscores the importance of sensory reliability in shaping the temporal and cortical localization of multisensory integration.
