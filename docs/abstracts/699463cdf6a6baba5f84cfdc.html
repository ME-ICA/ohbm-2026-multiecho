Introduction

It has become increasingly common to probe correlations between human brain imaging measures of receptor/protein binding and function using population-level brain maps, typically drawn from independent cohorts to estimate correlations across regions. This has followed from a rapid expansion in publicly available brain imaging datasets, with which researchers have explored evidence of molecular mechanisms underlying aspects of brain function. However, there are reasons to believe that this interpretation of across-regions correlations is fundamentally problematic and that such analyses may not inform about relations that are generalizable across individuals, irrespective of statistical significance correction strategies. We sought to highlight these pitfalls through a multimodal human brain imaging dataset including serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT2AR) binding with [11C]Cimbi-36 PET and cerebral blood flow with ASL MRI as well as an associated simulation study.

Methods

Twenty-four healthy volunteers (10 females; age, mean ± sd [range] = 32.5 ± 7.9 [24–58] years) were recruited as part of a broader study exploring neural effects of serotonin 2A receptor (5-HT2AR)
signaling (NCT03289949). Magnetic resonance imaging scans were acquired on one of two 3T Siemens Magnetom Prisma MRI scanners. A high-resolution T1-weighted MPRAGE was acquired, as well as either a 2D or 3D pseudo continuous arterial spin labeling (ASL) sequence to estimate cerebral blood flow (CBF). Positron emission tomography scans were acquired on a High Resolution Research Tomograph (HRRT) with [11C]Cimbi-36, a radioligand for measuring brain 5-HT2AR levels. A 120-min dynamic scan was acquired following a single bolus administration of [11C]Cimbi-36 using an establish imaging protocol.

Regional CBF was quantified from ASL MRI scans using BASIL in FSL (https://fsl.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/fsl/docs/physiological/basil.html). [11C]Cimbi-36 regional non-displaceable binding potential (BPND) was quantified from PET scans using Pvelab. An 18-region cortical/subcortical atlas from Pvelab was used to delineate regional CBF and BPND estimates.

The Pearson correlation coefficient between CBF and BPND was evaluated using four strategies to compare the across-regions strategy that is necessary if the data are pooled from different cohorts and across-participants correlations that can be estimated if both data are acquired in the same cohort.

  • Strategy 1.1: Across-regions strategy (one cohort). All participants have both BPND and CBF measuresments. Mean BPND and CBF values were computed for each region and the correlation between BPND and CBF across regions was estimated (ρ1.1).
  • Strategy 1.2: Across-regions strategy (two cohorts). Same as Strategy 1.1, except participants have either a BPND and CBF measuresment (not both). This is done by splitting the sample in half. As for Strategy 1.1, mean BPND and CBF values were computed for each region and the correlation between BPND and CBF across regions was estimated (ρ1.2).
  • Strategy 2: Across-participants strategy: Compute the correlation (ρ2) between the observed BPND and CBF across participants for each region, separately.
  • Strategy 3: Mixed model strategy: The correlation (ρ3) between the observed BPND and CBF values for all regions is fit with a single linear mixed model where each participant has 36 measurements (i.e., 18 regions by two modalities).

Complementary, we performed a simulation study under five conditions, including scenarios with and without a true underlying correlation between BPND and CBF and with and without region-specific mean and variance for modality-specific estimates. Simulations were run 10,000 times using the same structure a the real data (i.e., 18 regions) and either 1) N = 24 or or N = 1000 participants. Per scenario, we estimated relevant performance measures such as type-1 error, bias, coverage and statistical power.

Results

Estimating the across-regions correlation using either Strategy 1.1 and 1.2 indicated a strong correlation between CBF and 5-HT2AR (ρ1.1 = 0.67, p = 0.00023; ρ1.2 = 0.66, [0.57, 0.74], median [5-95% quantiles over 10,000 splits]). The across-participants regional correlations (Strategy 2) between CBF and 5-HT2AR ranged from ρ2 = -0.11 to 0.34 (average over all regions ρ2= 0.14), none of which were statistical significant. The across-participants linear mixed model estimating the correlation with region as a repeated measure was similarly not significant (ρ3 = 0.14, p = 0.26).

The simultation study results showed that the across-regions correlations (ρ1.1 and ρ1.2) were particularly sensitive to the presence of within-modality regional mean differences. The across-regions correlations showed poor type-1 error control in scenarios where the null hypothesis was true. In scenarios where there was a ground truth across-participants correlation, the across-regions correlations showed a biased correlation estimate vis-a-vis the ground truth across-participants correlation and the statistical power (1-β) = 1 even in smaller sample sizes of N = 24 participants. As expected, the across-participants approaches (Strategy 2 and 3) showed less bias, high coverage, and statistical power that increased from N = 24 to N = 1000 participants.

Conclusion

Here we analyze a multimodal human brain imaging dataset to highlight the discordance between across-regions and across-participants correlations, the latter of which is the only available correlation strategy where population-level imaging data are applied from separate cohorts, an increasingly common strategy. Our simulation results highlight the variability of the relation between the across-participants and across-regions correlation. This variation is particularly vulnerable in situations where there are regional differences in the mean signal, which is common for brain imaging measures. The discrepancy between across-participants and across-regions correlations emphasizes that they represent two distinct constructs that are not reliably related. This discordance highlights the ambiguity of how to interpret the across-regions correlation and whether it is an indication of a relation between the pair of biological constructs putatively being measured by the two imaging modalities.
