Introduction

Amyloid beta (Aβ) is the primary pathological characteristic of early Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and is associated with white matter disruptions. However, the impact of Aβ deposition on white matter microstructure, its spatial patterns, and associated transcriptomic signatures in cognitively normal individuals remains underexplored. 

Methods

We analyzed data from 257 cognitively normal individuals enrolled in the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative. T1-weighted imaging, diffusion weighted imaging and positron emission tomography scans were used to assess gray matter volume, white matter microstructure, and Aβ deposition. TractSeg was employed to segment fiber tracts across the whole brain, followed by the calculation of diffusion measures for each tract. Participants were categorized into Aβ-positive (Aβ+, n=83) and Aβ-negative (Aβ-, n=174) groups. Between-group comparisons of fiber tract diffusion measures were performed, and their associations with age, global Aβ deposition, and Aβ deposition in connected cortical and subcortical regions were assessed. Transcriptomic profiles from the Allen Human Brain Atlas were utilized to explore gene expression related to white matter damage.

Results

No brain regions exhibiting significant atrophy were identified in Aβ+ individuals compared to Aβ- individuals. White matter damage observed in Aβ+ individuals was characterized across eight fiber tracts, including anterior thalamic radiation, fronto-pontine tract, superior thalamic radiation, thalamo-premotor tract, thalamo-parietal tract, striato-premotor tract, corpus callosum rostral body, and corpus callosum anterior midbody. These microstructure alterations were primarily linked to cortical Aβ accumulation, but had little relationship with subcortical deposition. All of these results remained significant after adjusting for age. Gene-set expression enrichment analyses indicated that variations in the regional expression of genes involved in processes related to oligodendrocyte function and ontological terms pertaining to myelination were associated with white matter microstructure.

Conclusion

These results highlight that the white matter microstructure in Aβ+ elderly individuals has been altered during the cognitively normal stage, even before any detectable gray matter atrophy occurs. This suggests that white matter changes may serve as a focal point for early diagnosis and intervention.
