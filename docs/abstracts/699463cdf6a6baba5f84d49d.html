Introduction

Response inhibition is a core component of cognitive control and is critically implicated in a wide range of neuropsychiatric disorders. Prevailing neural models emphasize a right-lateralized activation of the inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) within the frontal-basal ganglia circuit during response inhibition.^1 However, evidence from lesion studies indicates that the left IFG also contributes to successful stopping,^2 motivating an extension beyond a strictly right-lateralized account toward a bilateral functional perspective. Moreover, clinical populations with inhibitory-control deficits exhibit multilevel alterations, including neurochemical imbalance in the γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA) and glutamate systems,^3 atypical functional lateralization,^4 and increased intra-individual behavioral variability.^5 Despite converging evidence, it remains unclear how neurochemical asymmetries support functional lateralization of inhibition and how variability across these levels shapes behavioral performance and stability.

Methods

To harness the broad variability in hemispheric lateralization among left-handers, forty-five left-handed adults were recruited (mean age = 22.7 years; 33% male). Response inhibition was assessed using the stop-signal task, with stop-signal reaction time (SSRT) estimated via the integration method. Intra-individual variability (IIV) was quantified using a Bayesian parametric approach implemented in BEESTS 2.0, yielding three parameters of the SSRT distribution: μ (mean), σ (standard deviation), and τ (exponential tail). Resting-state magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) was acquired from bilateral IFG voxels to quantify γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA+, macromolecule-contaminated) and glutamate–glutamine (Glx) levels (Figure1A shows individual and group voxel localization). Spectral fitting and metabolite quantification were performed using LCModel (v6.3-1R). Glx was quantified from edit-off spectra, whereas GABA+ was isolated using J-difference editing (see Figure 1B and 1C for off-spectra and difference-spectra fits, respectively). Metabolite concentrations were normalized to total creatine (tCr) to enhance inter-subject comparability. Neurochemical asymmetry indices for the bilateral IFG were calculated for GABA+, Glx, and the Glx/GABA+ ratio using the formula (left − right) / (left + right). Functional lateralization was assessed using task-based fMRI activation in bilateral IFG (Brodmann areas 44 and 45). Lateralization indices (LI) were computed using a bootstrap procedure implemented in the LI toolbox. Based on LI values, participants were classified into a typical group (right-hemisphere dominant, LI < 0) and an atypical group (left-hemisphere dominant, LI > 0) (Figure 1D-E). 

Results

Compared with the typically lateralized group, individuals with atypical lateralization exhibited significantly longer SSRTs (Figure 2A), along with greater μ and τ parameters (Figure 2B & 2D). Linear mixed-effects models of neurochemical asymmetry revealed a significant main effect of Hemisphere on Glx/tCr levels, reflecting a robust leftward Glx asymmetry across participants (Figure 2E). In contrast, no hemispheric differences were observed for GABA+ or the Glx/GABA+ ratio, and no significant interactions with functional lateralization direction were detected (Figure 2F-G). Crucially, mediation analyses conducted within the atypical group revealed a significant indirect effect of Glx/GABA+ asymmetry on σ_stop via functional lateralization strength (Figure 2H). Specifically, greater neurochemical asymmetry predicted reduced behavioral variability through stronger lateralized engagement of the IFG.

Conclusion

In conclusion, by integrating resting-state MRS, task-based fMRI, and Bayesian behavioral modeling in left-handed individuals, this study elucidates the neurobiological mechanisms underlying individual differences in response inhibition lateralization. Our results indicate that atypical lateralization is associated with poorer stopping performance and greater instability in inhibitory control. Notably, a robust leftward asymmetry in IFG Glx was observed independently of functional dominance, suggesting a dissociation between neurotransmitter organization and the direction of hemispheric lateralization. Importantly, Glx/GABA+ asymmetry magnitude, rather than direction, modulated intra-individual variability by influencing functional lateralization strength in the atypical group. Collectively, these findings establish a multilevel framework linking neurochemical asymmetry to the stability of inhibitory control and offer new insight into the neural basis of inhibitory dysfunction.
