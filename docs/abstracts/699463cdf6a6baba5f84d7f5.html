Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is increasingly conceptualized as a disorder of dysregulated large-scale brain networks and altered excitatory–inhibitory neurotransmission. Ketamine, a rapid-acting antidepressant, exerts its clinical effects through interactions with multiple receptor systems—including NMDA, AMPA, GABA-A, and opioid receptors—leading to rapid symptom improvement and alterations in intrinsic functional connectivity. 1,2 Converging evidence highlights the default mode network, and in particular the pregenual anterior cingulate cortex (pgACC), as a key target of ketamine’s antidepressant action. It is, however, not well understood which patients benefit from ketamine treatment and whether pgACC connectivity can be used as a candidate marker for a successful antidepressant response. Here, we examined whether pgACC resting state functional connectivity colocalization with normative receptor density patterns indicates the level of ketamine-related improvement in depressive symptoms.

Methods

We analyzed the publicly available Ketamine Mechanism of Action dataset (OpenNeuro ds005917, version 1.0.1), derived from a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial (NCT00088699). 3 The released dataset included 58 participants (33 MDD, 22 healthy controls, 3 bipolar disorder). Participants received a single intravenous infusion of ketamine hydrochloride (0.5 mg/kg over 40 minutes) and placebo (saline) in counterbalanced order, separated by a two-week washout period. Resting-state fMRI and clinical assessments were acquired at baseline and approximately 2 and 10 days following each infusion (Figure 1A). Resting-state fMRI scans (8 min, 3.75 × 3.75 × 3.5 mm) and high-resolution anatomical images (1 mm isotropic) were acquired on a 3T scanner. Depressive symptoms were assessed using the Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS). 4 fMRI data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep, and denoising controlled for 6 motion parameters, 5 white matter and 5 cerebrospinal fluid components, global signal, cosine drift parameters and scrubbing using Nilearn. Static seed-to-voxel connectivity was assessed in a first level general linear model with a 10 mm sphere at previously published coordinates of pgACC [x = 0, y = 42, z = 2] 5 for each of the fMRI sessions. Group-level connectivity was controlled for multiple comparisons with a two-sided false-discovery-rate adjustment. Spatial colocalization of individual pgACC connectivity at baseline with normative density distribution 6 of NMDA, GABA-A, k- and m-opioid receptors was examined using Spearman correlation coefficients restricted to voxels in a brain mask and with at least 10% grey matter probability in an average group-level grey matter mask. MADRS difference scores between baseline and Day 2/10 were assessed to quantify ketamine’s antidepressant effect, and inter-individual associations with baseline pgACC connectivity colocalization were tested as candidate markers.

Results

Ketamine induced a rapid antidepressant effect in participants with MDD (Figure 1B). Relative to baseline, MADRS scores were significantly reduced two days following ketamine administration (mean reduction = 9.22 points, t(26) = 4.61, p < .001), with symptom improvement persisting at Day 10 (mean reduction = 4.38 points, t(25) = 2.29, p = .031), consistent with previously reported rapid-onset and transient antidepressant responses to ketamine. 7 Resting-state functional connectivity with a seed in the pgACC showed widespread connectivity patterns, encompassing the posterior cingulate cortex—consistent with the medial frontoparietal default mode network—as well as dorsolateral prefrontal and posterior parietal cortices (Figure 1C). Spatial colocalization of baseline pgACC connectivity with κ- and μ-opioid receptor density showed negative associations with participants’ MADRS difference scores, particularly at Day 10 (Figure 1D, E). 

Conclusion

In this openly available randomized crossover dataset, we replicated rapid and temporally specific antidepressant effects of ketamine in individuals with MDD, with symptom improvement at Day 2 and partial persistence at Day 10. Baseline pgACC connectivity exhibited a molecular colocalization profile with κ- and μ-opioid receptors that was associated with the magnitude of antidepressant response to ketamine. Specifically, greater pgACC connectivity colocalization with κ- and μ-opioid receptors was associated with larger reductions in depressive symptoms. These findings complement existing models of ketamine’s mechanisms of action and suggest that baseline neurobiological characteristics, such as pgACC connectivity, may contribute to predicting which individuals show the most pronounced antidepressant response.
