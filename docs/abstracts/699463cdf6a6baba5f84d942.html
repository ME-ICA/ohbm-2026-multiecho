Introduction

Treatment-resistant depression (TRD) is a psychiatric disorder that has limited improvement with standard medications or psychotherapy. Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is one of the most effective treatments for TRD, yet it can result in adverse side effects (e.g., cognitive difficulty).1 Magnetic seizure therapy (MST) is a promising new antidepressant neurotherapeutic, offering similar antidepressant benefits with potential greater cognitive safety.2 In the context of depression, neural dysfunction consistently emerges in the default mode network (DMN) and frontoparietal network (FPN)3 and connectivity between these networks may underlie treatment outcome.4,5 Despite high effectiveness, the brain mechanisms underlying both ECT and MST remain unclear. Thus, the aim of this study is to investigate how convulsive therapy influences resting-state functional connectivity in TRD.

Methods

Adults with TRD enrolled in a large-scale, NIMH-funded, randomized clinical trial that compared the clinical and neurocognitive outcomes of ECT vs. MST (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT03191058; grant ID: R01 MH119285-01). Resting state functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) was collected pre- and post- treatment course. After pre-processing (MRIQC, fMRIPrep, XCP-D), we extracted time series data using Schaefer 400 and Tian 32 sub-cortical parcellations with the 7 Yeo networks. These data were harmonized using longCombat to account for differences across testing sites. Linear regression models assessed how intersectional factors such as age and sex affected average FPN, DMN, and FPN-to-DMN functional connectivity, accounting for motion and the number of total treatments received.

Results

Preliminary analyses included 76 patients (18 - 72 years old; 55% female, 45% male) with high quality pre and post fMRI scans. Results demonstrated a significant age by sex interaction in FPN-to-DMN connectivity (F(1,69)=4.11, p=.047). Differential age and sex effects were observed for between- but not within-network connectivity, which was partially unexpected. Specifically, pre-to-post changes in connectivity increased with age in males but not females. 

Conclusion

This study found differential effects of age and sex on neurocircuitry following either ECT or MST for TRD. Irrespective of clinical outcome, our results demonstrate that network changes following seizure therapy may differ across the lifespan in men and women. Future work is warranted to examine how this relationship may differ between neurotherapeutic treatments (e.g., ECT, MST) and link these network changes to clinical and cognitive outcomes.
