Introduction

Since Goldstein’s seminal descriptions of patients who lost the “abstract attitude” following brain injury, the capacity for abstraction has been a defining feature of human cognition^1. Abstraction is the ability to identify general rules that describe underlying principles in a way that transcends surface features, and has been closely linked to the frontal lobes. Frontal lesions impair the ability to generate hypotheses, extract rules from temporal and spatial sequences, and segment visual input into higher-order structures.

However, its functional organisation remains debated. Abstraction is closely related to reasoning and fluid intelligence (Gf); however, lesion evidence suggests a laterally differentiated organisation, with specific right frontal regions contributing uniquely to Gf.

Proverb interpretation offers a valuable probe, requiring suppression of literal meaning and flexible application across context. Unfortunately, existing studies rely on small samples and yield anatomically inconsistent findings. Resolving this necessitates larger, anatomically precise lesion cohorts and more informative behavioural measures.

A promising avenue is machine learning. To our knowledge, no study has yet modelled proverb interpretation in patients with focal lesions using Large Language Models (LLMs). LLMs generate abstract proverb interpretations and can move beyond literal meaning, demonstrating that proverb interpretation can be instantiated in an artificial neural network.

Here, in the largest cohort to date of patients with focal lesions (n = 158) and healthy controls (n = 134), we systematically degrade an LLM to simulate the impairment patterns observed in patients, assessing faithfulness quantitatively and qualitatively, employing our model to classify patient errors.

By integrating neuropsychology, computational modelling, and network-level lesion inference, we aim to faithfully characterise the neural and computational mechanisms underpinning abstraction.

Methods

Data were analysed from 52 Left Frontal (LF), 55 Right Frontal (RF), 64 Non-Frontal patients, and 134 healthy controls (HCs). All patients met inclusion criteria: lesion due to stroke or tumour; ≥70% of lesion volume within target region; no history of other neurological/psychiatric disease; and native English proficiency. Groups were matched for age, gender, and education.

The Proverb Interpretation Test (PIT) was adapted from our previous work^2 and comprises eight common English proverbs. Participants were asked to explain the meaning of each. Responses were manually scored for accuracy (0–2) and classified as "concrete errors" if they reflected literal interpretations (e.g., "The grass is greener" referring to a garden).

We used Google Gemma 7B^3, the state-of-the-art Large Language Model (LLM) in its scale at the time of our study. Gemma produced correct abstract interpretations of  proverbs, while remaining computationally tractable for large scale degradation experiments. Interpretations were elicited using the prompt “What is the meaning of the following proverb? {proverb}.” Gemma correctly interpreted all 8 proverbs administered to participants.

To selectively impair Gemma’s ability to generate abstract representations, we applied noise and pruning. Manual exploration showed that pruning the post-attention layer normalization block—positioned between attention and feed-forward layers—consistently disrupted abstract proverb interpretation. Hence, all subsequent experiments targeted post-attention layer normalization blocks.

We evaluated performance using the Open LLM Leaderboard Suite V2 at a variety of pruning levels (Figure 1). We computed both lexical and semantic similarity between LLM and participant responses, using cosine similarity of Term-Frequency Inverse-Document-Frequency (TF-IDF) weighted bag-of-words vectors and BAAI/bge-en-icl embeddings respectively.

To qualitatively examine whether degraded model outputs resembled participant responses, a blinded neuropsychologist, who was naïve to both LLM and participant responses, classified 64 harmonised responses as LLM vs participant in a Turing-style task.

To derive a continuous and fully automated measure of concreteness, we introduce the Equivalent Degradation Level (EDL). EDL maps each participant response to the most similar Concrete Gemma Variant for the same proverb. For each proverb and degradation level, we sampled 128 Gemma-generated interpretations to yield a diverse set of outputs. Each participant response was compared with all Concrete Gemma Variant responses using cosine similarity between embedding representations, and the degradation level showing the highest mean similarity was assigned as that response’s EDL.

Without any additional training, EDL was used as a continuous index of concreteness. Predictive accuracy was assessed using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC), which quantified discrimination between manually labelled concrete and abstract interpretations.

For each proverb, we calculated the proportion of concrete errors, yielding an empirical proverb difficulty profile that we could compare between participants and Concrete Gemma.

Magnetic resonance imaging data were available for 82 patients (59 frontal, 23 non-frontal). Lesions were manually delineated and normalised to MNI space. We applied a graph-based Bayesian Stochastic Block Modelling (SBM)^4 framework to disentangle functional lesion-deficit relationships from incidental lesion topology^5. We constructed a graph where nodes represented voxels and edges represented lesion co-occurrence and behavioural scores. We conducted two lesion-mapping analyses: 1) Using manually labelled concrete errors; and 2) Using the computational EDL metric. For each, we fitted a null model (random distribution) and a true layered model. Model entropy was used to assess fit.

Results

Frontal patients performed significantly worse than Non-frontal patients and HCs on overall PIT performance (p<0.001). Specifically, Left Frontal (LF) patients produced significantly more concrete errors than both Right Frontal (RF) and Non-frontal patients (p<0.001), while RF and Non-frontal did not differ significantly.

A random search over pruning proportion and sampling temperature identified a configuration we termed Concrete Gemma 50, corresponding to 50% probability of pruning and a low sampling temperature (0.1128), that produced concrete interpretations across all proverbs. Despite pronounced concreteness, Concrete Gemma 50 retained surface-level linguistic abilities (Figure 2A).

Degradation severity was adjusted in 5% increments from 0% (Intact Gemma) up to 100%. More severe pruning (>50%) degraded grammaticality. When we evaluated performance at the various degradation levels using the Open LLM Leaderboard Suite V2, 6 out of the 39 tasks were excluded because Intact Gemma did not perform better than chance.  We found that the most discriminative performance drops were observed at Concrete Gemma 60, where MUSR, BBH, MMLU-Pro, GPQA, IFEVAL, and MATH dropped 104%, 95%, 88%, 67%, 52%, and 17% respectively (Figure 1).

Lexical and semantic similarity analyses found that Intact Gemma’s responses were more similar to participants correct interpretations than to concrete errors (lexical cosine similarity was 0.120 vs 0.066; embedding was 0.707 vs 0.633). Progressively pruning post-attention normalization layers reduced this distinction, and the most degraded variants more closely resembled concrete errors.

The experienced neuropsychologist correctly identified 54.7% of responses as participant vs LLM, not above chance (p = 0.532). Classification accuracy did not vary significantly with degradation level (p = 0.75).

For each participant’s response, the most similar Gemma Variant defined its EDL. The EDL served as a continuous predictor of concreteness and achieved a mean AUROC of 0.790 across proverbs. Performance varied by item (AUROC range 0.67-0.97).

Proverb-specific difficulty was significantly correlated between Concrete Gemma and LF patients (p = 0.038, n = 8 proverbs; Figure 2B, permutation test). Proverbs eliciting more concrete errors in LF patients also elicited more concrete errors in degraded models.  

Our graph-based SBM analysis of manually labelled concrete errors revealed a strongly lateralised, extensive left frontal network, primarily encompassing the superior and middle frontal gyri and the left caudate. In contrast, the model using the computational EDL metric replicated this left frontal involvement but restricted it significantly, to a slightly more rostral network. Notably, the EDL analysis additionally implicated a distinct Right Fronto-Parietal (RFP) network, centred on the right inferior frontal gyrus, which was not detected by manual scoring.

We directly compared the fit of these two structurally identical models using entropy measures. The model employing the computational EDL achieved a significantly better fit (lower entropy) compared to the manual error model (889,175 nats vs 1,055,830 nats), suggesting it better captures the underlying neural architecture. To independently validate the behavioural relevance of this newly identified RFP network, we compared patients with lesions in this specific region (n=34) to all other right-hemisphere patients (n=59). Patients with RFP lesions produced nearly twice as many concrete errors as non-RFP patients (23% vs 12%), confirming the functional significance of this region for abstraction.

Conclusion

This study provides the first quantitative characterisation of proverb understanding in a large focal lesion cohort combined with a "lesioned" LLM. We confirmed that LF damage produces a profound deficit in abstraction, distinct from fluid intelligence, wherein patients default to literal interpretations.

We then “lesioned’’ Gemma 7B to model patient behaviour. Intact Gemma consistently produced fully abstract and correct interpretations, closely matching participants’ abstract interpretations responses across both lexical and embedding similarity measures.   As post-attention normalization layers were progressively pruned, outputs showed a graded shift towards concreteness. At moderate pruning, the gap between abstract and concrete interpretations narrowed; at higher levels, model outputs resembled patient-like concrete errors. Among industry standard benchmarks, those that require complex reasoning (MUSR, BBH) showed the greatest degradation in performance, while instruction following (IFEVAL) and knowledge (GPQA) were much less susceptible. These effects revealed a parametric dependence of abstraction on the integrity of layer normalization processes in Gemma 7B. A neuropsychologist was unable to distinguish human from model responses across degradation levels, and independent ratings showed an increase in concreteness with pruning. Despite this loss of abstraction, degraded variants maintained surface-level linguistic fluency while failing on tasks requiring multi-step or compositional reasoning. Proverb-specific difficulty aligned closely between Concrete Gemma and LF patients: proverbs most likely to elicit concrete errors clinically were more likely to elicit concrete errors in Concrete Gemma. An Equivalent Degradation Level (EDL) metric mapped each patient response to the model variant whose output it most resembled; without supervised training, EDL robustly predicted concreteness across proverbs.

Crucially, our graph-based SBM analysis demonstrated that the computational EDL metric was a superior predictor of neural damage than manual scoring. While manual scoring implicated a broad LF network, the EDL metric refined this to a focal rostral LF region and uncovered a previously unreported Right Fronto-Parietal (RFP) contribution, validated by a double dissociation in right-hemisphere patient error rates. Together, these findings indicate that proverb interpretation depends on a bilateral, but asymmetrically weighted, frontal system. We propose that concrete errors serve as a sensitive marker of dysfunction in this network.
