Introduction

Cerebral small vessel diseases (SVDs) contribute to cognitive decline, while clinical heterogeneity exists. The presence of tuberothalamic artery (TTA) may play as a potential vascular reserve marker, which affects cognition. In addition, systemic inflammation can also contribute to cognitive outcomes potentially. This study aimed to investigate the complex relationships among TTA variation, SVD markers, systemic inflammation and cognitive outcomes in SVD patients.  Invivo TTA pattern and SVD markers were identified for each subject using multi modal 7T magnetic resonance imaging (7T-MRI). Systemic inflammatory markers were evaluated through blood samples analysis, and cognitive function were assessed using a battery of established neuropsychological tests. It was hypothesized that (1) the presence of TTA can contribute to better cognition, (2) the impact of TTA pattern on cognition may be mediated by specific SVD markers, (3) systemic inflammatory markers may moderate the associations between TTA pattern and cognitive function. 

Methods

Study design and participants

This study included participants from an ongoing prospective longitudinal 7T MRI study on SVD conducted in Yueyang Hospital of Integrated Traditional Chinese and Western Medicine, Shanghai, China. 110 baseline participants between December 2020 and November 2022 were screened, of which 101 with serum sample measurements, complete neuropsychological tests, and qualified 7T MRI images were finally included. Demographic characteristics, including age, sex, years of education, and body mass index, were collected for each participant, as displayed in Table 1. A total risk factor score for each participant was assessed based on 6 traditional risk factors, including alcohol consumption, smoking, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, and history of cerebrovascular diseases. Higher score indicates higher vascular risk.

Systemic inflammatory markers

Venous blood samples were collected from participants in the morning after a 12-hour fasting using a Vacutainer K2 EDTA Tube (6.0 mL, #367863; Becton Dickinson, NJ, USA) and a Vacutainer SST II Tube (5.0 mL, #367955; Becton Dickinson, Plymouth, UK). The serum levels of IL-1β, IL-2R, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) were assessed using the Siemens kit in Immulite 1000 automatic chemiluminescence immunoassay analyzer (Siemens Healthcare Diagnostics GmbH, Berlin, Germany).  

MRI acquisition and assessment

All participants underwent 7T MRI scanning (Magnetom Terra; Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) within 14 days of enrollment, using a 1-Tx/32-Rx head coil. The MRI protocol included T1-weighted imaging, T2-weighted imaging, T2 dark-fluid imaging, susceptibility-weighted and time-of-flight imaging. SVD markers, including lacunes, WMH, EPVS, and CMB, were rated respectively. 

Participants were categorized into two groups according to the presence or absence of the TTA branch from PCoA, which was identified on 7T TOF. TTA-positive (TTA+) group was defined by the clear identification of a TTA originating from the PCoA. TTA-negative (TTA-) group was defined by the absence of a discernible TTA arising from the PCoA. Both hemispheric and global TTA patterns were considered. Global TTA+ indicated presence of TTA in either hemisphere, whereas global TTA- indicated absent TTA in both sides. 

Clinical phenotype assessment

Overall cognitive ability was assessed by adminiatering validated Chinese versions of the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) and the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) were administered for all subjects. Multidomain cognitive tests were examined. Memory function was tested using the Auditory Verbal Learning test (AVLT) and Rey-Osterrieth Complex Figure Test (CFT) delayed recall; visuospatial ability was tested using CFT-copy, language was tested using the Category Verbal Fluency Test (CVFT) and the Boston Naming Test (BNT); attention was tested using the Digit Span Test (DST); executive function was tested using the Trail Making Test part B (TMT-B) and the Stroop Color-Word Test (SCWT).

Statistical analyses

To first identify the hemisphere of interest, the influence of TTA pattern on SVD markers and cognitive scores were examined using multiple linear regression models separately for each hemisphere and the whole brain. The hemisphere who showed the strongest and most significant association with cognition was chosen for subsequent analyses. Then, we tested whether the relationship between TTA pattern and cognition was mediated by specific SVD markers. Next, interaction effect of the chosen TTA pattern and systemic inflammatory markers on cognition were examined. Since the moderation and mediation may occur together in a single model, moderated mediation analyses were finally conducted using appropriate model. The TTA pattern was considered as an independent variable and cognitive scores as dependent variables. Specific SVD marker and systemic inflammation marker were considered as the mediator and moderator respectively.

Results

Associations of TTA patterns with SVD markers and cognition

For SVD markers, the right TTA pattern was associated with the ePVS_BG positively, whereas left and global TTA patterns exhibited no similar effects (Table 1).

Since SVD burden has been evidenced associating with cognition in numerous work[19-22], it was taken as a covariate when we examed the association between TTA patterns and cognition. For cognition, right TTA+ subjects indicated higher MoCA score (P =.035, 95% CI, 0.089 to 2.47) and higher CFT-recall (P =.049, 95% CI, 0.017 to 5.29), suggesting better global cognition and memory function. No similar association of left and global TTA pattern with cognition was observed (Table 2). Multiple comparison correction was not performed in this case as most associations were nonsignificant. The right TTA pattern was recommended in the subsequent analyses. 

Mediating effects of SVD markers on the cognitive impact of right TTA pattern

Since right TTA pattern was indicated associating with both ePVS_BG and congnitive scores, ePVS in the basal ganglia was taken as the potential mediator in the mediation model and right TTA pattern was as independent variable. MoCA and CFT_recall scores were considered as dependent variables respectively. 

After controlling for age, sex, years of education and total risk factor score, ePVS_BG was found mediating the effect of right TTA pattern on MoCA score (Indirect Effect = -0.50, 95% CI, -1.16 to -0.04, excludes zero). The direct effect of right TTA pattern on MoCA score was also significant (Direct Effect = 1.61, 95% CI, 0.40 to 2.82, excludes zero). As to memory function, mediation effect of ePVS_BG was not significant (Indirect Effect = -0.27, 95% CI, -1.01 to 0.27, include zero) since no association of ePVS_BG with CFT_recall (P =.37, 95% CI, -3.19 to 1.20) was indicated .

Moderating effects of systemic inflammatory markers on the cognitive impact of  TTA pattern

Model 1 in the PROCESS macro was used to exam the moderation effect of systemic inflammatory markers. Right TTA pattern was taken as the independent variable. MoCA and MMSE scores were taken as dependent variables respectively. Log-transformed systemic inflammatory markers were examined as moderartors.

Moderating effect of IL-8 with right TTA pattern was domenstrated affecting both MoCA and MMSE scores. After controlling for age, sex, years of education and total risk factor score, the main effect of TTA pattern was significant for both MoCA (coeff = 6.08, P =.013, 95% CI, 1.32 to 10.85) and MMSE scores (coeff = 3.61, P =.018, 95% CI, 0.63 to 6.60). Similarly, the main effect of IL-8 level was also significant (MoCA: coeff = 2.61, P =.013, 95% CI, 0.55 ~ 4.66; MMSE: coeff = 1.83, P =.006, 95% CI, 0.55 to 3.12). Moreover, the interaction between right TTA pattern and IL-8 was also statistically significant (MoCA: coeff = -2.88, P =.042, 95% CI, -5.66 to -0.11; MMSE: coeff = -1.94, P =.029, 95% CI, -3.68 to -0.21). 

To probe the significant interaction, simple slop analyses at three levels of the IL-8 (low: mean – 1SD, mean, high: mean + 1SD) were conducted (Fig. 3). At low level of IL-8, right TTA+ group exhibited significant higher MoCA (Effect = 2.76, P =.005, 95% CI, 0.86 to 4.67) and MMSE (Effect = 1.37, P =.025, 95% CI, 0.18 to 2.57) scores, indicating better global cognition. At mean level of IL-8, the relationship between right TTA pattern and MoCA score remained significant (Effect = 1.38, P =.040, 95% CI, 0.067 to 2.70), but no longer significant with MMSE (Effect = 0.44, P =.29, 95% CI, -0.38 to 1.27). At high level of IL-8, the relationship of right TTA pattern with MoCA and MMSE scores were not significant (MoCA: Effect = -0.0003, P =.9997, 95% CI, -1.83 to 1.83; MMSE: Effect = -0.49, P =.40, 95% CI, -1.64 to 0.66).

Conclusion

There were 3 main findings in this study. First, the presence of right TTA indicated higher MoCA and CFT-recall scores in SVD petients. Second, the effect of right TTA on cognition can be mediated by the numbers of ePVS in the basal ganglia. Third, with the increase of systemic IL-8 level, the effect of TTA pattern on cognition was attenuated.
