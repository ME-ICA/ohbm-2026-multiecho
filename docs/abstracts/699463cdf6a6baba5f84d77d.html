Introduction

Localization of the epileptogenic zone (EZ) is critical for surgical planning in drug-resistant epilepsy. Interictal SEEG recordings contain spectral deviations reflecting pathological network dynamics, yet distinguishing persistent abnormalities from background variability remains challenging. Normative intracranial EEG (Norm-iEEG) maps quantify patient-specific deviations relative to a normative atlas, providing a framework for systematic identification of abnormal regions ^1–3. However, the stability of these deviations across long interictal recordings, their frequency specificity, and correspondence with clinically defined seizure-onset zones (SOZ) or Epileptogenicity Index (EI) maps remain unclear.

Here, we applied Norm-iEEG to 6-hour and 12-hour interictal SEEG recordings, as well as ictal segments from the same patient. We aimed to (1) characterize frequency-specific abnormality distributions across AAL3 parcels and channels, (2) assess reproducibility across recording durations, (3) compare interictal abnormalities with EI-derived epileptogenic regions^4,5, and (4) evaluate the added value of channel- vs parcel-level analyses.

Methods

SEEG recordings were obtained from a patient with drug-resistant temporal lobe epilepsy. Two long interictal datasets (6 h, 12 h) and 3 seizures (12 × 60-s segments) were analysed with two approaches (Figure 1). Approach 1- Band-wise normative z-scores: Spectral power in delta (0.5–4 Hz), theta (4–8 Hz), alpha (8–13 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz), and gamma (30–80 Hz) bands was computed per channel and segment using Welch’s method. Channel-level z-scores were calculated relative to a control cohort. Segments with |z| > 2 were flagged as abnormal. For each AAL3 region, counts of abnormal segments were aggregated, and high-frequency (beta+gamma) to low-frequency (theta+alpha+beta+gamma) ratios were computed to prioritize putatively epileptogenic regions. Approach 2- Ratio-based normative mapping: Raw band powers were combined into spectral ratios (e.g., HF/LF, HF/LF_excl_delta). Normative means and standard deviations for each ratio were computed from the control cohort. Patient z-scores and abnormal segments (|z| > 2) were computed per channel and aggregated per region. This approach quantifies deviations in spectral balance relative to normative expectations.

EI values were computed from ictal SEEG^4 and interpolated to generate volumetric Epileptogenicity Maps (EM) ^5. Each grid point was assigned the nearest electrode’s AAL3 parcel, and the maximum EI per parcel was extracted. Norm-iEEG abnormalities were compared with EI values and clinical SOZ labels using correlation analysis. Both 6 h and 12 h interictal recordings were analyzed independently. Analyses presented here focus on a single dataset for clarity; additional recordings (≥10) are planned for validation.

Results

Approach 1: Both 6 h and 12 h interictal recordings showed consistent spatial and frequency-specific abnormality patterns across AAL3 parcels. For interictal analysis, persistent elevated high-frequency (beta/gamma) AR values were observed in ParaHippocampal_R, while theta and alpha abnormalities were prominent in Amyg_Hipp_R and theta in Amyg_Hipp_L, comparable with clinically defined SOZ (Figure 2A, 2B). Channel-level analyses yielded similar patterns, confirming that the regional abnormalities reflected consistent electrode-level deviations (Figure 2D). For ictal analysis, channel- vs parcel-level comparisons revealed complementary insights. Channel-level analyses evaluate each electrode individually against the normative atlas, revealing focal peaks in abnormality. In contrast, parcel-level maps average the values of all electrodes within an AAL3 region before comparison to the normative model, highlighting recurrent abnormalities at the regional scale. Consequently, some focal ictal abnormalities (e.g., RA-2 electrode) that were clearly visible at the channel level were diluted in the parcel-level maps, resulting in lower averaged values that were not apparent in the regional representation (Figure 2C, 2E).

Approach 2: Frequency-ratio-based mapping showed distinct patterns between interictal and ictal periods. Parcel-level heatmaps (Figure 6) indicated that interictal elevations mainly reflected propagation regions rather than clinically defined SOZ, with inconsistencies across segments and weak effects in averaged maps. In contrast, ictal frequency-ratio elevations, particularly in the Amyg–Hipp R parcel, consistently aligned with the clinically identified SOZ. Channel-level analyses preserved focal effects, confirming that ictal frequency-ratio abnormalities are more robust and informative than interictal deviations, while overall spatial interpretation remained consistent with parcel-level maps

Parcels with the highest AR values corresponded to regions of maximal EI, validating Norm-iEEG as a reliable indicator of epileptogenic tissue. Ictal deviations were spatially heterogeneous but largely mirrored interictal abnormality distributions. Beta-band abnormalities showed the strongest alignment with EI and clinical SOZ, alpha-band exhibited moderate correspondence, and theta/gamma bands showed variable associations. Overall, both approaches demonstrated that long-duration interictal recordings capture recurrent spectral deviations that correspond well with clinically defined SOZ and EI. Interictal abnormalities were reproducible across 6 h and 12 h recordings, supporting stability of normative deviations across days.

Conclusion

Norm-iEEG provides stable, frequency-resolved maps of epileptogenic abnormalities across long interictal recordings. Analyses of 6 h and 12 h datasets demonstrated reproducible patterns, indicating that long-duration recordings can robustly capture recurrent spectral deviations. High-frequency abnormalities strongly corresponded with both clinical SOZ and EI-derived maps, confirming convergent validity. Channel- and parcel-level analyses offer complementary perspectives. While the present work focuses on a single dataset, a second recording produced similar results, and a larger cohort (≥10 recordings) is in progress. These findings support the use of Norm-iEEG as a practical, interpretable tool for pre-surgical evaluation of epileptogenic networks, highlighting both interictal stability and frequency-specific localization of pathological activity.
