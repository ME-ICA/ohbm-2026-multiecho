Introduction

This study applies contrastive learning to learn representations that capture neuroanatomical variability associated with psychiatric phenotype in two large-scale datasets, namely UKB (https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk/) and the ABCD Study (https://abcdstudy.org/). Aligned with the conceptual shift in psychiatry from categorical to dimensional models (e.g. RDoC¹), we define psychiatric phenotypes along continuous factors of psychopathology derived with Exploratory Factor Analysis. Our approach integrates these latent factors into the weakly supervised contrastive learning y-Aware framework,² hypothesizing that it enhances the clinical relevance of the learned representations.

Methods

Structural MRI data

We used T1-weighted MRI scans from the UKB (n = 42,786 unique participants and scans) and ABCD datasets (n = 11,460 unique participants, n = 21,216 scans). We preprocessed them using voxel-based morphometry (VBM) to extract normalized gray matter volumes in MNI space.

UKB behavioral metadata

Out of the data available in UKB, we selected 124 behavioral phenotypes that were deemed salient for the prediction of mental disorders. These phenotypes can be classified into twelve distinct categories: Anxiety, Cognition, Depression, Mania, Perinatal, Personality, Psychosis, Self-harm, Sleep, Social, Substance use, and Trauma. We converted all the responses to a continuous numerical scale and imputed missing values using the MICE method. An Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) was performed on these data. A five-factor solution provided the best fit and aligned well with the RDoC framework.¹ We labeled the five resulting factors based on the categories corresponding to the items with the strongest absolute loadings on each factor: “Acute stress”, “Chronic stress”, “Mood disorder”, “Impulsivity”, and “Cognition” (Fig. 1B).

ABCD behavioral metadata

For ABCD, following prior work,³ we derived latent factors of psychopathology using exploratory factor analysis. We used the Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) data, resulting in three dimensions of youth psychopathology: externalizing, internalizing and neurodevelopmental symptoms (Fig. 1B).

To ensure there is a baseline anatomy-behavior correlation, we first predicted these dimensions using Ridge regression on VBM features defined on the neuromorphometrics atlas.

Psy-Aware framework

We introduce the so-called psy-Aware framework (Fig. 1C), a weakly-supervised contrastive approach that incorporates behavioral factors into the loss function, extending y-Aware to a multidimensional loss, as in Dufumier et al..⁴ Formally, for a batch of N images, we write zi, zi+N the embeddings of the two views from image i and yi = yi+N the corresponding d factors of psychopathology. For a pair of embeddings zi, zj the psy-Aware loss uses a Gaussian kernel wΣ with bandwidth matrix Σ to modulate the loss based on the similarity of auxiliary labels yi, yj, pulling embeddings closer when psychopathology scores are more similar. In both ABCD and UKB, we compared psy-Aware against a baseline incorporating as y-Aware auxiliary variable the factor best predicted from VBM. In UKB, we also included age-Aware as a strong weakly-supervised baseline.² We added SimCLR⁵ as a standard unsupervised contrastive baseline to the comparison. All models used a ResNet18 encoder backbone with an embedding dimension of 512 and random erasing, random cropping and Gaussian noise as data augmentation. We trained on ABCD for 13,300 steps (100 epochs) and on UKB for 18,200 steps (60 epochs).

Downstream tasks

As in previous work,² pretrained representations were evaluated by linear probing for three classification tasks: bipolar disorder (BD) vs. healthy control (HC), schizophrenia (SCZ) vs. HC, and autism spectrum disorder (ASD) vs. HC (Fig. 1D). Each of these tasks was carried out on datasets containing, respectively, n=1,233, 1,352, and 2,117 distinct subjects obtained from the ABIDE 1+2, BIOBD, BSNIP, SCHIZCONNECT-VIP, PRAGUE, CANDI and CNP datasets. Performance was assessed using Monte-Carlo cross-validation (200 folds) with the AUROC metric.

Results

As a sanity check, all psychopathological factors, except internalizing, can be predicted better than chance level from VBM features. Neurodevelopmental symptoms and cognition are the factors best predicted respectively in ABCD and in UKB (Fig 2A).

Incorporating all psychopathological factors in pre-training improves the downstream representation compared to including only the single best predicted factor (Fig. 2C).

Surprisingly, psy-Aware on ABCD or UKB does not outperform unsupervised SimCLR model in most cases except for ASD, for which psy-Aware is superior when pretrained on UKB (Fig 2B).

Psy-Aware pretrained on UKB is competitive with age-Aware pretrained on the same cohort, even outperforming it in ASD classification (Fig. 2C).

Conclusion

We hypothesize that current psychiatric scales may lack sufficient variance in large datasets for an effective dimensional approach in psychiatry. It highlights the need for a new dataset tailored for psychiatry. Moreover, evaluating model performance on downstream tasks alone is not informative of the features captured by one or the other model. Further research is needed to improve the interpretability of the embeddings space’s structure.
