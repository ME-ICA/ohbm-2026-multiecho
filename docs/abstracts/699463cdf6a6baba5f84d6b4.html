Introduction

Functional connectivity matrices are a central representation in fMRI and EEG, capturing inter-regional or inter-channel coupling and summarizing large-scale network organization. Realistic generative models of these matrices are increasingly valuable, as they enable controlled studies of population variability, characterization of disease-related network disruptions, and data augmentation when working with a labeled fMRI dataset and limited resources [1, 2].

However, conventional generative models operate in unconstrained Euclidean spaces and do not account for the matrix structural properties of functional connectomes (e.g., matrix symmetry and matrix positive definiteness) [3]. As a result, many well-established generative models produce matrices that violate matrix symmetric and positive-definite (SPD) constraints and distort network topology, and even deviate from neurobiological plausible connectivity patterns. This mismatch highlights the need for generative models that explicitly respect the matrix properties of connectivity data. 

In this study, we present a novel Riemannian generative model that guarantees matrix-valued outputs while avoiding the high computational cost typically associated with SPD or correlation matrices for both EEGs and fMRIs and empirically evaluate it across three fMRI datasets and two EEG datasets [4].  

Methods

In the proposed generative model (See Figure 1), we start by computing empirical connectivity matrices from neuroimaging data, such as fMRI correlation matrices or EEG spatial covariance matrices. These matrices naturally lie on curved matrix spaces, rather than in ordinary Euclidean space.

To make learning easier, all matrices are then mapped through a smooth, invertible transformation into a Euclidean space, where they can be treated as standard vectors. In this Euclidean space, the model learns a time-dependent vector field using conditional flow matching [5]. Because all training and sampling takes place in Euclidean coordinates, the procedure remains computationally efficient and fits well within standard deep learning frameworks.

After generation, the samples obtained in Euclidean space are mapped back through the inverse transformation to the original matrix spaces. This final step guarantees that the outputs are valid connectivity matrices: they automatically satisfy the required structural constraints, including symmetry, positive definiteness, and unit diagonal entries for correlation matrices.

In summary, the pipeline first lifts connectivity data into Euclidean space for efficient learning and then returns them to their original geometric domain, producing synthetic brain connectivity matrices that remain consistent with the underlying manifold structure.

Results

Figure 2 presents four groups of results:


(1) Sample comparison: Real fMRI/EEG connectivity matrices alongside generated matrices selected as the closest in Frobenius distance, illustrating that the generated samples closely match the visual texture and structure of the real data.


(2) Quantitative performance: Compare real and generated data across multiple datasets using quality metrics (α-Precision, β-Recall, α,β-F1) and classification metrics (ROC-AUC, F1) and find that models trained on generated samples reach performance comparable to or better than those trained on real data.


(3) EEG topographies: Group-level topography maps based on the first common spatial filter derived from real and generated EEGs show highly similar spatial patterns, indicating that the generated data capture task-relevant scalp distributions.


(4) Disease-conditional fMRI connectivity: Real and generated Alzheimer’s disease connectivity are plotted on their respective Fréchet-mean networks, revealing comparable large-scale network topology and disease-related connectivity alterations.

Conclusion

In this study, we used a fully data-driven deep generative approach to synthesize fMRI and EEG functional connectivity matrices, ensuring that the generated matrices preserved their intrinsic matrix properties. We validated the method on several fMRI and EEG datasets, and the results suggest that the generated connectivity patterns have meaningful neurophysiological relevance. This work has been accepted to NeurIPS this year; for more details, please refer to paper [4].
