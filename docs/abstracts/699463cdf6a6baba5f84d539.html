Introduction

Combined oral contraceptives (OCs) are widely used and have been linked to changes in mood, socio‑affective processing, and large‑scale brain network organization [1,2]. Longitudinal work suggests alterations in resting‑state functional connectivity and effective connectivity (EC) within the Triple Network Model (TNM), comprising the Default Mode Network (DMN), Salience Network (SN) and Central Executive Network (CEN) [1,3]. However, it remains unclear whether OC‑induced changes in EC within and between these core networks are influenced by OC discontinuation and whether OC discontinuation relates to mood symptoms and socio-affective processing, e.g., empathy [1,3,4]. Building on Triple Network Model‑based spectral dynamic causal modelling (spDCM) work across the natural menstrual cycle and OC intake [1,3], the present longitudinal study investigates for the first time how discontinuing long‑term combined OC intake versus continued intake or natural cycling affects EC within the Triple Network Model and whether self‑reported depressive symptoms and empathy are affected by OC discontinuation.

Methods

Seventy-nine healthy women (18–31 years) were assigned to three groups: long-term OC users who discontinued intake (dOC, n=26, age: M=24.12, SD=3.96), ongoing OC users (OC, n=26,  age: M=23.62, SD=3.02), and naturally cycling women without hormonal contraception (fNC, n=27, age: M=23.67, SD=3.36). All women were assessed twice (T1, T2) approximately 4.5 months apart. dOC were scanned during their last OC cycle (T1) and the early follicular phase (T2) after discontinuation; OC during active intake at both sessions; fNC in the early follicular phase at both sessions. Resting-state fMRI and self-reports of depressive symptoms (BDI-II) and empathy (SPF, German Version of the Interpersonal Reactivity Index) were obtained at both time points. MRI data were acquired on a 3T Siemens scanner (T1-weighted structural scan, 8-min resting-state EPI). Preprocessing used fMRIPrep and 6mm smoothing with SPM12. Eleven regions of interest (ROIs) were selected [1] (see Figure 1), time series were estracted from 6-mm spheres. SpDCM [5] was used to estimate EC in a fully connected 11-node model. For each participant, models were inverted using variational Laplace on cross-spectral densities. Second-level effects were examined within a hierarchical parametric empirical Bayes framework to model 1) time-related changes in EC and 2) group differences in these time effects. All models controlled for age, inter-scan interval, and, in dOC and OC, OC duration and androgenicity. Bayesian model reduction and averaging identified the most plausible connectivity patterns. Only parameters with posterior probability ≥0.95 with a connection strength of ≥|.05| Hz were considered.

Results

Only the dOC group showed significant self-report changes, with depressive symptoms decreasing (p=.002) and empathy scores increasing (p=.003) after discontinuation. No significant time effects were found in the OC (BDI-II: p=.074; SPF: p=.709) or fNC group (BDI-II: p=.148; SPF: p=.914). In terms of time effects on EC, dOC showed reduced within-network EC after discontinuation, with the SN containing the highest proportion of connections with time-related effects. Between-networks, time-related effects were mainly seen from SN to DMN and from CEN to DMN and SN after discontinuation. Bilateral anterior insulae (AI) and supramarginal gyri (SMG) emerged as key source hubs with an inverse left-right hemispheric pattern (see Figure 2a). Compared to OC, dOC showed stronger reductions in within‑network EC, mainly in SN, with between-network differences standing out connections from SN to DMN and from CEN to DMN. Again, a persistent inverse left–right hemispheric pattern in bilateral AI and SMG emerged, while no connections targeted the CEN (see Figure 2b). Relative to fNC, dOC exhibited more pronounced time‑related EC increase within‑network EC, again mainly within SN, and stronger between‑network differences in connections originating from CEN (bilateral SMG) towards both SN and DMN regions (see Figure 2c). In addition, all DMN‑ and SN‑originating connections were more enhanced over time in dOC, accompanied by the same inverse left–right hemispheric pattern in SMG.

Conclusion

In terms of self-report, OC discontinuation in the dOC group was accompanied by reduced depressive symptoms and increased empathy. On a neural level, pronounced time‑related changes in EC, including decreased within‑network connectivity and altered SN‑to‑DMN and CEN‑to‑DMN coupling emerged. The role of bilateral AI and SMG as key source hubs aligns with previous findings on the Triple Network Model suggesting salience‑driven reconfiguration of self‑referential and control networks in affective states [1,3]. Differences in EC between dOC and both other groups may reflect large-scale network plasticity after cessation of synthetic hormone exposure. These findings extend prior work [1-4] by showing that OC discontinuation is associated with reshaped directed interactions within the Triple Network Model. Future studies may investigate whether and how changes in EC relate to improvements in mood and socio‑affective functioning, underscoring the relevance of large‑scale network plasticity for understanding OC‑related mental health effects.
