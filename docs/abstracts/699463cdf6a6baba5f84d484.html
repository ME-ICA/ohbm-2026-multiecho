Introduction

Neuroimaging is the methodological core of modern human neuroscience, providing detailed insight into brain structure, function, and physiology across both basic and clinical research. As the field develops, there is an increasing demand for efficient visualization tools that enhance data interpretation, advance biomedical education, and support clinical decision-making. Conventional neuroimaging tools often require high-end hardware, extensive and complex software, and substantial technical proficiency. To address these limitations and broaden access to brain imaging capabilities, web-based technologies offer notable advantages, including interactive graphics, advanced 3D rendering, and ease of use without extensive technical skills. Building on these advantages, we present a web-based, installation-free 3D visualization platform that converts numerical neuroimaging-derived outputs from various software packages and analysis pipelines into an interactive, easy-to-navigate interface.

Methods

To develop and prototype the web-based platform, we analyzed task-based fMRI data collected at three time points during a language task as part of a clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05844605). Preprocessing and statistical analyses were conducted using SPM12. Significant activation clusters were mapped onto predefined regions of interest (ROIs) from the Automated Anatomical Labeling (AAL) atlas. For each ROI, we extracted (i) the number of activated voxels, (ii) activation volume, (iii) the percentage of significantly activated voxels within the ROI, and (iv) the proportion of whole-brain activation localized to the ROI. These metrics were formatted and integrated using JavaScript and the Three.js library (https://threejs.org/) into the web-based visualization environment (https://open-brain-lab.web.app/).

Results

The web-based platform enabled interactive identification and visualization of ROI-level neuroimaging data within a standardized 3D brain atlas. In its current implementation, the system provided (i) an intuitive interface for selecting individual AAL ROIs and visualizing them within an interactive 3D brain (Figure 1A–C), and (ii) dedicated functionality for dynamic exploration of rendered fMRI activation maps and derived ROI-level metrics, facilitating comparison of activation strength, spatial extent, and topographical distribution across distinct neuroimaging time points (Figure 1D–F). Together, these functionalities supported efficient identification and rapid inspection of regional activation patterns and their evolution across fMRI sessions. Initial testing indicated smooth 3D rendering, accurate mapping of statistical outputs, and robust performance in standard web browsers, without the need for specialized hardware or software. 

Conclusion

OpenBrain, developed as a web-based, installation-free, open-source tool, has been designed to enhance the accessibility of brain exploration and to accelerate the interpretation of complex neuroimaging datasets by integrating multiple neural parameters within an interactive 3D environment. Although the current implementation relies on a structural atlas, upcoming versions will allow users to incorporate and extend the tool to additional parcellations. Moreover, future developments will expand support for multimodal imaging (e.g., MRI, fMRI, DTI, PET) and enable visualization of temporal dynamics (e.g., including those induced by neuromodulation protocols). Ongoing development efforts prioritize improving usability, expanding dataset compatibility with distinct neuroimaging tools, and initiating the implementation of basic statistical methods. Altogether, OpenBrain, conceived as an open and collaborative initiative, aims to build a comprehensive web-based platform that advances research, education, and clinical applications in the brain sciences while fostering widespread adoption of neuroimaging capabilities among a broader user community.
