Introduction

Painful temporomandibular disorder (p-TMD) is a prevalent chronic orofacial pain condition associated with substantial functional impairment [1,2]. Neuroimaging studies have identified functional alterations within pain-related brain networks in p-TMD [3]; however, evidence regarding structural brain differences remains limited and inconsistent. Surface-based morphometric approaches allow for the characterization of distinct cortical features, including curvature and volume, which may capture subtle anatomical differences associated with chronic pain. The present study examined cortical morphometric measures within an a priori pain-related cortical mask to identify regionally specific brain differences associated with p-TMD.

Methods

Nineteen individuals with chronic painful TMD and twenty pain-free controls underwent high-resolution T1-weighted MRI. Cortical reconstruction and surface-based morphometric analyses were performed using FreeSurfer. Measures of cortical curvature and gray matter volume were extracted. Analyses were restricted to an a priori pain-related cortical mask derived from a meta-analytic Neurosynth pain map[4]  to focus on regions consistently implicated in pain processing. Group comparisons were conducted using general linear models adjusting for age, sex, and intracranial volume where appropriate. Statistical significance was assessed within the predefined mask.

Results

Relative to controls, individuals with p-TMD demonstrated significantly greater cortical curvature and volume within a cluster located in the left mid-anterior cingulate gyrus/sulcus. No additional cortical regions within the a priori pain mask showed significant group differences in curvature or volume.

Conclusion

This study identifies focal morphometric differences in the left mid-anterior cingulate cortex in individuals with painful temporomandibular disorder. Greater cortical curvature and volume in this region indicate localized anatomical differences associated with chronic orofacial pain, rather than widespread cortical atrophy. These findings support the involvement of the anterior cingulate cortex in p-TMD and underscore the utility of targeted, surface-based morphometric approaches for characterizing regionally specific brain differences in chronic pain conditions [5]. Further research is needed to clarify the clinical relevance and temporal evolution of these structural features.
