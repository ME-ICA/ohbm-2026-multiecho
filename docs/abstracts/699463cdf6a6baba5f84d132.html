Introduction

Deep brain stimulation at subthalamic nucleus (STN-DBS) has emerged as an effective method for modulating aberrant central nervous system circuits and is used for refractory Meige syndrome (MS). However, the efficacy of DBS varies significantly among patients. Current research indicates that the efficacy of DBS treatment may be closely related to the many various factors. Xie et al.,1 found that disease duration, preoperative severity of psychiatric symptoms, and the overlap of activated tissue volume (VTA) with the thalamic sensory-motor region were potential predictors of motor outcomes in the DBS for MS. Moreover, the precise localization of DBS electrodes and the setting of electrical stimulation parameters directly determine VTA, also affect the clinical outcome.2 Besides these clinical information and stimulation parameters, functional and structural network metrics could also be used as features to predict efficacy of STN- DBS treatment. For example, Liu et al. revealed that high and low DBS responders exhibit significant differences in large-scale network architectures, suggesting that network characteristics may also serve as predictors of STN-DBS treatment outcomes in MS.3 Although several studies have analyzed structural and functional brain networks in MS,3 these brain network factors have not yet been systematically investigated as predictors of STN-DBS outcomes in MS. Moreover, studies in MS suggest that gray natter volume (GMV) in specific brain regions may represent potential biomarkers for predicting therapeutic outcomes.

Given the heterogeneity of motor symptoms across MS subtypes, which reflect distinct network patterns, broader brain network analyses across multiple subtypes are essential to understand the disorder’s pathophysiology. This study combined clinical features, stimulation parameters, connection scores, and gray matter volume as features to evaluated efficacy predictors of STN-DBS in the MS and across subtypes.


 

Methods

Patient Cohorts 

A total of 166 MS patients (MS_I: blepharospasm, MS_II: blepharon-oromandibular dystonia, and MS_IV: blepharospasm with oromandibular dystonia spreading to cervical or limb regions) who underwent STN-DBS were included. Baseline assessments, including age, sex, disease duration, Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA), Yale–Brown Obsessive–Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS), Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD), and Burke–Fahn–Marsden Dystonia Rating Scale (BFMDRS) and sub-scores were obtained. The treatment response was assessed using the BFMDRS at baseline, 1, 3, 6, and 12 months following STN-DBS implantation.

Extraction of the brain network scores

All patients received bilateral implantation of electrodes targeting the dorsolateral STN, and stimulation parameters were optimized by DBS neurologists based on symptom response and side effects. Preoperative T1-weighted images (repetition time = 2300 ms; echo time = 2.91 ms; inversion time = 900 ms; flip angle = 9°; matrix size = 256 × 256; and isotropic voxel size = 1 × 1 × 1 mm³) and postoperative CT scans were co-registered and normalized to MNI space using ANTs within Lead-DBS.4 Electrode trajectories and contact locations were reconstructed with PaCER, with manual refinement when necessary. Electric fields and volumes of tissue activated (VTAs) were modeled using a finite element framework with a 0.2 V/mm threshold. Sweetspot mapping was conducted by correlating voxel-wise VTA coverage with clinical improvement to generate group-level sweet spots, along with Sweetspot scores. Structural connectivity estimation was performed using fiber tractography from half of the dTOR-985 connectome. The connectivity between each patient’s VTA and all fibers was computed to build a Fiber T-score model. A two-sample t-test compared improvement between connected and unconnected groups, and the resulting t-values were assigned to the corresponding fibers. Each patient’s summed T-score was then correlated with clinical improvement using Spearman analysis. Voxel-wise functional and structural connectivity analyses were performed between each patient’s VTA and the rest of the brain using the network-mapping framework with half of the HCP1000 and dTOR-985 normative connectomes. Each VTA served as a seed to generate individualized functional connectivity fingerprints, and voxel-wise correlations with clinical improvement produced functional R-maps. Structural connectivity was assessed by identifying fibers intersecting each VTA and quantifying voxel-level connectivity strength based on the number of associated streamlines, followed by Spearman correlations to generate structural R-maps.  Model robustness for both functional and structural connectivity profiles was evaluated using 10-fold cross-validation, and individualized connectivity scores were computed for all patients (Figure 1).

Extraction of gray matter volume 

Based on regions previously reported to show GMV alterations in MS,5 13 ROIs which include parahippocampal gyrus, lingual gyrus, superior frontal gyrus, medial frontal gyrus, middle temporal gyrus, inferior parietal lobule, precuneus, and putamen were selected. GMV was extracted from preprocessed T1 images and incorporated into subsequent analyses.

Statistical analyses 

We included clinical features, stimulation parameters, connectivity metrics, and GMV as predictors. Spearman correlations were calculated between all features and BFMDRS improvement, followed by Holm–Bonferroni correction. Patients were classified into response and super-response groups based on whether their 12-month improvement exceeded 70%, and group differences were analyzed. All variables were entered into univariate Cox models, and those with p < 0.05 were retained for multivariate Cox analysis. Significant predictors were used to construct a nomogram estimating the probability of achieving ≥70% improvement at 6- and 12-month follow-up. Model performance was evaluated using time-dependent ROC curves.

Results

Clinical results

Among the 166 enrolled patients, 107 completed all imaging and clinical procedures and were included in the final analysis (23 MS_I, 63 MS_II, 21 MS_IV). Preoperatively, there were significant differences among the three sub-types in the BFMDRS_Total (p<0.001), BFMDRS_Oromandibular (p<0.001), BFMDRS_Swallowing (p<0.001), BFMDRS_Neck (p<0.001), and BFMDRS_Disability_Score (p<0.022; Figure 2.A). No statistically significant differences (p > 0.05) were observed among the three subgroups in most other clinical characteristics or stimulation parameters, indicating good baseline comparability across the subtypes. After STN-DBS, the total BFMDRS score decreased substantially and stabilized within one year, with a mean improvement of 78.85%. A total of 85 patients (out of 107) achieved a ≥70% improvement in BFMDRS scores, including 18 patients from MS_ I, 51 patients from MS_ II, and 16 patients from MS_ IV.

Results of Spearman correlation analyses

After Holm–Bonferroni correction, no individual feature remained significantly correlated with clinical improvement within subtypes. In the overall cohort, only Fiber-score (adjusted p = 0.002) demonstrated a robust negative correlation with improvement, highlighting its potential role as a structural biomarker. 

Differences of features between responders and super- responders

In the all patients, significant differences were found in HAMA (p = 0.036), BFMDRS_Eye (p = 0.035), L_Voltage (p = 0.033), R_voltage (p = 0.008), L_Widewave (p = 0.023), Fiber_score (p = 0.001), the gray matter volume of right opercular rolandic (Rolandic_Oper_R; p = 0.040), the gray matter volume of left precuneus (Precuneus_L; p = 0.004), and the gray matter volume of right precuneus (Precuneus_R; p = 0.047) . In MS_I, R_VTA (p = 0.045), L_Y_Coordinate (p = 0.013), and Structural network_Score (p = 0.013) showed significant differences. In MS_II, R_voltage (p = 0.034), Fiber_Score (p = 0.019) and Rolandic_Oper_R (p = 0.023) showed significant differences. In MS_IV, HAMA (p = 0.015), BFMDRS_Disability_Score (p = 0. 028), and L_Widewave (p = 0.014) showed significant differences.

Results of univariate and multivariate Cox analyses

In the overall MS cohort, the multivariate model identified HAMA, Fiber_score, and GMV of the left lingual gyrus as independent predictors. Higher HAMD tended to indicate greater improvement potential, whereas higher Fiber_score and reduced lingual GMV were linked to poorer outcomes. The nomogram based on these predictors showed moderate discrimination (AUC:0.729-0.758). Subtype analyses revealed distinct predictive patterns. In MS_I, HAMA, BFMDRS_Eye, and the structural network score emerged as strong independent predictors, and the nomogram demonstrated excellent performance (AUC:0.929-0.944). In MS_II, Fiber_score was the key predictor retained in the multivariate model, yielding modest predictive accuracy (AUC:0.676-0.747). In MS_IV, only HAMD remained significant in the multivariate analysis, and the corresponding model also showed good discrimination (AUC:0.787-0.800). Together, these findings highlight both shared and subtype-specific predictors of STN-DBS outcomes, emphasizing the importance of clinical, structural, and network-based features for individualized prognostic estimation across MS subtypes (Figure 2).

Conclusion

In this study, 107 MS patients who completed all clinical and imaging assessments demonstrated substantial and sustained improvement following STN-DBS, with an average symptom reduction of 78.85% and nearly 80% achieving ≥70% improvement at one year. These findings reaffirm the robust therapeutic efficacy of STN-DBS for Meige syndrome and are highly consistent with previous reports.

Beyond the overall clinical benefit, several key predictors of treatment response were identified, including structural connectivity (Fiber_score), emotional status, and gray matter volume (GMV) in specific brain regions. Structural connectivity, a fundamental determinant of DBS network modulation, emerged as a significant predictor: lower Fiber_scores, reflecting weaker connectivity to cortical motor areas, was generally associated with better therapeutic outcomes, possibly due to a greater capacity for circuit reorganization. Emotional status, particularly anxiety and depression, also appeared as independent predictors in the multivariate Cox analysis, underscoring the contribution of non-motor symptoms to DBS responsiveness. Higher HAMA and HAMD scores were linked to poorer postoperative improvement, suggesting that affective disturbances may interfere with sensorimotor integration or related neural pathways, thereby diminishing treatment efficacy. In addition, gray matter volume in regions such as the lingual gyrus and the rolandic operculum served as negative prognostic markers. Enlargement in these areas, which are involved in visual processing, motor intention, and cognitive control, may reflect structural alterations that reduce the efficiency of DBS-mediated network modulation. Subtype analyses revealed distinct predictor patterns across MS_I, MS_II, and MS_IV, indicating that the heterogeneity of clinical presentations corresponds to differences in underlying network involvement and pathophysiology.

Overall, our findings highlight structural connectivity, emotional state, and regional GMV as critical predictors of STN-DBS efficacy in MS. Incorporating these biomarkers into preoperative evaluation may enhance patient selection, support individualized stimulation strategies, and advance a network-based framework for DBS therapy. Future multicenter studies with larger, more balanced subtype cohorts and longer follow-up periods will be essential to validate and refine these predictive models.

Additionally, this study has limitations. First, all data were derived from a single center, ensuring consistency in imaging, surgery, and follow-up but limiting generalizability. Inter-center differences may introduce bias and complicate data handling; future multicenter cohorts with standardized protocols and cross-validation are needed. Second, subtype distribution was imbalanced, with MS_I and MS_IV underrepresented compared with MS_II. This imbalance, together with missing data, may have weakened subtype-specific associations and statistical power; future work should enhance representation. Third, follow-up was limited to one year. As STN-DBS efficacy changes with long-term stimulation, neural plasticity, and adherence, short follow-up may miss long-term effects. Extended follow-up and time-dependent models are needed.
