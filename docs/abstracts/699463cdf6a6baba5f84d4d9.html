Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and early-onset psychosis (EOP) are neurodevelopmental conditions that share substantial genotypic and phenotypic overlap, including difficulties in social communication and cognition.1 Despite their distinct clinical presentations, both conditions are characterized by impairments in social cognition - the mental processes underlying perception and interpretation of others’ intentions and emotions. Resting-state fMRI studies implicate the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), a “social brain” hub involved in emotion regulation and perspective-taking, as a shared locus of dysfunction in ASD and EOP.2 Both disorders are also linked to an excitatory-inhibitory (E/I) imbalance, involving altered glutamate (Glu) and gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) signaling.3 However, the relationship between E/I neurometabolic concentrations and ACC resting-state functional connectivity magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fcMRI) has not been well studied in ASD, especially in comparison to other neurodevelopmental disorders with overlapping social deficits, such as EOP. To address this gap, the current study used a multimodal approach, integrating rs-fcMRI and magnetic-resonance spectroscopy (MRS) to examine ACC connectivity and neurometabolite concentrations, and their cross-modal associations in adolescents with ASD, EOP, and typically developing (TD) controls. 

Methods

Seventy-five adolescents and young adults aged 12-21 years – 24 ASD (Mage=15.89, 13% female), 25 EOP (Mage=16.42, 52% female), and 26 TD (Mage=16.86, 58% female) completed structural MRI, rs-fcMRI, and single-voxel MRS targeting the dorsal ACC (dACC). High-resolution T1-weighted MPRAGE images (TR=2400ms, TE=2.22ms, voxel size=0.8mm3, 208 slices) and rs-fcMRI (TR=720ms, TE=37ms, voxel size=2.0mm3) were preprocessed using FMRIB’s Software Library (FSL) and Analysis of Functional NeuroImages (AFNI), including skull stripping, motion correction, 6mm FWHM smoothing, ICA-AROMA denoising, bandpass filtering (0.01-0.1 Hz), and regression of white matter (WM), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), and derivatives. ACC connectivity was examined using a 5-mm seed (MNI coordinates: -6, 18, 30).4 The concentration of GABA was measured using MEGA-PRESS (TR=2000ms, TE=68ms, 30x38x10mm dACC voxel), and Glu and glutamine (Gln) were quantified using a short-TE (20ms) STEAM-MRSI (TR=2000ms, 16x16x8 matrix). MEGA-PRESS data were processed using Osprey for linear-combination modeling, voxel coregistration, and tissue segmentation (SPM12). STEAM-MRSI data were analyzed using LCModel with standard quality thresholds (linewidth<15 Hz, SNR>8, CRLB<20%). Tissue fractions (gray matter (GM), WM, CSF) were applied to derive creatine-referenced (Cr), tissue-corrected metabolite values (GABA, Glu, and Gln), and estimated using Visual Display Interface (VDI) regression to estimate GM-specific concentrations. A one-way analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed to examine group differences in GABA and Glu concentrations across ASD, EOP, and TD groups, controlling for age, gender, and IQ. Additionally, Pearson’s correlations were performed to examine the relationship between parameter estimates extracted from rs-fcMRI in the ACC and GABA and Glu concentrations in ASD, EOP, or TD youth. 

Results

Results revealed that ASD youth exhibited ACC underconnectivity with the bilateral superior frontal gyrus, left precuneus, and left middle frontal gyrus, compared to TD controls (p<.001). EOP youth exhibited ACC underconnectivity with the bilateral precuneus, right precentral gyrus, and right calcarine gyrus, compared to TD controls (p<.001). Additionally, ASD youth exhibited ACC underconnectivity with the left middle frontal gyrus and left angular gyrus, but ACC overconnectivity with the right supramarginal gyrus, compared to EOP youth (p<.001). MRS findings revealed that EOP youth exhibited significantly lower GM-corrected Gln levels in the ACC compared to both ASD and TD youth (p<.05), whereas no group differences were observed for GABA or Glu concentrations. Pearson’s correlations further revealed that higher ACC Glu+Gln/Cr ratios were significantly correlated with ACC underconnectivity with the bilateral superior frontal gyrus, left precuneus, and left middle frontal gyrus in ASD (r=.45, p=.04). In contrast, there were no significant metabolite-connectivity associations found in EOP or TD youth.

Conclusion

Collectively, these findings identify diagnosis-specific patterns of ACC dysconnectivity and neurometabolic imbalance during adolescence. In ASD, fronto-parietal underconnectivity was associated with greater excitatory metabolite levels, suggesting E/I imbalance underlying social-affective network disruption. This pattern aligns with the E/I imbalance theory, which suggests that excessive glutamatergic activity may interfere with neural communication with other regions supporting attention, mentalizing, and self-referential processes.5 In contrast, although youth with EOP also exhibited ACC underconnectivity, this pattern was not associated with either excitatory or inhibitory metabolite levels, suggesting that functional network and neurochemical alterations may not be working in tandem in this group. Furthermore, these results provide insight into how disruptions in excitatory neurotransmission may shape functional network organization in ASD and EOP and contribute to social cognition.
