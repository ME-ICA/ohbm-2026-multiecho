Introduction

Kiwifruit contains high levels of micronutrients, in particular Vitamin C1 and polyphenols2. The RubyRed variety has higher phenolic and Vitamin C contents, compared with Green and SunGold kiwifruit. Studies have shown that consumption of kiwifruit is associated with improved mood and increased vitamin C plasma levels in the blood3, while polyphenol consumption has been associated with reduced incidence of neurodegeneration and cognitive decline5. Here, we assess the brain’s response to a N-back working memory task following the acute consumption of kiwifruit varieties. 

Methods

Data Acquisition: Fourteen healthy volunteers (7 Female, Age (Mean:28y(stdev:7y)) took part in a familiarisation and sensory evaluation session outside the MR scanner, followed by four MRI visits (3T Philips Achieva). 24 hours prior to each MR imaging visit, volunteers were instructed to refrain from foods that contained vitamin C and polyphenols. Volunteers consumed two kiwifruits - either Green, “SunGold” (Gold) and “RubyRed” (Red) - or a maltodextrin control drink prepared with the same calorific content as the kiwifruit. At 30-minutes after consumption an N-back task fmri scan was acquired (GRE-EPI, Multiband 3, SENSE 2, 3x3x3mm voxels, TR2s, Double-echo (TE1=20ms, TE2=45ms)). Data were preprocessed including NORDIC denoising, RETROICOR, and TOPUP distortion correction (FSL). 

N-back Task: Each participant was instructed to press a button when they saw a letter that was repeated with a gap of 1 letter (1-back), or 2 letters (2-back) between them. For the “0-back” task, the participant was instructed to press the button when they saw the letter “x”. During a task period, 15 letters, 4 of which were targets, were presented for 1 s each in a pseudorandom order, with 1 s of blank screen between sequential letters. Each task period was followed by a 15 s rest period, during which a fixation dot was shown on the screen. The instruction screen, task period, and rest period formed an experimental block lasting 47 s, with 4 blocks per condition collected.

Analysis: Data were analysed using SPM12 with standard motion correction, spatial and temporal filtering, and a GLM to derive t-stat maps from which at a 2nd-level to generate group (across subjects) maps of the effect of the task (0-back, 1-back, 2-back) and the comparison between tasks (e.g. 2-back vs. 0-back for the most versus least difficult task) following acute consumption of each of the kiwifruit. The Harvard Oxford brain atlas was used to parcellate the functional brain responses.

Results

There were significant fMRI responses when performing the N-back task in visual and working memory areas. Specifically, for the 2-back task: Occipital Pole, Lateral Occipital Cortex, Superior Parietal Lobule, Precentral Gyrus, Middle Frontal Gyrus, SMA (Fig1). Following maltodextrin consumption there was a larger brain response at all task difficulty of the N-back compared to the kiwifruit. Kiwifruit (especially the red) displayed a more graded response to the N-back task in areas involved in working memory: Middle & Superior Frontal Gyrus, Frontal Pole, Paracingulate Gyrus, Superior Parietal Lobule, Angular Gyrus, SMA (Fig2). There were no behavioural differences in task accuracy or reaction time between conditions.

Conclusion

The N-back task led to more activation across all conditions of the task when consuming Maltodextrin as compared to kiwifruit. Comparing of the 2-back vs 0-back contrast showed the RubyRed kiwifruit had more graded activation when compared to all other conditions in regions related to working memory, object recognition, motor planning, and visual encoding and the insula, which has been implicated in working memory tasks, as a processor of emotional information that aids conflict resolution. This suggest that the nutrients within kiwifruit enhanced the response to a working memory task as shown for other foods5.
