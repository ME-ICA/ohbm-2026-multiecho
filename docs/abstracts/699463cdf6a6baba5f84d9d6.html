Introduction

Vision-language models (VLMs) trained on medical images and text provide a unified framework for cross-modal reasoning and have achieved state-of-the-art performance in medical visual question answering (VQA), captioning, classification, and retrieval. However, uncertainty calibration (i.e., alignment between predictive confidence and correctness), remains understudied, particularly for decoder-only generative architectures that produce free-form text. In clinical and medical research settings such as diagnostic radiology, mis-calibrated overconfidence can be consequential because confident but incorrect predictions may contribute to misleading diagnoses. While post-hoc calibration methods such as temperature scaling and conformal prediction have improved reliability in discriminative models (CNNs, ViTs), they have not been widely extended to generative VLMs, whose outputs are token sequences (next-token conditional probabilities) rather than explicit class-probabilities.

We propose CALM-VLM, which integrates confidence calibration and selective prediction into a generative 3D VLM for brain MRI diagnosis. We focus on Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and stroke, two common brain conditions where calibrated confidence is critical for AI-assisted radiologic decision support. Future work will test this approach for broader diagnostic coverage that is needed for real-world deployment.

Methods

For AD classification, we used ADNI and OASIS3 as our main datasets from 62 sites and one site respectively. Dementia diagnoses in ADNI and OASIS3 were treated as AD under the clinical definition (rather than biological AD defined by amyloid positivity, Aβ+).1 ADNI was split into 2,577/302/1,219 T1-weighted (T1-w) scans for train/validation/test, with subjects confined to a single fold to prevent leakage in longitudinal data; 600 OASIS scans were used for zero-shot evaluation. For stroke classification, we used ENIGMA Stroke Recovery data2 spanning 10 countries (one of the largest multisite retrospective stroke datasets), with 1,077/120/300 T1-w scans for train/validation/test and 83 held-out scans from a single site for zero-shot testing.

All scans were preprocessed using standard neuroimaging steps3: skull stripping, N4 bias correction, 9-DOF linear registration to a template, 2-mm isotropic resampling, and z-transformation. Volumes were resized to 128×256×256 voxels to match Med3D-VLM4 defaults.

As vision-only baselines, we trained 3D DenseNet121 and DCFormer (a 3D convolutional transformer-hybrid). We fine-tuned Med3D-VLM,4 pairing a 3D DCFormer encoder with Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct following the LLaVA paradigm, using an MLP-mixer projector and LoRA fine-tuning.

Training used a VQA format: inputs included: (1) a T1-weighted volumetric image, (2) a prompt such as “What is the diagnosis for this {age}-year-old {sex} based on the MRI scan?” with templated (synthetically varied) variants for augmentation, and (3) a single-token label (“AD”, “stroke”, “control”). At inference, we mapped token-level outputs to class probabilities by extracting logits for the first generated token and aggregating over vocabulary tokens corresponding to each label, enabling discriminative calibration metrics (ECE, Brier score). We applied post-hoc temperature scaling to decoder logits (single scalar T>0 optimized by validation NLL),5 and evaluated selective prediction by abstaining when confidence < τ for τ∈[0.5,0.95], reporting accuracy–confidence reliability curves and identifying thresholds that achieve target accuracy at fixed coverage. Performance was assessed with receiver-operator characteristic curve-area under the curve (ROC-AUC) and accuracy, and we also used standard NLP metrics - bilingual evaluation understudy (BLEU), Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation - Longest Common Subsequence (ROUGE-L), and BERTScore - to quantify overlap/semantic similarity between generated outputs and ground truth.

Results

Compared with vision-only models, CALM-VLM improved classification performance, achieving ~5% (AD) and ~10% (stroke) higher ROC-AUC. The VLM also showed stronger zero-shot generalization to out-of-distribution sites (~5% ROC-AUC boost for AD and ~13% for stroke). Calibration improved the alignment between confidence and correctness: calibrated CALM-VLM achieved ROC-AUC=0.951 for AD at coverage=0.88, indicating reliable automated diagnosis for >88% of unseen test subjects while deferring remaining cases for expert review. The VLM also leveraged patient context (age/sex) as relevant confounders for AD, information unavailable to vision-only baselines.

Conclusion

CALM-VLM is a calibrated, uncertainty-aware generative 3D VLM for reliable brain MRI diagnosis. By combining temperature scaling with confidence-based selective prediction, CALM-VLM improves both confidence calibration and diagnostic performance relative to vision-only baselines. The fact that joint text-image embedding improves disease classification, relative to vision-only classifiers, may be because the embedding generates a low-rank subspace that makes classifiers more sample-efficient to train. This work advances trustworthy foundation models for neuroimaging that can answer diagnostic questions and abstain when they should. Future work will extend evaluation to a broader range of conditions to test generalizability of the CALM-VLM framework
