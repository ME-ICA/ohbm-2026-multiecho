Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a common sleep-breathing disorder characterized by repeated episodes of upper airway obstruction during sleep, resulting in intermittent oxygen desaturation and sleep fragmentation. Oxygen desaturation in OSA contributes directly to cognitive problems, with greater severity in desaturation linked to poorer verbal memory performance.^1 Additionally, sleep disruptions in OSA manifest as significant changes in sleep architecture, particularly reductions in slow-wave sleep (SWS) duration and delta-band EEG power during non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, changes that also occur during normal aging. In healthy adults, normal aging is characterized by a progressive decline in SWS and frontal delta activity, which represent key stages for memory consolidation during sleep.

However, the differences in EEG-based sleep aging patterns between healthy adults and OSA patients, as well as the association with the memory of OSA patients, remain unclear. Therefore, this study hypothesized that OSA patients exhibit abnormal EEG-based sleep aging patterns, which are associated with poorer memory performance. The study aims were: (1) to establish EEG-based sleep aging patterns in healthy adults and OSA patients; and (2) to explore the associations between EEG-based sleep aging patterns and memory performance.

Methods

This study included 1,104 OSA patients (mean age 51.58 ± 12.2 years, 34.5% female) and 720 healthy adults (mean age 61.17 ± 18.06 years, 49.6% female).We analyzed overnight polysomnography data obtained from the National Sleep Research Resource database^2, which included recordings from both healthy adults and patients with OSA. Data preprocessing was performed using MATLAB R2024b and the EEGLAB toolbox. Gaussian process regression was applied to construct EEG-based sleep aging trajectories, including sleep macroarchitecture, slow-wave sleep features, and delta-band power for both groups. Differences between groups were evaluated using Bayesian confidence interval analysis and permutation testing.

We examined associations between Buschke Selective Reminding Test (BSRT) scores, a measure of verbal learning and memory that distinguishes between storage and retrieval deficits, and EEG-based sleep aging trajectories in OSA patients using multiple linear regression, with covariate adjustment for age, sex, race, body mass index (BMI), and education level. Subsequently, stratified analyses were conducted by OSA severity and blood oxygen–related indicators, including oxygen desaturation index (ODI), time below 85% SpO₂ (T85), time below 90% SpO₂ (T90), mean oxygen saturation (SpO₂), and hypoxic burden.

Results

 Significant between-group differences were observed in age, sex, BMI, and race, with OSA patients being older and having higher BMI. Analysis of EEG-based sleep aging trajectories revealed significant differences in all sleep macroarchitecture parameters between groups (p ≤ 0.004). Most notably, OSA patients exhibited markedly shorter total sleep time (TST) (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = -0.432) and substantially reduced stage 3 sleep (N3) compared with healthy adults (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = -1.778), with compensatory increases in stage 1 sleep (p < 0.001, Cohen's d = 1.181). Bayesian confidence interval analysis demonstrated that group differences in total sleep time were consistent across the entire age spectrum, whereas other sleep parameters showed no age-specific divergence.

After adjusting for age, sex, BMI, race, and education, significant associations were observed between EEG-based sleep aging trajectories and memory performance measured by the BSRT in OSA patients. TST was positively associated with Consistent Long Term Retrieval (β = 0.011, p = 0.037, R² = 0.166), while wake after sleep onset (WASO) was negatively associated with Sum Recall (β = -0.012, p = 0.021, R² = 0.179). Stratified analyses by blood oxygen–related indicators revealed differential associations, with effects persisting after controlling for T90, T85, hypoxic burden, and mean SpO₂ (β range: 0.010–0.013, all p < 0.05). When stratified by AHI severity, associations varied substantially: mild OSA showed REM sleep associations with Long Term Storage (β = 0.341, p = 0.04), moderate OSA showed WASO associations with Consistent Long Term Retrieval (β = 0.017, p = 0.007), and severe OSA showed TST associations with Long Term Retrieval (β = 0.015, p = 0.013).

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that OSA patients exhibit distinct EEG-based sleep aging patterns compared with healthy adults, characterized by profound reductions in TST and N3 sleep that remain consistent across the lifespan. These sleep architectural disruptions, along with oxygen desaturation severity, are independently associated with poorer memory performance. These findings suggest that specific sleep architecture parameters may serve as biomarkers for cognitive risk stratification in OSA. Targeted interventions addressing sleep continuity and deep sleep restoration may offer therapeutic avenues for preserving memory function in this patient population.
