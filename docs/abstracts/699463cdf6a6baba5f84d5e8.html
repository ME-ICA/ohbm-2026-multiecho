Introduction

Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a major modifiable risk factor for cerebral small vessel disease and cognitive decline. Regional gray matter volume (GMV) loss is consistently reported. Cross-sectional meta-analyses have demonstrated GMV differences between T2DM patients and healthy controls (HC), with six cortical regions being reported to decline in at least two studies. Four regions showed consistent T2DM vs HC GMV reductions in the same hemisphere, while the superior temporal gyrus (STG) and middle temporal gyrus (MTG) exhibited inconsistent hemispheric involvement. Hemispheric asymmetry represents a fundamental organizational principle of the human brain. Disruptions of asymmetry have been linked to cognitive decline and neurodegenerative diseases, establishing it as a biomarker potentially more sensitive than volume alone. However, there are gaps in prior investigations that we aimed to address in this study. First, cross-sectional studies cannot separate pre-existing asymmetry from T2DM-related longitudinal GMV change, leaving unclear whether differences reflect progression or baseline variation. Second, Longitudinal studies rarely include HC when comparing left–right atrophy rates in T2DM thus unable to attribute observed changes in asymmetry to T2DM or to normal aging. Third, because meta-analyses are largely based on cross-sectional contrasts, baseline GMV differences cannot be separated from pre-study atrophy. Moreover, the effect of glycemic control, measured by glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), an indicator of average 3‑month blood glucose levels, on longitudinal GMV change remains underexplored. We analyzed 24-month longitudinal MRI data from the Diabetes & Dementia (D2) and Cognition And Neocortical Volume After Stroke (CANVAS) studies. The primary aim was to assess longitudinal asymmetric atrophy in the six meta-identified regions, including group x time interaction and the effect of glycaemic control, as measured by HbA1c. 

Methods

Study Cohorts

We included 150 participants from the Diabetes & Dementia (D2) Study, a longitudinal cohort of community-dwelling individuals with T2DM who met the following inclusion criteria: WHO 2019 diagnostic criteria for T2DM, age 50–75 years, and absence of neurological comorbidities, and availability of baseline MRI. Among these, 27 participants lacked 24-month follow-up MRI data; these missing data were accommodated using linear mixed models (LMM) to preserve statistical power. The control group was derived from 40 healthy control participants in the Cognition And Neocortical Volume After Stroke (CANVAS) Study, a multi-centre cohort comparing adult ischaemic stroke participants with age- and sex-matched community controls. After excluding 6 participants (n = 4 with T2DM at baseline; n = 2 with incomplete MRI data), 34 healthy controls remained who met all inclusion criteria: age 50–75 years, no T2DM or neurological disease, and complete baseline and 24-month MRI data.

2. MRI Acquisition and processing

Imaging Protocol

All scans used 3T Siemens Prisma scanners at baseline and 24 months. T1-weighted images used a 3D MPRAGE sequence: 1 mm³ isotropic resolution, TR=2300 ms, TE=2.98 ms, flip angle=9°, FOV=256×256 mm. 

Gray Matter Volume (GMV) Extraction

GMV was extracted via FreeSurfer v7.2 longitudinal pipeline using the Desikan-Killiany atlas, targeting six bilateral regions: STG, MTG, Inferior Temporal Gyrus (ITG), Postcentral Gyrus (PoCG), Precuneus (PCun), insula.

3. Derived metrics

  • Asymmetry Index (AI): We adopted the ENIGMA consortium convention (positive = left > right, larger absolute value = greater asymmetry) AI = (Left GMV − Right GMV) / [(Left GMV + Right GMV)/2].
  • Atrophy Rate (%): Quantifies 24-month GMV change (positive = decrease), formula: [(Baseline GMV − 24-Month GMV)/Baseline GMV] × 100.

4. Statistical Analysis

Analyses used SPSS v28.0.  LMMs employed Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) for missing data, with random Participant ID intercept. Pre-fitting assumptions (residual normality and homoscedasticity) were verified no violations, ensuring robustness.

4.1 Primary LMM (Full Cohort: T2DM + HC)

Dependent variable: regional GMV. Fixed effects included Group, Time, and Hemisphere, together with their interaction terms (including Group×Time×Hemisphere), and covariates comprising total intracranial volume (TIV), age, gender, and diabetes duration (coded as 0 for HC) to account for established confounders of GMV and group differences in age and TIV. F statistics tested the Time×Hemisphere interaction as the primary aim and the Group×Time×Hemisphere interaction as the secondary aim. Multiple testing was controlled using Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction across 10 fixed effects per region to ensure robust inference.

4.2 Exploratory LMM (T2DM-Only)

Dependent variable: regional GMV. Fixed effects included Time, Hemisphere, and HbA1c, along with their interactions (Time×Hemisphere×HbA1c), and the same covariates as described above. F statistics were used to test HbA1c-related effects. Multiple comparisons were controlled using FDR correction (12 fixed effects per region) to ensure robust inference.

Missing Data & Effect Sizes

Missing data were random, as indicated by t-tests and χ² tests comparing participants with and without 24-month MRI data in the T2DM group (all p > 0.05), supporting the validity of LMM handling of missingness. Effect sizes were quantified using partial η² and interpreted according to neuroimaging conventions: negligible (≤ 0.001), small (0.002–0.019), medium (0.020–0.059), and large (≥ 0.060).

Results

Healthy controls were slightly older than T2DM participants (68 vs 65 years, p=0.03) and had larger TIV (p=0.04), despite the age difference. The two groups were matched for sex distribution (p=0.87). T2DM participants had a mean diabetes duration of 16 years and mean HbA1c of 8.2%.

We examine T2DM-related accelerated brain atrophy, and found a predominantly left-hemispheric pattern emerged in T2DM compared to controls. Near-significant group differences were observed in the left PoCG (T2DM 1.36% ± 2.66% vs. HC 0.36% ± 2.50%, raw P=0.049, FDR P=0.095) and left PCun (T2DM 1.87% ± 2.31% vs. HC 1.00% ± 1.73%, raw P=0.030, FDR P=0.082). Similar but weaker trends were present in the left STG (raw P=0.093) and left MTG (raw P=0.063), where atrophy rates in T2DM were 1.7 to 2.2 times higher than in HC. No significant group differences were detected in the right hemisphere (all FDR P>0.146). None of the covariates showed significant effects (all P>0.05).

Both groups showed significant GMV reduction over time, but the spatial patterns differed. In HC, significant atrophy was observed in most regions after FDR correction, whereas the left PoCG and left insula remained preserved (FDR P>0.277). In contrast, people with T2DM exhibited widespread and more pronounced atrophy. All selected regions showed highly significant GMV loss (FDR P<0.001), with mean reductions 1.5 to 2.2 times greater than those in HC. Importantly, the two regions preserved in HC showed robust atrophy in T2DM (both P<0.001).

Symmetry: There were no significant changes in symmetry patterns. The insula showed the most notable numerical patterns. It exhibited the largest baseline AI difference between groups (HC 0.038 ± 0.050 vs. T2DM 0.022 ± 0.059, FDR P=0.278) and a marginal trend toward reduced AI in T2DM at 24 months (adjusted P=0.081, FDR P=0.162). Intragroup paired t-tests indicated that asymmetry remained largely stable over time (all FDR P>0.122). HC showed a marginal increase in insular asymmetry (raw P=0.061), whereas T2DM showed minimal AI change across regions (all raw P≥0.187). Together, these results indicate that T2DM is associated with accelerated GMV loss while preserving long-term hemispheric asymmetry.

Associations with glycaemic control: HbA1c. No significant Group by Time by Hemisphere interactions were detected in any region (all FDR P>0.8), indicating the absence of hemisphere-specific progression in T2DM-related atrophy. The PCun showed a significant main effect of Group (raw P<0.001, FDR P<0.001), reflecting lower baseline GMV in T2DM. All regions demonstrated significant main effects of Hemisphere (all raw P<0.001), consistent with known brain asymmetry. Covariate effects were in the expected directions.

Finally, to explore metabolic modulation of GMV change within T2DM, a T2DM-only linear mixed model (n=123) was used to examine the effects of HbA1c, adjusting for gender, diabetes duration, baseline age, and TIV. No significant HbA1c-related effects were observed across most regions. The MTG showed marginal associations, including a main effect of HbA1c (raw P=0.052) and a Time by HbA1c interaction (raw P=0.051), neither of which survived FDR correction (both FDR P=0.119). TIV, age, and Hemisphere remained significant covariates (all raw P<0.001).

Conclusion

Three principal findings emerged. T2DM was associated with accelerated GMV loss that was 1.5–2.2 times greater than in healthy controls, predominantly affecting left-hemisphere regions, while intrinsic hemispheric asymmetry remained preserved. Notably, T2DM-related atrophy extended beyond regions typically vulnerable to aging to include regions that were relatively spared in healthy aging, such as the left postcentral gyrus and left insula, indicating disease-specific susceptibility. Linear mixed-model analyses identified the precuneus as the only region exhibiting significantly lower baseline GMV in T2DM, independent of subsequent atrophy. Given the central role of the precuneus within the default mode network and its involvement in memory and self-referential processing, this finding suggests that precuneus structural alteration may represent an early neuroanatomical footprint of T2DM-related brain involvement, preceding overt functional decline. Although HbA1c showed no significant global association with GMV change, marginal trends in the middle temporal gyrus suggest region-specific metabolic sensitivity. While these effects did not survive correction for multiple comparisons, they raise the possibility that long-term glycemic control may modulate structural trajectories in select cortical regions.

Several limitations warrant consideration. The 24-month follow-up may be insufficient to capture longer-term structural trajectories, particularly for regions showing marginal metabolic effects. The focus on six predefined regions limits inferences regarding whole-brain vulnerability patterns. In addition, the modest size of the healthy control group may reduce sensitivity to subtle intergroup differences in asymmetry, and the absence of cognitive data precludes direct structure–function mapping.

Future studies incorporating longer follow-up, larger control samples, whole-brain analyses, and integrated cognitive assessments will be critical to determine the temporal and functional significance of the identified structural vulnerabilities. Validation of region-specific metabolic modulation in larger T2DM cohorts will further clarify whether glycemic control influences cortical integrity over time.

In summary, this study demonstrates that T2DM selectively accelerates gray matter loss, including in regions relatively preserved during normal aging, while leaving intrinsic hemispheric organization intact. The identification of the precuneus as a baseline-affected region and the middle temporal gyrus as a potential site of metabolic sensitivity provides new insight into the neuroanatomical specificity of T2DM and establishes a framework for future investigations into targeted neuroprotective strategies. This 24-month longitudinal study disentangled baseline structural differences from progressive brain atrophy in T2DM by combining complementary statistical approaches to examine gray matter volume and hemispheric asymmetry. By explicitly separating inherent neuroanatomical variation from longitudinal change, this design provides a clearer characterization of T2DM-related brain alterations than cross-sectional analyses.
