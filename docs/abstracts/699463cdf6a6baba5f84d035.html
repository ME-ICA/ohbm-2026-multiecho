Introduction

Physio-cognitive decline syndrome (PCDS), characterized by concurrent physical and cognitive impairments, significantly increases the risk of disability, dementia, and mortality in older adults.1 Elucidating its neurobiological mechanisms is essential for developing targeted interventions. The cerebellum plays a critical role in PCDS pathophysiology, contributing not only to motor coordination but also to cognitive operations through extensive cerebello-cerebral connectivity.1 

Previous neuroimaging studies have demonstrated that cerebellar gray matter atrophy and associated structural covariance networks (SCNs) alterations predict PCDS conversion.2 However, these investigations predominantly employed whole-brain or cerebral-centered approaches, potentially overlooking intrinsic cerebellar network organization. Given that functionally distinct cerebellar subregions form coherent structural networks reflecting shared pathological vulnerability, a cerebellum-centered approach may better delineate specific networks underlying PCDS. Furthermore, the cerebral pathways through which cerebellar network disruption influences functional decline remain uncharacterized.

In this study, we aimed to identify intrinsic cerebellar SCNs associated with PCDS using non-negative matrix factorization.3 We further characterized cerebral regions mediating these associations through voxel-wise mediation analyses, elucidating cerebello-cerebral interactions underlying healthy aging.

Methods

The analytical pipeline is illustrated in Figure 1. This study enrolled 1,193 community-dwelling participants aged ≥50 years (mean age 62.8±8.7 years, 47.4% male) from the I-Lan Longitudinal Aging Study. Physical function was assessed using handgrip strength and 6-meter walk test, while cognitive function was evaluated using multiple neuropsychological tests across memory, language, visuospatial, and executive domains. Composite scores for both domains were derived by averaging z-transformed scores from constituent measures.

T1-weighted images were acquired on a 3T Siemens scanner and preprocessed using voxel-based morphometry with DARTEL normalization in SPM12, yielding modulated gray matter volume (GMV) maps. Cerebellar SCNs were identified using non-negative matrix factorization (SOPNMF toolkit, https://anbai106.github.io/SOPNMF/) to decompose cerebellar GMV into seven network components and participant-specific loading scores. Split-half validation confirmed high network reproducibility (Dice coefficients: 0.56-0.98). Networks were spatially characterized using established cerebellar anatomical and functional parcellations.4,5

Associations between cerebellar network integrity and functional performance were examined using partial Spearman correlations, adjusting for age, sex, education, and total intracranial volume. Bonferroni correction across seven networks established significance at p<0.007. For networks demonstrating significant associations with both physical and cognitive domains, voxel-wise mediation analyses employed bias-corrected bootstrap resampling. The mediation framework treated cerebellar network loading scores as the independent variable, functional composite scores as the dependent variable, and whole-brain cerebral GMV as the mediator, with significance at FDR-corrected p<0.05. Conjunction analysis identified cerebral regions serving as common mediators for both domains.

Results

Seven distinct cerebellar structural covariance networks (SCNs) were identified through non-negative matrix factorization (Figure 2a). Among these, SCN-2 exhibited predominantly posterior cerebellar localization, primarily in Lobules V-VI. Functional characterization revealed a hybrid profile, overlapping with both the Ventral Attention Network and Somatomotor Network.

Among the seven networks, SCN-2 uniquely demonstrated significant associations with both physical (ρ=0.083) and cognitive (ρ=0.154) performance (Figure 2b), while other networks showed domain-specific patterns. SCN-1 and SCN-5 were associated with cognitive function only, whereas SCN-6 was associated with physical function only.

Voxel-wise mediation analyses were performed separately for physical and cognitive performance to identify cerebral regions mediating SCN-2 associations. Conjunction analysis revealed six cerebral regions demonstrating common mediation effects for both domains (Figure 2c), including bilateral thalamus, bilateral hippocampus, left frontal orbital cortex, right occipital fusiform gyrus, and right temporal pole.

Conclusion

This study identified SCN-2, a cerebellar structural network predominantly localized to posterior cerebellum, as a unique neural substrate simultaneously associated with both physical and cognitive performance in middle-aged and older adults. Its hybrid functional profile spanning both somatomotor and attention systems provides a network-level explanation for the coupled nature of PCDS. Mediation analyses revealed that six cerebral regions, including bilateral thalamus, hippocampus, and distributed cortical areas, serve as common mediators linking SCN-2 to both functional domains, demonstrating a cerebello-thalamo-cortical/limbic circuitry underlying dual-domain impairments.

These findings highlight the value of network-based approaches in capturing distributed neurobiological patterns underlying physio-cognitive decline. SCN-2 and its associated cerebral mediators may serve as potential targets for early detection and intervention strategies. Future investigations should examine the longitudinal trajectories of these networks and determine whether SCN-2 disruption predicts subsequent functional decline and adverse health outcomes in aging populations.
