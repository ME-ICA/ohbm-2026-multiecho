Introduction

Major depressive disorders (MDD) is highly associated with anxiety with the prevalence of MDD with anxious symptoms ranging from 42% to 78%1. Moreover, MDD with co-occurring anxiety symptoms is particularly important to consider, since such anxious symptoms are associated with more severe disease progression2

The relationship between anxiety and depression has mainly been investigated in resting state fMRI, where pathophysiological similarities between patients with MDD and patients with social anxiety disorder were found3. In another study, significantly lowered fractional amplitude of low frequency fluctuations was observed in MDD patients with high anxiety symptom severity, particularly in the bilateral anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and the bilateral insula4.

This study aimed to identify the pathophysiological changes in the extended reward system associated with the severity of anxiety symptoms in MDD, using a conditioned reward task during fMRI.

Methods

The sample consisted of 134 patients with MDD (81 female and 53 male) with an age between 19 and 61 years (M = 34.5, std = 11.3). To measure the severity of anxiety symptoms, the state aspect of the state-trait-anxiety index is used (STAI-S). Additionally, to ensure that the results are correlated to the anxiety symptoms and not the general depression severity, the Montgomery-Asberg-Depression-Rating Scale was used as a covariate of no interest. 

The participants underwent fMRI scanning using a conditioned sequential forced choice task, which measures the bottom-up activation of the reward system by conditioned reward stimuli5. The analyzed first-level contrast compared the activations evoked by the conditioned reward stimuli with the implicit baseline. 

The correlation between the STAI-S values and the beta values from the first level analysis was investigated using a linear regression model. We also performed between-group comparisons of patients with high and low STAI-S scores. Since there was no relevant difference between the results, the results presented are from the linear regression whole-brain-analysis computed with a primary search criterion of p<0.05, uncorrected.

Results

A significant positive correlation between anxiety scores and reward-related brain activation was found in the head of left caudate nucleus (-12, 18, 3 t= 2.77), bilateral intraparietal cortex (right: 36, -60, 48 t= 4.16, left: -33, -57, 39 t = 3.24), bilateral inferior frontal sulcus adjacent to the inferior frontal junction area (right: 39, 15, 36 t = 3.32, left: -33, 24, 24 t = 3.09) and the anterior insula / deep fronto-opercular cortex (right: 36, 15, 3 t = 2.45, left: -39, 24, 3 t = 2.70). – (Fig. 1 A) 

On the other hand, negative correlations between anxiety scores and reward-related brain activation were observed in the bilateral anterior prefrontal cortex (right: 30, 42, 9 t = 2.18, left: -18, 48, 6 t = 2.08), the central orbitofrontal cortex (-18, 33, -12 t= 2.96), the ventral putamen (-30 3 -3, t=2.46), the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (6, 33, -9 t = 2.36) as well as the right amygdala (30, -6, -12 t = 1.99). – (Fig. 1 B)

In addition we found a significant correlation between the anxiety severity (STAI-S scores) and general depression severity (MDRS scores) (R= 0.378, p = 6.87e-6). Therefore, in order to control for more general effects of depression, the MADRS-score was added as an additional covariate of no interest into the linear regression analysis. This additional analysis confirmed that the results presented above were indeed related more specifically to the severity of anxiety symptoms.

Conclusion

In the present study, we found that the severity of anxiety symptoms in MDD patients positively correlated with brain activation in several parietal and frontal cortical regions, some of them associated with the extended reward system, while it negatively correlated with activation in areas associated with top-down control and emotional regulation. In line with previous research findings, these results may help to identify and characterize a pathophysiological subtype of MDD, which is associated with pronounced anxiety symptoms.
