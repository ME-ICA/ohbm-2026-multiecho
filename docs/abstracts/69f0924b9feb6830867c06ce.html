Introduction

The arcuate fasciculus is a crucial fiber pathway supporting language functions. This pathway is predominantly left-lateralized and comprises distinct subsegments connecting the temporal cortex to the posterior part of Broca’s area (dorsal pathway to BA44) and the premotor cortex (dorsal pathway to BA6). In adults, these subsegments support distinct language functions: the dorsal pathway to BA44 supports grammar, while the dorsal pathway to BA6 supports speech articulation. These pathways also show a differential trajectory during development. While the dorsal pathway to BA44 undergoes prolonged maturation, the dorsal pathway to BA6 is already relatively mature at birth.1 Furthermore, while both pathways show pronounced left-lateralization in preschoolers, only increased left-lateralization in the dorsal pathway to BA44 has been linked to sentence processing abilities.2 Much less is known about the maturation and lateralization of these two dorsal pathways during infancy, despite being a critical period for language acquisition.

Methods

To address this, we investigated a cross-sectional sample of 64 monolingual German infants aged 9 to 15 months (M = 11.85 months, SD = 1.65 months, 34 female), centered around 12 months when first words are typically produced. We reconstructed the two dorsal pathways using diffusion-weighted MRI data acquired during natural sleep (see Figure 1). Preprocessing was performed using the infant-specific pipeline of QSIPrep.3 Fiber quantification was conducted using pyAFQ with Constrained Spherical Deconvolution (CSD) as the local model and probabilistic whole-brain tractography.4 We relied primarily on the default tract definition provided by pyAFQ for the arcuate fasciculus to reconstruct both subsegments, but defined BA44 and BA6 as the endpoints of each dorsal pathway in the frontal lobe, respectively. To assess the maturation and lateralization ([left – right] / [left + right]) of each pathway, we analyzed the total streamline count and the fractional anisotropy (FA) profile along 100 equidistant nodes. Additionally, we assessed infants’ language development using a standardized behavioral battery for general development and a standard parental questionnaire. We tested for significant lateralization using two-sided one-sample t-tests. To examine the relation between fiber pathway lateralization and infants’ age as well as language development, we performed multiple linear regressions, controlling for infants’ sex and motion, and additionally their general cognitive development in the models testing the relation with language. When analyzing FA along each fiber pathway, we utilized cluster-based permutation to identify significant clusters (5,000 permutations with a significance threshold of 0.025, Bonferroni-corrected for two brain maturation measures).

Results

Our results indicate that the dorsal pathway to BA6 exhibits significant left-lateralization in streamline count (M = 0.34, SD = 0.35, t(63) = 7.87, p < 0.001; see Figure 2a). Additionally, the tract profile revealed two clusters with pronounced left-lateralization of FA (central-anterior portion: cluster size = 19, FWE-corrected p < 0.001; central-posterior portion: cluster size = 14, FWE-corrected p = 0.002; see Figure 2b), consistent with previous findings in preschool children.2 In contrast, the dorsal pathway to BA44 showed no clear lateralization in streamline count on the group-level (M = 0.10, SD = 0.41, t(62) = 1.92, p = 0.06; see Figure 2a), though we observed high variation across individuals. The tract profile of the pathway to BA44 revealed a cluster with pronounced left-lateralization of FA in the posterior region towards the parietal lobe (cluster size = 12, FWE-corrected p = 0.03; see Figure 2c), similar to preschool aged children reported earlier.2 In contrast, the anterior region of the pathway exhibited a cluster with pronounced right-lateralization (cluster size = 46, FWE-corrected p = 0.002). Left-lateralization in this region increased with age between 9 and 15 months (anterior-to-central portion: cluster size = 38, FWE-corrected p = 0.005; central portion: cluster size = 17, FWE-corrected p = 0.013). When investigating how these fiber pathways support language functions, we found that maturation of the left dorsal pathway to BA6 was significantly related to infants’ language development (streamline count: p = 0.004; FA in the posterior portion: cluster size = 12, FWE-corrected p = 0.012).

Conclusion

Overall, these results suggest that the dorsal pathway to BA6 is largely mature and left-lateralized in infancy, which aligns with previous findings in newborns. The lateralization of the dorsal pathway to BA44 on the other hand appears to follow a posterior to anterior developmental trajectory, with the anterior portion being still relatively immature. Unlike the pattern seen in preschoolers,2 this anterior segment was not left-lateralized but instead showed pronounced right-lateralization. The increase in left-lateralization of this segment in infants aged 9 to 15 months suggests a critical maturational process. Interestingly, language functions were related to the maturation of the left dorsal pathway to BA6. Given that the pathway to BA44 is still immature, the dorsal pathway to BA6 may support early language functions in 1-year-old infants. While previous work has often investigated the arcuate fasciculus as a single collective tract, our findings demonstrate that the maturation trajectory and relation to language in infancy differ between its subsegments. This sheds light on the ontogeny of the arcuate fasciculus and the specific roles its subsegments play in language development.
