Introduction

On average, nearly 100 neuroimaging studies are published daily. To keep pace, a clinician/scientist would need to read a new article every five minutes for eight hours a day, on top of maintaining their own research program, clinical responsibilities, teaching, mentoring, and engagement with adjacent literatures. Meta-analyses help make this growing body of work tractable, but conducting a meta-analysis remains an arduous process. Some difficulty is both necessary and desirable, such as operationalizing constructs of interest and rigorously defining inclusion criteria. However, many steps are unnecessarily burdensome, including identifying studies that report relevant data, extracting coordinates and key metadata, and configuring software environments to execute analyses. We present NeuroSynth Compose, an open and reproducible platform that reduces these avoidable barriers through automation, while keeping researchers firmly in the intellectual driver’s seat.

Methods

NeuroSynth Compose is a modular platform that connects an ecosystem of tools to curate, ingest, annotate, and execute neuroimaging meta-analyses. NeuroStore serves as the primary storage backend, housing articles and associated structured data. Open workflows leveraging natural language processing (NLP) and large language models (LLMs) populate NeuroStore with semantic embeddings derived from titles and abstracts, as well as structured information such as imaging modality, participant demographics, study topics, and stereotactic coordinates extracted from tables. NeuroStore functions as an independent backend that can be queried directly or extended by external tools. NeuroSynth Compose primarily provides a powerful and intuitive frontend for curating studies stored in NeuroStore. Once curated, the platform generates a fully reproducible bundle containing both data and analysis specifications. Meta-analyses are executed using the Neuroimaging Meta-Analysis Research Environment (NiMARE), a well-documented and comprehensive Python library. Analyses can be run on Amazon Web Services (AWS), Google Colab, or local hardware capable of running NiMARE. Platform development has been guided by continuous user engagement across multiple feedback channels.

Results

The platform currently serves over 2,600 users across diverse geographic regions, demonstrating substantial global uptake. Users actively curate data on the platform, extracting or editing coordinates from 2,351 unique articles and expanding the pool of usable studies to 32,820. To date, users have completed well over 1,000 meta-analyses, many of which have organically resulted in peer-reviewed publications. The available corpus spans a wide range of neuroimaging topics and clinical conditions, supporting research questions that reflect the breadth and diversity of the contemporary neuroimaging literature.

Conclusion

The organic adoption, sustained use, and publication outcomes associated with NeuroSynth Compose highlight its intuitive design and comprehensive functionality. Because workflows are open, reproducible, and clonable, researchers can systematically probe the robustness of their findings, while independent groups can transparently evaluate analytic choices. The platform also lowers the barrier to updating meta-analyses over time, enabling benchmark analyses to evolve as new studies are ingested and annotated. NeuroSynth Compose supports both coordinate-based and image-based meta-analyses, spanning conventional and semi-automated approaches and enabling a broad range of use cases. Overall, NeuroSynth Compose provides an open platform for curating, executing, sharing, and iteratively updating neuroimaging meta-analyses through an accessible user interface.
