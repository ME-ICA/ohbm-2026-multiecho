Introduction

Identifying subtle, patient-specific abnormalities in brain structure is critical for clinical translation of MRI biomarkers. Normative modelling enables such individualised assessment by comparing subjects against population-derived benchmarks, offering advantages over traditional case-control approaches. However, practical questions remain: How robust are pre-trained models when applied to new sites with demographic imbalances? Can these methods be used if limited local controls are available? And what effect do differences in scanning protocol between cases and controls have on model calibration and outputs? Here, we systematically evaluate these factors to define calibration requirements for reliable implementation in clinical neuroimaging.

Methods

Using cortical thickness data from 23 patients with left mesial temporal lobe epilepsy (mTLE) and 69 healthy controls (IDEAS dataset1, 3T MRI, FreeSurfer Desikan-Killiany parcellation), we performed systematic validation experiments using the Brain MoNoCle normative modelling platform2. We derived regional z-scores and effect sizes for patients with mTLE from pre-trained normative models, using varying subsets of healthy controls for model calibration. First, we assessed the robustness of outputs to demographic (age and sex) biases between cases and controls. We then examined control sample size requirements via bootstrapped subsampling, assessing stability of regional effect against ENIGMA-epilepsy benchmarks. Finally, we investigated the extend of scanner effects on model calibration using controls with matched versus mismatched acquisition protocols.

Results

Robustness to demographic biases: Despite the age mismatch between patients and controls, regional effect sizes showed strong agreement with outputs derived using age-matched data (mean absolute difference=0.19, r=0.84). An analysis using sex-mismatched samples produced even higher consistency (mean absolute difference=0.12, r=0.94) with analysis of demographically unbiased samples. Effect of control sample size: Effect size accuracy improved substantially with the sample size of healthy controls used for model calibration. With n=10 controls,  less than 30% of estimates fell within the ENIGMA-epilepsy informed confidence interval; with n=30, accuracy improved to 50%, and it reached 71% with all n=69 controls. Variability decreased markedly above n=30. Reliability of scanner-mismatched calibration: A normative model calibration using scanner-mismatched controls produced uncorrelated regional effects (r=-0.09, mean absolute difference=0.66) with substantially altered individual abnormality patterns, demonstrating the critical importance of site- and protocol-specific calibration.

Conclusion

Pre-trained normative models demonstrate remarkable robustness to demographic imbalances between cases and controls from new sites, enabling reliable application even with mismatched control cohorts. However, scanner-specific calibration is essential, with mismatched controls producing invalid results. Even relatively small sample sizes of site-matched controls provide reasonable stability, enabling researchers to leverage population-level models, though larger calibration samples improve precision. These findings offer practical guidelines for responsible implementation of normative modelling in neuroimaging research, underscoring it as a powerful alternative to traditional case-control designs for individual-level abnormality detection, bridging population research and clinical translation.
