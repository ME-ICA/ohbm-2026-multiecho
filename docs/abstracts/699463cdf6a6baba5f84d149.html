Introduction

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), as a prevalent neurodevelopmental disorder, exhibits aberrant brain structural connectome compared to neurotypical controls, yet the mechanistic origins of these connectomic alterations remain elusive. The geometric eigenmode model (GEM) derived from Neural Field Theory offers promising mechanistic insights for understanding brain organization by incorporating geometrical constraints that govern brain dynamics. We therefore hypothesized that connectome abnormalities in ADHD arise from altered geometric constraints on brain dynamics. To test this hypothesis, we derived individual-specific geometric eigenmodes from cortical mesh for each child to model wave propagation dynamics, thereby reconstructing empirical connectivity patterns obtained from diffusion MRI. We subsequently assessed whether connectome abnormalities of ADHD manifest within this geometry-constrained framework.

Methods

Leveraging multisite datasets comprising 713 ADHD children and 304 neurotypical controls (aged 6-18 years), empirical structural connectomes were constructed from diffusion imaging data with probabilistic tractography. Individual geometric eigenmodes of the left hemisphere were obtained by solving the eigenvalue problem associated with the Helmholtz equation of the cortical midthickness meshes. Given that the GEM framework shapes connectivity architecture by preferentially interconnecting cortical regions that enable resonant excitation of geometric modes, we constructed optimized connectome models for each participant. These models incorporated individual-specific geometric modes and eigenvalues, using optimal parameter combinations that maximized reconstruction accuracy (i.e., the sum of correlations of binary degree, weighted degree, and edge weights). We subsequently investigated case-control differences in GEM performance and explored how long-range connection and spectral removal patterns contribute to ADHD-related abnormalities in cortical geometric constraints. Parcel-wise constraint strength, defined as the proportion of empirical connections successfully recapitulated by GEM, was quantified and projected onto large-scale brain networks. Finally, we assessed whether topological deviations in ADHD can be explained by model parameters and investigated neurobiological underpinnings through Neuromaps spatial correlations. 

Results

At the group-averaged level of ADHD and controls, the optimized GEMs accurately reproduced their structural connectomes, exhibiting robust topological and topographical alignment (reconstruction accuracy=1.67 and 1.70 respectively, all p<.001; true positive rate of edges=0.75 and 0.76, p<.001). Individual-level reconstruction of structural connectomes via subject-specific geometric eigenmodes revealed significant between-group differences: ADHD children exhibited inferior reconstruction accuracy (1.34 vs 1.41, Cohen’s d=-0.15, p<.001) and true positive rates (0.67vs 0.69, d=-0.32, p<.001) compared to controls. Both groups exhibited comparable distance thresholds (short-range: 18.07 vs 18.07, d=-0.01, p=.923; long-range: 37.97 vs 36.50, d=0.07, p=.133). Although the GEM demonstrated equivalent recapitulation fidelity for short-range connections (area between curves: 0.22 vs 0.22, d=-0.06, p=.183), a greater proportion of long-range connections in ADHD deviated from geometric constraints relative to controls (area between curves: 100.25 vs 88.41, d=0.18, p<.001). We also found diminished spatial scale differentiation in ADHD brain network organization (area between curves: 22.53 vs 23.74, d=-0.14, p=.001). Parcels showing differential geometric constraint rankings between ADHD and TDC were predominantly situated in the medial surface, and connections in sensorimotor and limbic networks particularly deviated from geometric predictions in ADHD. The PLS1 of deviations in regional small-worldness derived from normative modeling could explain 9.4% variance of model parameters (p=.012). Regional abnormal geometric constraints were spatially correlated with specific pathophysiological patterns across three domains: genomics, structural features, and metabolism.

Conclusion

Pediatric brain connectivity patterns appear to follow organizational principles that promote shortcuts enabling resonant excitation of cortical geometric eigenmodes, as evidenced by our model's capacity to recapitulate landmark features of topology and topography. Atypical geometric constraint patterns revealed through GEM analysis may contribute to ADHD-related structural connectome abnormalities, highlighting its potential for elucidating mechanistic foundations of altered brain structural connectivity in the realm of psychiatry.
