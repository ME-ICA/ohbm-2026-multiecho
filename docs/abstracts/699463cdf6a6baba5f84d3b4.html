Introduction

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic autoimmune disease of the central nervous system that affects more than 2.8 million people worldwide. It is characterized by the formation of demyelinating white matter lesions in the brain, which disrupt neural signal transmission and lead to diverse neurological symptoms, including motor impairment, sensory loss, and cognitive decline. The spatial distribution of white matter lesions on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is central to MS diagnosis and monitoring, making reliable lesion detection a cornerstone of modern MS research and clinical practice.

Traditionally, MS lesion segmentation has been performed manually, which is the most accurate, yet most time-consuming method. Over the past decade, several AI MS lesion segmentation algorithms have been proposed. However, these algorithms tend to have high variability in lesion size, shape, and location in the brain, in addition to across-site loss of accuracy, leading to to inconsistencies in lesion detection algorithm performance. An ensemble approach that combines the results of several algorithms, leveraging their respective strengths, has been shown to improve the accuracy of automated MS lesion segmentation compared to select individual algorithms. 

The 2021 revision of the McDonald diagnostic criteria added lesion count as one of the key indicators for MS diagnosis. Traditionally, Connected Components (CC) analysis has been used on binary lesion masks outputted by AI segmentation algorithms to estimate individual lesion counts. CC counts adjacent voxels as individual lesions, failing to account for confluent lesions that share a border, but that formed distinctly from each other. To address this limitation, the ConfLUNet algorithm has been developed to perform instance segmentation on the basis of a lesion center heatmap1. ConfLUNet provides a promising alternative to CC for individual lesion segmentation as it does not consistently underestimate individual lesion counts. 

Methods

Three AI brain lesion segmentation algorithms: LST-AI2, AllNet3, and Samseg4 were run on three MS Datasets: the MICCAI 2016 challenge test5 (called “France”) with n=53, the MSLesSeg6 (called “Italy”) with n=75, and the private Weill Cornell Medicine MS Menopause dataset (called “US”) with n=13. We propose here our Ensemble3 approach wherein the probability masks from each algorithm were averaged per patient. The most optimal probability threshold for converting the Ensemble3 probability masks to binary segmentation masks was found for each dataset by testing different thresholds at increments of 0.01 and determining which threshold resulted in the highest mean Dice score. Connected Components analysis and ConfLUNet postprocessing were applied to the Ensemble3 outputs to estimate individual lesion counts.

Results

Across all datasets, LST-AI resulted in the highest mean dice score (0.522), followed by Ensemble3 (0.515), AllNet (0.404), and Samseg (0.387). LST-AI performed best on the “France” and “Italy” datasets, while Ensemble3 performed best on the “US” dataset. The number of individual instances predicted by ConfLUNet for each patient on average greatly exceeded the number predicted by Connected Components analysis, and was consistently closer to the ground truth individual lesion counts.

Conclusion

The Ensemble3 approach combining the results of LST-AI, AllNet, and Samseg outperformed select individual brain lesion segmentation algorithms on several MS datasets. ConfLUNet significantly outperformed Connected Components analysis with regards to individual MS lesion segmentation. This ensemble approach to combining information from multiple lesion segmentation algorithms shows promise for being robust to varied sample populations and scanner types.
