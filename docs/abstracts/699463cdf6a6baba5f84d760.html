Introduction

Parallel-transmit (pTx) for 7 T MRI is widely used, as steering the multiple radiofrequency (RF) signals is required to overcome the increased RF inhomogeneity and specific absorption rate (SAR) challenges in ultra-high field. However, at present, there is no evidence in the literature of EEG-fMRI at 7 T employing pTx, as this approach requires prior knowledge of coil and EEG cap modelling, as well as complex SAR calculations associated with pTx. Here, we present the first evidence supporting the safety of EEG-fMRI using pTx coil arrays.

Methods

An 8-channel pTx head coil array1 was simulated using Sim4Life 8.0 (ZMT, Switzerland). Each channel comprised 4 strip line elements (length=174mm, width=12mm) with a conducting and ground plane (with a 2.5mm-central-gap) separated by ~29mm, and modelled as perfect-electric conductor (PEC). The array was placed on a circular coil holder with inner/outer diameter of 290/348mm positioned in a RF shield (diameter = 320 mm, length = 1150 mm), and loaded with human model Duke 3.02 (Figure 1a). Each channel tuned to 297.2 MHz and matched to 50Ω (Optenni Lab,Finland) using capacitors (0.1 – 56 pF with effective series resistances < 3Ω).
 
A realistic model of our previously reported 7 T EEG cap3 was built to fit Duke’s head, containing 34 electrodes (32 EEG, 1 ground, 1 reference) and 4 artefact detection loops (ADLs). Electrodes were modelled as PEC, projected 4.5 mm from the scalp surface, and connected to Duke’s scalp through dielectric cylinders with the assigned material properties of Abralyte Gel (εr=68, =4.7, σ = 4.7 S/m). Electrode wires were connected to the electrodes through 5 kΩ current-limiting resistors, and subsequently routed into PCBs, segmented by a 5 kΩ resistor, before exiting the RF coil through a carbon wire cable tree (εr = 5, σ = 62695 S/m). ADLs projected 7 mm from the scalp surface were modelled as resistive polymer (εr = 5, σ = 5.6 S/m) (Figure 1a). Simulations were performed with and without the EEG cap to determine perturbed and unperturbed and values.
 
B1+ and SAR10g maps in CP mode (channel phases = 0:45:315°) , and individual channel E-fields, were interpolated at 1 mm-isotropic resolution, normalized to 1 W total input power, and exported to Matlab (MathWorks, Natick, MA) for 10-gram tissue averaged Q-matrix calculation.4 150k random shims were applied to the VOPs to generate a histogram of all possible maximum SAR10g levels. B1+ and SAR10g Maximum Intensity Projections (MIPs) were calculated in all three directions, and MIP difference maps were obtained by subtracting the Duke-only condition from the condition with Duke + EEG cap.
 
For validation, simulated and measured individual/combined maps were compared using an AFI sequence (voxel size = 4.3x4.3x4mm, TR = 126ms, TE1/TE2/TE3 = 2.04/4.08/6.12 ms) on a 7 T Magnetom Terra (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) with an 8Tx/32Rx head coil (Nova Medical Inc., MA, USA) loaded with a head-shaped phantom (SAM,εr = 45.3, σ = 0.87 S/m, ZMT). Figure 1(b) demonstrates close agreement between measured and simulated  maps in the condition without EEG cap, while the S matrix presented in figure 1(c) demonstrates adequate coil performance, reflecting the validity of the simulation setup for the purposes of making EEG-fMRI pTx safety inferences in our results going forward.  

Results

The introduction of the EEG cap gave rise to minimal B1+ and SAR10g perturbations, the most notable being a slight increase in both cases around the scalp's superior periphery proximal to the EEG wire bundle gathered to exit the RF coil. While the mean SAR10g is increased by around 14% with the inclusion of the EEG cap (consistent with findings using 1Tx coils5), peak SAR10g (located proximal to the nose) remains unperturbed, which is the main limiting factor for 7 T MRI. Otherwise, stability is seen in both maps across cap-on and cap-off conditions. (Figure 2a)

Similar peak SAR10g  distribution is seen with or without the EEG cap for 150k random shims, showing that it does not alter the SAR irrespective of B1+ shim and therefore the worst-case SAR10g are unalerted by the EEG cap (Figure 2b). This suggests that PTx MRI does not require altered safety limits for this EEG cap design.

Conclusion

This study presents the first evidence supporting the feasibility and safety of performing EEG-fMRI with pTx technology at 7 T. Simulations show minimal perturbation of B1+and SAR10g distributions with the addition of a resistively segmented 7 T EEG cap. Key design elements — including 5 kΩ resistor segmentation, carbon wire cabling, and resistive polymer ADLs— reduce RF coupling. Experimental validation of these simulations remains essential, but these findings suggest that pTx-enabled EEG-fMRI at 7 T can be conducted safely, representing an important step toward UHF multimodal neuroimaging.
