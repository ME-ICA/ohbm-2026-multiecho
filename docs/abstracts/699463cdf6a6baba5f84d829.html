Introduction

Emerging deep-learning techniques have the potential to identify patterns of brain atrophy associated with cognitive decline during the early stages of Parkinson’s disease (PD). This study aimed to investigate whether cognitive decline in patients with de-novo PD is associated with structural changes of the hippocampus, amygdala and inferior lateral ventricle using a deep-learning approach. 

Methods

Data was acquired from the Parkinson’s progression markers initiative (PPMI) cohort, including 134 patients with newly diagnosed PD and 70 control participants. T1-weighted MRI scans acquired at baseline (diagnosis) and four years after baseline were analysed alongside cognitive and clinical assessment data. This included cognitive assessments (Montreal cognitive assessment (MoCA)), sleep scores, motor phenotype classifications and motor symptom severity assessments, to assess relationships between imaging data and PD symptomatology. Atrophy scores of the hippocampus, amygdala, and inferior lateral ventricle, as well as a combined hippocampal-amygdalo-ventricular atrophy score (HAVAs), were generated using Assembly-Net-AD, a deep-learning tool trained on Alzheimer's disease (AD) lifespan models 1–4.

Results

Patients who developed cognitive impairment within four years after diagnosis showed significantly higher HAVAs (p= <0.01) and hippocampal (p= 0.013) atrophy scores at baseline. Moreover, at four-year follow up, declining cognitive performance correlated with increased baseline HAVAs (r= -0.30, p= <0.001), and hippocampal (r= -0.20, p= 0.021) and amygdala (r=-0.19, p= 0.030) atrophy scores. Patients with PD exhibited progressive atrophy of these regions from baseline to year four, with significant increases in HAVAs (p=<0.0001), hippocampal (p= 0.012) and amygdala (p= 0.017) atrophy scores, while healthy controls showed no significant changes. No associations were observed between baseline inferior lateral ventricle expansion and cognitive performance, nor did ventricular expansion scores change significantly between baseline and year four time points in patients with PD. No significant associations were found between structural imaging measures and motor or autonomic symptom scores.

Conclusion

These findings reveal an association between medial temporal lobe structure and cognitive impairment through the early course of PD. We report a relationship between the HAVAs predictive score and cognitive impairment in PD at baseline and at a four year follow up, but no association with motor and autonomic symptoms, suggesting that mesial temporal lobe structural changes are specifically associated with cognitive decline. In a clinical context, this evidence may support the development of structural biomarkers using a novel deep-learning approach and may help clinicians identify patients at risk of early cognitive decline.
