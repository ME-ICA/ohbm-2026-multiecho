Introduction

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by social communication difficulties, restricted and repetitive behaviors (RRBs), and sensory atypicalities.1 Its prevalence ranges from 1% to 2%, representing a major societal challenge. The etiology and pathophysiology of ASD remain poorly understood. However, growing evidence supports the hypothesis of an imbalance in the neuronal excitation/inhibition (E/I) ratio, involving abnormalities in GABAergic and glutamatergic neurotransmission.2 Currently, one of the main challenges in understanding this balance and its implication in ASD lies in the development of biomarkers that allow for a better investigation of it. 

Electroencephalography (EEG) is a non-invasive, low-cost technique that is easily applicable in routine clinical settings, making it an ideal tool for deriving biomarkers of the E/I balance. 

Based on previous literature, various biomarkers can be extracted from EEG signals to provide an indirect measure of the excitation/inhibition (E/I) balance.3 Among these, we analyzed beta power, gamma power and signal entropy. 

The aim of this study is therefore to determine whether these E/I-related markers differ between individuals with ASD and typically developing controls, which could reflect an imbalance in the E/I ratio, and whether these differences are correlated with the severity of ASD symptoms.

Methods

Participants: Analysis was conducted on data from 321 participants with ASD (57 % females) and 284 control individuals (51 % females), aged between 5 and 21 years, collected from the SoNeTAA and HBN cohorts.4 Specific clinical measures related to social, repetitive, sensory and emotional symptoms were collected for ASD participants to assess symptom severity. Not all participants completed all clinical scales, resulting in the formation of subgroups based on the measures collected.

EEG and Recording Paradigm: All participants underwent resting-state EEG recordings with eyes closed for a duration of 2 to 5 minutes using a high-resolution EEG setup (128-channel GSN HydroCel Geodesic system).

Preprocessing: EEG data were preprocessed using an automated, multi-step pipeline combining algorithmic procedures and visual inspection. Signals were downsampled to 500 Hz, bandpass filtered between 1 and 45 Hz, cleaned of noisy channels (pyprep), re-referenced (common average), and corrected for ocular artifacts using IClabel. A final epoch rejection step (Autoreject) ensured high-quality data for analysis.

Beta and gamma powers analysis : The absolute power spectral density (PSD) of the beta (12–30 Hz) and gamma (30–45 Hz) bands was computed for each electrode using Welch’s method (2-second Hann window with a 50% overlap, and a spectral resolution of 0.5 Hz).

Multiscale entropy analysis : Multiscale Entropy (MSE) was computed using the open-source EntropyHub toolbox.5 The algorithm generated 20 coarse-grained time series from the original EEG signal, with parameters m = 2 and r = 0.2×SD. Sample Entropy was then used to estimate signal complexity at each electrode and each scale.

Statistical analysis: Electrodes were grouped and averaged into six distinct regions of interest (ROIs): frontal, central, parietal, occipital, and left and right temporal regions. Group comparisons were performed for each ROI and each scale using linear regression models, controlling for age, sex and site. To investigate the relationship between E/I markers and ASD symptoms, multiple linear regression models were then applied to each ROI and each scale, with the same covariates included. All analyses were performed using Python 3.9.

Results

Beta and gamma powers analysis :  Beta power was positively correlated with sensory hyposensitivity across all regions (all pcorr<0.02 and f 2>0.12, ASD=57) and with sensory hypersensitivity in the central, parietal, and left temporal regions (all pcorr<0.05 and f 2>0.12, ASD=57). In addition, an exploratory result suggested that an increase in gamma power may be associated with the severity of restricted and repetitive behaviors (RRBs) in the right temporal region (puncorr=0.01, f 2=0.08, ASD=127).

Multiscale entropy analysis :  The results showed a decrease in entropy in individuals with ASD compared to control subjects over central and left temporal regions at larger time scales (all puncorr  between 0.005 and 0.04, ASD = 205, controls = 272).

Additionally, increased social symptoms (assessed using ADI-R and SRS-2 scores) and restrictive and repetitive behaviors (assessed using RBS-R scores) in the ASD group were associated with reduced entropy values across several regions, predominantly at larger time scales (all puncorr between 0.005 and 0.04, between 30 and 205 ASD subjects depending on clinical measures). 

Emotional symptom severity (assessed using GARS Emotional Responses score) was also associated with reduced entropy in the central, parietal and right temporal region at larger time scales (all puncorr  between 0.01 and 0.04, ASD = 29).

Conclusion

Exitation/Inhibition EEG markers analysis holds promise as potential biomarkers to improve the diagnosis of autism, while also offering valuable insights into the underlying neural mechanisms associated with the disorder.
