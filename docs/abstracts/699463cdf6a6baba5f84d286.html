Introduction

Typical brain maturation across childhood and adolescence involves substantial reorganization of intrinsic functional architecture.^1,2 Resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) enables characterization of these processes, yet many studies rely on single metrics that capture only one dimension of spontaneous activity. Multimetric approaches can instead reveal complementary aspects of functional maturation and provide stronger developmental benchmarks. Although neurodevelopmental research has documented age-related changes in activity amplitude, local synchrony, and interhemispheric coordination, large voxel-wise studies integrating these three properties remain limited.^3
To address this gap, we applied a multimetric framework encompassing fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (fALFF), regional homogeneity (ReHo), and voxel-mirrored homotopic connectivity (VMHC). These measures capture spontaneous signal amplitude, local temporal synchrony, and interhemispheric functional coordination, respectively.^4
Using a large harmonized sample from ABIDE I/II, we aimed to map whole-brain developmental effects, identify shared and metric-specific age trajectories, and describe the transition from childhood to adolescence. Prior work has highlighted age-related refinement of functional hubs and increasing hemispheric specialization,^1,5 underscoring the relevance of integrative analytic strategies. By combining voxel-wise analyses across metrics, this study offers a multidimensional depiction of typical rs-fMRI maturation and provides reference patterns for future developmental and clinical research.

Methods

We analyzed resting-state fMRI and anatomical MRI data from 395 typically developing participants aged 6–20 years from ABIDE I/II. Ten sites met inclusion criteria based on age range, availability of IQ data, minimum sample size, and eyes-open resting-state acquisition. Rigorous quality control included visual inspection of structural/functional alignment and exclusion based on framewise displacement exceeding group mean + 2 SD.

Preprocessing was performed using DPABI/DPARSF (SPM12). Functional data underwent slice-timing correction, motion estimation, nuisance regression (WM, CSF, linear trends, and 24-parameter Friston model), DARTEL normalization to MNI space, and spatial smoothing (4 mm FWHM for fALFF/ReHo; 8 mm for VMHC). For ReHo and VMHC analyses, time series were bandpass-filtered (0.01–0.10 Hz). Structural images were segmented and normalized using DARTEL, and gray-matter volume (GMV) was extracted for use as a covariate.
To minimize scanner/site variability, harmonization was applied using ComBat and CovBat implemented in DPABI, following recommended parametric settings and 90% PCA covariance retention.
Voxel-wise maps were computed for fALFF (FFT-based frequency-domain estimation), ReHo (Kendall’s coefficient of concordance across 27-voxel neighborhoods), and VMHC (Fisher z-transformed correlations between mirrored voxels using a symmetric MNI template).
Statistical analyses included (1) voxel-wise Pearson correlations between each metric and age (n=395), and (2) voxel-wise ANOVA/ANCOVA comparing six discrete age groups (7–9, 9–11, 11–13, 13–15, 15–17, 17–19 years; n=384). GMV was included as a covariate in primary models. Multiple-comparison correction used GRF theory (voxel p<0.01, cluster p<0.05) with metric-specific minimum cluster sizes to ensure anatomical reliability. Significant clusters were localized using the AAL atlas.

Results

Across metrics, we observed converging age-related decreases in intrinsic activity and connectivity. Voxel-wise correlations revealed significant negative associations between age and fALFF in medial orbitofrontal, fusiform, caudate, and cerebellar regions. ReHo showed age-related decreases in the right insula, whereas VMHC exhibited widespread reductions in interhemispheric coupling, particularly in the putamen, superior parietal lobules, and posterior cerebellum. These effects persisted across models with and without GMV covariates and after harmonization, underscoring robustness.
Group-level comparisons demonstrated that younger children (7–9 years) exhibited greater fALFF and ReHo in subcortical and occipito-temporal regions, and higher VMHC within cerebellar and striatal structures compared with mid-to-late adolescents. Developmental transtion were not linear: several clusters showed pronounced decreases during early adolescence (11–13 years), followed by more gradual changes thereafter. Cerebellar regions displayed the most consistent age-related reductions across all metrics, highlighting their centrality to protracted functional maturation.
Collectively, results revealed a shift from localized and bilaterally synchronized intrinsic activity toward more differentiated, specialized patterns with advancing age. While effect sizes were modest, as expected in healthy developmental samples, the spatial convergence across three distinct metrics strengthened the interpretability of findings. Harmonization procedures effectively reduced site-related variability, enabling reliable detection of subtle developmental effects in a multisite sample.

Conclusion

This multimetric rs-fMRI study provides a comprehensive whole-brain characterization of functional maturation across childhood and adolescence. Age-related reductions in fALFF, ReHo, and VMHC suggest a coordinated refinement of intrinsic brain architecture, marked by decreased local synchrony, diminished bilateral coupling, and reduced spontaneous signal amplitude. These patterns align with neurodevelopmental models emphasizing synaptic pruning, increased network specialization, and enhanced top-down integration during adolescence,^2,4 as well as prior evidence of decreasing homotopic connectivity and progressive functional asymmetry across development.^5

Importantly, the convergence of developmental effects across three complementary metrics underscores the value of multimodal analytic approaches for capturing distinct yet interrelated aspects of spontaneous neural activity. The consistent involvement of cerebellar and subcortical structures highlights their underappreciated contribution to functional maturation,^1 supporting earlier findings that functional hubs shift with age. By providing voxel-wise developmental benchmarks across amplitude-, synchrony-, and connectivity-based metrics, this work offers a valuable reference framework for future studies examining deviations from typical maturation in clinical or risk populations and contributes to growing efforts to characterize normative developmental trajectories.^3
