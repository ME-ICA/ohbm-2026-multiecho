Introduction

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the accumulation and spread of misfolded α-synuclein protein across interconnected brain regions. Computational models of disease progression offer a mechanistic framework for understanding how pathological proteins propagate through neural networks and drive patterns of neurodegeneration. This study aimed to characterize functional connectivity alterations in PD and evaluate the Aggregation Network Diffusion (AND) model for predicting individualized atrophy progression by simulating the spatiotemporal dynamics of misfolded α-synuclein spread and aggregation across functionally connected brain networks.

Methods

Eighty-seven PD patients and 60 controls underwent clinical evaluation, 3D T1-weighted and resting-state functional MRI at baseline, with PD patients followed longitudinally over 7 years. An independent validation cohort of 34 PD patients from the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative with 4-year follow-up was included. Genetic screening for GBA mutations stratified participants into 99 non-carrier and 22 GBA-positive PD patients. Structural images were processed using FreeSurfer longitudinal processing, parcellating the cerebral cortex into regions of the Desikan atlas. Grey matter volumetry was obtained using FIRST in FSL with normalization by head size through SIENAx analysis. All measurements were normalized and z-scored to extract observed atrophy. Resting-state fMRI preprocessing included registration to T1-weighted images using FLIRT, followed by processing with SPM12 and FSL. Functional connectivity networks were constructed by computing Pearson's correlations between mean time series, converted to z-scores using Fisher's r-to-z transformation. Functional connectivity matrices were filtered based on anatomical connections from structural connectomes of healthy controls. Graph theoretical analysis assessed network topology through clustering coefficient, transitivity, local and global efficiency, characteristic path length, nodal degree and strength, betweenness centrality, and eigenvector centrality. Network-based statistics with multivariate analysis of covariance and 5000 permutations investigated functional connectivity differences between groups. The AND model simulated alpha-synuclein spread across the functional connectome through a system of ordinary differential equations modeling monomer production, protein aggregation, and network diffusion based on parameters adaptable to individual patient dynamics.The model tracked six protein forms from monomers to mature fibrils. Patient-specific parameters were optimized using surrogate optimization to reproduce observed atrophy at first follow-up available. Model performance was assessed by Pearson's correlation between predicted and observed atrophy. Optimized parameters were then inserted in the AND model to predict atrophy at subsequent timepoints to evaluate predictive power. AND model was further applied to the GBA-positive cohort to assess model generalizability

Results

Relative to controls, PD patients exhibited functional network alterations in temporal, frontal, and sensorimotor lobes, while GBA-positive patients additionally showed parietal lobe impairment. NBS identified affected nodes primarily in basal ganglia and frontal lobes for PD patients, with additional parietal involvement in GBA-positive patients. The AND model successfully accurately predicted atrophy trajectories in both groups. Model performance was predominantly determined by network transmissibility, with protein aggregation and production parameters showing limited impact. Prefibrillar α-synuclein species combined with intermediate-weight protein aggregates most frequently yielded optimal atrophy predictions. Seed regions were predominantly localized to temporal and frontal lobes across both patient groups. The model revealed two distinct atrophy progression patterns: one associated with low transmissibility, characterized by focal atrophy and gradual spread, and another linked to high transmissibility, marked by early, widespread bilateral involvement. 

Conclusion

This study demonstrates significant functional connectivity alterations in PD, with network disruptions in temporal, frontal, sensorimotor, and parietal regions, as well as affected nodes in basal ganglia and frontal lobes. Additionally, this study demonstrates the feasibility of using the AND model for personalized prediction of PD atrophy progression. By accounting for subject-specific patterns of protein spread and optimizing key model parameters, this approach provides new insights into the mechanisms driving neurodegeneration in Parkinson's disease.
