Introduction

To facilitate the flexible implementation and deployment of cognitive processes, the human brain exhibits remarkable dynamic fluctuations in its structural-functional coupling (SFC), which refers to the statistical dependence between a brain region's function on its underlying anatomical structure. A growing number of works have demonstrated the importance of time-resolved SFC patterns; whereas, no prior study has quantified the temporal variability of dynamic SFC, reflecting the fluctuating extent to which functional connections interact with anatomical structures over time.

Methods

Leveraging multimodal neuroimaging data and behavioral measures of 1,206 participants from the Human Connectome Project (HCP, S1200 release) and 366 participants from the Chinese HCP (CHCP), we first quantify the replicable temporal variability pattern of cortical dynamic SFC (see Fig. 1a). The temporal variability analysis of dynamic SFC integrates high spatial resolution details of both structure and function, supplemented by the fine-grained temporal dynamics of human cognition, facilitating a more precise and lightweight delineation of the high-dimensional spatiotemporal characteristics of the human cerebral cortex. Thereafter, the multivariate correlations between the temporal variability of SFC and various cognitive and behavioral traits spanning multiple domains encompassing cognition, emotion, motor, sensory, alertness, and personality are examined by partial least square analysis (Fig. 1b). By integrating transcriptomics data from Allen Human Brain Atlas (AHBA) and positron emission tomography (PET) images from a cohort of over 1,200 healthy individuals, we explore how brain SFC variability is shaped by brain-wide gene expression (Fig. 1c) and neurochemical architecture (Fig. 1d), and whether clinical significance can be derived by the overlap between SFC variability-related genes and those involved in diseases.

Results

Results indicate that the temporal variability of dynamic SFC exhibits regional heterogeneity throughout the brain, with stable SFC predominantly observed in the intermediate cortex, while high variability spanned in anterior and posterior regions, reflecting the existence of distinct cortical hierarchies. Additionally, SFC temporal variability distributed in the dorsal attention, somatomotor, and visual networks is closely related to a composite emotion dimension. More interestingly, the transcriptomic organizations of cortical SFC variability are enriched in synapse- and cell cycle-related biological processes, associated with excitatory neurons, and consistently implicated in disorders pertaining to emotions. Finally, we identify an integrated spatial topography of serotonin, glutamate, γ-aminobutyric acid, and opioid systems that manifest as the regionally specialized SFC variability in human cortical areas (Fig. 2). Of note, these findings are further replicated and generalized to an independent replication dataset encompassing 366 healthy subjects.

Conclusion

Cortical SFC variability mapping provides a biologically plausible framework for understanding how SFC fluctuates over time to support macroscale neurocognitive specialization, underpinned by convergent transcriptomic and chemoarchitecture signatures.
