Introduction

Imbalance between excitation and inhibition (E/I) is a key characteristic of seizure generation in preclinical models of epilepsy. A non-invasive, in vivo estimation of E/I may serve as a diagnostic and treatment biomarker in epilepsy. Hurst exponent (H), a fractal-scaling metric derived from physiologic timeseries such as resting-state fMRI is a proxy of cortical E/I balance.1 Widespread reductions of H across the cortex have been reported in patients with temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE),2 however the test-retest reliability required for clinical translation as a biomarker remains untested. We examined the reproducibility of H at the individual-subject level using data collected by the Epilepsy Connectome Project (ECP),3 a high quality, multi-session, and highly characterized imaging dataset of TLE and healthy control (HC) participants in the United States. We also compared fMRI-derived H’s relation to key clinical features including seizure laterality, age of onset, illness duration, and presence of mesial temporal sclerosis (MTS).

Methods

Data from 78 adults with TLE and 70 healthy control (HC) participants in the ECP dataset were selected for this analysis. Laterality of seizure onset in TLE was determined clinically as left (n=21), right (n=57). Images were acquired on a 3T GE MR750 scanner: T1-weighted (MPRAGE; 0.8 mm isotropic); and rsfMRI (gradient-echo EPI; TR = 802 ms, TE = 33.5 ms, 2 mm isotropic, multiband factor=8). Each subject completed at least two resting state fMRI sessions, each consisting of four approximately 5-minute runs, with participants instructed to fixate on a crosshair. Images were preprocessed using fMRIPrep with standard steps including motion correction, susceptibility distortion correction, spatial normalization, and nuisance regression, including global and motion parameters. Resting BOLD time series were then extracted from 400 cortical regions defined by the Schaefer atlas4 plus 32 subcortical regions defined by the Melbourne atlas5.

The Hurst Exponent (H) was calculated using a discrete wavelet transform and fractionally integrated processes model using maximum likelihood estimation (bfn_mfin_ml MATLAB function; ‘haar’ filter), separately for each fMRI run and session, and subsequently averaged for each ROI and participant. For each ROI, a general linear model (GLM) tested for group differences in H controlling for age. Results are reported at an FDR-adjusted p<.01. Each participants’ whole-brain pattern of H across ROIs was also compared to the average pattern in the HC group using Pearson correlation to yield a “typicality” measure. Within the TLE group, relationships between H and age at disease onset, illness duration, laterality of seizure focus and presence of medial temporal lobe scarring (MTS) were then assessed with partial correlations and GLMs (controlling for age) for the typicality measure and cortical (averaged by network) and subcortical H.

Results

TLE and HC groups did not differ in biological sex or number of fMRI runs acquired but differed modestly in age (TLE mean = 39.4 years; HC mean = 35.3 years; p = .03). Age was therefore included as a covariate in all analyses. TLE subgroups did not differ by age, sex, age at onset, illness duration, or MTS status.

Test–Retest Reliability of H: We found high test–retest reliability, with whole-brain average H across all cortical ROIs showed a mean Pearson correlation of 0.61, within-session reliability r = 0.80 and across-session reliability r = 0.47 (Figure 1A). Similar reliability was observed at the individual network level. Importantly, comparable group-level results were obtained even when analyses were restricted to a single fMRI run.

Group Differences in H: we found significantly reduced H in TLE compared to HC across cortical regions (FDR p < .01), spanning multiple functional networks (Figures 1B, 1C). Significant reductions in subcortical regions were observed in the right anterior and posterior hippocampus, right dorsoposterior thalamus, right shell of the nucleus accumbens, right anterior globus pallidus, bilateral anterior and posterior putamen, and bilateral anterior and posterior caudate.

Changes in Whole-Brain Typicality and Clinical Associations: The whole-brain H pattern showed reduced typicality in TLE relative to HC (p < .001). Typicality was significantly lower in patients with left- compared to right-lateralized seizure focus (p < .001). This effect remained significant after controlling for age at onset and illness duration.

No significant effects of seizure laterality or MTS were observed for network-averaged cortical H. Age at disease onset and illness duration were not significantly associated with cortical H.

Age at onset was negatively correlated with H in several subcortical structures, including the right posterior globus pallidus, left anterior putamen, and left posterior caudate (all p < .05). Additionally, age showed a robust negative association with average cortical H in both TLE (r = –0.30, p = .006) and HC (r = –0.67, p < .001) groups.

Conclusion

This study independently replicates recent findings of widespread reductions in H in TLE, providing evidence for large-scale alterations in E/I balance. By leveraging deeply sampled, multi-session fMRI data, we further demonstrate that H exhibits high test–retest reliability across runs and sessions, supporting its potential as a stable individual-level biomarker. Some prior relationships between whole-brain H and clinical characteristics were not replicated, which could relate to cohort or imaging parameters differences, but warrant further investigation in multi-center cohorts [5].

Together, these results support the adoption of H as a reproducible and scalable resting-state fMRI marker of E/I in epilepsy. The strong age dependence of H across both patients and controls further emphasizes the importance of lifespan modeling in future applications. Future longitudinal studies should determine whether H predicts pharmacoresistance, surgical outcome, or disease progression, either alone or in combination with complementary structural and functional biomarkers.
