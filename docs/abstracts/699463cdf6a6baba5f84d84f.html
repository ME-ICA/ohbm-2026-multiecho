Introduction

Recovery after ischemic stroke is understood as a network-level process rather than a direct consequence of focal tissue loss1,2. Structural lesions disrupt white matter pathways and alter communication across distributed systems, leading to functional consequences beyond the infarct. Resting-state fMRI has characterized these changes, showing that alterations in large-scale networks relate to cognitive and functional outcomes. However, findings remain heterogeneous due to differences in acquisition strategies, preprocessing choices, lesion handling, and BOLD signal sensitivity3,4,5,6.

A key challenge in stroke fMRI is that BOLD fluctuations reflect both neural activity and vascular timing and reactivity, which are perturbed after stroke. Perfusion delays, impaired autoregulation, and small-vessel disease can introduce hemodynamic lag, causing apparent reductions in functional connectivity that may not reflect neural disconnection. These effects impact cognitive networks near watershed regions and peri-lesional tissue where vascular and neural processes interact.

Functional reorganization evolves over time. Early recovery shows diffuse network engagement, followed by progressive differentiation in patients with favourable outcomes. While conventional single-band (SB) fMRI may underestimate neuroplastic interactions, multi-band (MB) acceleration increases temporal sampling and may enhance sensitivity to low-amplitude coupling, though its value for lesion-centred analysis remains unclear4,5,6,7,8.

This study compared SB and MB resting-state fMRI within a lesion-centred framework. Functional and effective connectivity were assessed in lesion, peri-lesional, and disconnectome-defined regions across networks at 1, 3, and 6 months post-stroke. This work examines how acquisition strategy influences detection of network interactions to inform imaging biomarkers of recovery.

Methods

A dual-cohort longitudinal design was used to test how acquisition strategy influences lesion-centred connectivity estimates after stroke. The primary cohort used a conventional single-band (SB) resting-state EPI sequence on a 3T Philips Achieva (TR = 2.0 s; TE = 30 ms; voxel size ≈ 3×3×4 mm³; ~7 min/run; typically 2 runs). The secondary cohort used a multiband (MB) sequence on a 3T Philips Ingenia (TR = 1.4 s; TE = 30 ms; flip angle 69°; 2.5 mm isotropic; in-plane SENSE=2; MB factor=4; readout ~42 ms; ~7.2 min/run; typically 2 runs). Participants entered around 1 or 3 months post-stroke and were followed toward a common 6-month endpoint. Forty-three stroke patients were included (mean age 49±13 years; 36 male; 35 ischemic, 8 haemorrhagic). Imaging included 3D T1-weighted anatomy, 3D FLAIR, and rs-fMRI. Spin-echo EPI pairs with reversed phase encoding (AP/PA) were acquired and linked as IntendedFor to enable susceptibility distortion correction.

Lesions were delineated in native space using FLAIR with reference to diffusion/ADC and T1, and curated under neuroradiologist supervision9. Structural preprocessing applied N4 bias correction and brain extraction, with FLAIR rigid/affine registered to T1 and resampled to the T1 grid. For registration-sensitive steps, lesion-aware normalization was implemented using cost-function masking to avoid lesion-driven warping artefacts. Lesion maps were transformed to MNI space and used to compute lesion load and to derive lesion-centred ROIs. Concentric peri-lesional rings (2-4, 4-6, 6-8 mm) were generated to quantify distance dependent effects. Disconnectome probability maps were generated in MNI space using BCBtoolkit10, with tract probability thresholds p=0.3-0.7 to define “disconnection zones” and tract-level disconnection scores.

Resting-state preprocessing followed a custom Nipype workflow harmonized across SB and MB data, differing only in protocol-specific timing. Preprocessing included motion correction, slice-timing correction, and distortion correction using TOPUP-based field estimation from AP/PA spin-echo EPIs. BOLD images were co-registered to T1 with boundary based registration and normalized to MNI space using the lesion masked transforms, with single step resampling to limit interpolation blurring. Standard confounds were estimated (24-parameter motion model, WM/CSF signals, CompCor components, global signal, polynomial trends), and high-motion volumes were censored (FD>0.5 mm and DVARS outliers)11,12.

Given stroke-related vascular delay, hemodynamic lag was explicitly modelled. Voxelwise lag maps were estimated using rapidtide by lagged cross-correlation of each voxel with a gray-matter reference signal (±15 s window). ROI-level lag indices were extracted within lesions, rings, disconnectome zones, and canonical atlas regions. Lag regressors (and summary lag covariates) were appended to the nuisance model to reduce spurious connectivity driven by timing offsets rather than neural coupling12.

Connectivity and network metrics were computed in CONN (v23) using lesion, ring, and disconnectome informed ROIs alongside standard cortical/subcortical parcellations. After nuisance regression and band-pass filtering (0.008–0.09 Hz), ROI-to-ROI and seed-based functional connectivity were derived across canonical networks, and effective connectivity was estimated using CONN’s directional modelling13. Graph measures (degree/strength, clustering, global/local efficiency, participation) were computed on thresholded connectivity matrices for each subject and timepoint. Group contrasts (patients vs controls; 1M→3M→6M) were assessed with FDR correction (p<0.05). Results

Both single-band (SB) and multi-band (MB) cohorts showed consistent early (1 month) through late (6 months) recovery. Lesion-centred, peri-lesional, and disconnectome-derived regions exhibited temporal patterns of functional reorganization, showing progressive network changes across recovery phases.

At 1 month, both acquisition showed prominent lesion-centred hyperconnectivity. The SB cohort displayed diffuse co-activation involving salience, fronto-parietal, and visual networks, with broad peri-lesional coupling across 2–8 mm rings. Connectivity patterns showed low specificity, consistent with early compensatory recruitment. Directional analyses showed dominant effective connectivity from salience network to fronto-parietal regions, reflecting engagement of cognitive control systems.MB acquisitions at 1 month showed similar patterns but with enhanced resolution of short-range interactions. Stronger coupling occurred within 2-4 mm peri-lesional rings, linking salience, fronto-parietal, sensorimotor, and visual nodes. MB data showed greater sensitivity to lower-probability disconnectome pathways (p ≈ 0.03-0.05, FDR-corrected), suggesting earlier detection of lesion-network reorganization less apparent in SB data.

By 3 months, network organization became structured in both cohorts. SB showed connectivity consolidation within dorsal attention, salience, and language networks, with constrained peri-lesional activity aligned with lesion boundaries. Effective connectivity analyses showed hierarchical patterns, with salience-driven influences extending to fronto-parietal and language systems. Disconnectome coupling strength peaked, reflecting maximal interaction of structural disconnection and functional adaptation. MB acquisitions at 3 months refined these patterns. Multi-network integration occurred across salience, fronto-parietal, language, and sensorimotor systems, with stronger effective connectivity than SB data. Lesion, ring–disconnectome overlap remained robust, indicating MB improved sensitivity to coordinated interactions during reorganization.

At 6 months, differences between acquisition became apparent. In SB, functional connectivity persisted at low amplitude within sensorimotor and dorsal attention networks, with weak peri-lesional and high-probability disconnectome coupling. No clear hierarchical organization survived thresholding, indicating network stabilization without directional control. Connectivity patterns were coarse with reduced variance.

MB acquisitions at 6 months showed weak but consistent lesion-centred coupling linking salience, sensorimotor, and visual networks. Peri-lesional ring interactions remained detectable, and disconnectome associations extended to lower-probability pathways absent in SB data. Residual directional effective connectivity persisted in MB data, indicating fine-scale network control mechanisms beyond SB resolution.

Both cohorts showed common lesion-centred organization, early hyperconnectivity, mid-phase integration, and late stabilization. MB fMRI demonstrated superior sensitivity to short-range peri-lesional interactions, lower-probability disconnection pathways, and residual directional coupling. These findings show that while SB captures dominant recovery networks, MB reveals persistent fine-scale neuroplastic processes beyond conventional measures.

Conclusion

These results demonstrate that acquisition strategy shapes the observed trajectory of post-stroke network reorganization. Across both cohorts, lesion-centred functional connectivity followed a consistent temporal sequence, early coupling, mid-phase consolidation, and late-stage stabilization. However, the apparent endpoint of recovery differed by sampling approach. Conventional single-band (SB) fMRI reliably captured broad, large-scale network engagement particularly early salience/fronto-parietal recruitment and later sensorimotor–dorsal attention stabilization but showed reduced sensitivity to weak, spatially restricted interactions in the chronic phase. In contrast, multi-band (MB) fMRI by virtue of temporal sampling preserved detectability of low-amplitude coupling and residual directional influences at later timepoints, suggesting that late recovery contains structured control signals that can be missed when sampling is coarser.

The 1 month phase in both protocols was dominated by widespread compensatory recruitment involving salience, fronto-parietal, and visual/sensorimotor systems, together with broad peri-lesional ring coupling. This pattern is consistent with an early “macro-network” response in which control and sensory networks co-activate in a relatively non-specific manner. MB data did not fundamentally alter this early signature, but sharpened lesion-adjacent detection particularly within the 2–4 mm peri-lesional zone and increased sensitivity to lower-probability disconnection pathways. This suggests that MB may better resolve rapid or subtle lesion-centred interactions that occur near the boundary between damaged and viable tissue.

At 3 months, The strengthened alignment between structural disconnection and functional coupling, together with the emergence of hierarchical effective connectivity patterns, indicates a mid-phase stage of network consolidation. The increased concordance between lesion/disconnectome structure and functional organization supports the view that recovery is constrained by structural scaffolding, but expressed through network topology. The similarity between SB and MB at this phase also implies that the recovery signature is robust to acquisition strategy, while MB may refine weaker edges and improve detection.

The clearest divergence emerged at 6 months. SB data continued to show measurable functional connectivity, but primarily as low-amplitude coupling within sensorimotor and dorsal attention systems, with weak peri-lesional and high-probability disconnectome associations. Directional effective connectivity patterns did not survive thresholding at this stage, consistent with reduced dynamic variance and a stabilized network state. MB data, in contrast, retained weak but consistent lesion-centred coupling linking salience, sensorimotor, and visual networks, with detectable peri-lesional interactions and broader disconnectome involvement, including lower-probability pathways. The persistence of supra-threshold effective connectivity in MB suggests that a component of top-down modulation and fine-scale neuroplastic control continues into the chronic phase, but requires higher temporal sampling for reliable detection.

These findings support an acquisition-aware interpretation of stroke connectivity studies. Because BOLD measures are sensitive to vascular timing and post-stroke hemodynamics, differences in temporal sampling may influence whether late-stage coupling appears absent or below detectability thresholds. Within a lesion-centred framework integrating lesion cores, peri-lesional rings, and disconnectome zones, SB and MB are complementary - SB captures dominant macro-network recovery signatures, while MB increases sensitivity to subtle chronic-phase connectivity and residual directed influences. These results motivate protocol-specific benchmarks and harmonized lesion-aware pipelines when deriving connectivity-based biomarkers or planning longitudinal neuromodulation studies.
