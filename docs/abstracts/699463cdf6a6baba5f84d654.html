Introduction

Transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS) is a noninvasive neuromodulation technique that engages vagal afferent pathways and is thought to influence interoceptive processing. Prior work suggests that taVNS can modulate behavioral performance on interoceptive tasks1, alter cardiac activity2, and change cortical activity linked to cardiac signals3. However, taVNS effects on cardiac activity2 and treatment outcomes of interoception-related symptoms4 show substantial inter-individual variability, which limits generalizability and hinders the development of precision taVNS. Identifying neurophysiological biomarkers that capture this variability is therefore critical. Heartbeat-evoked potentials (HEPs), an EEG measure time-locked to cardiac activity, reflect cortical processing of cardiac afferent signals and may provide an informative marker of individual sensitivity to taVNS during interoceptive behavior.

In this study, we simultaneously recorded EEG and ECG from healthy adults during a heartbeat counting task (HCT). Following baseline HCT, participants completed three taVNS-HCT runs (Protocols A–C), with 120 s of taVNS delivered before each counting period to evaluate protocol-dependent behavioral and neurophysiological effects. We quantified individual differences in heartbeat counting performance and HEP features across protocols. Finally, we examined the association between baseline EEG markers (derived from HEP-related activity) and taVNS-related gains in heartbeat counting to identify candidate biomarkers relevant to precision taVNS.

Methods

Thirty-four healthy adults completed one baseline run of the heartbeat counting task (baseline-HCT) and three taVNS runs (taVNS-HCT) during simultaneous 32-channel EEG and single-lead ECG recordings. One participant was excluded due to abnormal trigger markers, leaving 33 participants for analysis. The experiment was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of National Cheng Kung University, and all participants provided written informed consent.

In each run, participants counted perceived heartbeats over six durations (21, 25, 33, 39, 47, and 55 s) presented in a randomly permuted order. During counting, participants maintained fixation on a cross displayed at the center of the screen. In the baseline-HCT run, each trial consisted of a 10-s preparation period, a heartbeat counting period, and a response period (Fig. 1(A)). A visual countdown preceded heartbeat counting, and an auditory beep indicated the end of the counting period. In the taVNS-HCT runs, each trial consisted of a 5-s rest period, 120 s of taVNS, a 5-s preparation period, a heartbeat counting period, and a response period (Fig. 1(B)). Auditory beeps were presented at the end of the taVNS period and at the end of the heartbeat counting period. 

Each taVNS-HCT run employed one of three stimulation protocols defined by stimulation location, frequency, and pulse width: Protocol A (tragus, 25 Hz, 250 µs), Protocol B (tragus, 25 Hz, 500 µs), and Protocol C (cymba conchae, 25 Hz, 250 µs). The order of protocols was randomized across participants. The interval between consecutive taVNS-HCT runs was 5 min to reduce carryover effects. 

Heartbeat counting accuracy for each trial was calculated using the Schandry formula and then averaged across trials to obtain a single interoceptive accuracy score (IAc) for each run. IAc was compared between the baseline run and each taVNS protocol (A–C) to assess protocol-dependent modulation and was also compared across runs to evaluate potential order effects using paired t-tests, followed by FDR correction. For each protocol, modulation efficacy was defined as the change in IAc relative to baseline: ΔIAcA = IAcA − IAcbaseline, ΔIAcB = IAcB − IAcbaseline, and ΔIAcC = IAcC − IAcbaseline. For each taVNS-HCT run, modulation efficacy was defined as the change in IAc relative to baseline: ΔIAcrun1 = IAcrun1 − IAcbaseline, ΔIAcrun2 = IAcrun2 − IAcbaseline, and ΔIAcrun3 = IAcrun3 − IAcbaseline. Participants with positive ΔIAc were classified as responders, whereas those with non-positive ΔIAc were classified as non-responders. To further characterize neural activity associated with modulation efficacy, participants were ranked by ΔIAc within each protocol, and the top 10 and bottom 10 participants were selected as the High-ΔIAc and Low-ΔIAc groups, respectively, for subsequent analyses.

Baseline HEPs were computed from the baseline-HCT run using EEG epochs extracted during the heartbeat counting phase. EEG was bandpass-filtered at 1–50 Hz and denoised using independent component analysis. ECG was bandpass-filtered at 0.5–50 Hz. R-peaks were detected using the Pan–Tompkins algorithm, followed by visual inspection and manual correction. EEG epochs were time-locked to R-peaks from −200 to 652 ms and baseline-corrected using the −200 to −100 ms interval. EEG and ECG preprocessing were performed using EEGLAB and HEPLAB.

For each taVNS protocol, baseline HEP amplitude was quantified as the mean EEG amplitude in the 350–500 ms window. For each protocol, regional baseline HEP amplitudes were compared between the High-ΔIAc and Low-ΔIAc groups using two-sample t-tests. Additionally, electrodes were grouped a priori into six scalp regions (Fig. 1(C)), and regional HEP amplitude was computed by averaging across electrodes within each region. Pearson correlations were computed between regional baseline HEP amplitude and ΔIAc. FDR correction across the six regions was applied to control for multiple comparisons.

Results

Interoceptive accuracy (IAc) increased significantly under all three taVNS protocols when 120-s taVNS was delivered immediately before each heartbeat counting period (pFDR < 0.05; ΔIAcA = 0.040±0.109, ΔIAcB = 0.045±0.116, ΔIAcC = 0.063±0.119; Fig. 2(A)). No significant differences in IAc were observed between protocols after FDR correction (pFDR > 0.05). When runs were examined irrespective of protocol, IAc in each of the three taVNS-HCT runs was also significantly higher than baseline (pFDR < 0.05; ΔIAcrun1 = 0.038±0.100, ΔIAcrun2 = 0.059±0.126, ΔIAcrun3 = 0.048±0.116; Fig. 2(B)). Direct comparisons among the three stimulation runs revealed no significant differences after FDR correction (pFDR > 0.05), suggesting that the improvements were not attributable to run order.

Substantial inter-individual variability in taVNS modulation efficacy was observed across protocols. The numbers of responders (ΔIAc > 0) were 18/33 (55%) for Protocol A, 18/33 (55%) for Protocol B, and 24/33 (73%) for Protocol C, indicating the highest responder rate for Protocol C. Across protocols, 12 participants (36%) showed improved IAc in all three conditions (consistent responders) and 6 participants (18%) showed decreased IAc in all three conditions (consistent non-responders). The remaining 15 participants (45%) exhibited mixed response patterns across protocols, implying that some individuals may benefit only under specific protocols.

In an initial channel-wise analysis, baseline HEP amplitudes within the frontal region showed larger values in the High-ΔIAc group than in the Low-ΔIAc group at multiple electrodes (puncorrected < 0.05; Fig. 2(C)). In the complementary ROI-level analysis, frontal ROI-averaged baseline HEP was positively associated with ΔIAcB (r = 0.415, puncorrected = 0.016) and ΔIAcC (r = 0.350, puncorrected = 0.046). These effects did not survive FDR correction. Therefore, we performed a focused analysis within the frontal ROI. Among the eight frontal electrodes, baseline HEP amplitude at Fz was positively correlated with modulation efficacy for all three protocols (ΔIAcA: r = 0.503, pFDR = 0.023; ΔIAcB: r = 0.593, pFDR = 0.002; and ΔIAcC: r = 0.541, pFDR = 0.009; Fig. 2(D)). Together, these findings indicate that baseline Fz HEP amplitude indexes individual differences in taVNS-related gains in heartbeat counting performance.

Conclusion

To our knowledge, this work is among the first to jointly examine stimulation-parameter effects, interoceptive behavioral performance, and neurophysiological markers of cardiac interoception within the same taVNS paradigm. Across healthy adults, taVNS delivered immediately before heartbeat counting significantly improved IAc across all three protocols relative to baseline, with no evidence that these gains were driven by run order. At the same time, modulation efficacy showed marked inter-individual heterogeneity, including a substantial subgroup exhibiting protocol-dependent response patterns—highlighting the need for biomarker-informed stratification in precision taVNS. Extending prior parameter-focused work that has largely relied on autonomic readouts such as heart rate variability2, we integrated behavioral outcomes with brain–heart coupling measures and found that baseline frontal EEG markers of cortical heartbeat processing (HEP) were linked to taVNS-related gains. In particular, baseline HEP amplitude at Fz indexed modulation efficacy across all protocols, supporting its potential utility as a candidate biomarker of individual susceptibility to taVNS-induced interoceptive enhancement. Future studies linking HEP features to vagus-nerve anatomy and computational models of vagus nerve stimulation5 may further advance mechanistic biomarkers and guide personalized selection or optimization of stimulation parameters.
