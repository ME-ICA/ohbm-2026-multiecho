Introduction

Epilepsy stands as one of the most prevalent and debilitating chronic neurological disorders globally, affecting an estimated 50 million individuals. Among those individuals, approximately 30% of patients develop drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE). Drug-resistant temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) is the most common form of focal epilepsy in adults and anterior temporal lobectomy (ATL) is the established gold standard surgical treatment for this condition. It yields significant clinical benefits, with sustained seizure freedom (Engel Class I outcome) ranging between 60% and 70% 1. Assessment of post-operative anatomy is important for clinical purposes in evaluating the residual tissue when evaluating seizure outcomes. Current clinical practice often relies on visual inspection, using 2D slices of the resection cavity.  From a research standpoint, evaluating the surgical lesion can both 1) help inform clinical outcomes and 2) serve as a source for lesion-symptom mapping approaches that can inform the cruciality of neuroanatomy for different brain functions. It is exceptionally challenging to conduct reliable, large-scale studies that accurately link the precise volume and location of the resection cavity—the anatomical lesion—to the long-term clinical symptom (seizure and cognitive outcomes). This lack of precision hinders the crucial work of lesion-symptom mapping in surgical epilepsy 2. 

We propose a novel, data-driven solution to overcome this obstacle, using Domain Randomization with a deep learning framework. Our objective is to train on a UNet model utilizing DR on synthetically generated brain MRI data containing various simulated ATL resection cavities. The final model will be designed to accurately and automatically segment and quantify the surgical resection mask directly from standard chronic post-operative T1-weighted MRI scans. 

Methods

Our approach leverages the Human Connectome Project (HCP) healthy young adult dataset, which provides whole-head T1-weighted MRI scans with corresponding segmentations into 104 anatomical regions. To simplify the segmentation target for the simulated resection, these 104 labels are consolidated into four primary classes: Left Temporal Lobe, Right Temporal Lobe, White Matter (WM), and General Grey Matter (GM). This reduction minimizes label complexity while preserving anatomically relevant distinctions for ATL simulation

Simulated Resection

The simulation begins by probabilistically selecting either the left or right temporal lobe for simulated resection. To standardize the surgical approach, we use Principal Component Analysis (PCA) applied to the voxel coordinates of the selected temporal lobe segmentation mask to determine its geometric center and axes, providing a reproducible anatomical reference frame. The anterior-most extent along the principal axis is identified and used as a surgical landmark

Simulated Anterior Temporal Lobectomy (ATL) is initiated by applying a spherical resection mask. This mask is centered relative to the anterior temporal pole landmark, ensuring the removal encompasses the key mesial temporal structures (e.g., hippocampus, amygdala) that form the core anatomical contrasts typically targeted in a clinical ATL. The resected structure is then replaced into the corresponding head scan with a unique label imitating the post-operative cavity.

Domain Randomization and Data Generation

The modified head scan is then passed through the SynthSeg data generator 3. This process employs Domain Randomization (DR), introducing randomized photometric, and geometric perturbations, with each simulated resection populating 1000 synthetic T1-weighted MRI scans and their corresponding resection mask labels, ensuring the trained model generalizes robustly to the variability encountered in real-world clinical imaging. 

Model Training

The model is trained using a standard 3D UNet architecture with Nchannel = 1 input and Nlabels = 2 output (resection cavity and background). Combined Dice and cross-entropy loss function is used. The model is trained solely on synthetic data 4. We use the Adam optimizer with a OneCycleLR scheduler for dynamic learning rate management, which is known to accelerate convergence. The primary evaluation metric is the Macro Dice Score computed across all classes

Conclusion

This study successfully validates a novel approach: training a robust 3D UNet model for post-operative brain cavity segmentation entirely on synthetic data. Leveraging Domain Randomization (DR) within a pipeline featuring a geometrically constrained PCA-based simulation of anterior temporal lobectomy, our model achieves high-quality segmentation when tested on real clinical MRI data.

The resulting tool provides a cost-effective and scalable solution for quantitative post-operative studies, facilitating the standardized analysis of resection delineation for the evaluation of surgical lesions with patient outcomes. Future work will focus on enhancing segmentation detail to achieve granular anatomical precision of surgical mask boundaries for precise LSM.
