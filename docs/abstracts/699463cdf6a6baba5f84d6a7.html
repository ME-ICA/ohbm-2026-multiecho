Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a group of heterogeneous neurodevelopmental conditions. A large body of studies have indicated that individuals on the autism spectrum deviate from typical neurodevelopmental programs. Structural covariation is a remarkable phenomenon during typical development, subserving particular behavioural or cognitive functions. However, how the autistic brains deviate from typical structural covariation norms and the nature of their heterogeneous patterns remain unclear.

Methods

Here, we first employed a regression model to define structural covariance norms between each pair of cortical regions. Then, we applied a signed anatomical imbalance (AMI) mapping to 266 individuals on the autism spectrum and 341 typically developings to measure the individual deviations from structural covariation norms. Subsequently, we utilized a Bayesian model to decompose altered AMI patterns in ASD into latent factors and their corresponding loadings. Finally, a post hoc analysis linked the latent factors to different clinical phenotypes and neurotransmitters.

Results

AMI mapping revealed that ASD exhibited a decreased theoretical “rate” and increased theoretical “starting point” of development in morphological integration. In the early stage, all large-scale networks contained the vertex with atypical AMI, and atypical regions were widely detected in default mode network and somatomotor network across a longer neurodevelopmental period (6-36 yrs). Bayesian analysis yielded three latent factors with dissociable AMI patterns. Most ASD expressed more than one factor with varying degrees. Factor 1 was associated with social responsiveness motivation, factor 2 was associated with social-communication and intelligence, factor 3 was related to restricted, and repetitive behavior. Furthermore, we found that factor 1 could be predicted using neurotransmitters, and the acetylcholine, cannabinoid, and opioid systems significantly contributed to this factor.

Conclusion

In summary, our study investigated the altered AMI and its heterogeneous pattern in ASD. Individuals on the autism spectrum exhibited notable alterations in AMI, especially in DMN and SMN. Heterogeneous patterns of AMI could be further anchored to three distinct latent factors. Each individual with ASD expressed these factors to varying degrees, and each factor was aligned with specific clinical and neurobiological underpinnings. Our results underscore the altered AMI and its interindividual variation in ASD, providing a framework to unravel the disorder's complexity and advance the understanding of its pathophysiological mechanisms.
