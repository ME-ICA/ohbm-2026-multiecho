Introduction

Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

            Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is categorized by field strength into Low-Field (LF, <1.5T), High-Field (HF, 1.5/3.0T), and Ultra-High-Field (UHF, ≥7T). Compared to standard HF systems, UHF offers superior Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR), Contrast-to-Noise Ratio (CNR), and spatial resolution . However, UHF devices are prohibitively expensive and rare; for instance, Taiwan currently lacks 7T scanners for human clinical use. Consequently, this study aims to bridge this hardware gap by generating 7T-quality UHF images directly from widely available 3T HF inputs.

The Necessity of Unpaired Image Translation

         Conventional deep learning methods for super-resolution typically rely on "paired" data, which is resource-intensive and difficult to align in clinical practice. To address this, we focus on "unpaired" image translation, learning robust mappings between independent image distributions without requiring pixel-wise correspondence.

Technical Innovation: Introducing Diffusion Models

         Unlike Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), which often suffer from training instability and artifacts, Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPMs) provide superior stability and high-fidelity generation. We propose an innovative cross-axial training strategy based on an open-source diffusion framework to effectively handle unpaired medical data.

Research Objectives

            The primary objective of this study is to bridge the quality disparity between HF and UHF MRI using unpaired data. By leveraging a stable diffusion-based architecture and a novel cross-axial data integration strategy, we aim to overcome the limitations of traditional GANs and produce anatomically accurate 7T images from 3T sources. The efficacy of the proposed method will be rigorously validated through quantitative metrics and clinical qualitative assessments, providing a feasible solution for low-cost medical imaging upgrades.

Methods

Datasets

            To ensure model generalization, we utilized unpaired training data to learn the mapping between independent 3T and 7T domains. The 3T images were sourced from the Human Connectome Project (HCP) 1, a large-scale database providing diverse T1-weighted images (N=75) that capture universal 3T features. The 7T images were derived from the AHEAD 2 and CEREBRUM 3 projects (N=247), which offer high-resolution scans capable of visualizing fine structures like cortical layers. These independent datasets capture the distinct distributions of different scanning protocols without subject overlap.

            For quantitative quality assessment, we employed paired testing data provided by the Institute of Neuroscience and Medicine, Forschungszentrum Juelich. This dataset consists of 4 pairs of 3T/7T MRI scans acquired from the same subjects. These matched pairs serve as the ground truth for calculating rigorous performance metrics.

Preprocessing

            High-quality preprocessing is crucial for ensuring spatial and intensity consistency in MRI images, which serves as the foundation for successful cross-field strength translation. To avoid inherent discrepancies between images from different datasets, all training and testing data undergo the following standardization steps: Reorientation to RAS+ Coordinate System, Skull Stripping, 3T/7T Image Registration (only for test data), Outlier Removal and Intensity Normalization, and 3D to 2D Slice Segmentation.

Model Architecture

            Diffusion models operate through a Forward Process, which adds Gaussian noise to an image until it degrades to pure noise, and a Reverse Process, where the model learns to denoise and restore the original image. This study employs the ContourDiff model 4, which integrates diffusion generation with structural edge constraints. The noise predictor uses an improved U-Net architecture with skip connections to retain both low-level details and high-level semantics. ContourDiff innovatively introduces edge information as a condition; a structural edge map extracted via a Canny detector is fed into the U-Net encoder. This constraint ensures that the generated 7T images maintain the anatomical contours of the 3T input while learning the target domain's high-frequency details.

            Originally designed for cross-modality (CT-to-MRI) tasks, we adapted ContourDiff for cross-field strength (3T-to-7T) translation. Since the model operates on 2D slices, training on a single axis could ignore 3D spatial continuity. To overcome this, we implemented a cross-axial training strategy. Independent models were trained for Axial, Coronal, and Sagittal planes . During testing, the 3T input is processed through these three models, and the resulting slices are reassembled into a 3D volume. This approach integrates structural information from all directions, significantly enhancing isotropy and anatomical realism.

Training Strategy and Loss Function

            Training utilized unpaired 3T and 7T data with the AdamW optimizer. Parameters were set to a learning rate of 1×10−4, batch size of 16, and 2000 epochs, executed on high-performance Nvidia GPUs (RTX 3090/H100/A100). We employed the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss function to minimize the difference between the noise predicted by the U-Net and the actual added noise, which is the standard objective for training diffusion models to accurately strip noise at any time step.

Evaluation Metrics

            To quantify generation quality, we calculated Mean Squared Error (MSE), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), SNR, and CNR on the four paired test datasets provided by Juelich Research Center. MSE, PSNR, and SSIM were computed against Ground Truth to assess fidelity. The medical-specific metrics, SNR and CNR, were calculated within Regions of Interest (ROI)—specifically the Thalamus (gray matter) and Corticospinal Tract (white matter)—to evaluate signal enhancement and tissue contrast in critical structures.

Results

Qualitative Evaluation of Single-Axial vs. Multi-Axial Training

         We evaluated the cross-axial training strategy by comparing independent axial model outputs against their integrated average. First, we analyzed the three independently trained 2D ContourDiff models. While all successfully transformed 3T structural information into 7T-like textures, reassembling these 2D slices into 3D volumes revealed pronounced layered artifacts, particularly in planes perpendicular to the training axis. This confirms that while 2D diffusion models excel at intra-slice details, they lack constraints for 3D spatial continuity, leading to inter-slice contrast inconsistencies.

         To address this, we averaged the outputs of the three axial models. Qualitative observations confirmed that averaging partially ameliorated the layered artifacts, improving visual smoothness. However, residual checkerboard artifacts remained, indicating that simple averaging is insufficient to fundamentally correct the intensity distribution errors caused by independent 2D generation.

Effectiveness Analysis of Histogram Matching Post-Processing

         To eliminate layered artifacts stemming from global intensity differences, we applied histogram matching post-processing. Using the central slice of each axis as a reference target, all other slices were matched to unify contrast and intensity distribution. Qualitative comparisons showed that histogram matching successfully corrected slice contrast. When reassembled into 3D, the layered artifacts on planes perpendicular to the training axis were significantly reduced. This confirms histogram matching as a necessary step for resolving 3D discontinuity. Finally, the matched outputs from the three axes were averaged to form the final generated image.

Performance on Medical-Specific Metrics: SNR and CNR

         We analyzed SNR and CNR to evaluate the diagnostic value of the final generated images. Intensity was measured in the Thalamus (gray matter) and standard deviation in the Corticospinal Tract (white matter). The Synthesized 7T image achieved an SNR of 24.89 ± 1.87, surpassing the input 3T SNR (20.30 ± 2.72), indicating superior detail representation and noise suppression.

         Crucially, the synthesized 7T CNR reached 7.47 ± 1.59. This value significantly outperforms the original 3T CNR (1.52 ± 0.38) and even surpasses the 7T ground truth CNR (4.53 ± 0.93) and all other datasets (HCP3T: 2.25 ± 0.59). This demonstrates the method's advantage in tissue contrast enhancement, which has significant implications for distinguishing between tissues.

Quantitative Comparison with the WATNet Model 5

         We quantitatively compared our method with the WATNet model using PSNR, SSIM, and MSE. Our method outperformed WATNet on pixel-error-based metrics: PSNR reached 21.8618 (vs. WATNet's 21.7433) and MSE decreased to 0.0085 (vs. 0.0089). These results demonstrate the superior fidelity of the diffusion-based approach.

         Regarding SSIM, our method recorded 0.8873, slightly lower than WATNet's 0.8975. Despite this, qualitative comparisons revealed that our method exhibits better structural consistency and overall image quality, successfully reducing the visible artifacts present in WATNet outputs while maintaining structural realism.

Conclusion

Summary of Core Research and Technical Contributions

         This study successfully implemented an innovative unpaired image translation framework for high-quality 3T to 7T MRI synthesis. Utilizing the open-source ContourDiff diffusion model, we overcame the challenge of acquiring large-scale paired data. The core innovation lies in integrating a cross-axial training strategy with histogram matching post-processing. By training independent axial models and unifying slice contrast during inference, we effectively addressed the spatial continuity issues inherent in 2D generative models. This approach fundamentally eliminated layered artifacts observed in 3D reconstruction, significantly improving the anatomical realism of the synthesized images.

Discussion of Results and Trade-off Analysis

         Quantitative results demonstrate the robustness of our strategy. Our method outperformed the baseline WATNet on pixel-error metrics, achieving a PSNR of 21.8618 and MSE of 0.0085. Crucially, medical-specific metrics revealed that the Synthesized 7T image achieved an SNR of 24.89±1.87, superior to the input 3T image (20.30±2.72). The CNR reached 7.47±1.59, surpassing even the real 7T ground truth (4.53±0.93), indicating successful optimization of inter-tissue contrast valuable for lesion detection.

         Although our SSIM (0.8873) is slightly lower than WATNet’s (0.8975), this represents a strategic trade-off. We hypothesize that histogram matching, while eliminating artifacts, introduced minor global brightness shifts affecting the SSIM luminance term. However, in clinical contexts, structural realism and artifact suppression are prioritized over minor luminance deviations. The results confirm that our workflow effectively compensates for the lack of Z-axis constraints in 2D diffusion models, prioritizing structural continuity.

Theoretical Contributions, Application Value, and Impact

         Theoretically, this study demonstrates the potential of diffusion models in unpaired cross-field translation. Our proposed cross-axial strategy provides a standardized solution for 3D medical image processing based on 2D slices, enhancing isotropy. practically, the realized 3T to 7T synthesis offers a cost-effective virtual ultra-high-field data source, accelerating neuroscience research. The significant enhancement in CNR directly improves tissue distinguishability, promoting the democratization of high-end MRI diagnostic technology.

Conclusion and Future Work

         This study establishes a new benchmark for diffusion-based unpaired MRI synthesis, effectively resolving 3D structural discontinuities. Future research should focus on three directions: First, developing computationally optimized native 3D diffusion models to learn spatial continuity directly, eliminating the need for post-processing. Second, integrating fast samplers (e.g., DDIM) or Knowledge Distillation to reduce inference latency for real-time applications. Finally, expanding the framework to other modalities or field strengths (e.g., 1.5T to 3.0T) to further verify generalization. This work provides a solid foundation and technical guidance for future intelligent medical imaging systems.
