Introduction

Transcranial laser stimulation (tLS) is an emerging neuromodulation technique that delivers near-infrared light to modulate neural activity[1,2] . However, the immediate, delayed, and cumulative effects of repeated tLS sessions, as well as their propagation beyond the stimulated cortical site, remain poorly characterized[3,4]. Here, we used densely sampled functional MRI to examine neural responses to 11 consecutive days of 1064-nm tLS delivered to the right prefrontal cortex, focusing on the temporal dynamics and network-level propagation of tLS-induced modulation.

Methods

A total of 20 healthy participants (age range: 18.08–31.44 years, 9 males) were recruited. Participants received 11 consecutive days of transcranial laser stimulation(Figure 1A). On each day, 1064-nm tLS was delivered to the right prefrontal cortex for 12 minutes (250 mW/cm²). Each participant underwent simultaneous MRI–tLS acquisition on a randomly selected subset of at least five stimulation days, with functional MRI data collected in pre–during–post tLS phases on each imaging day(Figure 1B).

Functional MRI data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep and further processed with xcp-d, from which the amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF) was derived and used as the primary index of brain activity in all subsequent analyses[5]. Primary analyses was focused on the dorsal attention (DA) network.

To characterize tLS effects across timescales, immediate effects were defined as within-session changes from pre- to post-stimulation (post − pre), delayed effects as changes from post-stimulation to the next day’s pre-stimulation (next-pre − this-post), and cumulative effects as the effect of stimulation day across the 11-day intervention(Figure 1B).

All tLS-induced ALFF effects were estimated using linear mixed-effects models, with stimulation day and effect type as fixed effects and subject as a random effect. To assess network-level propagation, functional connectivity maps seeded from the stimulation target and corresponding structural connectivity maps were computed. At the group level, regression analyses compared the predictive power of functional and structural connectivity for the spatial distribution of tLS-induced modulation effects(Figure 2A). At the individual level, functional connectivity between the stimulation target and parietal DA subregions was related to individual differences in immediate ALFF modulation(Figure 2C).

Results

A single 12-minute session of tLS induced a significant immediate reduction of ALFF throughout the dorsal attention  network (β = −0.014, t = −3.525, p < 0.001; Figure 1C), followed by a robust post-stimulation rebound (β = 0.016, t = 3.750, p< 0.001; Figure 1C). In contrast, long-term cumulative effects across the 11-day stimulation course were spatially confined to parietal subregions of the DA network, which exhibited a significant increase in ALFF as a function of stimulation day (β = 0.002, t = 2.919, p = 0.003; Figure 1F).

Network-coupling analyses further showed that the whole-brain functional connectivity map, after accounting for structural connectivity and inter-regional distance, accounted for a meaningful proportion of variance in the spatial distribution of both immediate and delayed ALFF modulation effects at the group level (immediate: R² = 0.051, p = 0.013; delayed: R² = 0.042, p = 0.021; Figure 2B), whereas structural connectivity did not exhibit predictive power. At the individual level, stronger functional connectivity between the stimulation site and parietal DA subregions was associated with larger immediate ALFF reductions (β = −0.141, FDR = 0.001; Figure 2C).

Conclusion

Taken together, our findings demonstrate that transcranial laser stimulation elicits robust changes in dorsal attention network activity across distinct temporal phases—including immediate, delayed, and sustained effects—that extend beyond the stimulation site. These effects are constrained by intrinsic functional connectivity, underscoring a network-level framework for understanding tLS-evoked brain dynamics and informing the design of tLS stimulation protocols.
