Introduction

Iron and myelin are key determinants of cortical microstructure and both influence MRI contrast.1,2 Myelin supports rapid and synchronous conduction across long-range pathways, and non-heme iron contributes to oxidative metabolism, neurotransmitter synthesis, and mitochondrial function while also participating in myelin production. Both postmortem and quantitative MRI studies suggest that iron and myelin are co-distributed in the cortex1,3, following the known sensory-to-association cortical hierarchy. However, most prior work has focused on high-field acquisitions or voxel-based analyses. In comparison, cortical surface-based mapping enables precise anatomical localization and depth profiling, which are essential for evaluating the laminar organization of iron and myelin in the cortex.

Several MRI methods have been proposed to date to estimate iron and myelin content in the cortex, including R2* mapping and T1w/T2w ratio. Effective transverse relaxation rate (R2*) is a quantitative property sensitive to iron that has been validated against histology.2 T1w/T2w ratio is a widely used semi-quantitative index of cortical myelin that captures known variations in myeloarchitecture4. Despite the biological interdependence between iron and myelin, direct surface-based comparisons between MRI markers of these properties in the cortex remain limited, particularly at 3T. Here, we evaluated spatial and depth-wise agreement between cortical R2* and T1w/T2w ratio in healthy adults at 3T. We hypothesized that both measures would exhibit similar regional and laminar patterns, resulting in significant positive correlations across cortical regions and along the cortical hierarchy.

Methods

Twenty-six healthy adults were scanned on a 3T Siemens Prisma with a 32-channel head coil. T1-weighted (MPRAGE, 0.8 mm isotropic, TR/TE/TI=2400/2.24/1060 ms, flip angle=8°) and T2-weighted (T2-SPACE sequence, 0.8mm isotropic, TR/TE=3200/563 ms) images were acquired, along with multi-echo gradient echo (mGRE) data (1.3mm isotropic resolution, 14 echoes, TE=1.90-32.98ms, echo spacing=2.39ms, TR=92ms, and flip angle=15o). 

T1w and T2w images were preprocessed using FreeSurfer, FSL, and the Human Connectome Project PreFreeSurfer pipeline to generate T1w/T2w maps and reconstruct cortical surfaces. mGRE images were denoised and Gibbs-ringing corrected using FSL. R2* maps were fitted voxel-wise to a mono-exponential decay function using the magnitude images and registered to each subject’s T1w image. 

For regional analysis, mean R2* and T1w/T2w were extracted from 68 cortical regions according to the Desikan-Kiliany atlas. For vertex-wise analysis, both measures were sampled using FreeSurfer mri_vol2surf at 50% cortical depth to minimize partial-volume contamination from CSF and white matter. Subject-space surface maps were resampled to the fsaverage template for inter-subject alignment, and vertex-wise group means were computed in MATLAB and visualized in Freeview. 

To assess intracortical variation, R2* and T1w/T2w were sampled across 11 uniformly spaced surfaces (1-99% cortical thickness, in 10% increments).5 Mean cortical depth profiles were constructed for the bilateral precentral and rostral middle frontal gyri. The precentral gyrus was selected for its high myelin and iron content, established by histology and also reflected in our vertex-wise maps. Conversely, the rostral middle frontal gyrus was included to represent a region with comparatively low myelin and iron content. Pearson’s correlation was used to assess the relationships between regional mean R2* and T1w/T2w values.

Results

Vertex-wise mean R2* (Figure 1A) and T1w/T2w (Figure 1B) maps show a high degree of similarity with higher values noted in the precentral gyri and auditory-visual cortices. The regional mean R2* and T1w/T2w values across cortical regions are significantly correlated in both hemispheres (left: R=0.42, p=0.025; right: R=0.54, p=0.002). The cortical profiles (Figure 2) show that both R2* and T1w/T2w increase with cortical depth, consistent with higher iron and myelin content toward the grey-white matter boundary. Precentral gyri (Figure 2A) exhibited steeper, more linear depth-wise increases with lower inter-subject variability, whereas the rostral middle frontal gyri (Figure 2B) showed more flattened profiles close to the pial surface and greater variability across individuals.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that R2* and T1w/T2w ratio exhibit converging regional and laminar patterns across the cortical surface, reinforcing their shared sensitivity to underlying microstructure. Strong correspondence in motor, auditory, and visual cortices align with postmortem evidence of dense intracortical myelination and higher iron concentrations. In contrast, weaker correspondence in heteromodal frontal regions likely reflects their lower baseline iron and myelin content, a pattern also visible in the vertex-wise maps. Together, these spatial distributions recapitulate the well-established cortical hierarchy, extending from early sensory to association cortex. 

Depth-dependent increases in both R2* and T1w/T2w follow expected laminar gradients, with higher values near the grey-white boundary consistent with denser myelin, increased axonal packing, and greater iron-rich oligodendrocyte populations. The steeper, more linear depth profiles in the precentral gyrus, coupled with low intersubject variability, reflect the dense intracortical myelin and iron typical of early sensory-motor cortex, whereas the flatter and more variable profiles in the rostral middle frontal regions are consistent with the lower myelin and iron density characteristic of higher-order association cortex. Notably, the laminar patterns observed here at 3T also closely match those previously described at ultra-high field, suggesting that cortical microstructural gradients can be reliably captured at clinically available field strengths. This enhances the translational relevance of this surface-based cortical iron and myelin mapping approach.

Surface-based sampling improves anatomical precision and reduces partial-volume effects compared to voxel-based analyses, enabling more accurate assessment of laminar trends. While R2* and T1w/T2w are usually interpreted as proxies for iron and myelin content, respectively, we note that they may be sensitive to both, along with other tissue microstructural properties. Future studies incorporating additional quantitative metrics of iron and myelin, such as magnetization transfer or quantitative susceptibility mapping, may help disentangle their contributions to the metrics examined here and further clarify the relationship between iron and myelin in the cortex. 

Extending these approaches to developmental, aging, or clinical populations may further reveal how iron-myelin coupling varies across the lifespan or may become disrupted in disease. Overall, these findings highlight that R2* and T1w/T2w exhibit converging regional and laminar organization in healthy cortex, reflecting their shared sensitivity to microstructural features. Their correspondence supports complementary use of iron- and myelin-sensitive MRI contrasts for noninvasive surface-based mapping of cortical architecture.
