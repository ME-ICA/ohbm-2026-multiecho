Introduction

Non-invasive stimulation of the human hippocampal network using, for example, transcranial magnetic stimulation, holds the promise of maintaining memory function 1–7, an unmet need in the management of neurodegenerative diseases. Recent studies suggest that better neuromodulatory outcomes can be obtained by tailoring the stimulation targets using personalized fMRI  2,8. Using the data from the Human Connectome Project 9 , we previously reported the coordinates and distributions of cortical nodes, including the medial prefrontal cortex and posterior parietal cortex in the hippocampal network 10. However, these data were derived from healthy young adults. Males and females both decline in memory similarly with age 11–16. A higher incidence of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias has been reported in women, especially after the age of 80 years 17–21 . However, how age and gender affect the hippocampal network coordinates remains elusive.

    Here we extended the exploration of the human hippocampal network by first examining the difference between male and female individuals. We then studied how hippocampal network features vary with age. These results are instrumental in tuning the stimulation targets to maximize the neuromodulatory effect and reliability. 

Methods

The resting-state fMRI data for 1,133 young adults were downloaded from the Human Connectome Project (HCP-YA) database. We separately calculated functional connectivity measures for male and female participants to explore the gender difference. Separate resting-state fMRI data for 725 adults between 36 and 100 years old were also obtained from the Human Connectome Project aging (HCP-A) database 22. Functional connectivity measures were separately calculated for participants younger than 50 years old and older than 60 years old to study aging effects.

    The hippocampus was automatically segmented from an individual’s structural MRI using FreeSurfer23. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated and transformed to Z scores using Fisher’s transformation. Group-level functional connectivity was calculated by testing if the Z score was non-zero at each image voxel across all participants. Bonferroni correction was applied to suppress the inflation of type-I error in multiple comparisons. For each individual, we calculated the centroids of the functional connectivity to the hippocampus at the medial frontal lobe and parietal lobe. Two targets were calculated at each region: the centroid of the group-level functional connectivity (“grp”) and the average of individuals’ functional connectivity centroids (“ind”). Distances from individuals’ functional connectivity centroids to both targets were compared by two-sample t-test. Coordinates of individuals’ functional connectivity centroids between groups were compared with Hotelling’s t-squared statistics 24.

Results

Figure 1 shows the group average of the mPFC and PPC functionally connected to the hippocampus for male and female participants. Centroids of the functional connectivity at the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) and lateral parietal cortex (PPC) of “grp” and “ind” groups were shown. No significant difference in the coordinate at PPC and mPFC between males and females was found. The coordinates between older (60+ y.o.) and younger (50- y.o.) individuals show no significant differences at mPFC and PPC in either grp or ind targets. The MNI coordinates of functional connectivity targets and metrics representing the dispersion of individuals’ functional connectivity centroids at mPFC and PPC are avaiable at https://github.com/fahsuanlin/hippocampal_network_for_TMS. Distributions of individuals’ centroids to grp and ind targets are shown at https://github.com/fahsuanlin/hippocampal_network_for_TMS. The distance of individual’s left PPC functional connectivity centroids to the grp target was significantly different between male and female participants. Between age groups, the distance of individual’s left and right mPFC functional connectivity centroids to the grp target was significantly different. However, this difference was only significant between age groups with respect to the left mPFC ind target.

Conclusion

We examined the age and gender difference in the cortical nodes of the hippocampal network from two databases with large cohorts. Briefly, no significant difference in the functional connectivity target coordinates at mPFC and PPC between genders and age groups, regardless of the targets derived from group-level analysis or the average of individual analysis. However, some significant differences were found at the distance between the individual’s functional connectivity centroids to the cortical targets. The distance between an individual’s functional centroid and the ind target was smaller than the grp target, suggesting the value of using ind target for neuromodulation when an individual’s fMRI is not available. This is particularly important in optimizing the TMS effect, because the induced magnetic field strength drops to 50 % or less at 5mm from the target 25.
