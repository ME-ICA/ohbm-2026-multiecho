Introduction

Cognitive flexibility in volatile environments depends on using prediction errors (PEs) to revise internal rules, yet it remains unclear how PE signals reshape distributed neural representations on a trial-by-trial timescale. Prior work in probabilistic reversal learning suggests that lateral orbitofrontal cortex (lOFC) rapidly encodes outcome information immediately after a reversal.^1 Here we tested whether PE-related ventral striatal outcome signals predict local multivariate pattern change in lOFC specifically during the early post-reversal adaptation period compared with a stable pre-reversal period.

Methods

We analyzed human fMRI data acquired during a probabilistic reversal learning task with alternating stable and volatile epochs Task phases were defined as learning-naïve, learning-expert, reversal-naïve, and reversal-expert following the original phase segmentation. Choice behavior was fit per participant with a two-parameter Q-learning model, yielding signed trial-wise prediction errors. Whole-brain general linear models modeled outcome events with parametric modulation by signed prediction errors to identify outcome-related responses, with right ventral striatum selected as the primary seed region reflecting prediction error signals. To quantify representational updating at cue time, we estimated single-trial cue responses using a least-squares-separate approach and computed whole-brain searchlight-based multivoxel pattern change between adjacent trials as the Fisher-z transformed correlation distance within a local spherical neighborhood constrained to a group gray-matter mask.^2,3 For each phase, trial-wise ventral striatal outcome responses were regressed against trial-wise pattern-change maps to produce voxelwise coupling maps. In the reversal-naïve phase, trials were further divided by prediction error sign to test valence-specific effects.

Results

Pattern change contrasts revealed larger representational updates immediately after reversals compared with stable learning, with prominent effects in medial temporal lobe regions encompassing the hippocampus and amygdala, consistent with rapid representational reconfiguration following rule reversals.^4 Using right ventral striatal outcome responses as a trial-wise regressor of whole-brain pattern change, no significant ventral striatal–orbitofrontal coupling was observed during the learning-expert phase. In contrast, the reversal-naïve phase was characterized by a selective negative coupling between ventral striatal signals and multivariate pattern change in right lateral orbitofrontal cortex.^5 Further valence-specific analyses revealed that this orbitofrontal effect was driven by negative prediction errors: during reversal-naïve trials with negative prediction errors, stronger negative prediction errors predicted greater orbitofrontal representational updating. No corresponding orbitofrontal effect was observed for positive prediction errors, which instead showed associations with pattern change in anterior prefrontal and parietal regions.

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate a phase- and valence-specific relationship between negative prediction errors and representational updating in lateral orbitofrontal cortex during early post-reversal adaptation. Together with enhanced hippocampal and amygdala pattern reconfiguration following reversals, the results support a distributed mechanism in which negative outcomes selectively gate orbitofrontal rule revision under uncertainty, clarifying how prediction error valence shapes cortical representational dynamics to support flexible decision making in changing environments.
