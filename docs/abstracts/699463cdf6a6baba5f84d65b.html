Introduction

Introduction:

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder primarily associated with dopaminergic neuronal loss in the nigrostriatal pathway1, resulting in motor impairments such as bradykinesia, gait abnormalities, and postural instability, along with emerging cognitive and affective dysfunctions2. Beyond dopaminergic degeneration, growing evidence highlights the involvement of cortical regions, altered energy metabolism, oxidative stress, and membrane dysfunction in the early stages of the disease. Identifying sensitive, non-invasive biomarkers that capture these early neurometabolic changes and their relationship with functional decline remains critical for improving disease monitoring and therapeutic evaluation. Proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy (¹H-MRS)3 enables in vivo assessment of brain metabolites reflecting neuronal integrity, neurotransmission, oxidative balance, and bioenergetics, while NMR-based serum metabolomics provides complementary insights into systemic metabolic alterations associated with central pathology. In this study, we aimed to characterize early cortico-striatal neurometabolic changes and their association with motor and cognitive deficits in a bilateral 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA) rat model of PD by integrating longitudinal behavioural testing, in vivo brain ¹H-MRS, and serum ¹H-NMR metabolomics.4

Methods

Twelve adult male Wistar rats (260–280 g) were randomly assigned to a PD group (n = 6) or a sham group (n = 6). All animals underwent baseline behavioural testing, MRI/MRS acquisition, and blood sampling before surgery. 

PD was induced by bilateral stereotaxic infusion of 6-OHDA (7.5 µg/µL in 0.2% ascorbate-saline; 2 µL per site at 1 µL/min) into the striatum (AP +1.5 mm, ML ±3.0 mm, DV −6.0 mm). Desipramine (20 mg/kg, i.p.) was administered 30 minutes before surgery to protect noradrenergic neurons. Sham animals received vehicle injections under identical surgical conditions. 

Behavioral assessments, namely, the Open Field Test (locomotor activity and anxiety-like behaviour), Rotarod Test (motor coordination and balance), and Novel Object Recognition Test (short-term recognition memory) were performed longitudinally.

In vivo ¹H-MRS was conducted at baseline and follow-up time points (15 and 30 days post-surgery) on a 7T MR scanner (BioSpec 70/20, M/s Bruker) using a PRESS (TE/TR = 17/2500 ms) sequence with a single 3×2×3 mm³ voxel. Voxels were placed in the striatum and prefrontal cortex. Metabolite ratios (Glutamate, Glutamate+Glutamine(GlX),Total PCh(GPC+PCh),NAA,Total NAA(NAA+NAAG)) were quantified relative to Cr+PCr using LCModel.

For serum metabolomics, approximately 2 mL of blood was collected from each rat and centrifuged at 5,000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4 °C within 1 hour of collection, after which the supernatant serum was separated and stored at −80 °C until analysis. Before NMR acquisition, samples were thawed at room temperature, and 200 µL of serum was mixed with 340 µL of D₂O, 30 µL of trimethylsilyl propionic acid (TSP), and 30 µL of sodium formate (0.5 mM), and transferred into a 5 mm NMR tube. All serum samples were analyzed on a 700 MHz NMR spectrometer at 25 °C. One-dimensional (1D) proton NMR spectra were acquired using the Carr–Purcell–Meiboom–Gill (CPMG) pulse sequence with presaturation of the water signal to suppress macromolecular resonances, using a spin-echo delay of 15 ms, a relaxation delay of 70 s, 64 scans, and 32 K data points. Spectra were processed using phase correction, baseline correction, and chemical shift calibration, with TSP serving as the internal chemical shift reference (δ = 0.00 ppm) and sodium formate used as a quantitative reference for metabolite concentration estimation during Chenomx-based metabolic profiling. Identified metabolites were quantified using Chenomx NMR Suite, and multivariate statistical analyses, including partial least squares–discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) and variable importance in projection (VIP) scoring were performed to identify metabolites contributing to group separation, with statistical significance set at p < 0.05.

Results

Behavioural outcomes

Bilateral 6-OHDA–lesioned rats exhibited significant behavioural impairments compared with sham animals. In the Open Field Test, PD rats showed a marked reduction in total distance travelled and decreased time spent in the central zone, indicating reduced locomotor activity and increased anxiety-like behaviour. Rotarod testing revealed a significant reduction in latency to fall, reflecting impaired motor coordination and balance. In the Novel Object Recognition test, PD rats demonstrated reduced discrimination and recognition indices, indicating deficits in short-term recognition memory. Sham animals did not exhibit significant behavioural changes across time points.

In vivo brain ¹H-MRS findings

Longitudinal ¹H-MRS revealed region- and time-dependent neurometabolic alterations across the cortico-striatal axis in PD rats. Both the striatum and prefrontal cortex showed a progressive decline in neuronal integrity markers, with notabe N-acetylaspartate (NAA), changes in the prefrontal cortex. In the prefrontal cortex, glutamate levels were significantly reduced at 15 and 30 days post-lesion, accompanied by significant reductions in NAA and NAA+NAAG at 30 days, indicating early disruption of excitatory neurotransmission and neuronal–glial metabolic coupling. In contrast, the striatum exhibited a delayed metabolic response, with a significant reduction in NAA observed only at 30 days post-lesion, while glutamate, Glx, and choline-containing metabolites showed non-significant downward trends. The absence of significant changes in GPC+PCh across regions suggests relative preservation of membrane turnover during early disease progression.

Serum ¹H-NMR metabolomics

Paired univariate analysis of serum ¹H-NMR data did not reveal statistically significant metabolite changes between baseline and follow-up time points. However, multivariate PLS-DA demonstrated clear separation between baseline and follow-up samples in the PD group, whereas sham samples showed substantial overlap. VIP analysis identified amino acids, including branched-chain amino acids, choline-related metabolites, acetate, trimethylamine, and creatinine as major contributors to group separation, indicating subtle, but coordinated systemic metabolic reorganization.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that bilateral 6-OHDA lesions produce early and coordinated behavioural, neurometabolic, and systemic metabolic alterations in a rat model of Parkinson’s disease. Behavioural deficits encompassed both motor and cognitive domains, reflecting the multidimensional nature of Parkinsonian pathology. In vivo ¹H-MRS revealed a temporal divergence in cortico-striatal vulnerability, with the prefrontal cortex exhibiting earlier and more pronounced reductions in glutamate and neuronal integrity markers, followed by delayed striatal involvement. The relative preservation of choline-containing metabolites suggests that early pathology is dominated by metabolic dysregulation rather than overt structural degeneration. Although serum metabolomics did not show significant univariate changes, multivariate analyses captured disease-related metabolic pattern shifts, supporting the presence of early systemic involvement. Overall, this multimodal approach demonstrates the feasibility and sensitivity of combining behavioural phenotyping with central and peripheral NMR-based measures to map early Parkinsonian pathology. Despite the limited sample size, the findings support the concept of Parkinson’s disease as a distributed network disorder involving early cortical dysfunction, and they provide a strong rationale for future longitudinal studies with larger cohorts to validate neurometabolic biomarkers for disease monitoring and therapeutic evaluation.
