Introduction

The human neocortex exhibits a six-layered organization that varies in microstructure across brain regions. A striking feature in the striate cortex (V1) is the stria of Gennari1— a dense band of myelinated fibers within its middle layer 4 that marks where visual stimuli enter the neocortex. This stria is absent from extrastriate areas dedicated to later stages of visual information processing. Despite clear anatomical and functional distinctions between V1 and the extrastriate cortex, prior research emphasizes a canonical three-compartment model of layer organization for cortical information processing2, where cortical layers are subdivided into superficial, middle (layer 4) and deep compartments. Stimulus-driven input primarily enters the middle compartment via ascending fibers, from which it propagates to the superficial and deep compartments. Conversely, contextual information provided by later visual areas arrives predominantly in superficial and deep compartments via descending fibers. Whether laminar computations in functionally distinct brain regions mirror this canonical architecture or reflect their anatomical distinctions remains unknown.

Methods

Here, we used population receptive field (pRF) modelling3 at ultra-high field (7T) fMRI with submillimetre resolution (voxel size: 0.8 mm isotropic) to investigate visual information processing across the cortical depth along the visual hierarchy (V1-hV4, LO). To this end, we estimated laminar profiles of pRF size, a neural property reflecting the extent of the visual field to which a population of neurons responds. In total, thirteen healthy adult volunteers (mean age ± SD: 34 ± 9 years, seven females), with normal or corrected-to-normal visual acuity, participated in this study. The (f)MRI data preprocessing workflow (Fig. 1) included data upsampling to 0.4mm isotropic resolution, thermal denoising, and both susceptibility distortion and motion correction. The segmented gray matter was divided into eight equivolumetric cortical surface layers. To determine the curvature and concavity of the laminar profiles, we fitted quadratic models for each visual field map, and verified the sign and significance of their quadratic term.Significance level was α = 0.05, Bonferroni-corrected for multiple comparisons.

Results

In V1, pRF size followed a U-shaped profile (p < 10-10; Fig. 2), being the smallest near the stria of Gennari. These results in V1 are consistent with previous neuroimaging4 and neurophysiology5  studies. Conversely, V2 displayed a flat profile (p > 0.05), while V3, hV4, and LO displayed inverted U-shaped profiles (V3: p < 10-5, hV4: p < 10-15, LO: p < 10-10; Fig. 2). These results show distinct laminar profiles for striate and extrastriate cortex.

Conclusion

Our results suggest that the three-compartment model organization primarily applies to V1 and does not generalize to the extrastriate cortex. We propose that recurrent processing in later visual areas blurs the distinction between stimuli-driven and contextual information along the ascending and descending visual pathways presumed by canonical models. Our findings suggest an area-specific computational role for the stria of Gennari and highlight the need for more nuanced models of layer organization that account for both functional and anatomical specialization.
