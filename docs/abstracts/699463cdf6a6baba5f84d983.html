Introduction

Better characterizing brain dynamics, particularly in fMRI, is a crucial step to advancing neuroscience. Previous work with MEG links non-linear features of the signal with improvement in brain FC identification [1] (i.e. connectome brain fingerprinting [2]). Also, recent findings supports [3] a relationship between a proxy measure of metastability [4] and the idiosyncratic and shared components of subjects' functional connectivity—decomposed from the fingerprinting matrix [5]. Here, we extend these findings by examining multiple heterogeneous datasets spanning distinct naturalistic contents, cognitive tasks, scan durations, longitudinal sampling schemes, and clinical settings.

Methods

This work used data from the Naturalistic Neuroimaging Database (500 Days of Summer: 20 subjects, ages 19-53; Citizenfour: 18 subjects, age 19-58); the Sixth Sense dataset (17 subjects, ages 18–24); the Midnight Scan Club dataset (10 subjects, ages 24-34); a HC-bvFTD-PD dataset (Control: 20 subjects, age 62-82; Parkinson: 18 subjects, age 59-87; bvFTD: 13 subjects, age 42-87); and a subset from the HCP S900 release with resting-state, movie-watching and cognitive tasks scans (20 subjects, age 22-35). Analyses were performed using the Gordon cortical parcellation. The proxy measure of metastability was calculated as the standard deviation of the spatial coherence of regions. The Iself and Iothers were computed as the diagonal part and the average the non-diagonal values of the connectome matrix, respectively. The measures were computed using a sliding window across the  datasets. Mixed-effects models were fitted for each condition, including age, sex, scan parameters, and head motion as nuisance regressors.

Results

For the NNDB dataset, we found, at the whole-brain level, a negative relationship between metastability and both fingerprinting indexes, with a steeper slope for Iothers (−0.23, SE = 0.024, CI [−0.27, −0.18]) compared to Iself (−0.074, SE = 0.027, CI [−0.12, −0.02]). At the network level, this relationship was also observed across all networks, showing bidirectional effects depending on the index and the network.

When compared by stimuli, the Romantic Comedy condition presented a steeper slope in both cases (Iself: −0.11, SE = 0.034, CI [−0.18, −0.04]; Iothers: −0.29, SE = 0.030, CI [−0.34, −0.22]) relative to the Documentary.

When comparing versions of the naturalistic stimulus, in the Sixth Sense dataset, the Spoiler version showed a stronger positive prediction for Iothers (0.148, SE = 0.030, CI [0.09, 0.20]), whereas the Original version showed a stronger—also positive—prediction for Iself (0.233, SE = 0.038, CI [0.16, 0.30]).

For the HCP dataset, cognitive tasks presented a distinct configuration compared to resting-state and movie tasks (Iself: −0.050, SE = 0.029, CI [−0.10, 0.00]; Iothers: 0.025, SE = 0.033, CI [−0.04, 0.09]).

Considering a long resting-state data collection, the Midnight Scan Club dataset showed that predictions varied across days for the same participants.

Finally, for the neurological disorders dataset, Iself showed similar positive relationships among HC, PD, and bvFTD groups, whereas for Iothers, the bvFTD group showed a distinct direction in the association compared with the others (0.165, SE = 0.116, CI [−0.06, 0.39]).

Conclusion

Metastability and connectome fingerprinting indices show a consistent association at whole-brain and network levels across neuroimaging contexts. This association varies with stimulus content, narrative engagement, task demands, and neurological status, suggesting that multiple interacting factors shape large-scale brain dynamics.
