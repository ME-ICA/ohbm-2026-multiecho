Introduction

Neural activity for spatial attention is generally distributed across a cortical frontoparietal network. Within this segregated large-scale network, each brain regions participate in distinct aspects of attentional processing. For instance, frontal areas such as the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) and frontal eye fields encode task rules and associated premotor activity, respectively. Parietal areas, including the lateral intraparietal area (LIP), are thought to harbor a priority map that integrates frontal inputs with topographical stimulus features. Previous studies have been overly focused on these cortical substrates of attention, largely ignoring subcortical contributions to the attentional control process. Recent evidence has highlighted a thalamic role in attentional processing, including the gating of sensory signals and the regulation of interactions within and between cortical areas. Nevertheless, the specific computational mechanisms by which the thalamus contributes to attentional orienting and selection under conditions of uncertainty remain incompletely understood.

Methods

Here, we integrated computational modeling with human fMRI to characterize the neural computational mechanisms of thalamocortical connectivity using a location-cueing paradigm. In the paradigm, the percentage of cue validity (90%, 70%, and 50%) varied dynamically over time, systematically modulating target detection response speeds. Trial-wise estimates of precision-weighted prediction error (pwPE) were derived from a hierarchical Bayesian model (hierarchical gaussian filter, HGF) fitted to individual participants’ trial-by-trial response speeds.

Results

While a broad bilateral frontoparietal network, including frontal eye fields, intraparietal sulcus, and temporoparietal junction, showed significantly higher activity on invalid relative to valid trials, responses in the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) during invalid trials were significantly modulated by pwPE. Nonlinear dynamic causal modeling revealed that pwPE-related responses in the ACC modulate trans-thalamic connectivity, rather than direct corticocortical (frontoparietal) connections. This transthalamic processing may enable the control of information flow between frontoparietal areas, enabling error monitoring in the ACC to trigger executive remapping in the prefontal cortex upon attentional switches. Using a dynamic multivariate pattern analysis, we furthermore show that pwPE-related responses in ACC predict trial-by-trial changes in multivariate neural patterns in the mediodorsal thalamus (MD) and orbitofrontal cortex (OFC). Importantly, the pattern changes in the MD predicted upcoming changes in response speed and thus highlight the importance of these pattern changes for behavioral swithes.

Conclusion

Overall, these findings highlight how frontoparietal transthalamic pathways implement the flexible attentional swithes.
