Introduction

Anorexia nervosa (AN) has one of the highest mortality rates among psychiatric disorders.1 Emerging evidence suggests that disrupted serotonergic signalling, particularly reduced 5-HT2A receptor binding in cortical regions, plays a key role in its underlying neurobiology.2 Preclinical studies have shown that serotonergic psychedelics, such as psilocin, can promote the growth of neurites and dendritic spines via 5-HT2A signalling.3 These findings suggest that psilocybin-assisted therapy may benefit patients with AN by inducing structural plasticity in neural networks. In this study, we investigated whether high doses of psilocybin induce changes in structural brain networks over a six-week period in individuals with AN, and whether these changes relate to clinical improvement.

Methods

Nineteen female participants with a DSM-5 diagnosis of AN (age: 32.3 ± 7.2 years; BMI: 16.3 ± 1.4 kg/m²) received three oral doses of psilocybin (up to 25 mg) over six weeks in a therapeutic setting. Participants underwent brain MRI at baseline and six-week follow-up. Diffusion-weighted MRI was acquired using a multi-shell protocol with 60 directions at b = 1000 and b = 2000 s/mm² (TR/TE = 4900/92 ms, 2.5 mm isotropic voxels), with reverse phase-encoded images acquired for distortion correction. Clinical outcomes were assessed at both timepoints using the Eating Disorder Examination (EDE) interview and the self-report EDE Questionnaire (EDE-Q) to measure eating disorder psychopathology, and the Readiness and Motivation Questionnaire (RMQ) pre-contemplation subscale to assess motivation for recovery, with reductions indicating symptomatic improvement. Diffusion images were preprocessed using MRtrix3 (denoising, Gibbs ringing removal, motion and eddy-current correction, and bias-field correction) and rigidly registered to each participant’s T1-weighted image using ANTs. The cerebral cortex was parcellated into 200 regions using the Schaefer 2018 atlas. Whole-brain probabilistic tractography was performed using anatomically constrained tractography, and streamlines were filtered using spherical-deconvolution informed filtering of tractograms (SIFT) to obtain biologically plausible reconstructions. Neurite orientation dispersion and density imaging (NODDI) was fitted to the diffusion data, and neurite density index (NDI) values were sampled along streamlines to derive microstructure-weighted structural connectivity matrices. Graph-theoretical network topology was assessed across proportional density thresholds (10-50%). Nodal metrics (local efficiency, clustering coefficient, strength, betweenness centrality) were computed for 27 cortical regions showing the highest 5-HT2A receptor density based on publicly available PET maps4, alongside network-level metrics (global efficiency, characteristic path length, modularity). Pre-post differences were evaluated using paired t-tests with permutation testing (10,000 iterations). Associations between clinical and network changes were examined using Kendall's tau-b correlations between pre-post differences in graph metrics and behavioural measures. Analyses were performed at the whole-brain level, with graph metrics averaged across thresholds prior to statistical testing. To maximize sensitivity for detecting effects within the 27 a priori selected regions, statistical results are reported without correction for multiple comparisons at p < 0.05.

Results

At the whole-brain level, network metrics showed no significant pre-post changes in global efficiency (t(18) = -0.14, p = 0.893), characteristic path length (t(18) = 0.53, p = 0.606), or modularity (t(18) = 1.24, p = 0.188), indicating preserved global network architecture. Nodal-level analyses focusing on 27 cortical regions with highest 5-HT2A receptor density revealed significant localized changes. Clustering coefficient increased in the right superior temporal gyrus (STG; t(18) = -2.35, p = 0.028), indicating that its neighbours became more densely interconnected and formed a more cohesive local community structure. Conversely, betweenness centrality decreased in the left STG (t(18) = 2.08, p = 0.004), suggesting that shortest paths now bypass this hub more frequently, potentially reflecting emergence of alternative routes. Local efficiency decreased in the left middle temporal gyrus (MTG; t(18) = 2.17, p = 0.042), indicating that communication among its neighbours became more dependent on this hub itself, with fewer direct connections between neighbouring regions.

Brain-behaviour correlations suggested that greater reductions in betweenness of the right angular gyrus (ANG; τ = 0.43, p = 0.010) and greater increases in clustering in the left pars opercularis of inferior frontal gyrus (IFGoper), supramarginal gyrus (SMG), and MTG (|τ| > 0.35, p < 0.041) were associated with greater reductions in EDE scores, indicating that localized changes in these frontal-parietal regions supported symptom improvement. Additionally, greater increases in clustering of the left IFGoper (τ = -0.44, p = 0.009) and greater reductions in betweenness of the right inferior temporal gyrus (ITG; τ = 0.39, p = 0.038) were associated with greater reductions in pre-contemplation scores on the RMQ, indicating that greater changes in IFGoper and ITG related to increased motivation for recovery.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that high-dose psilocybin induces regionally specific structural plasticity within 5-HT2A-enriched cortical networks over six weeks in individuals with AN. The preservation of global network metrics alongside significant nodal-level changes suggests targeted adaptive reorganization rather than widespread disruption of brain network architecture. The observed pattern also reveals an important functional dissociation within the assessed 5-HT2A-enriched regions: structural changes in temporal regions, which possess the highest receptor density, were most reliably detected across participants, indicating sensitivity to psilocybin-induced plasticity over six weeks, whereas changes in frontal-parietal regions correlated more strongly with individual variability in clinical improvement, suggesting these networks modulate the magnitude of therapeutic response. It should be noted that statistical results were reported without correction for multiple comparisons across nodal tests and correlation analyses and should therefore be considered preliminary and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory. Future research with larger samples and longer follow-up periods is needed to confirm these findings and determine whether psilocybin-induced network changes in 5-HT2A-enriched regions predict sustained clinical recovery in AN.
