Introduction

The neonatal period constitutes a critical window for the emergence of functional brain networks, during which neural organization shifts from predominantly local interactions to increasingly distributed and integrated systems.¹ While population-level trajectories of the functional connectome are increasingly mapped, their fundamental link to the moment-to-moment individual brain dynamics remains unexplored. To bridge this critical gap, we introduced an event-driven analysis2 framework that investigated how fluctuating neural processes at the individual and seconds level underlie and shape the emergent population-level developmental dynamics. This approach unveils the fundamental principles governing cross-scale brain organization during a critical developmental period.

Methods

We analyzed resting-state fMRI data from 440 healthy full-term neonates (200 females; GA: 39.9±1.2 weeks; scan PMA: 41.2±1.7 weeks) from the Developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP).3 High-temporal-resolution fMRI was acquired using a 3T Philips scanner (TR=392ms). Standard preprocessing included motion/slice-timing correction, bandpass filtering (0.01-0.08Hz), and global signal regression. We constructed two parallel connectome time series: (1) Population developmental series: 440 FC matrices ordered by ascending PMA; (2) Individual dynamic series: 230 non-overlapping sliding windows (10 frames each) per subject.Time-resolved community structure was identified using the Infomap algorithm.4 Four evolutionary events were defined: “Birth” (emergence of a newcommunity), “Death” (disappearance of a community), “Growth” (>10% size increase), and “Contraction” (>10% size decrease). For each event, we computed 90×90 co-occurrence matrices: “Birth”/“Death” captured pairwise  combinations within the appearing/disappearing community; “Growth”/“Contraction” captured pairs between changing (added/removed) ROIs and stable ROIs. For  the population level series, we obtained 4 population-development co-occurrence probability matrices. For 440 × 4 individual dynamic matrices, we calculated their averages separately and obtained 4 individual-average co-occurrence probability matrices. These matrices were mapped onto eight canonical functional net-works.

Results

“Birth” and “Death” events exhibited nearly identical spatial co-occurrence patterns characterized by predominantly within-network dynamics (Figure 2A). This pattern was particularly prominent in visual (VIS) and somatomotor (SM) systems, suggesting that functional modules emerge and dissolve as coherent units to maintain modular integrity. In contrast, “Growth” and “Contraction” events showed predominantly between-network dynamics (Figure2A). Cross-network interactions were most prominent between SM-FPN, SM-DMN, VA-FPN, and SUB-DMN, revealing that network plasticity operates through boundary regulation. Individual-averaged co-occurrence matrices showed striking similarity to population-level developmental matrices (“Birth”: r=0.855; “Death”: r=0.848; “Growth”: r=0.574; “Contraction”: r=0.573; all p<10⁻⁵) (Figure2B,C). Regional analysis revealed heterogeneous individual-group consistency (Figure2D): primary sensorimotor cortex showed highest coupling (r≈0.65), while olfactory cortex and subcortical structures showed lowest coupling (r≈0.28-0.33). Furthermore, age-related changes revealed a developmental gradient: subcortical structures showed increasing individual-group coupling with age (Figure2E), whereas parietal association cortices showed decreasing coupling, supporting a "subcortical-to-cortical" maturation principle.

Conclusion

This study establishes an event-driven framework demonstrating that neonatal brain network evolution relies on two complementary mechanisms: intra-modular stability (lifecycle events) and inter-modular integration (boundary events). Critically, we provide evidence that population-level developmental trajectories are not merely statistical abstractions but mirror the temporal integration of rapid, moment-to-moment network transitions within individuals. The observed regional heterogeneity in individual-population coupling further suggests a transition from genetically-driven development in primary regions to increasingly experience-dependent refinement in association cortices, These findings offer a novel, dynamic perspective on early brain maturation and present potential event-based markers for early neurodevelopmental assessment.
