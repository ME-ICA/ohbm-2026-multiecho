Introduction

Early differential diagnosis between the behavioral variant of frontotemporal dementia (bvFTD) and major depressive disorder (MDD) remains clinically challenging due to overlapping behavioral symptoms and often subtle early-stage neurodegeneration.^1 Reliable biomarkers are urgently needed to support diagnostic accuracy at the single-patient level. Serum neurofilament light chain (sNfL), a marker of neuroaxonal damage, is typically elevated in neurodegenerative diseases but not in primary psychiatric disorders.^2,3 In addition, automated atlas-based volumetry (ABV) allows the quantification of subtle regional brain atrophy beyond the sensitivity of visual MRI inspection.^4 This study investigated (1) whether sNfL differentiates bvFTD from MDD, (2) whether volumetric measures of six a priori defined regions - frontal lobe, temporal lobe, cingulate gyrus, cerebellum, hippocampus, and amygdala - improve diagnostic discrimination, and (3) how the diagnostic performance of these biomarkers compares using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses. Particular emphasis was placed on the early and diagnostically relevant subgroup of “possible bvFTD,” which presents with clinical symptoms but no clear visual atrophy on MRI.^5

Methods

Serum NfL levels were quantified in 166 bvFTD patients, 84 individuals with MDD, and 36 healthy controls. bvFTD cases were additionally stratified into possible, probable, and definite bvFTD based on visual MRI ratings and genetic/pathological confirmation. sNfL concentrations were measured using an automated microfluid immunoassay (Ella). T1-weighted MPRAGE 3T MRI scans underwent quality control, anonymization and conversion from DICOM to NifTI format. Subsequently, atlas-based volumetry was performed using the LONI Probabilistic Brain Atlas (LPBA40) and the Harvard-Oxford atlas of subcortical structures (HOS). Regional brain volumes were normalized to intracranial volume. Statistical anlaysis were conducted in R. Group differences in sNfL and volumetric measures were assessed using general linear models with age as a covariate and Tukey-corrected post-hoc tests. ROC analyses based on logistic regression-derived predicted probabilities (adjusted for age) were used to evaluate the discriminative performance of sNfL and regional brain volumes for distinguishing bvFTD from MDD.

Results

sNfL levels differed significantly across diagnostic groups (p<.001). bvFTD patients showed substantially elevated sNfL (MW 51.1 ± 45.5 pg/mL) compared with MDD (16.4 ± 12.0 pg/mL) and controls, whereas MDD did not differ from controls. Within bvFTD, probable cases demonstrated the highest sNfL, significantly exceeding possible bvFTD and MDD. Volumetry revealed widespread regional atrophy in bvFTD relative to MDD and controls across all predefined regions except the cerebellum. Even the possible bvFTD subgroup, which appeared visually normal on MRI, showed significant volumetric reductions in frontal lobe, temporal lobe, cingulate gyrus, hippocampus and amygdala compared with MDD. ROC analyses demonstrated excellent discriminative performance for both sNfL and volumetric measures. sNfL distinguished bvFTD from MDD with an AUC of 0.94 (95% CI 0.91–0.97). Volumetric biomarkers showed comparable or superior accuracy, with the frontal lobe volume achieving the highest AUC of 0.97 (95% CI 0.95–0.99). Temporal lobe, hippocampal, and amygdala volumes also performed strongly (AUCs 0.95–0.96). Subgroup ROC analyses showed best performance for probable bvFTD (e.g., frontal lobe AUC = 0.99), while discrimination of possible bvFTD from MDD remained highly accurate (AUCs 0.93–0.96 across biomarkers).

Conclusion

Serum NfL and atlas-based volumetric MRI provide powerful and complementary biomarkers for differentiating bvFTD from MDD, achieving diagnostic accuracies well within the range required for clinical utility. Even in visually normal possible bvFTD, volumetry detected subtle atrophy and contributed to robust classification. The frontal lobe volume emerged as the single best discriminator, outperforming sNfL, though both measures achieved excellent accuracy. These findings support integrating quantitative biomarkers, particularly sNfL and automated volumetry, into routine diagnostic workflows to improve early detection of bvFTD and reduce misdiagnosis as depression.
