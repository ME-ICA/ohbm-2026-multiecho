Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is characterized by progressive loss of neuromelanin-positive noradrenergic neurons in the locus coeruleus (LC), which has been linked to non-motor dysfunction, and by iron accumulation in the substantia nigra (SN), which contributes to both motor and non-motor symptoms1. Ultra-high field 7 tesla (7T) MRI with magnetization transfer-weighted (MTw) and R2* imaging enables in vivo assessment of LC and SN microstructure at high spatial resolution. Our previous cross-sectional 7T MRI studies revealed that PD patients had lower neuromelanin signal in LC and SN, and higher iron accumulation in the SN compared to healthy controls2,3. However, longitudinal 7T studies that assess LC and SN integrity and their relationship to symptom progression remain scarce. Only two longitudinal neuromelanin-sensitive MRI studies at 3T have demonstrated a progressive decrease of neuromelanin in SN in PD4,5. We aimed to map progressive neurodegeneration in LC and SN over ~3.5 years in PD using 7T MRI, test its association with clinical symptom progression.

Methods

We included 18 patients with PD who underwent two 7T MRI scans approximately 3.5 years apart. All participants provided written informed consent to participate in the study, which was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT03866044).

Clinical measurements

On the day of clinical examination, patients had taken their usual dopaminergic treatment and motor severity was assessed around 10-11 AM in the on-medication state. Clinical assessments at both time points comprised motor and non-motor symptom severity (Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale [UPDRS], Non-Motor Symptom Scale [NMSS]), apathy (Lille Apathy Rating Scale [LARS]) and depression (Beck Depression Inventory-II [BDI-II]), global cognition (Montreal Cognitive Assessment [MoCA]), and systolic blood pressure.

Magnetic resonance imaging acquisition and processing

Structural MRI data were collected with a Philips Achieva 7T scanner (Philips, Best, The Netherlands) equipped with a 32-channel Nova head coil (Nova Medical, Inc., MA, USA). We acquired (1) T1-weighted (T1w, voxel size = 1 mm isotropic) Magnetization Prepared RApid Gradient Echo (MPRAGE) images, (2) a high-resolution (voxel size = 0.4 mm isotropic), single-echo, T2*-weighted (T2*w) 3D fast field echo (FFE) sequence, (3) neuromelanin-sensitive MTw images using a 3D high-resolution (voxel size = 0.4×0.4×1.0 mm) ultrafast gradient echo sequence and (4) a multi-echo, 3D FFE sequence was acquired for the calculation of R2*-maps with a field-of-view covering the entire head (voxel size = 1 mm isotropic). 

Imaging data were co-registered and normalized to template space using the ANTs software (2.2.0). For the MTw imaging, first, a T1w group template was generated from individual MPRAGE images acquired at baseline and follow-up using rigid, affine, and nonlinear diffeomorphic registration with five resolution levels. Second, the T1w group template was then registered to MNI standard space (the MNI 0.5 mm ICBM152 T1w) using rigid, affine, and nonlinear registration, yielding T1w group template in MNI space. Third, for each subject, the MTw image was rigidly registered to the corresponding noMTw image, which was subsequently rigidly registered to the individual T1w image. Finally, using the transformation fields derived in steps 1 and 2, the MTw images registered to the individual T1w images were then transformed into MNI space. For the R2* maps, within subject co-registration of R2* maps was achieved through rigid-body registration of the first echo from the multi-echo T2*w acquisition to the reference images and the single echo T2*w magnitude image to a combined magnitude image from the multi-echo T2*w acquisition.

Calculation of MTw neuromelanin contrast-to-noise ratio (MT CNR) maps was performed in template space in FSL (6.0.4). For the LC, the whole brain stem was chosen as the reference. For the SN, a midbrain search-space was manually segmented on the T1w MNI template and divided into right and left midbrain search-spaces and two 3.5×4 mm cylindrical reference regions were placed in the right and left crus cerebri using the ITK-SNAP software (3.8.0). Neuromelanin maps were calculated as CNR from normalized MTw images by subtracting the mean signal intensity of the lateralized reference regions from the voxel signal intensity and dividing by the standard deviation of these reference regions.

Voxel-wise quantitative R2* maps were calculated from the multi-echo T2*w magnitude images using the Auto-Regression on Linear Operations algorithm for fast mono-exponential fitting. 

SN was segmented into the nigrosome-1 (N1) subregion and SN without N1 in order to find more details of the degeneration of the SN.

Statistical analysis

Paired t-tests were used to investigate longitudinal changes in imaging measures. Spearman correlation analyses were used to detect the associations between changes in imaging measures and changes or levels of clinical scores.

Results

Neuromelanin-sensitive MRI of the LC

In the LC, the neuromelanin signal was significantly reduced at follow-up: Patients had lower MTw CNR in bilateral and the overall LC compared to baseline (left: P = 0.007; right: P = 0.003; Overall: P = 0.004). Larger changes of overall LC CNR were associated with higher worsening change of BDI-II scores (P = 0.023, ρ = -0.38) and the LARS scores (P = 0.003, ρ = -0.43).   

Neuromelanin- and iron-sensitive MRI of the SN

For the neuromelanin-sensitive MTw CNR in the SN, patients at follow-up had a trend-level lower CNR in the left SN (without N1, P = 0.052), but not in bilateral N1 (both P > 0.05). A larger change of CNR in the left N1 showed a trend-level association with a greater worsening change in BDI-II scores (P = 0.061, ρ = 0.51), and a larger change of CNR in right N1 showed a trend-level association with a greater worsening change in NMSS scores (P = 0.054, ρ = 0.48).

For the R2* values in SN, patients at follow-up had higher R2* values in bilateral N1 (left: P = 0.012; right: P = 0.004), and the right SN (without N1, P = 0.024). A larger change of R2* values in left N1 showed a trend-level association with a greater worsening change in MoCA scores (P = 0.083, ρ = -0.42).

Conclusion

Using ultra-high field 7T neuromelanin-sensitive MTw imaging and iron-sensitive R2* mapping, we demonstrated longitudinal changes in both LC and SN over approximately 3.5 years in patients with PD. LC neuromelanin CNR showed significant bilateral reductions, and a greater overall LC CNR decrease was associated with stronger worsening of depressive symptoms and apathy, suggesting that progressive LC degeneration may contribute to the non-motor affective burden in PD.

In the SN, we observed trend-level reductions of MTw CNR in the left SN (without N1), together with significant longitudinal increases in R2* in bilateral N1 and right SN (without N1). These iron-related changes in the left N1 showed a trend-level association with a greater decline in cognition, indicating that progressive nigral iron accumulation, particularly in N1, may be linked to cognitive deterioration in PD.

Taken together, our findings support 7T MTw CNR and R2* measures in LC and SN subregions as promising candidate markers of disease progression, especially for depression, apathy and cognitive symptoms. The results highlight the importance of simultaneously assessing noradrenergic LC and dopaminergic SN pathology and underscore the potential of ultra-high field MRI to capture spatially specific microstructural changes relevant for future disease-modifying trials in PD. Studies with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up are warranted to validate these longitudinal imaging markers and clarify their prognostic value.
