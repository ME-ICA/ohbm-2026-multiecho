Introduction

A simultaneous measurement of electroencephalogram (EEG) and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) is an effective method for epilepsy diagnosis. Leveraging the spatiotemporal synergy between EEG’s temporal precision and fMRI’s spatial resolution provides novel insights into epilepsy diagnostics. This research investigates the clinical efficacy of various analytical frameworks applied to pediatric epilepsy.

Methods

Simultaneously recorded EEG–fMRI data from 19 patients with pediatric epilepsy at a 3-T MRI system (MAGNETOM Skyra; Siemens Healthcare, Ger­many). Functional MRI data were acquired via GE-EPI (TR/TE=2,000/30 ms, FOV=192 mm², 3 mm slice, FA=80°, matrix size=64×64), followed by anatomical 3D T1-weighted imaging (TR/TE=2,530/1.67 ms, FOV=256×256  mm², 1 mm slice, FA=7°, matrix size=256×256). Simultaneously, 64-channel EEG (GES 300; EGI, USA) was sampled at 500 Hz (0.7–55 Hz bandpass). Gradient and pulse artifacts were corrected using template subtraction and optimal basis sets (Net Station software), respectively. Brain activation was analyzed using SPM12 (version 12, https://www.fil.ion. ucl.ac.uk). The pre-processing pipeline included realignment, coregistration, normalization, and spatial smoothing(8mm). The hemodynamic timing relative to IEDs by modeling the HRF is investigated with a single gamma function(5 HRF in Fig.1) and HRF(10 HRF in Fig.1), systematically varying the temporal parameters surrounding the peak (±3, ±5, ±7, ±9). The equivalent dipole modeling within the EEGLAB environment(https://eeglab.org/) employed for source localization. The functional connectivity was measured using the EpiNetFlow pipeline, which included ANTs SyN normalization to MNI152 space, 6mm smoothing, and quality control using framewise displacement (FD) and temporal signal-to-noise ratio (tSNR) metrics. Subsequent connectivity analyses utilized AAL 116 ROIs to compute functional connectivity (Pearson/partial correlation), graph metrics, Independent Component Analysis (ICA), and Effective Connectivity (Granger Causality/Transfer Entropy).

Results

Figure 1 provides a comprehensive visualization of the neural landscape for a representative subject, integrating activation maps, functional connectivity, and source localization results. The primary somatosensory cortex displayed a lateral shift (right-to-left) dependent on peak latency. Functional connectivity exhibited a general spatial correspondence -though not an exact match- with activation areas. The source localization revealed a component congruent with these patterns, but this was identified as a non-dominant component rather than the principal source.

Conclusion

By integrating high-resolution EEG and fMRI for the diagnosis of pedatric epilepsy, this study reveals that spatiotemporal dynamics, such as latency-dependent shifts, are critical for understanding epileptic networks. Although this study identified significant findings across brain activation, functional connectivity, and source localization, elucidating the interplay among these modalities is essential for a comprehensive diagnostic application. While temporal variations exhibited analogous patterns, the current findings remain insufficient for a fully unified interpretation. Consequently, further investigation focusing on the neurophysiological underpinnings and advanced analytical integration is required to bridge these results.
