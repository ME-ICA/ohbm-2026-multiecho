Introduction

Large-scale connectivity gradients have emerged as a unifying framework for characterizing the hierarchical organization of the human brain. Previous study has demonstrated that functional connectivity gradients capture transitions from unimodal sensory regions to transmodal association cortices, and that neurotransmitter receptor distributions exhibit systematic relationships with these functional hierarchies. Recent multi-tracer PET atlases further revealed that receptor profiles collectively form cortical hierarchies, including contributions from the GABA system measured with flumazenil.

However, prior studies have primarily relied on either fixed group-level receptor maps or multi tracer receptor spaces, leaving open the question of whether a single neurotransmitter system alone exhibits an intrinsic gradient organization when inter-individual variability is explicitly considered. In particular, it remains unknown whether GABAergic inhibitory architecture, as indexed by [11C]flumazenil binding, forms a coherent cortical hierarchy across individuals.

Here, to address this gap, diffusion map embedding was applied to region-by-subject flumazenil PET data, thereby estimating a flumazenil-specific cortical gradient derived from inter-individual covariance of binding patterns. This approach enables us to characterize the hierarchical organization of inhibitory synaptic architecture and to examine its relationship to large-scale functional networks.

Methods

Preprocessed [11C]flumazenil PET binding potential (BPnD) images of 25 healthy subjects were analyzed. Cortical regions of interest (ROIs) were defined using the Schaefer 2018 atlas with 100 parcels and Yeo’s seven functional networks. For each subject, voxel-wise BPnD values were averaged within each parcel, yielding a region-by-subject matrix representing cortical flumazenil binding patterns accross individuals. 

Inter-regional similarity was estimated based on the normalized angle between regional binding profiles across subjects, producing a region-by-region affinity matrix that captures the similarity of inter-individual flumazenil binding patterns. Cortical gradients were derived from the regional affinity matrix using diffusion map embedding as implemented in the BrainSpace toolbox. Diffusion embedding was chosen to capture non-linear manifold structure in inter-regional similarity.

To facilitate interpretation at the system level, gradient values were averaged within the seven canonical functional networks defined by the Schaefer atlas (visual, somatomotor, dorsal attention, salience/ventral attention, limbic, frontoparietal control, and default mode). Gradient eigenvalues were inspected using scree plots to assess the relative contribution of each component. 

Results

The principal flumazenil gradient revealed a marked hierarchical organization of GABA receptor binding, with the salience/ventral attention network occupying the upper end of the gradient and limbic regions anchoring the opposite extreme. This organization in consistent with the established role of salience network regions as hubs for rapid network switching and excitatory-inhibitory balance regulation. The prominent positioning of the salience network along the flumazenil gradient suggests that inhibitory synaptic architecture is most coherently organized with circuits responsible for adaptive control and state transitions. In contrast, limbic regions exhibited lower gradient values and greater inter-individual variability, consistent with prior reports of heterogeneous GABAergic receptor expression and heightened vulnerability to affective and neurodegenerative processes. Notably, default mode regions occupied an intermediated position along the gradient, indicating that inhibitory synaptic organization does not simply mirror functional transmodal hierachies.

Conclusion

The principal flumazenil gradient revealed a hierarchical organization of cortical GABAergic inhibition, with the salience/ventral attention network forming the core of this inhibitory architecture and limbic regions anchoring the opposite end. These findings indicate that flumazenil binding varies along a cortical axis that reflects distinct modes of inhibitory organization, rather than simply mirroring canonical functional hiterarchies. The resulting gradient highlights a configuration i which networks involved in regulatory control exhibit tightly coordinated inhibitory structure, whereas regions implicated in affective and memroy functions display more diffuse and variable patterns.
