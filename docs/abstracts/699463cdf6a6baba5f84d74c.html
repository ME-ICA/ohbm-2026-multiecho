Introduction

The associative learning is an ability to deduce the contingency between the context (including stimuli), behavior and consequences, enabling learning from experience and adaptive behavior.1 The associative learning is a dynamic process that incorporates early, rule acquisition, stage, and late, rehearsal, stage. The behavioral development of the associative learning is the most rapid over the school years’ span. 

The P300 parameters are modulated by attention and working memory allocation and relevance for decision-making, action planning and entire internal state of the individual including emotions and motivation.1 Developmental changes in P300 characteristics during the associative learning performance relates to brain structure maturation. Based on previous functional MRI studies the basal ganglia and prefrontal cortex are involved in associative learning with reinforcement.1 DTI studies suggest the striatum-dorsolateral prefrontal cortex pathways are related to associative learning efficiency.2

However, it is still unclear whether functional and tractographic data on striatal and prefrontal involvement are supported by gray matter morphometry. Thus, the current study aim is to determine relations between P300 parameters during associative learning task performance and  gray matter volume.

Methods

Participants were 176 healthy subjects (age range: 7.1-21.1 years; mean=14.47, SD=4.03, 85 females, 91 males). The study protocol was approved by the Local Ethics Committee of the al-Farabi Kazakh National University. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants, and minors, from their legal guardians, prior to participation.

The behavioral task required series of runs with a cue stimulus presentation on a left or right half of a screen. The task demanded responding with a left or right button press, followed by a feedback whether button choice was correct or incorrect. Correct button was assigned by a covert rule that participants had to deduce via trial-and-error. Each 5-7 runs rule changed. Runs prior to successful rule acquisition were considered early (rule acquiring) stage, while runs evidencing successful rule acquisition were considered late (rehearsal) stage.

Structural MRI data were acquired at Medical Center “Sunkar” using a 3T GE SIGNA Architect scanner using a 3D T1-weighted BRAVO sequence (TR = 7.7 ms, TE = 3.1 ms, slice thickness = 0.6 mm, gap=1, voxel size = 0.47 × 0.47  × 0.6 mm3). The MRI analysis was conducted using the SPM12 (Wellcome Trust Centre for Neuroimaging, UCL, UK) and comprised images reorientation, segmentation, and normalization to the MNI space with resampled isotropic voxel of 1 mm3 using DARTEL utility that provided modulated gray matter maps smoothed with 2 mm FWHM Gaussian kernel. 

The EEG was recorded by using the ANT Neuro eego mylab 64-channel amplifier and 64-channel electrode cap at sampling rate of 4 096 Hz. EEG was preprocessed using EEGLab and ERPLab toolboxes for MATLAB (The MathWorks, Inc, Natick, MA, USA). Specifically, it was resampled to 512 Hz, filtered at 0.3-40 Hz, bad channels were interpolated, data were referenced to average, and bad independent components were deleted. Then data was re-referenced to linked mastoids, epoched at [-200; 800 ms] around each event, bad epochs were discarded, and ERPs were averaged separately for 4 bins, assuming combinations of 1) early or late stage and 2) stimulus onset or feedback demonstration events. P300 was defined as the largest positive peak in the 300-450 ms range. Data on peak amplitudes and latencies were averaged across 1) F3, F4, FZ, FC3, FC4,FCZ, C3, C4, CZ leads and 2) P3, P4, PZ, PO3, PO4, POZ, O1, O2, OZ leads.

To test the relationship between the morphometry and ERP results, we run the SPM12 2-nd level GLM, separately in male and female samples . The ERP measures were entered into the multiple regression model one-by-one as covariates of interest, while age and primary language served as nuisance regressors. The results were considered significant at voxel-level p < 0.001, uncorrected and cluster-level p < 0.05 FWE-corrected.

Results

In female participants, the posterior P300 amplitude early stage onset-related positively correlated to gray matter volumes in regions within the bilateral middle and inferior temporal gyri and also in right caudate (Fig. 1). The corresponding late-stage parietal occipital P300 amplitude was positively linked to gray matter morphometry index in clusters located in the left angular and middle temporal gyri (Fig. 1), posterior and middle cingulate gyrus.

Also in females, the amplitude of posterior P300 in response to early stage feedback presentation was positively linked to gray matter amount in the clusters located in the left middle frontal / orbitofrontal cortices, left superior and middle frontal gyri, and right caudate (Fig. 1). Larger late-stage parieto-occipital P300 amplitude was related to greater gray matter volumes in the left fusiform gyrus / VI cerebellar area, left orbitofrontal cortex, and left angular and middle temporal gyri (Fig. 1).

In male subjects, the posterior P300 amplitude in response to early-stage stimulus onset was positively related to larger gray matter volumes in the left calcarine sulcus / precuneus cluster. The early-stage feedback-related anterior P300 amplitude was positively linked to gray matter amount in a cluster located in the left supramarginal and superior temporal gyri. Male group demonstrated no gray matted morphometry correlates of their P300 amplitudes on the late stage of the paradigm.

Regarding latencies, in females, the latency of fronto-central P300 elicited by a feedback presentation was positively related to gray matter volumes in several clusters located in the left-hemispheric gyrus rectus, superior temporal gyrus and temporal pole, supplementary motor area and superior frontal gyrus, and cerebellar crus. For the corresponding posterior P300 latency, positive gray matter correlates were located in the IX cerebellar area and right superior parietal lobule.

In males, late-stage feedback-related posterior P300 latency was positively related to gray matter morphometry index in clusters within the right supramarginal gyrus, left cuneus, and left XI cerebellum area and right crus. These latencies also were inversely related to gray matter amount in a region within the left cuneus. 

Conclusion

Right caudate gray matter volume is positively associated with early-stage posterior visual P300 amplitudes in females in an associative learning task for both stimulus onset and feedback (Fig. 1). No comparable associations are observed in males. The caudate volume might be related to its more robust gating function and efficient filtering and bottom-up transmitting the reinforcement-related information (even non-incentivized one),3 supporting the learning of an associative rule via trials-and-errors.

Yet also specifically in females, P300 amplitudes on the rehearsal stage are correlated with gray matter amount in middle temporal gyrus and angular gyrus (Fig. 1), which is typically considered the source of P3b,4 meaning larger available neuronal pool enables greater synchronized P300 response amplitude.

Interestingly, our findings together suggest greater role of a task stage (rule acquisition vs rehearsal) that of a event type (cue stimulus onset vs feedback presentation) for structural correlates of P300 amplitudes in girls. Our results also may point to a possible diversity of structural correlates of associative learning-related activity between the samples of boys and girls.

Last, the gray matter volume in a number of cerebellar regions is also correlated with P300 characteristics, especially latencies. This relationships are possibly related to cerebellar projections to parietal and prefrontal cortices.
