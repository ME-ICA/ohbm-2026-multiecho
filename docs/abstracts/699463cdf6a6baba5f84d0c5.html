Introduction

Interventions for social, communication, and adaptive difficulties in autism show highly variable outcomes, yet the moderators of this variability are unclear. Generalizing learned skills is critical for flexible adaptive behavior, and difficulties in generalization are linked to variability in forming abstract representations (Minshew et al., 2002). Prior work revealed differences among autistic youth in how reliably they used abstract representation during category learning (Chen et al., 2025). We tested the hypothesis that reliability of abstract representation will moderate response to intervention targeting cognitive flexibility (Unstuck-and-on-Target, UOT (Kenworthy et al., 2014)). Autistic youth exhibiting abstract representation over time (“stable”) were expected to derive greater benefit from the intervention.

Methods

Sixty-four autistic adolescents (69% males, 14–18 years) without intellectual disability participated in UOT intervention trial. To infer learning strategies, a category learning task (Bowman & Zeithamova, 2018) coupled with fMRI and behavioral assessments were performed 3 times spaced eight months apart: baseline (T1), pre-UOT (T2), and post-UOT (T3). This within-subject design allowed each subject to serve as their own control by comparing the non-intervention (T1→T2) with the intervention interval (T2→T3). The task involved out-of-scanner feedback-based training followed by in-scanner no-feedback generalization. Two computational models of learning strategies were fit, prototype, posited to rely on abstract representations and exemplar, posited to rely on individual category members. The neural basis of these strategies was identified with two regressors modulated by prototype and exemplar model predictions. Subjects were grouped by consistency of abstract representation: those showing stronger prototype fits at both pre-UOT timepoints were labeled stable, and all others unstable. Cognitive flexibility was assessed using both parent- and self-report Shift scores from the Behavior Rating Inventory of Executive Function–II. Autism traits were assessed using the Social Responsiveness Scale–II (SRS) total score. Hierarchical linear modeling (HLM) was used to examine longitudinal changes. We tested whether pre- to post-intervention changes differed between groups (group × time interaction), using the non-intervention interval as a control.

Results

Fifty-four subjects completed all three assessments and the intervention, of whom 26 were classified as stable. The group × time interaction from pre- to post-intervention was significant for BRIEF-Shift and SRS scores (ts(103)>2.3, ps<.02), such that stable subjects improved in all scores after UOT (t(53)>3.6, p < .001), whereas unstable did not change (abs(t(53))<1.8, ps>.07); this interaction was not significant for he non-intervention interval (ps>.13). Thus, stable subjects showed significant improvement in both cognitive flexibility and autistic traits, selectively during the intervention period.

Whole-brain analysis from subjects passing quality control revealed greater prototype-related activation in stable (N=22) than unstable (N=16) group in a precuneus cluster at post-UOT (volume=432 mm3; peak: x=37, y=27, z=62; voxel p<.005, cluster p<.05). HLM of prototype-related activation in this cluster showed a significant group × time interaction from pre- to post-intervention (t(44)=-3.4, p=.002), such that stable subjects exhibited an increase following the intervention (t(32)=2.6, p=.01), whereas a significant decrease was observed in the unstable group (t(10)=-2.8, p=.02). No such interaction was observed in the non-intervention interval (t(44)=1.1, p=.27).

Conclusion

Pre-intervention capacity for abstract representation predicted gains in cognitive flexibility and reduced autism traits following intervention. Increased involvement of precuneus, a region which integrates visual-spatial and memory functions, distinguished those showing behavioral improvement.
