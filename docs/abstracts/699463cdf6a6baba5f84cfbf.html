Introduction

Handedness is a preference for one extremity side generalized over different sensorimotor skills and seems to be unique to humans. Lateralization of functional representation in primary motor cortex of unilateral upper limb movements is highly dependent on both task complexity/demand (e.g., simple tapping movements versus sequential finger movements) and brain morphology (e.g., quantification of pyramidal tract directedness, transcallosal connectivity). Prior work on lateralization differences between right- and left- handed people did not consider both main drivers of lateralization.

Methods

We applied fMRI to investigate functional lateralization of upper limb movements with different complexity together with white matter quantification in 26 strongly right- and 26 strongly left-handed healthy volunteers. We were especially interested in possible differences in fMRI- lateralization between handedness groups but also in possible associations between pyramidal tract quantification and fMRI-activation. We also analyzed possible effects of ipsilateral activation/deactivation on hand dominance.

Results

Our study replicated prior findings on the impact of increasing demand on decreasing lateralization M1 in a comparable amount for both right- and left-handed. White matter quantification of the pyramidal tract generating from M1 was also comparable for both groups (1). We did not find any effects for ipsilateral deactivation (negative BOLD) and handedness as reported before (2) but right-handed participants showed higher ipsilateral M1-activation.

Conclusion

Our data support prior findings that handedness does not impact lateralization of functional representation of the dominant or non-dominant hand even if different complexity dimensions are tested. Ipsilateral activation was stronger in right-handed participants pointing to a stronger suppression of the non-dominant M1 during dominant hand use in right handers.
