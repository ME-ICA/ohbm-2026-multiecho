Introduction

Tau pathology is a central feature of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and is closely linked to disease progression and cognitive decline. Beyond its regional distribution, converging evidence suggests that tau accumulation disrupts large-scale brain function by altering spontaneous neuronal activity and network dynamics. The amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF) quantifies slow (generally 0.01–0.08 Hz) BOLD signal oscillations from resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) and is commonly interpreted as a proxy for local spontaneous neuronal activity, providing complementary information to the well-known connectivity-based measures. ALFF alterations have been demonstrated across the AD continuum, including preclinical stages, and preferentially involve medial temporal, posterior cingulate, precuneus, temporoparietal, and frontal regions overlapping canonical AD-vulnerable networks1. To date, studies directly linking tau burden to ALFF remain limited. However, a recent study has reported region-specific associations within medial temporal structures in preclinical and prodromal populations, particularly in the entorhinal cortex, suggesting a link between tau pathology and local intrinsic functional alterations2. It remains unclear whether tau–ALFF relationships extend beyond medial temporal regions or vary across different stages of AD. In addition, analyses that focus on predefined regions and predominantly employ univariate approaches may fail to capture the distributed and interdependent patterns of tau-related functional disruption.

Here, we apply multivariate Partial Least Squares (PLS) analysis to derive latent patterns of covariance between whole-brain tau burden and ALFF across the AD continuum, aiming at identifying spatially distributed tau-functional relationships possibly linked to disease stage and cognitive impairment.

Methods

A total of 265 participants were selected from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), including 128 cognitively normal and amyloid-negative individuals (CN Aβ−; age 71 ± 6 years, 61% female), 57 CN and Aβ positive individuals (CN Aβ+; age 75 ± 8 years, 61% female), and 80 cognitively impaired and Aβ+ individuals (CI Aβ+; mean age 76 ± 8 years, 46% female; MCI or AD). All participants underwent tau-PET (18F-flortaucipir) and rs-fMRI within six months.

Rs-fMRI data were pre-processed using a combined FSL-ANTs pipeline, including slice-timing, rigid-body motion correction, brain extraction, and spatial smoothing with a 6 mm FWHM Gaussian kernel. Denoising was performed by regressing out nuisance signals derived from the mean time series extracted from white matter (WM), cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) masks, along with linear trend component, head motion regressors that included the six rigid-body realignment parameters and their first-order temporal derivatives, all of which were demeaned prior to regression. The resulting denoised functional images were then nonlinearly normalized to MNI space via subject-specific T1-weighted images. ALFF was computed from denoised data by transforming voxel-wise time series into the frequency domain, extracting the mean absolute amplitude within the 0.01–0.08 Hz band, and normalizing the voxel-wise values by the mean ALFF within the subject-specific gray matter (GM) mask. Tau-PET images were spatially normalized to MNI space and intensity-normalized using the inferior cerebellum as a reference region. Both ALFF and tau-PET measures were summarized across 200 regions defined by the Schaefer parcellation, organized according to the seven-network cortical partition proposed by Yeo et al.3

Demographic variables were compared across groups using the Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous measures and the χ² test for categorical variables. All regional imaging measures were z-standardized and deconfounded according to demographic variables showing significant differences and the adjusted measures were used in all subsequent analyses. Between-group effects on regional ALFF and tau-PET were evaluated using a FDR-corrected Kruskal-Wallis test, followed by Tukey post-hoc comparisons. Multivariate relationships between regional ALFF and tau-PET were then examined using PLS analysis. In this framework, the X variable consisted of a subject-by-region matrix of regional ALFF values across the 200 cortical regions, while the Y variable consisted of the corresponding subject-by-region matrix of regional tau-PET measures. The optimal number of components was selected to explain at least 60% of the cumulative cross-covariance between X and Y. The PLS model was estimated using the NIPALS algorithm without internal scaling. Statistical significance of each latent component was assessed using the permutation test (1,000 permutations), and the stability of regional weights was evaluated using bootstrap resampling (10,000 iterations, FDR-correction)4. For components surviving permutation testing and showing significant regional contributions after bootstrap resampling, subject-level scores were extracted for both ALFF and tau-PET. Group differences in component scores were assessed using the Kruskal–Wallis test, followed by post-hoc comparisons where appropriate, to evaluate whether the expression of the latent covariance patterns differed across diagnostic groups.

Results

Group comparisons revealed widespread between-group differences in regional tau-PET. CI Aβ+ individuals showed markedly higher tau burden than CN Aβ+ across extensive cortical regions, including visual, parietal, frontal, and temporal association areas. In contrast, differences between CN Aβ+ and CN Aβ− were already detectable, but spatially constrained, primarily in the orbitofrontal and temporal limbic regions, as well as in some salience and default-mode prefrontal areas. Together, this patterns indicate a progressive increase in tau burden from CN Aβ− to CN Aβ+, followed by a pronounced and spatially widespread neocortical spread associated with clinical impairment. In contrast, ALFF group differences were spatially restricted and network specific. Relative to CN Aβ−, CI Aβ+ showed significantly lower ALFF in dorsal attention and frontoparietal control regions, particularly within posterior dorsal attention areas and lateral parietal cortex, alongside higher ALFF in limbic regions involving the temporal pole, as well as in salience or ventral attention and somatomotor areas. A highly similar pattern was observed when comparing CI Aβ+ with CN Aβ+, with reduced ALFF in frontoparietal control regions and increased ALFF in limbic, salience-related, and somatomotor regions. No significant ALFF differences were detected between CN Aβ− and CN Aβ+.

Beyond regional and network-level group differences, the multivariate association between regional ALFF and tau-PET was examined using PLS analysis. Eight latent components were retained to account for the targeted proportion of total cross-covariance. Permutation testing indicated that the overall model captured a significant cross-covariance structure between ALFF and tau-PET (p < 0.001). Among these, only the first component showed robust regional contributions after bootstrap correction and was therefore selected for further interpretation. This component accounted for 45% of the total cross-covariance. Bootstrap analysis revealed that significant tau weights were widely distributed across the cortex, consistent with a diffuse tau-related pattern. In contrast, ALFF weights were spatially restricted and showed network specificity (Fig. 1B). ALFF contributions displayed an opposite sign relative to tau in dorsal attention, frontoparietal control, and default mode networks, whereas concordant tau–ALFF weights were observed in a subset of visual regions. Together, these results indicate that the ALFF–tau covariance is primarily shaped by widespread tau pathology, with selective and network-specific functional correlates.

Conclusion

This study identifies a tau-driven latent axis linking molecular pathology to spontaneous neural activity across the AD continuum. At the group level, ALFF alterations revealed a dissociation between networks, characterized by reduced intrinsic activity in the dorsal attention and frontoparietal control systems, and increased ALFF in limbic and temporal pole regions in CI Aβ+ individuals. This pattern is consistent with prior rs-fMRI evidence indicating reduced efficiency of higher-order attentional and control networks, alongside relative hyperactivity in medial temporal regions, likely reflecting a network imbalance rather than effective compensation5. 

Beyond categorical group differences, multivariate analysis revealed that tau burden was the primary driver of the shared ALFF–tau covariance. While tau contributions were spatially widespread across the cortex, ALFF alterations were selectively expressed within specific large-scale networks. In this covariance pattern, higher-order association networks and visual regions contributed in distinct and opposing directions, highlighting network-specific involvement of spontaneous activity in relation to tau pathology. At the subject level, the latent component organized individuals along a continuum that broadly reflected inter-individual variation in overall tau burden, while preserving network-specific functional signatures.

Together, these findings suggest that tau accumulation may define a global pathological dimension, while its functional correlates are expressed in a network-specific manner. By integrating tau-PET and rs-fMRI within a multivariate framework, this work demonstrates that latent modelling can capture significant tau-functional relationships that may be overlooked in univariate analyses, shedding light on the way tau pathology relates and possibly reshapes intrinsic brain dynamics across the AD spectrum.
