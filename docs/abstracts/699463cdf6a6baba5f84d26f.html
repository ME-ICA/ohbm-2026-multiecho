Introduction

Alcohol use disorder (AUD) is characterized by a disrupted functioning of the prefrontal cortex (PFC), with studies showing increased activity in the PFC if individuals are presented with alcohol-related cues. At the same time, research also reports hypoactivity in the PFC, more specifically in the dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, when individuals are presented with inhibitory control tasks.1 Excessive salience is attributed to drug-related stimuli and the ability to inhibit disadvantageous behavior is decreased which, together with increased craving, elevates the risk of repeated relapse.1 Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) has been shown to be a promising treatment option in substance use disorders by stimulating the prefrontal cortex, however, literature is still inconsistent regarding the lateralization of the optimal electrode placement.2      
Resting state EEG, the activity of the brain at rest, is a neurophysiological correlate for disrupted functioning in the brain. Especially the alpha frequency range between 8 and 14 Hertz has been of interest since it is thought to represent active inhibition of sensory input processing.3 Studies investigating changes of the brain at rest in patients with AUD have found decreased alpha power as well as lower alpha mean frequencies, suggesting a higher level of arousal and deficiencies in attention.4 Examining resting state alpha band power as well as peak alpha frequency (PAF) in individuals with alcohol use disorder before and after a longitudinal tDCS stimulation has the potential to provide important information on the efficacy of tDCS as an add-on treatment for patients with AUD and to give insights into the mechanisms of the addicted brain.

Methods

Within the project funded by Wellcome Leap as a part of Untangling Addiction, we investigate the effects of tDCS as an add-on treatment in AUD on inhibitory control and craving by using neurophysiological correlates to examine the underlying neural mechanisms. From the total pilot sample of 28 participants, resting-state EEG data was analyzed for 22 individuals (mean age = 45.8 years). The target group consists of recently abstinent and detoxified AUD patients at the Department of Addictive Behavior and Addiction Medicine at the Central Institute of Mental Health in Mannheim, Germany and the Clinic for Addiction Therapy and Withdrawal at the Psychiatric Centre Nordbaden in Wiesloch, Germany. The present study attempts to produce beneficial effects of tDCS in craving within a shorter period, requiring sensitive and high-resolution measurements. By means of a randomized assignment, participants were part of one of six of the following groups: (1) anodal tDCS stimulation over the right DLPFC N = 5; (2) anodal tDCS stimulation over the left DLPFC N = 5; (3) anodal tDCS stimulation over the occipital cortex N = 5 (4) a sham-stimulation N = 7; (5) no tDCS stimulation but computerized inhibition training N = 2; (6) no tDCS stimulation and treatment as usual N = 4. The three tDCS groups receive  20-minute tDCS sessions using the Sooma Duo™ device (Sooma Oy, Helsinki, Finland) over five consecutive days at an intensity of 2 mA (≈0.08 mA/cm²) following a placebo-controlled single-blind study design. Before electrophysiological recording, all participants completed questionnaires and computer-based neuropsychological assessments. Resting state data in two conditions, eyes-open and eyes-closed, was collected for 3 minutes respectively from all groups at day one as a baseline (T1) and on day five (T5) as a post-treatment measure. Data was recorded with a 32-channel electrode cap in a sampling rate of 1000Hz using the BrainVision Recorder software package. The EEG signal was pre-processed offline using EEGLAB running within the MATLAB environment, which entailed resampling to 250Hz, filtering, manual channel rejection, interpolation of missing channels, re-referencing to the average and conducting an independent component analysis with consecutive noise rejection. Finally, the EEG was segmented into 5-second epochs. Resting-state EEG epochs were subjected to frequency-domain analysis using a multitaper Fast Fourier Transformation (2–30 Hz), yielding power spectra averaged across trials. A complex of posterior–occipital channels were extracted as an area of interest to compute mean alpha-band (8–14 Hz) power and identify individual peak alpha characteristics. Mean alpha band power and peak alpha frequency per participant were analyzed using linear regression models.

Results

A direct comparison of the eyes-open and eyes-closed condition at T1 by means of a linear model shows a significant difference between both conditions, with the eyes-closed condition showing significantly higher alpha power, consistent with prevalent resting state literature.  Linear models with average alpha power or peak alpha frequency on participant level as outcome variables and time (T1 and T5) as well as group as predictor variables were fitted for the eyes-closed condition. The interaction model did not yet yield significant group differences or differences between T1 and T5. However, a descriptive increase of alpha power and peak alpha frequency after the tDCS stimulations compared to the baseline could be observed, specifically for the groups receiving tDCS stimulation in the left or right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, while the sham group did not display said descriptive increase.

Conclusion

The pilot results provide first information to show the efficacy of longitudinal tDCS stimulations in individuals with alcohol use disorder using resting state alpha power and peak frequency as neurophysiological correlates. Further analysis in a larger sample should be conducted to provide statistically reliable evidence, which is expected to be achieved throughout the ongoing data gathering and analysis process in this project. In addition, by comparing the tDCS treatment groups to a non-clinical control group, resting state alpha power and peak frequency could have the potential to act as a predictor for whether tDCS as an add-on treatment can be effective for patients with alcohol use disorder on an individual basis. Triangulating the resting state data with craving data could further act as potential biomarker for the severity of the maladaptive neural changes at baseline, consequently predict negative treatment outcomes and warrant an add-on tDCS treatment.
