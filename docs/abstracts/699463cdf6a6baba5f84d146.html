Introduction

Motion sickness (MS) is a common uncomfortable response to sensory conflicts, particularly between visual-vestibular stimulation. Individual susceptibility to these conflicts varies significantly, and the underlying neural mechanisms remain unclear. Recent functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) [1] and electroencephalography (EEG) [2, 3, 4] studies have revealed that activity in the parietal cortex is closely related to motion sickness susceptibility. However, the role of occipito–parietal functional connectivity, relative to local activation, in distinguishing MS-susceptible from MS-resistant individuals has not been systematically investigated. To address this gap, we used fNIRS to examine occipito–parietal HbO activation and functional connectivity during coherent rotation (COH) and random (RAN) visual stimulation in relation to MS, and to compare these features as fNIRS predictors of motion sickness susceptibility.

Methods

Participants and task: Twenty right-handed healthy adults (18–24 years) took part in the study. Trait motion sickness susceptibility was quantified with the Motion Sickness Susceptibility Questionnaire (MSSQ), and state symptoms were indexed on each trial using the Fast Motion Sickness (FMS) scale. Participants, seated throughout, viewed wide-field visual motion in a blocked design in which coherent rotation (COH) and random (RAN) motion blocks were interleaved with rest (rest–COH–rest–RAN–rest); COH simulated consistent self-rotation, whereas RAN served as a directionally random control condition. 

fNIRS analysis: HbO time series were preprocessed in Homer3. For each predefined cluster, block-averaged HbO changes for COH and RAN were extracted as activation features, and cluster-wise HbO time series were used to compute functional connectivity (Pearson correlations between clusters). Finally, linear SVM classifiers with leave-one-subject-out cross-validation (LOOCV) were then trained on activation or FC features to distinguish MS-susceptible from MS-resistant groups. Label-permutation tests (N = 5000) were used to assess above-chance AUC and to compare the informativeness of activation versus connectivity features.

Results

Figure 1a,b show a right parietal cluster around P4 that exhibited a significant main effect of group, with larger COH-evoked HbO responses in MS-susceptible than in MS-resistant participants (channel-wise t≈1.5–2.3, p < 0.05). This spatial pattern is consistent with previous findings implicating right parietal/temporo-parietal regions in motion sickness and visual–vestibular conflict processing. Using these cluster-level HbO features, a linear SVM classified MS-susceptible versus MS-resistant individuals with a LOOCV AUC of 0.709; however, this did not achieve statistical significance in the permutation test (p = 0.054). In contrast, the FC-based model achieved a substantially higher AUC of 0.872 with a highly significant permutation p-value (p=0.0002; Figure 1d), suggesting that large-scale HbO connectivity encodes more information about motion sickness susceptibility than regional activation alone.
