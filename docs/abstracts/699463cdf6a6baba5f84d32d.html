Introduction

Most cortico-cortical connections follow the Exponential Distance Rule (EDR), whereby the probability of a connection decays exponentially with spatial distance [1]. However, a subset of connections deviates from this rule, forming so-called topologically complex connections (TCCs), also called EDR exceptions, and most of them are long-range connections [2]. Although sparse, these connections are hypothesized to play a critical role in coordinating distributed neural activity. The functional contributions of TCCs to large-scale brain dynamics remain poorly understood.

Methods

We used a whole-brain neural mass model to investigate how TCCs influence attractor dynamics [3]. The human structural connectome was derived from diffusion MRI tractography of the HCP dataset, and TCCs were identified as edges exceeding predicted weights under the EDR fit. We compared the attractor dynamics across three different model brains to identify the effect of TCCs on brain attractor dynamics, and quantified the attractor dynamics by computing the number of attractors, transition energy between attractors (average and maximum), and the overall diversity of the attractor repertoire.

Results

Models including TCCs exhibited significantly reduced average and maximum transition energies between attractors compared to models without them. In addition, the presence of TCCs expanded the attractor repertoire, leading to a richer and more diverse attractor landscape. These effects highlight the disproportionate role of a small number of spatially atypical TCCs in shaping brain dynamics.

Conclusion

TCCs enhance the efficiency and diversity of large-scale neural dynamics by lowering energetic barriers and broadening the repertoire of attractors. These findings suggest that spatially atypical TCCs, though rare, are critical for supporting the diversity and flexibility of brain attractor dynamics, with implications for understanding the structural constraints underlying cognition and behavior.
