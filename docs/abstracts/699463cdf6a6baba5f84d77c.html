Introduction

Large-scale pediatric neuroimaging is essential to understand how environmental exposures shape attentional development. The SEPAGES cohort (https://cohorte-sepages.fr) provides a unique framework linking detailed exposome measures to cognition and brain development, yet MRI acquisition in children remains challenging. To address this, we developed at the IRMaGe Facility a fully gamified MRI environment, transforming the scanner into a narrative adventure set in the Zoo de Beauval, guided by standardized video sequences featuring two virtual zookeepers. This immersive protocol was designed to maximize engagement, reduce anxiety, and improve data quality. We adapted two tasks from the CAM (Computerized Attention Measure) battery (1) validated on more than 800 preteens and added a visuospatial working-memory task. We assessed feasibility, replication of CAM behavioral signatures in the MRI context, and the neural correlates of attentional processes.

Methods

A total of 75 children (age 9) have been included to date, from a planned sample of 200 participants, with data collection currently ongoing. MRI was performed at 3T (Achieva dStream 3.0T TX, Philips, NL) at IRMaGe MRI facility (Grenoble, France). Functional images were collected using a T2-weighted EPI sequence (TR = 2500 ms, TE = 30 ms, flip angle = 80°, voxel size = 3 × 3 × 3 mm, 42 slices). The narrative environment, zoo-themed decorations and story-based transitions were used throughout structural and functional sequences (Figure 1). Feasibility metrics included refusal, motion, and MRIQC scores; 96% of fMRI datasets were retained. MRIQC confirmed excellent data quality, with low motion (mean FD = 0.24 mm) and high tSNR (Mean = 57)/SNR (Mean = 3.90) values, supporting the effectiveness of the gamified protocol in stabilizing children during scanning. Functional tasks included CAM-derived Selective Attention and Inhibition, as well as Working Memory. Standard preprocessing and GLMs were applied. Second-level analyses included group contrasts, ROI analyses, and voxelwise brain–behavior regressions using CAM performance.

Results

Exceptional feasibility and engagement: The gamified protocol yielded 95% acceptance and 96% usable fMRI datasets. Motion was low, and children consistently reported the session as fun and non-threatening[SLC1] ; remarkable outcomes for population-based MRI at this age. 

Replication of CAM behavioral signatures: In-scanner performance was statistically equivalent to CAM school-based norms (Two One-Sided Tests, TOST, all p < .05; deviations <0.2 SD). The selective-attention set-size × presence interaction and the strong No-Go inhibition cost replicated the CAM signatures with comparable effect sizes. Inter-task associations fell within CAM confidence intervals. 

Neurofunctional extension of CAM: NoGo > Go contrast elicited robust activation across the canonical inhibitory-control network, including right inferior frontal gyrus (rIFG), pre-SMA / anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), dorsolateral and ventrolateral prefrontal cortex, basal ganglia, posterior parietal cortex and insula. This pattern is fully consistent with modern models of action stopping in children. Inhibition performance significantly predicted activation strength in rIFG and ACC (pFWE < .05). Selective-attention contrasts revealed strong recruitment of the dorsal attention network (DAN), including intraparietal sulcus / superior parietal lobule (IPS/SPL), dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), anterior cingulate cortex, thalamus and insula (2,3). This pattern matches contemporary models of attentional filtering and aligns closely with the CAM behavioral signatures (set-size × presence effects). Working memory load produced the expected fronto-parietal pattern: bilateral DLPFC, superior parietal cortex (IPS/SPL), premotor cortex, thalamus. These activations replicate large-scale meta-analytic findings (4) and demonstrate high stability of fMRI signals in this pediatric cohort. Additionally, voxelwise regressions revealed that better inhibition performance predicted stronger NoGo>Go activation in right IFG and ACC (pFWE < .05), while better selective-attention performance predicted stronger recruitment of the dorsal attention network for high vs low set size (pFWE < .05). Working-memory load elicited expected DLPFC–IPS activation patterns. These results provide the first neurofunctional validation of CAM-derived attentional components. 

Conclusion

A fully gamified, child-centered MRI protocol with experimented fieldworkers, enables high-quality, low-motion neuroimaging in children, with exceptional acceptability (95%) and data retention (96%) in a large population cohort. This protocol yielded robust brain–behavior relationships and highly consistent activation patterns across tasks, fully aligned with contemporary neurocognitive models of inhibition, attentional selection, and working memory. Together with the demonstrated behavioral equivalence to CAM norms, these findings represent the first neurofunctional extension of the CAM battery in children and highlight the exceptional data quality achieved through gamified MRI. This approach provides a strong foundation for investigating how early-life environmental exposures in SEPAGES shape attentional development and brain function.
