Introduction

Diffusion MRI tractography provides dense reconstructions of white matter pathways that play an important role in connectomics, tractometry, and surgical planning.[4] Yet the resulting tractograms contain millions of streamlines that must be parcellated into anatomically meaningful bundles while rejecting false positives. Manual delineation is laborious, subjective, and poorly suited to large multi-site cohorts or time-critical clinical workflows.[5] Automatic parcellation methods traditionally rely on deformable registration to population atlases, characterized by high computational cost and faltering when lesions distort anatomy or truncate streamlines. Registration-free approaches such as TractCloud alleviate some of these limitations, but still process each streamline with a dedicated local-global context, limiting throughput and subjecting performance to tractogram centering choices.[3]

We present RapidParc, a registration-free Transformer-based approach that treats streamlines as tokens within a global self-attention context. RapidParc processes many subtractograms in parallel, exchanging contextual information across thousands of streamlines at once, thereby reducing inference costs while preserving translational invariance through [-1,1] unit cube normalization. The model is designed to handle anatomical disruptions, including cases post-hemispherotomy featuring missing or highly displaced tracts. We benchmark RapidParc against TractCloud and analyze ablations that disentangle the effect of context design and tractogram centering. Our evaluation spans the TractCloud benchmark dataset and two clinical cohorts. RapidParc delivers state-of-the-art accuracy, two orders-of-magnitude faster inference compared to TractCloud, and improved lesion robustness.

Methods

We employed RapidParc on the TractCloud training and test data.[3] The primary dataset comprises 100 Human Connectome Project subjects with whole-brain tractograms clustered into 1600 streamline prototypes that map onto 42 anatomical bundles plus an outlier class via the ORG atlas taxonomy.[5] RapidParc processes batches of randomly sampled subtractograms of 2000 streamlines (context size) drawn from single subjects. Each streamline is resampled to 15 vertices, flattened into a 45-dimensional vector, and projected to a 128-dimensional token embedding via a learned linear layer. Tokens are normalized to [-1,1]3 per axis, implicitly centering tractograms without assuming symmetry. During training, every subtractogram undergoes stochastic augmentations: random rotations (±45° left-right, ±10° anterior-posterior and superior-inferior) Gaussian jitter (σ =0.001), and orientation flips applied with probability 0.5. Hemisphere-dropout augmentation is optionally activated by sampling only streamlines with centroids x̄ < 0 or x̄ ≥ 0 for 30 % of batches, encouraging the model to rely on contralateral context when ipsilesional fibers are absent.


The network backbone is an eight-layer transformer encoder with single-head self-attention and 256-unit feed-forward blocks, regularized with dropout 0.1 and layer normalization. A two-layer classifier culminating in 1600 logits predicts ORG cluster memberships, which are mapped to 42 bundle labels and one outlier class during evaluation. We optimize cross-entropy loss using Adam with learning rate 8.5×10-4, weight decay 10-3, and cosine annealing over 10,000 epochs. Training leverages two NVIDIA A40 GPUs (48 GB) with distributed data parallelism and batch size 64, amounting to 5.25 hours wall-clock time. RapidParc variants explore different evaluation context sizes (500 and 2000 streamlines), hemisphere-dropout augmentation, and CPU-only inference.

To compare performance to TractCloud, we reproduce TractCloud and implement two ablations: GlobalTractCloud, which removes the local contextualization for each streamline, and TractCloudbb, which replaces the mean-based tractogram alignment with bounding-box centering similar to RapidParc's implicit normalization. External qualitative validation uses examples from four unseen datasets (dHCP, ABCD, HCP, PPMI), while a clinical cohort of 22 individuals post-hemispherotomy, 30 individuals post sAH and 20 healthy controls tests lesion robustness. Clinical tractograms originate from 3T Siemens Magnetom Trio acquisitions, undergo FSL-based preprocessing, UKF-based low-rank tracking, and streamline filtering below 40 mm. [1,2]

All experiments compute accuracy, macro-averaged F1, per-tract precision/recall, and inference runtime inclusive of local streamline contextualization overhead for methods that require such features. We additionally log GPU memory footprint and CPU inference latency. Statistical significance between paired method outputs is assessed via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Bonferroni correction across tracts. For hemispherotomy analyses we exclude commissural bundles and report group-level medians and ranges.

Results

RapidParc achieved state-of-the-art benchmark performance while reducing inference time by two orders of magnitude. On the TractCloud test set (200k streamlines), RapidParc with context size 2000 attained 94.44% streamline-level accuracy and 93.20% macro F1, surpassing TractCloud by +2.32 and +2.98 percentage points, respectively, and improving over GlobalTractCloud by +2.26 and +3.06 points. Evaluation on the 6.2M-streamline synthetic transform augmentation (STA) benchmark retained high accuracy (93.86%) with macro F1 92.36% when using hemisphere-dropout augmentation. On this dataset, RapidParc completed inference in 5.44 seconds on a single NVIDIA A40, compared to 703.92 seconds for TractCloud (129 × slower) and 117.61 seconds for GlobalTractCloud. Reducing the evaluation context to 500 streamlines further shortened runtime to 3.15 seconds (223 × faster than TractCloud) while maintaining accuracy above 92.6%. GPU memory usage peaked at 9.42 GB, dropping to 3.54 GB for the smaller context, enabling deployment on mid-range hardware. CPU-only inference on a 6th-generation Intel i7 processed the STA dataset in under four minutes, demonstrating practicality for resource-limited clinics.

RapidParc generalizes well across a variety of external data such as dHCP, ABCD, HCP, and PPMI tractograms, RapidParc consistently matched or exceeded TractCloud, with median macro F1 improvements of 1.6-3.4 percentage points and visibly cleaner bundle delineations in qualitative assessments. In the hemispherotomy cohort, where commissural bundles are surgically severed and ipsilateral tracts severely disrupted, RapidParc improved median macro F1 by 6.1 points relative to TractCloud (p<0.01) and reduced variance across subjects. Hemisphere-dropout augmentation yielded substantial gains across several tracts compared to TractCloud. Bounding-box centering contributed an improvement over center-of-mass centering in TractCloudbb, affirming the importance of robust normalization under asymmetric white matter loss.

Ablation analyses revealed that TractCloud's local neighborhood branch contributes little to accuracy but accounts for much of the runtime. GlobalTractCloud ran 1.8× faster yet still trailed RapidParc by nearly 3 macro-F1 points. In contrast, RapidParc variants without hemisphere-dropout and with reduced context size maintained comparatively higher scores, indicating that global self-attention suffices to aggregate discriminative features for tractogram parcellation. Runtime profiling highlighted that RapidParc, because the model operates on independent subtractograms, seamlessly streams batches and therefore sustains throughput on tractograms containing millions of streamlines without exceeding memory limits.

Conclusion

RapidParc establishes accuracy on par with or superior to atlas-registered methods, inference fast enough for million-streamline tractograms in seconds, and robustness to severe white-matter disruptions. Its transformer backbone replaces local-global context graphs with learned global self-attention, allowing thousands of streamlines to share context efficiently. Hemisphere-dropout augmentation and implicit bounding-box normalization enable clinically relevant generalization to hemispherotomy patients without additional fine-tuning. The resulting speedups allow for CPU-only inference in low-resource environments lacking modern GPUs.

Beyond tract labeling, RapidParc's global contextualization opens possible future directions for the anlysis of tractogram parcellation such as the visualization of subtractogram attention. Our findings collectively demonstrate that global-context transformers can simultaneously deliver accuracy, robustness, and throughput unattainable by prior approaches.
