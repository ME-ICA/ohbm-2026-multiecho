Introduction

Sleep disturbance is highly prevalent in chronic pain, with recent evidence suggesting that poor sleep further exacerbates pain1. Slow wave sleep, or stage N3 sleep, is particularly important in relation to pain 2,3. Slow wave sleep is underpinned by the presence of delta waves (0.5–4 Hz), with somewhat inconsistent reports of delta power associated with decreased pain4, or increased pain5 . Sex differences were previously suggested to moderate the relationship between the effect of sleep on pain1. However, very few studies3 investigated sex differences in the relationship between delta power and pain. The current study examined whether the relationship between delta power and pain severity differs by sex in individuals with osteoarthritis. 

Methods

Sixty-eight participants (N=42 female) were recruited into the current study. Participants completed three nights of home-based EEG sleep monitoring (Somfit®, Compumedics) and self-reported their average pain over the last two weeks on a numerical rating scale (NRS) 0-10. Relative delta power in the 0.5-4 Hz range was computed from one of the night recordings using the ‘bandpower’ function of the YASA 0.6.5 toolbox. Outliers in the relative delta were removed using the interquartile range (IQR) method. Multiple regression was conducted focusing on the delta power × sex interaction on NRS, controlling for age. 

Results

A significant delta power × sex interaction emerged (t(51) = 2.289, p = .026, with a medium effect size Cohen’s f=0.32). In female participants lower delta power was associated with higher pain, whereas men showed the opposite pattern. 

Conclusion

In addition to previously documented sex differences in the effect of poor sleep on pain in healthy young adults1 and chronic widespread musculoskeletal pain3, we here report the sex-dependent relationship between delta power and pain in an osteoarthritis population. These findings highlight differences in how lower power in the delta frequency range affects men and women with osteoarthritis, underscoring the role of delta power during sleep for women. The potential of interventions increasing the duration of slow wave sleep and delta power with the aim of improving pain needs to be assessed, considering potentially different outcomes across sexes.
