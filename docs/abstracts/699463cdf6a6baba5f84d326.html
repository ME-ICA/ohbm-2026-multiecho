Introduction

Suicide attempts and suicidal ideation are prevalent in individuals with psychiatric disorders, especially those with bipolar disorder (BD), for whom the suicide risk is approximately 20 times greater than that of the general population.^1 Previous neuroimaging studies have reported that patients with suicide ideation in major depressive disorder (MDD) exhibit hippocampal subfield abnormalities in the subiculum and dentate gyrus, whereas reduced thalamic volume has been specifically associated with suicide attempts. However, the structural brain alterations associated with suicidal ideation and attempts in patients with BD remain elusive, particularly within the limbic system and the thalamus. Furthermore, given that the adolescent brain is undergoing maturation, previous studies suggest that the relationship between suicidal behavior and neuroplasticity must be interpreted separately in youths and adults.^2 This study aimed to investigate the association between suicidal behaviors (suicidal ideation and suicide attempts) and the volumes of the limbic system (specifically the amygdala, hippocampus, and thalamus) in patients with BD. We analyzed the youth and adult groups separately to determine potential age-specific mechanisms.

Methods

We collected MRI and psychological/clinical assessment data from 186 patients with BD. The psychological/clinical assessment included the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS), Young Mania Rating Scale (YMRS), Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS), and the history of suicide attempts. Suicidal ideation is defined as the MADRS suicide item (item 10) score of 2 or greater. Patients were subgrouped based on the presence or absence of suicidal ideation (denoted as SI and NSI, respectively) and suicide attempts (SA and NSA, respectively). High-resolution T1-weighted brain images were obtained using a 3-Tesla MRI system (Discovery MR 750, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, USA). We employed automated parcellation using the FreeSurfer software package to segment the hippocampus, amygdala, thalamus, and its subfields. To investigate age-related differences, participants were divided into youth (n = 95, ≤24 years) and adult (n = 91, >24 years) group. Separate two-way ANCOVAs with Bonferroni correction were conducted on the volumes of hippocampal, amygdalar, and thalamic subfields for both cohorts, with SA and SI as independent variables and age, sex, and whole gray matter volume as covariates.

Results

In the adult BD group, significant interactions between SA and SI were observed in the left hippocampal-amygdala transition area (HATA) (F=7.1, p=0.009), left CA1 head (F=4.1, p=0.047), and bilateral granule cell-molecular layer of the dentate gyrus (GC-ML-DG) head (left: F=4.8, p=0.032; right: F=4.0, p=0.049), and medial geniculate nucleus (MGN) (F=4.306, p=0.041) (Table 1). Post-hoc pairwise comparisons for adult BD group with SA revealed that patients with SI exhibited greater volume increase in the left HATA (p = 0.013) and GC-ML-DG (p = 0.040) head compared to the NSI group. Additionally, adult BD group without a suicide attempt (NSA) showed a significantly larger volume of the left MGN (p = 0.041) in the group with SI than NSI (Figure 1). However, there was no interaction between SA and SI in the youth BD group.

Conclusion

In the present study, we identified a significant interaction between suicide ideation and suicide attempt on the volume of the left hippocampal HATA and the bilateral GC-ML-DG head in the adult BD group only. These regions are involved in emotional contextualization, pattern separation, and stress responsivity. The HATA integrates amygdala-driven affective salience with hippocampal contextual memory, and the GC-ML-DG head is a core site of neurogenesis, stress-related plasticity, and encoding of new emotional experiences. According to the Three-Step Theory of suicide, progression from suicidal ideation to attempt is determined by the capability for suicide, which is dependent on genetic, dispositional, acquired, life experience, and practical factors.^3 We suggest that enlargement of these structures, specifically in adults who exhibit both suicidal ideation and attempts, may reflect activity-dependent remodeling of emotion memory circuits repeatedly engaged during recurrent or intense suicidal crises. However, this interaction was not observed in youth patients with BD. We speculate that this may be related to a lower incidence of recurrent crises and life stress compared to adults, which may be insufficient to induce significant activity-dependent remodeling. Identifying these specific structural alterations provides crucial insight into the biological mechanisms that distinguish suicide attempts from ideation in patients with BD.
