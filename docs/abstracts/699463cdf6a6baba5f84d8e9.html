Introduction

Large-scale collaborative efforts, such as the ENIGMA consortium, have demonstrated the power of coordinated multi-study analyses to identify replicable and generalizable microstructural signatures across diverse diffusion MRI (dMRI) protocols and geographically distributed study populations. To date, these effects have largely been characterized by averaging microstructural properties along coarse regions of interest along the white matter (WM) skeleton. Tractometry is an emerging technique to measure the local microstructural variations along tracts, providing high-resolution maps of individual-level WM variation for the study of genetic, developmental, and illness-related brain circuit disruption. However, tractography, bundle segmentation pipelines, and downstream tractometry analyses are highly sensitive to site, scanner hardware, acquisition protocols, atlas selection, and processing choices [1]. Developing a standardized approach for along-tract analyses that generalizes across independently collected study samples requires extensive validation, testing, and benchmarking. Here, we introduce the ENIGMA-Tractometry toolbox, a standardized end-to-end tractometry pipeline developed in collaboration with the widely used DSI-Studio [2] that includes a custom WM atlas and user-friendly quality assurance tools to support scalable along-tract statistical analyses.

Methods

The toolbox includes: 1) a streamline-based symmetric WM atlas; 2) automated and optimized tractography and segmentation methods; 3) a quality control workflow and visualization tools for segmented bundles; and 4) streamline- and volumetric-based tractometry methods for deriving along-tract microstructural profiles for multi-site statistical analysis (Figure 1a). 

Atlas. Starting from the HCP-1065 atlas, we built a symmetric 82 bundle WM atlas with uniform streamline density to avoid biases from left–right asymmetries in anatomical definitions. Several bundles were redefined to either 1) improve functional interpretability or 2) ensure coherent bundle morphology and directionality to support reliable along-tract analyses (Figure 2b). 

Tractography Optimization + Validation. WM bundle reconstruction was performed in DSI-Studio using modified default parameters to improve compatibility and reproducibility across variable dMRI acquisition protocols and image quality. Modifications included automatic atlas field-of-view adjustment for cropped scans, fixing the step size to 0.5 for all bundles, and revised tractography and segmentation parameters for 69 of 82 bundles to increase coverage, reduce spurious streamlines, and accommodate sharp curvature or complex bundle geometry. Segmentation quality under default and optimized parameters was evaluated using volumetric Dice coefficients and streamline-based shape-similarity metrics as defined in [4], complemented by independent rater visual quality assessments.

Visual Quality Assurance. We implemented visualization tools for group- and subject-level QC, including subject-level summed tractograms and registration checks, and cohort probability-density maps for each bundle (Figure 1c). 

Datasets. Multiple heterogeneous datasets were used to assess versatility and robustness across cohorts. These included ABCD, ADNI, HABS-HD, HCP-Aging, NIMHANS, QTIM, and PING, which span a broad range of acquisition parameters and preprocessing pipelines (Figure 1d).  Test subjects were specifically selected to include a wide age range, poor image quality, high distortion, and neurodegeneration.

Multiverse Tractometry Feature Extraction. Along-tract microstructural profiles can be generated using two complementary methods: 1) Medial tractography analysis (MeTA) [3], a volumetric approach using hyperplane-based parcellation of the bundle’s core volume, and 2) Bundle analytics (BUAN) [4], a well-established approach using atlas bundle centroid proximity and weighted microstructural averaging. These tools are complemented by separate visualization packages: Diffusion Visualization and Explorer (DiVE) and trksnapshot, respectively.

Multi-Study Applications. We conducted several experiments to assess whether the ENIGMA Tractometry Toolbox was sensitive to known age- and neurodegenerative-related WM alterations in widely used study samples: age associations were examined in a subset of 168 participants (aged 55–80 yrs, 50% female) from UK Biobank, HCP-Aging, and CamCAN, while group differences between 273 cognitively normal (CN) and 137 mild cognitive impairment (MCI) participants were assessed across 5 ADNI3 dMRI protocols (aged 55–80 yrs, 50% female). We applied optimized DSI-Studio segmentation to the datasets and extracted MeTA and BUAN along-tract features for ENIGMA atlas WM bundles. Age effects on DTI FA and MD were tested using linear regressions adjusted for sex and dataset, and MCI versus CN DTI differences were modeled using linear mixed effects with age and sex modeled as fixed effects and imaging protocol as a random effect. Multiple comparisons were corrected using FDR across all bundles and segments within each method (q=0.05).

Results

For each of the 82 bundles, Dice coefficients and bundle-shape similarity indices between the ENIGMA Tractometry Atlas and bundles generated using either default DSI Studio parameters or modified, bundle-specific parameter settings were averaged across 57 scans. Paired t-tests showed significantly higher Dice coefficients (p=9.4x10-17) and bundle-shape similarity indices (p=2.6x10-32) for modified compared to default parameters. For 13 bundles with only minimal parameter changes (i.e., step size), the modified parameters performed worse than the default (Dice p=3.6x10-29; shape similarity p=2.0x10-21); for those, default parameters will therefore be retained in future. For the remaining 69 bundles, which incorporated more extensive bundle-specific parameter modifications, Dice coefficients and bundle-shape similarity were significantly improved across datasets (Dice p=2.3x10-23; shape similarity p=9.5x10-35). In these tracts, optimized tract reconstruction reduced the visual QC fail rate to 9.6% compared to 12.0% for tracts reconstructed based on the default parameters; this includes scans with highly compromised image quality. Examples of visual and quantitative improvements are illustrated in Figure 2a and 2b. 

In validation analyses, we found age and MCI diagnosis associations with local WM microstructure using both MeTA and BUAN approaches (PFDR<0.05). As expected, lower FA and higher MD were associated with both greater age and an MCI diagnosis. While effects were widespread, limbic tracts were consistently implicated (Figure 2c,d).

Conclusion

We developed an end-to-end ENIGMA tractometry toolbox that is robust to heterogeneous imaging quality and acquisition protocols and accessible to users with different levels of dMRI expertise. By standardizing tractography, segmentation, quality control, and along-tract analysis, the toolbox enables consistent, reproducible workflows across diverse datasets. Importantly, the pipeline reliably detected established age- and neurodegeneration-related WM microstructural signatures, consistent with prior findings, supporting its biological validity. Future work will test replicability/scalability when applied to the ENIGMA consortium dMRI samples, representing an even wider spectrum of neuroimaging acquisition, data quality and clinical phenotypes. The full toolbox, including code and documentation, will be publicly available on ENIGMA’s GitHub to support widespread adoption and collaborative research.
