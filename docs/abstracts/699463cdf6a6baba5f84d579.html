Introduction

We apply the Xi-AlphaNET model to analyze cortical activity, representing  EEG source aperiodic (Xi) and alpha-rhythm components as Hida–Matérn processes, constrained by structural priors 1. The model estimates source spectral parameters and effective connectivity using structural data from diffusion MRI (dMRI) and cortico-cortical evoked potentials (CCEP) 1. We decompose Spectral Granger Causality (SGC) to map feedforward and feedback connectivity patterns, using cortical myelination gradients (T1w/T2w) as a proxy for cortical hierarchy 2. Using Bayesian inversion on cross-spectral resting-state EEG data from 1,965 participants (ages 5–100, 9 countries) in the HarMNqEEG dataset 3, we estimate cortical activity, showing high test-retest reliability, effective connectivity, and spatial accuracy. The results reveal that the aperiodic component is localized in the frontal cortex, while the alpha-rhythm component is localized in the posterior cortex, with opposite directional networks: feedforward for Xi and feedback for alpha. These findings confirm the consistency of these patterns across the lifespan, validating the model in a large normative cohort.

Methods

We applied Xi-AlphaNET to the HarMNqEEG dataset to estimate cortical spectral parameters for each spectral process. These parameters were then mapped into spectral Granger causality (SGC) using the Geweke-Dhamala formula and the Xi-AlphaNET parametric spectral factorization for each participant. Feedforward and feedback connectivity were classified using the Directional Asymmetry Index (DAI), with cortical myelination (T1w/T2w) serving as a proxy for cortical hierarchy. Model performance was evaluated by benchmarking with 19 and 120 electrodes, comparing the results to high-density EEG (hdEEG) reference methods. Test-retest reliability was assessed using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) on independent eyes-closed sessions (N=60) 4. Spatial accuracy was quantified using Point-Spread Localization Error (PLE) and Spatial Dispersion (SD). Yeo-7 functional networks (Yeo-7NET) were used to summarize and organize the results.

Results

Benchmarking with 19 electrodes demonstrated that Xi-AlphaNET achieves strong spatial identifiability, with a Point-Spread Localization Error (PLE) of 35.9 mm, a Spatial Dispersion (SD) of 48.7 mm, and a Ratio of Spurious Activity (RSA) of 9.1%, closely matching or surpassing the hdEEG reference data. Test-retest reliability was high for alpha-band parameters (ICC = 0.70–0.86) and moderate for aperiodic (Xi) parameters (ICC = 0.45–0.70) [Fig. 1]. Directed connectivity analysis revealed feedback dominance in the alpha process (from frontoparietal to sensory regions) and feedforward dominance in the Xi process (from sensory regions to higher-order networks). These directional patterns were consistent across the lifespan, confirming the stability of alpha-band feedback and aperiodic feedforward connectivity in a large normative dataset.

In Fig. 2, the alpha process exhibits feedback-dominated connectivity, with edges originating from the frontoparietal, dorsal attention, and default-mode networks, and targeting the visual and somatomotor networks. This reflects top-down inhibitory and attentional control, particularly along FP↔DAN/DMN and DAN↔VIS pathways. In contrast, the Xi (aperiodic–delta) process exhibits feedforward-dominated connectivity, originating in sensory and limbic regions, projecting to FP and DMN networks. Prominent VIS→DAN/FP and SMN/LIM→DMN links suggest ascending drive from perceptual and subcortical areas to higher-order regions. These findings show a clear double dissociation between feedback alpha and feedforward Xi processes, supporting hierarchical models of neural oscillations 5.

A cross-dataset replication confirmed the robustness of these results. The model inverted on two independent datasets—a test-retest cohort (N = 60) and the large-scale HarMNqEEG dataset—yielded highly consistent spatial probability atlases, with nearly identical cortical localization gradients (r > 0.9, P_spin < 0.01). Directed connectivity patterns were qualitatively preserved, with the alpha process showing predominant feedback and the Xi process showing predominant feedforward organization.

Conclusion

This study uniquely addresses the gap in understanding the directed dynamics of effective connectivity in brain oscillations by demonstrating the consistency of feedforward and feedback connectivity patterns across the lifespan in a large normative dataset. Looking ahead, future work could incorporate additional spectral processes, specifically gamma, and explore cross-frequency interactions, particularly their role in feedforward connectivity, to deepen our understanding of neural dynamics across different frequency bands.
