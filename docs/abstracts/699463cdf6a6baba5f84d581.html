Introduction

Cerebral small vessel disease (SVD) is enhanced in patients with Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and contributes to cognitive worsening in AD. However, the biological underpinnings of SVD in AD remain unclear. The glycoprotein MFGE8 (also called lactadherin) and its cleavage product medin are key candidate proteins that have been associated with cerebrovascular dysfunction in aging mice1 and with cerebral amyloid angiopathy related alterations in mouse models of AD-like cerebral amyloid deposition2. Motivated by these preclinical findings, the major aim of the current study was to test whether alterations in MFGE8 and associated protein networks contribute to explain SVD in patients with AD. Using proteomics derived from cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), we investigated whether 1) CSF MFGE8 levels are associated with MRI-detected SVD markers in patients with AD, and 2) which proteome signatures may explain the association between MFGE8 and SVD in AD.
 

Methods

We included 606 individuals from the Alzheimer Dementia Cohort (ADC), EMIF-AD preclinAD and the 90+ study3. Eleven individuals were excluded due to a history of major infarcts, and two individuals were excluded as outliers with exceedingly low MFGE8 levels (more than 4 SDs from the mean), resulting in a final sample size of N = 593 (mean age = 65.5 yrs, SD = 9.4). Individuals were classified as abnormal (amyloid-positive) or anormal based on CSF amyloid 1-42 values using standard cut-off criteria for Alzheimer’s disease, resulting in n = 185 amyloid-negative cognitively normal controls and n = 408 amyloid-positive individuals (including those with normal cognition, mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer’s dementia). Quantitative proteomics in the CSF samples was conducted using liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) as previously described3. Analytes were normalized using the internal reference scaling method and z-score normalized to the mean concentration of each analyte in the amyloid-negative control group. Only analytes detectable in all subjects were retained (N = 1314 proteins) for further analysis. White matter hyperintensities were computed based on T1 MRI segmentations in all subjects. The number of lacunes was manually rated by expert readers based on T1, and the number of microbleeds was rated based on T2* images, which were available in a subset of individuals (N = 440). The SVD composite score was estimated as a latent variable combining WMH (adjusted for intracranial volume), lacunes and microbleeds in a structural equation model. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis was conducted to derive modules of correlated proteins across controls and amyloid-positive individuals. Linear regression analyses were used to test CSF MFGE8 as a predictor of SVD composite score and module eigengene values in amyloid positive subjects, controlling for age, sex, education and clinical diagnosis. Structural equation modelling was used to test module eigengene values as mediators of the association between MFGE8 and SVD composite scores.

Results


 Lower CSF MFGE8 levels were associated with higher SVD composite scores in amyloid positive individuals, controlling for clinical diagnosis and demographic covariates (B = -0.17, p = 0.004, Figure 1A). Weighted gene co-expression analysis applied to 1313 proteins (excluding MFGE8) resulted in 6 major protein modules that ranged from 50 to 549 proteins in size (Figure 1C). Lower eigengene values of two modules were associated with higher SVD composite scores, and one module - labeled brown -  was also associated with higher MFGE8 levels (β = 0.19, p < 0.001, Figure 1B). Gene set enrichment analysis applied to the top 50 proteins linked positively to the brown module revealed overrepresentation of pathways involved in neurogenesis, synapse organization, extracellular region and plasma cell development (Figure 1D & E). Mediation analysis showed that the association between lower MFGE8 levels and higher SVD composite scores was mediated by the eigengene values of the brown module (indirect effect: -0.01, p = 0.02, Figure 1F). Exploratory analysis of MFGE8-linked proteins among the entire measured proteome confirmed that 20% of the top 50 MFGE8 associated proteins were overrepresented in the top 50 proteins of the brown module (20% overlap, p value = 10E-8, Figure 1G), and top MFGE8 proteins were also overrepresented in biological pathways consistently involving neurogenesis, cell adhesion and morphogenesis.

Conclusion

Lower CSF MFGE8 levels and its associated protein signature involved in biological pathways of axonal and neuronal morphogenesis contribute to explain SVD-related lesions in patients with Alzheimer’s disease. Our results in humans converge with previous preclinical studies in mouse models of amyloid pathology suggesting that MFGE8 plays a role in altered cellular adhesion and endothelial extracellular matrix remodeling that may contribute to cerebrovascular damage4.
