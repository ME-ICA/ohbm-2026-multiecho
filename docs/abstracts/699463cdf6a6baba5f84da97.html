Introduction

Huntington’s disease (HD) is characterized by progressive gray matter (GM) loss, especially in the striatum, but the relationship between neuroinflammatory signaling and regional atrophy remains poorly defined. Ultrasensitive NULISA [1] panels now enable multiplex quantification of cytokines and CNS-related proteins from small-volume cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). Here we test whether NULISA-derived cytokine signatures are associated with subcortical and cortical GM volume loss across the HD spectrum.

Methods

We studied 88 gene-expanded HD participants (premanifest + manifest). High-resolution T1-weighted MRI was processed with FastSurfer to obtain GM volumes for caudate, putamen, and broad structural metrics. Volumes were adjusted for intracranial volume, age, and sex. CSF was assayed with NULISA CNS Disease and Inflammation/Neuroinflammation panels. We examined correlations between proteomic analytes and regional brain volumes using linear models with false-discovery rate (FDR) correction. Models were compared against established neurodegeneration markers (NEFL/NEFH) to test for shared vs. unique variance.

Results

As expected, structural biomarkers (NEFL, NEFH) showed robust negative associations with caudate and putamen volumes (r ≈ -0.58, p < 10^-14), validating the cohort [2]. In the immune panel, we identified TNFRSF8 (CD30) as the most significant correlate of striatal atrophy (FDR < 0.01). Contrary to a simple pro-inflammatory hypothesis, TNFRSF8 levels decreased significantly as caudate and putamen volumes shrank (r ≈ 0.40). While classical pro-inflammatory markers (CXCL8/IL-8) showed trends toward upregulation with increasing disease burden (CAP score, FDR=0.06), they were not significantly associated with volumetric loss after multiple testing correction [4]. Crucially, CSF biomarkers showed high spatial specificity, correlating strongly with striatal volumes while showing minimal association with white matter or global cortical metrics [2, 4].

Conclusion

These findings reveal a nuanced link between fluid proteomics and regional atrophy in HD. While structural markers (NfL) provide a high-sensitivity gauge of aggregate damage, the immune signature is characterized by the specific downregulation of TNFRSF8 rather than a generic "cytokine storm." This suggests that striatal degeneration involves the exhaustion of protective immunomodulatory pathways or the loss of CD30+ regulatory cells [3]. NULISA cytokine signatures may therefore provide a fluid correlate of specific immune dysfunction in the degenerating striatum, complementing standard neurodegeneration markers in future HD progression and treatment studies.
