Introduction

The pituitary gland is a structure within the brain that plays pivotal roles in the human endocrine system, growth hormone, sexual development and thyroid gland hormone. Accurate segmentation of the pediatric pituitary gland (PG) is essential for assessing neuroendocrine health, particularly in children with histories of perinatal insults [1]. However, accurate pituitary gland segmentation remains challenging due to its small size, as well as anatomical variability in the developing brain and the limited availability of pediatric datasets, which together hinder the direct application of adult-trained deep learning models due to domain shift [2]. This study aimed to develop a robust transfer learning (TL) pipeline adapting an adult-trained 3D U-Net for 6-year-old children, incorporating multi-expert consensus and dynamic region-of-interest (ROI) cropping and to investigate the relationship between automated PG volume and perinatal factors. 

Methods

A total of 75 children around 6 years of age were recruited for this study.

MRI Acquisition: T1-weighted MPRAGE images were acquired using a 3.0-T Siemens scanner with the following parameters: repetition time (TR) = 1900 ms; echo time (TE) = 2.93 ms; flip angle = 8°; pixel bandwidth = 170 Hz/pixel; matrix size = 256 × 208; field of view (FOV) = 256 mm; number of excitations (NEX) = 1; slice thickness = 1 mm; and total acquisition time = 4 min 9 s.

Ground Truth Generation: To create a high-reliability ground truth for the pediatric training cohort (n=75), three independent pediatric neuroradiologists manually segmented the pituitary gland on all T1-weighted scans using ITK-SNAP (http://www.itksnap.org/), and the final ground truth was established by a majority-voting consensus among them. (Figure 1) 

Pituitary gland Segmentation Pipeline: The cohort was divided into a training/validation set (n=61) and an independent test set (n=14). We adapted a "Legacy" 3D U-Net model [3], pre-trained on 153 adults, using a specialized TL strategy. This strategy featured subject-specific dynamic ROI cropping based on consensus centroids and extensive data augmentation, including ±8-pixel spatial jitter to enforce spatial robustness.

Statistical analyses: Model performance was evaluated on the held-out test set using Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC), Sensitivity, and Volume Correlation. For clinical analysis, we applied the validated TL model to the full cohort (n=75). We performed ANCOVA to assess the effects of Sex, GA, and BW on PG volume, controlling for age at scan to account for developmental growth. All statistical analyses were performed using Python (v3.9).

Results

Methodological Validation: On the independent test set, the TL model significantly outperformed the adult-only Legacy model. The mean DSC improved from 0.772 ± 0.149 (Legacy) to 0.895 ± 0.044 (TL) (p=0.0037), approaching expert inter-rater reliability (0.846). Crucially, sensitivity increased from 0.759 to 0.921 (p=0.0022), reducing under-segmentation. Volume correlation with ground truth improved substantially from r=0.314 to r=0.703 (Figure 2. A).

Clinical Analysis: In the full cohort analysis (n=75), after controlling for age at scan, there was no statistically significant association between automated PG volume and Sex (p=0.29), Gestational Age (p=0.14), or Birth Weight (p=0.12) (Figure 2. B).

Conclusion

This study developed and validated a specialized transfer learning (TL) pipeline for automated pituitary gland (PG) segmentation at 6-year-old children and to evaluated the association between automated PG volume and perinatal factors. As a result, we successfully validated a transfer learning pipeline that overcomes the adult-to-child domain shift, providing a highly accurate tool for pediatric PG segmentation. Clinically, the lack of significant association between perinatal factors and PG volume at age 6 supports the neurodevelopmental hypothesis of structural normalization or "catch-up growth" by school age [4]. This automated framework enables large-scale quantitative analysis of pediatric endocrine health and neurodevelopmental research.
