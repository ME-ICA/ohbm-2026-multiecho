Introduction

Focal cortical dysplasia (FCD) is the most common etiology of drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE) in children. In particular, focal to bilateral tonic-clonic seizures (FBTCS) mark a high risk of DRE in FCD1, are associated with lower quality of life, worse surgical outcomes, and risk of sudden unexplained death in epilepsy. Evidence highlights the thalamus and its interacting cortical circuits in FBTCS propagation as part of the epileptogenic network.2 While resting-state functional MRI (rsfMRI) offers a valuable tool3 for examining the integrity of thalamocortical circuitry and the interaction between FCD lesions and thalamus, few studies have evaluated the thalamus-lesional connectivity signature of FBTCS in FCD.

Methods

Participants:

Forty-three pediatric epilepsy patients with FCD (14 with FBTCS) were included within the age range of 5-22 years at time of scan, and had a minimum of 18-month of follow-up. Age- and sex-matched typically-developing (TD) controls were included from CNH (n=16) and from Human Connectome Project-Development public dataset (n=100). 

Preprocessing:

The 3T resting-state data underwent standard preprocessing using fMRIprep, followed by nuisance signal regression, polynomial trend removal, bandpass filtering (0.01-0.08Hz), no spatial smoothing was performed to enhance spatial specificity. The FCD lesions were manually segmented on preoperative 3T T1/T2/FLAIR images, then co-registered to the T1w image and warped into the MNI space.

Z-scored thalamo-lesional functional connectivity:

Following harmonization using NeuroCombat, we employed a “winner-takes-all” approach to parcellate the thalamus into seven nonoverlapping parcels by assigning each thalamic voxel to the Yeo network exhibiting the strongest group-averaged connectivity (Figure 1). We calculated the thalamo-lesional functional connectivity between the thalamic functional parcels and FCD lesion. We used TD connectivity as a reference to generate z-scored FCD-thalamus connectivity profiles to facilitate comparison across FCD locations. The z-scored thalamo-lesional connectivity profiles were entered to train an XGBoost classifier to predict the presence of FBTCS.

Results

The thalamus was divided into seven non-overlapping functional parcels based on its FC to Yeo networks (Figure 1). Fifty repetitions of 5x5-fold nested cross-validation achieved a median accuracy of 0.72, which is significantly above the chance level (ppermutation = 0.025), as shown in Fig. 2A. We examined the contribution of thalamic functional parcels in classification by examining the feature importance. The SMN-thalamus parcel, the functional parcel preferentially connected to the somatomotor network (SMN), which overlaps with the ventral posterolateral and intralaminar nuclei, significantly contributed more to FBTCS classification than any other thalamic parcels (Fig. 2B, ps < 0.001). Post-hoc logistic regression analysis revealed that increased ipsilateral (on the same side as the FCD lesion) SMN-thalamo-lesional FC is associated with worse surgical outcomes (t = -2.23, p = 0.026), which is exacerbated by ipsilateral thalamic atrophy. The findings were specific to ipsilateral FC and remained significant after controlling for duration of seizure.

Conclusion

Our analysis demonstrates that z-scored connectivity between FCD lesions and thalamic functional parcels predicts FBTCS. Examining the FC between the known seizure generator (FCD lesions) and the amplifier (thalamus), we revealed altered thalamo-lesional connectivity associated with FBTCS, particularly involving the SMN-thalamus parcel, a region implicated in sensory processing and loss of consciousness5. Furthermore, post-hoc analysis showed a significant association between increased ipsilateral SMN-thalamo-lesional FC, thalamic atrophy, and surgical outcomes, highlighting the clinical potential to help identify patients at high risk for FBTCS and suboptimal surgical outcomes, guiding personalized clinical management.
