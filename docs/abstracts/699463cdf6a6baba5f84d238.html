Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD), characterized by profound mood disturbances and anhedonia1, is the leading non-fatal cause of disability worldwide.2 Approximately one-third of patients do not respond to standard treatments, resulting in treatment-resistant depression (TRD). Deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the subcallosal cingulate area (SCC)—a region involved in mood regulation—has shown promise for TRD, with around 50-60% of patients reporting long-term symptom relief.3,4 Despite these outcomes, substantial response variability persists, reflecting the need to better understand the mechanisms by which SCC-DBS elicits its effects. Our group's prior functional MRI (fMRI) work in implanted SCC-DBS patients identified acute stimulation-related changes in resting state activity within corticolimbic regions—particularly the precuneus, dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, and posterior cingulate cortex—providing a foundation for understanding how SCC-DBS may modulate dysregulated circuits.5

Building on this work, the current study employed task-based fMRI to examine how SCC-DBS modulates symptom-relevant neural circuits during emotional face evaluation and reward processing paradigms, aiming to identify task-evoked biomarkers for personalized DBS programming. Unlike resting-state approaches, this strategy was devised to capture symptom-specific neural patterns directly linked to behavioral deficits. This allows us to probe the mechanisms through which DBS exerts its effects and to identify biomarkers that support faster, more precise tailoring of stimulation to each patient’s circuit profile.

Methods

Following institutional approval (UHN #14-8255), five patients with MDD implanted with SCC-DBS systems (n=5; 4 female, 1 male; age 34–62 years; 8–20 years post-surgery) were enrolled in the study. Participants were scanned on a Siemens 3T Prisma MRI system under two stimulation conditions: DBS-OFF and DBS-ON using clinically optimized parameters. Anatomical images were acquired with a T1-MPRAGE pulse sequence (FOV, 256×256 mm2; flip angle, 8°; TR, 2400 ms; TE, 3.69 ms; voxel size, 1×1×1 mm3), followed by functional imaging using a multiphase 2D gradient echo–echo planar imaging (GRE-EPI) sequence (FOV, 220×220 mm2; flip angle, 60°; TR, 2000 ms; TE, 25 ms; voxel size, 2×2×2 mm3). 

Under each stimulation state, participants completed two task-based event-related fMRI paradigms: the Facial Emotion Recognition Task (FERT) and the Monetary Incentive Delay Task (MIDT) (Figure 1). The FERT paradigm presented three categories of facial stimuli—happy, neutral, and sad—to assess affective processing circuitry. The MIDT paradigm assessed reward anticipation and outcome processing using cues associated with monetary gain (+1$), loss (-1$), or neutral (0$) conditions. Functional data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep within a BIDS-compliant pipeline, including slice-timing and motion correction, susceptibility distortion correction, co-registration to each participant’s T1-weighted anatomical images, and nonlinear normalization to MNI space. Images were smoothed with a 6 mm FWHM Gaussian kernel. Individual BOLD response maps and group maps were computed using AFNI (3dDeconvolve, 3dREMLfit, 3dttets++) and cluster thresholded at bi-sided voxel-wise p < 0.05 with a minimum cluster extent of 10 voxels (k ≥ 10, NN = 2) for FERT and 20 voxels (k ≥ 20, NN = 2) for MIDT (3dClustSim). Exploratory behavioural analysis of FERT responses was performed, specifically focusing on accuracy, precision, and reaction time. 

Results

Whole-brain voxelwise analysis of the FERT interaction contrast [(ON Happy–ON Sad) – (OFF Happy–OFF Sad)] (voxelwise p<0.05, cluster-corrected k≥10, NN=2; n=5) revealed how SCC-DBS modulates emotional face processing networks. Active SCC-DBS was associated with increased emotional face-related BOLD activation in the left middle frontal gyrus (encompassing dlPFC), bilateral fusiform face area, bilateral insula, and precuneus, alongside decreased activity in the left posterior insula and left amygdala. Single-subject analysis of the MIDT interaction contrast [(ON Win–ON Loss) – (OFF Win–OFF Loss)] (voxelwise p<0.05, cluster-corrected k≥20, NN=2) revealed SCC-DBS modulation of reward processing networks during both anticipation and outcome phases. Clusterized t-maps identified BOLD signal changes in the nucleus accumbens, right caudate, middle frontal gyrus. insula, and globus pallidus. 

Behavioral analysis in three patients (n=3) demonstrated that the DBS-OFF condition was characterized by a marked negative interpretive bias, with neutral faces frequently misclassified as sad, overuse of negative response categories, reduced accuracy and precision for neutral expressions, and faster reaction times to sad stimuli—all of which were attenuated during DBS-ON.

Conclusion

These results provide early mechanistic insight into how SCC-DBS modifies behavioral changes that are disrupted in depression, revealing that active stimulation modulates brain networks involved in emotional evaluation, salience processing, and reward responsivity. Attenuation of negative interpretive bias and recruitment of basal ganglia–thalamic pathways during reward processing suggest that SCC-DBS influences both affective and motivational systems in ways that map onto core depressive symptoms. By demonstrating that task-evoked BOLD signatures reflect stimulation-dependent changes across these domains, our findings directly address the need for biomarkers that capture patient-specific circuit dynamics that may inform DBS programming strategies. Task-based fMRI therefore offers a promising avenue for developing individualized, circuit-informed biomarkers capable of guiding parameter optimization and advancing a more precise, network-targeted approach to SCC-DBS therapy in TRD.
