Introduction


Aging related changes in white matter are a major topic in neuroscience. Diffusion MRI-derived tensor measures and tractography have been widely used to characterize age related alterations in white matter pathways and tract level properties1,2. Structural connectome analyses go one step further by summarizing these tractography outputs into whole brain connectivity matrices3. However, it is unclear to what extent the resulting patterns reflect genuine neurobiological reorganization versus biases inherited from the tractography and from subsequent connectome construction and analytic choices. Because tractography algorithms reconstruct streamlines from local estimates of the orientation distribution function (ODF), age related changes in tissue microstructure can distort these estimates and lead to apparent gains or losses of connections that reflect algorithmic bias rather than true changes in anatomical connectivity. To address this question, we construct group level connectomes using several standard weighting schemes and compare an older cohort to a fixed young reference group and to an independent young control group, while also deriving permutation based null distributions from a broader young pool.

Methods


Participants

We analyzed two predefined comparison groups and an additional broader young pool. Data were obtained from the TAU-SNBB database. Multi shell diffusion weighted and T1-weighted MRI scans were acquired from healthy adult participants under informed consent. The main comparison involved 54 older adults (60+) and 54 young adults (20-40), the fixed young group. In addition, we had a broader pool of young participants (20-40), nonoverlapping with the fixed young group. A subset of this pool served as a young control group for an independent young control vs fixed young comparison (to assess within young differences), and the full pool (excluding the fixed young group) was used as the sampling frame for permutation based null distributions.

Tractography and Connectome Construction

Multi shell diffusion weighted imaging (b ≈ 1000, 2000, 4000 s/mm², 1 mm isotropic) was preprocessed using MRtrix3 and FSL. Diffusion data were modeled using multi tissue constrained spherical deconvolution (MT-CSD) in MRtrix3, with response functions estimated via the Dhollander method. Whole brain probabilistic tractography was performed using the iFOD2 algorithm, seeding in the white matter and generating one million streamlines per subject. For each participant, the Brainnetome atlas4 was registered to individual diffusion space via the structural T1-weighted and diffusion images.structural connectomes were derived using this parcellation. For each participant, we computed connectivity matrices with three weighting schemes: number of streamlines (NOS), SIFT2 weights, and ROI size normalized weights. For each group and weighting scheme, a group mean connectivity matrix was created by averaging edge weights across participants and then binarized using a streamline based threshold (≥ 1e2) to indicate the presence or absence of each connection.

Gain Classification

From the binarized group mean matrices, we identified edges that were present only in one group and absent in the other and labeled these as gained connections for the group in which they appeared. This produced maps of gained connections for the old vs fixed young comparison and, separately, for the young control vs fixed young comparison.

Permutation Based Null Models from Young Population

To test whether the old-young pattern of gained connections exceeded normal variability within the young age range, we built null distributions from the broader young pool (ages 20-40, excluding the fixed young group). For each permutation, we randomly drew a pseudo group of young participants, computed its group mean binarized connectome, compared it to the fixed young reference, and derived a gained connections map (pseudo group vs fixed young). We then counted the total number of gained edges (present only in the pseudo group), yielding a null distribution of gained connection counts against which the observed old-young value was evaluated using an empirical p-value.

Results

Using connectomes constructed with three weighting schemes (NOS, SIFT2, ROI size-normalized), the old group’s mean connectome contained many more gained connections than the fixed young group and is typical for young to young comparisons. 

Circular diagrams (Fig. 1A-B) show that, for most subnetwork pairs, the percentage of gained connections was higher in the Old vs fixed young comparison than in the Control vs fixed young comparison. The heatmap of Old- Control differences in gained connection percentage (Fig. 1C) indicates particularly strong excesses for connections involving the attention network and the default mode network, whereas several other subnetwork pairs show only small differences. Permutation testing with young only pseudo groups (Fig. 1D) revealed that the old group’s total number of gained connections lay in the extreme right tail of the young derived null distribution (observed value ≈580 gained edges vs a null centered around ≈440, empirical p ≈ .016), while  the young control comparison fell well within this null range.

Conclusion

This study suggests that commonly used tractography based connectome pipelines may introduce a systematic bias toward apparent connection gain when comparing older adults to younger reference groups: the older cohort showed many more gained connections than would be expected based on null models derived from young only permutations, whereas a young control group did not deviate meaningfully from these null distributions. Because this pattern was robust across  different weighting schemes (NOS, SIFT2, ROI size normalization) and after aggregation to group mean matrices, it is unlikely to reflect only biological change. Our results are consistent with previous work suggesting that  free water contamination biases diffusion derived tissue metrics and can degrade tractography in aging and disease5.  Although we did not explicitly model or quantify free water in this dataset, this literature underscores the need for bias aware methods when interpreting diffusion based tractography and connectomic measures in older adults, and motivates a cautious approach to tractography based connectomes in aging research.
