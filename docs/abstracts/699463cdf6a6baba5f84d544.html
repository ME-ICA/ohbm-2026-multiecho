Introduction

White matter across the lifespan typically follows a characteristic trajectory of rapid growth, relative stabilization, and gradual decline1-3. Fiber tractography is highly sensitive to parameter settings, yet most studies still rely on empirical choices or default values, which can introduce reconstruction bias and result in incomplete or unstable outcomes, especially in infant data where white matter is not fully mature.

Brain volume and mean Fractional Anisotropy (FA) reflect the overall state of white matter microstructure. Brain volume captures differences in brain size and fiber scale, while mean FA represents the degree of organization and affects the trackability of fiber pathways. Since these measures vary systematically with age and developmental stage, they are likely closely related to optimal tractography parameters.

Motivated by these observations, we propose Development-Informed Tractography (DIT), which automatically adjusts ﬁber tractography parameters according to brain volume and mean FA across the human lifespan. By generating adaptive parameters tailored to individual structural characteristics, this approach effectively reduces bias from manual tuning and enhances the stability and reliability of tractography reconstruction.

Methods

Our goal is to optimize the entire fiber tractography pipeline based on individual brain characteristics. The overall workflow is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Brain volume data were obtained from 4, which provides the percentage of maximum brain volume across different age groups. Mean FA values were obtained from 5, which fitted data for ages 0–8 using:

FA(age) = a · exp(-b ·age) + c,

where a, b and c are obtained by model fitting. Furthermore, our evaluation shows that Eq. (1) is suitable for estimating FA values across the lifespan.

For adults, we followed the MRtrix HCP pipeline6. Two key parameters, maximum fiber length and FA cutoff value, were set to 250 and 0.06, respectively. For newborns, we performed parameter grid search under expert guidance and changed two parameters to 60 and 0.01, respectively. Two sets of settings reflect the biological constraints of white matter maturity on tractography results at different developmental stages.

To generate adaptive tractography parameters for individuals of different ages, we employed a linear function:

p = λ · FA + γ,

and separately fitted the two types of parameters:

  • Maximum fiber length: Brain volume was used as the independent variable, and maximum length as the dependent variable, with a linear mapping function fitted accordingly.
  • FA cutoff value: Mean FA was used as the independent variable, and FA cutoff as the dependent variable, with a linear function generating adaptive threshold values.

Results

In this study, we used the CBCP7, HBN8, HCP9 and ADNI10 datasets to cover a wide age range from infants to older adults. Specifically, the CBCP dataset includes children aged 0–6 years, the HBN dataset includes participants aged 5–22 years, the HCP dataset covers adults aged 22–36 years, and the ADNI dataset includes older adults aged 55–92 years. To demonstrate the applicability of our method across different developmental stages, we selected several representative individuals for analysis and used the HCP standard pipeline as a baseline for comparison. Conclusion

We propose an adaptive method for estimating tractography parameters based on brain volume and mean FA, enabling automatic generation of individualized thresholds and maximum streamline counts. Experiments show that the method improves the completeness of fiber tracking, reduces spurious streamlines, and provides more stable and biologically plausible results across the lifespan.
