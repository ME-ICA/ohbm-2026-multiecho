Introduction

Human brain development during the first 1000 days of life is rapid and highly nonlinear, supporting the emergence of sensory-motor, socio-emotional, and cognitive functions. The thalamus, as a central relay hub for nearly all sensory information reaching the cortex, plays a critical role in coordinating interactions among sensorimotor, salience/attention, and executive control systems. Its developmental trajectory may therefore serve as a critical indicator of early functional maturation. Thalamic functional connectivity (FC) development involves both changes in connection strength1 and the shape of age-related trajectories.2 However, longitudinal studies of thalamocortical development remain limited, further challenging in-time sensing of those subtle deviation for early intervention during early childhood. Meanwhile, most evidence derived from western populations lack cross-population validation and are inadequate for early risk detecting among Chinese children. Resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) provides an effective means to characterize emerging neural circuits in infancy. The present study aims to delineate the spatiotemporal development of thalamocortical FC from 0 to 36 months, identify functional thalamic subregions based on developmental trajectories, and compare patterns across the U.S. and Chinese infant cohorts.

Methods

Participants and Data Acquisition

We included 476 scans from 231 healthy infants (106 males, 125 females) from the Baby Connectome Project (BCP) and 185 scans from 171 infants (121 males, 57 females) from the Chinese Brain Connectome Project (CBCP) after careful quality control. All functional MRI scans were preprocessed in native space using infant-specific pipelines. Preprocessing included head motion correction, geometric distortion correction, aligning fMRI to subject-specific T1-weighted scans using tissue boundary sensitive registration, one-step resampling with concatenated transformations, ICA-based denoising, and censoring of frames with framewise displacement (FD) > 0.5 mm.3

Thalamus ROI Construction

We defined the entire thalamus region using the atlas proposed by Behrens.4 This atlas was nonlinearly registered to the UNC 6-month infant template and then refined through morphological erosion and interhemispheric symmetry correction to create an infant-appropriate thalamic mask.

Spatiotemporal Thalamocortical Trajectory Modeling

Functional connectivity was computed between 955 thalamic voxels and 200 cortical parcels defined by the Schaefer-200 atlas, yielding a 955 × 200 FC matrix for each scan. For each voxel-parcel pair, we fit generalized additive mixed models (GAMMs) to estimate nonlinear age trajectories from 0 to 36 months. Models included age as a smooth term, subject as a random effect, and sex, site, and motion as covariates. Predicted FC values were generated at 37 monthly intervals, forming a developmental FC profile for each voxel-parcel connection.

Voxel-wise Spatiotemporal Clustering

For each voxel, FC profiles across 200 parcels and 37 age segments were concatenated into a dimension 7400 trajectory vector, preserving both spatial and developmental information. Pearson correlations (r) between voxel trajectories produced a similarity matrix that was converted to a dissimilarity matrix defined as (1 − r). Hierarchical agglomerative clustering with average linkage was applied to identify voxel clusters representing thalamic subregions with similar developmental trajectories. The number of clusters was determined by jointly evaluating within-cluster sum of squares (k = 2~15) using the elbow criteria and silhouette coefficients. Clustering was performed separately in BCP and CBCP cohorts to assess whether similar spatial patterns emerged across populations.

Developmental Characterization of Thalamic Subregions

Using the resulting thalamic subregions as seeds, we computed seed-based FC with the seven Yeo’s functional networks and modeled their age trajectories with GAMMs. Significant trajectories were classified into linear (monotonic), U-shaped or inverted U-shaped, or other nonlinear patterns based on curve shape and model characteristics. To highlight subregion’s unique developmental effects, connectivity profiles of other thalamic subregions were included as covariates in voxel wise analyses. We selected 7 time points at 6-month intervals across 0~36 months and visualized the predicted FC values to examine developmental changes in the spatial patterns of functional connectivity between thalamic subregions and the cortex.

Results

Our results showed similar pattern with classic thalamic parcellations reported by Behrens and Morel4,5, indicating that developmental regionalization generally follow thalamocortical connectivity-based parcellation. We applied the same thalamic parcellation method to both the U.S. and Chinese datasets and obtained the following similarity metrics between the two solutions: Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) = 0.486, Adjusted Mutual Information (AMI) = 0.471, Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) = 0.473, Homogeneity = 0.493, Completeness = 0.454. Based on the spatiotemporal information integrated parcellation, we further conducted our research to thalamic subregions and their developing trajectories and to capture the differentiation in thalamic development between Chinese and the U.S population.

Across thalamocortical connections from four thalamic subregions to 200 parcels belonging to Yeo’s seven cortical networks, the composition (800 parcel-level trajectories per cohort) of developmental trajectory types differed between cohorts. In BCP, trajectories were dominated by insignificant/flat profiles (62.4%), followed by linear increases (26.5%); all other patterns were comparatively rare (each ≤3.8%). In CBCP, the proportion of insignificant/flat trajectories was lower (45.4%), accompanied by a higher prevalence of nonlinear developmental patterns (47.0%), including U-shaped (14.9%), inverted U-shaped (11.1%), exponential decreasing (11.0%), and logarithmic increasing (10.0%) trajectories. Network-wise, BCP showed its strongest linear-increase enrichment in the Control (Cont) network (46.7% linear increasing), whereas CBCP exhibited high nonlinear fractions in Visual (Vis) (57.8% nonlinear), Default (53.3% nonlinear), and Salience/Ventral Attention (SalVentAttn) (52.3% nonlinear) connections. Representative trajectories and percentage matrix were selected from CBCP.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that thalamocortical functional architecture undergoes rapid strengthening and spatial refinement across the first three years of life, with pronounced subregion-specific trajectories that reflect emerging network-level specialization. Cross-cohort differences between BCP and CBCP further indicate that early thalamocortical maturation is not uniform but may be shaped by cohort-dependent factors, underscoring the need for harmonized, multi-cohort validation when developing developmental biomarkers.
