Introduction

The fMRI community continually seeks to improve BOLD sensitivity, and one effective strategy is through advances in hardware and RF coil design. It is well established that coils with smaller elements provide higher image SNR[1, 2], which can be leveraged to achieve higher spatial resolution. However, it remains unclear whether this increase in image SNR translates into higher temporal SNR and BOLD sensitivity. Currently, 2 mm isotropic resolution is standard for whole-brain fMRI and 0.8 mm for laminar/columnar studies, with 2 mm typically dominated by physiological noise and 0.8 mm by thermal noise. Whether the higher image SNR of localized coils improves tSNR—or instead amplifies physiological noise—remains uncertain. We benchmarked a dedicated visual-cortex coil against a standard whole-head array at 7 T across two spatial resolutions to assess the benefits and limitations of localized designs for human BOLD fMRI. The visual-cortex coil, introduced at ISMRM 2024 [3] , was compared with an eight-channel transmit/32-channel receive head array optimized for whole-brain structural and functional imaging.

Methods

RF coils: Figure 1 shows the 7 T whole-head and visual coils used in this study. The head coil is a 32-channel receive / 8-channel transmit array optimized for whole-brain structural and functional imaging, whereas the visual coil is a localized array designed specifically for the occipital lobe. The visual coil employs smaller receive loops (3.2 cm diameter) compared with the head coil (8.9 cm near the occipital region). In both coils, adjacent elements were geometrically overlapped to minimize mutual coupling and arranged in a soccer-ball geometry to enhance image SNR. Full design specifications are provided in our previous work [3, 4].

Image acquisition: Functional BOLD data were acquired at 7 T using a multiband 2D gradient-echo EPI sequence [5] . The imaging slab was positioned obliquely over the occipital pole, parallel to the calcarine fissure. Each participant completed one functional run (288 volumes) at 0.8 mm and 2 mm isotropic resolutions (TR/TE = 1250/24 ms, flip angle = 45°, GRAPPA = 3 with FLEET ACS [6] ). FOV = 192 × 192 mm², 15 slices, slice gap = 50%. The 0.8 mm protocol used partial Fourier 6/8, matrix = 240 × 240; the 2 mm protocol used matrix = 96 × 96 and no partial Fourier. Visual stimulation (8 Hz LED flicker, Grass OVS goggles) alternated 25 s rest and 15 s ON blocks (8 cycles), providing full-field binocular stimulation with identical visual angles across coils.

Image preprocessing: Time-series data were motion-corrected in SPM12, and voxel-wise GLM analysis was performed to identify BOLD activation (p < 0.05). For each subject and resolution, a gray-matter ROI was derived from the mean EPI acquired with the visual coil by thresholding voxels above the 85th percentile of nonzero intensities. The resulting mask was visually inspected, rigidly registered to the head-coil EPI, and applied identically to both datasets to ensure voxel-wise comparability for tSNR estimation. For BOLD sensitivity analysis, voxels above the 50th percentile of the mean-EPI intensity were retained, and spatial extent was quantified from thresholded t-maps (t > 2.3) within the ROI.

Results

Figure 1 shows photographs of the head and visual-cortex (VC) coils used in this study. Across both spatial resolutions, the VC coil produced substantially higher image SNR—approximately 2.5 to 2.8× that of the head coil. Figure 2 presents the corresponding tSNR and BOLD activation maps. The VC coil achieved up to 43% higher tSNR at 0.8 mm and 30% at 2 mm, accompanied by stronger BOLD responses, illustrating the expected sensitivity–coverage trade-offs between coil types. The spatial extent of BOLD activation with the VC coil was ~4.5× larger at 0.8 mm, while being comparable to the head coil at 2 mm, demonstrating its clear advantage for high-resolution fMRI.

Conclusion

These findings indicate that the VC coil operates in a thermal-noise-dominated regime at sub-millimeter resolution, where the higher image SNR directly translates to improved tSNR and BOLD sensitivity. At 2 mm resolution, both coils enter a physiological-noise-limited regime, resulting in comparable BOLD sensitivity. Overall, localized surface coils provide significant benefits for laminar and columnar fMRI by enabling higher spatial resolution or reduced scan times through increased sensitivity and reduced reliance on extensive signal averaging.
