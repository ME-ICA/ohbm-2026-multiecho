Introduction

Low-field magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) enables the creation of portable and highly compact systems, allowing for bedside access. However, these systems may be susceptible to severe geometric inhomogeneity in the images. Poojar et al.1 have investigated the repeatability of image quality metrics such as SNR and the impact of electromagnetic interference on image quality. Váša et al.2 have reported segmentation-based metrics to assess the potential of low-field MRI for morphometry studies. Pretzsch et al.3 have assessed high- field/low- field correspondence between vertex-level measures of cortical volume, surface area, and cortical thickness. To complement these studies, we propose quantifying local geometric distortion at low fields in in vivo datasets of human subjects scanned at both low field and 3T. By utilizing the displacement field obtained from nonlinear image registration between images of the same subject, we can identify focal areas of spatial uncertainty. 

Methods

Imaging data used in this work have been made openly available by Váša et al.2 MRI data for each of the 23 participants were acquired during two scanning sessions. In the first session, participants underwent a 45-minute scan on a GE SIGNA Premier 3T scanner, followed immediately by a one-hour protocol (HFC) on a Hyperfine Swoop 64 mT scanner. During the second session (HFE), participants were scanned on a second Hyperfine Swoop system, identical to the first. The 3T protocol included T1w (MPRAGE) and T2w scans, acquired at a resolution of 1 × 1 × 1 mm³. The 64 mT protocols included both T1w and T2w scans, acquired using non-isotropic product sequences (T1w: 1.6 × 1.6 × 5 mm³, T2w: 1.5 × 1.5 × 5 mm³) with high resolution in the axial, sagittal, or coronal plane. 

All images have been denoised using SCUNet4  and bias field inhomogeneity has been corrected using N4 from ANTs5. Similarly to Váša et al.2, low-field isotropic data have been estimated using multi-­resolution registration (MRR) at a resolution of 1 × 1 × 1 mm³ by combining non-isotropic axial, sagittal and coronal images using the template building script from ANTs5. We then estimated local geometric distortions using ANTs5 non-linear registration with mutual information similarity measure, for non-isotropic and isotropic low-field brain-masked data for T1w and T2w contrasts, compared to the reference 3T data. Magnitudes of the estimated deformation fields are then non-linearly propagated to a template space, computed from the high resolution 3T scans of the participants of the study using ANTs5 template building algorithm.

Results

The mean displacement magnitude between low-field (0.55T) and high-field (3T) MRI was quantified in template space across different acquisition schemes and tissue types. Overall, both white matter and cortex exhibited similar geometric distortions (1.836 ± 0.446 mm vs 1.811 ± 0.309 mm, respectively). Among acquisition types, coronal slices demonstrated the lowest distortions (1.6 mm), while multi-resolution reconstruction (MRR) showed the highest (2.1 mm). Axial and sagittal acquisitions yielded intermediate values (~1.7-1.8 mm). Results show moderate inter-subject variability (SD: 0.15-0.46 mm) across sessions (HFC/HFE) and contrasts (T1w/T2w), suggesting consistent registration quality across the cohort (n=23 subjects).

Conclusion

Váša et al.3 evaluated between-scanner reliability of 64 mT morphometry and correspondence to 3 T measurements, using correlations of tissue volume and Dice spatial overlap of segmentations. Pretzsch et al.4 assessed using the same dataset high-field/low-field correspondence between vertex-level measures of cortical volume, surface area, and cortical thickness. In this work, we complement these two studies by quantifying geometric distortion using nonlinear image registration. Results show consistent geometric distortion patterns between the two low-field acquisition sessions, highlighting local variations in cortical areas.
