Introduction

Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) is a neurodegenerative disorder affecting the frontal and temporal lobes, with behavioral variant FTD (bvFTD) representing the most common clinical presentation, characterized by changes in social behavior, personality, and executive function. Functional MRI (fMRI) has become a valuable non-invasive technique to measure regional and global brain activity, most frequently as baseline connectivity or evoked blood oxygenation level-dependent (BOLD) responses. However, time course entropy, a surrogate of hemodynamic complexity, has shown promise for providing a more complete perspective on condition severity1, as changes in entropy may relate to both aging and cognitive impairment1. Indeed, entropy-based fMRI studies have shown promising in Alzheimer’s disease, mild cognitive impairment, and healthy adults2,3, but more limited information is available in bvFTD. Here, we investigated group differences in resting-state functional entropy between bvFTD patients and healthy controls using voxel-wise and whole-brain analytic approaches to test the hypothesis that bvFTD participants exhibit reduced entropy, consistent with diminished neural complexity and network-level degeneration.

Methods

Twenty-nine bvFTD patients (27.5% females; mean age = 64.7 years) and 31 healthy controls (HC; 19.3% females; mean age = 63.3 years) were included in the study. MRI data were acquired using the following sequences: (i) a T1-weighted 3D MPRAGE (TR = 8.1 ms, TE = 3.7 ms, flip angle = 8°, field-of-view = 256 × 256 mm, slice thickness = 1 mm, spatial resolution = 1 × 1 × 1 mm³, 150 slices), and (ii) a resting-state T2*-weighted functional MRI (fMRI) with eyes closed (TR = 3,000 ms, TE = 30 ms, spatial resolution = 3.39 × 3.39 × 3.39 mm³, 197 dynamics).

Functional images were preprocessed using standard steps, including slice timing correction, motion correction, normalization to MNI space, and nuisance regression. Sample entropy (SampEn) was computed from each time series following the algorithm proposed by Richman and Moorman4. The signal of length Nwas embedded into template vectors of dimension m and m+1 using time-delay embedding, with the embedding dimension set to m = 2. For each embedding dimension, pairwise similarities between template vectors were evaluated using the Chebyshev distance, defined as the maximum absolute difference across corresponding elements. For each template vector, the number of matches with other vectors (excluding self-matches) whose distance was less than a tolerance threshold r was counted and normalized by the total number of possible comparisons, with r defined as 0.2 times the standard deviation of the time series. The probabilities of finding matches of length m and m+1 were then averaged across all templates. SampEn was defined as the natural logarithm of the ratio of these probabilities, such that higher SampEn values indicate greater temporal irregularity and complexity of the signal, whereas lower values reflect more regular and predictable dynamics (Figure 1). Analyses were restricted to cortical voxels defined by a group-derived gray matter mask. 

First, voxel-wise comparisons of SampEn maps between HC and bvFTD participants were performed using Statistical Non-Parametric Mapping (SnPM). Additionally, to assess global differences, mean entropy across the whole brain was calculated for each participant. These whole-brain sum entropy values were subsequently entered into a linear regression model including group (HC vs. bvFTD), age, and gender as predictors. The effect of group was assessed via the regression coefficient for the group variable, and the associated p-value was reported as the age- and gender-adjusted significance of the HC vs. bvFTD difference. 

Results

Voxel-wise functional entropy: Voxel-wise comparisons of resting-state fMRI sample entropy between bvFTD patients and healthy controls revealed no significant differences after correction for multiple comparisons (SnPM, p > 0.05). However, visual inspection of the voxel-wise entropy maps suggested a general trend of higher entropy in HCs compared to bvFTD patients across widespread cortical regions (Figure 2A–B). Specifically, Figure 2A and 2B show the mean voxel-wise entropy maps for HCs and bvFTD patients.

Whole-brain sum functional entropy: To quantify global differences, the sum of entropy across the whole brain was computed for each participant, indicated that HCs exhibited significantly higher whole-brain mean entropy compared to bvFTD patients (p < 0.001; Figure 2C). This result suggests a global reduction in functional entropy in bvFTD, consistent with the trend observed in the voxel-wise maps.

Conclusion

A consistent pattern emerged in which healthy controls showed higher entropy than bvFTD patients. When entropy was summed across the whole brain, these differences became strong and significant. These findings suggest that reductions in neural complexity in bvFTD are present but distributed in a manner that makes global, rather than focal, metrics more sensitive for detecting group differences. 

Our results align with a broader literature showing that entropy decreases in several neurological and psychiatric conditions and is positively associated with cognitive capacity in healthy adults. The current findings extend this work to bvFTD and highlight the value of quantifying signal complexity as a complementary approach to traditional connectivity or activation analyses. Because entropy captures nonlinear temporal dynamics, it may reflect aspects of neural health that are not reflected through connectivity-based or structural measures alone.

Overall, this study provides initial evidence that resting-state functional entropy is reduced in bvFTD at the global level, even when voxel-wise differences do not reach significance. These findings suggest that entropy may serve as a sensitive marker of large-scale network dysfunction in bvFTD. Future work should investigate ROI based entropy, examine whether entropy relates to clinical measures, differentiates disease stages, or predicts progression over time
