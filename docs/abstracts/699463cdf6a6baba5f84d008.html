Introduction

Large-scale resting-state fMRI studies have enabled systematic characterization of functional connectivity (FC), yet classical FC measures exhibit modest test–retest reliability^1[PG1]  and limited single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) – based heritability. These limitations reduce statistical power for imaging–genetics and constrain the interpretability of connectivity-based biomarkers. Recent advances in deep learning suggest that data-driven representations may capture more stable and biologically meaningful patterns of brain organization, but scalable frameworks for deriving such FC embeddings across heterogeneous datasets remain underdeveloped.

This project aims to construct a unified, reproducible pipeline for generating deep learning–derived FC phenotypes (DL-IDPs) from large neuroimaging cohorts and evaluating their biological relevance. Specifically, we integrate UKB-style preprocessing^2, multi-scale ROI-based FC estimation, and graph-based supervised and self-supervised learning^3 to derive latent representations of connectivity. These embeddings are assessed for demographic sensitivity, stability across repeat scans, and genetic signal using standard imaging–genetics tools^4, 5. By combining rigorous QC with modern representation learning, this work seeks to determine whether deep models can extract FC features that better reflect individually varying neural architecture, while avoiding overclaims regarding improvement until quantitative analyses are completed.

Methods

We processed structural MRI and resting-state fMRI from the Queensland Twin Imaging (QTIM) and Queensland Twin Adolescent Brain (QTAB) datasets using a fully automated FSL 6.0.7 + Neurodesk workflow aligned to the UK Biobank preprocessing strategy. Steps included structural brain extraction (BET), tissue segmentation (FAST), motion correction (MCFLIRT), distortion correction (TOPUP where available), temporal filtering (0.01–0.1 Hz), spatial smoothing (5 mm FWHM), and linear/nonlinear registration to MNI152 2 mm (FLIRT + FNIRT). Each session generated standardized QC outputs (head motion metrics, SNR maps, registration overlays), and outliers were identified using a multi-metric ±3 SD criterion.

For each participant, ROI-based FC matrices were computed using Schaefer atlases (100–1000 parcels). Time series were extracted in MNI space, nuisance-regressed (WM/CSF signals; ICA-AROMA for motion components), and converted into Fisher-z–transformed correlation matrices. We further derived node-level features (e.g., time series variance) for graph modeling.

A two-stage modeling framework was implemented.

  1. Supervised Baseline: Graph Convolutional Networks, Graph Attention Networks, and Graph Isomorphism Networks were trained to predict age and sex, establishing whether multi-scale FC retains demographic signal after QC. Global pooling layers generated subject-level embeddings.
  2. Self-Supervised Learning: Contrastive learning and masked graph autoencoders were used to learn latent FC representations without labels. For contrastive learning, graph-specific augmentations such as edge dropout, node masking, and additive noise on edge weights were applied to generate positive pairs, and representations were optimized using a contrastive loss (e.g., InfoNCE). In parallel, masked graph autoencoders reconstructed randomly masked nodes or edges using a reconstruction loss (mean squared error), encouraging the encoder to capture global connectivity structure rather than local correlations.

Embeddings will be assessed via:

• Test–retest reliability using QTIM repeat scans.

• Associations with demographics (age, sex, motion).

• Heritability using linkage disequilibrium (LD) score regression on UKB genotypes.

• GWAS of DL-IDPs following UKB imaging-genetics pipelines.

Implementation used PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, mixed-precision training, and GPU clusters at Queensland institute of medical research (QIMR).

Results

Preprocessing and quality control were completed for >1,400 scans across QTIM and QTAB, with analyses using UK Biobank (UKB) imaging data currently underway. The automated Neurodesk/FSL pipeline successfully standardized data across structural modalities (T1 and MP2RAGE-derived UNIT1) and across differing EPI acquisition protocols. Quality control combined motion correction and ICA-based artifact removal (ICA-AROMA), followed by exclusion of sessions with extreme motion spikes (framewise displacement > 2 mm). At the group level, mean and 90th-percentile framewise displacement, mean DVARS, and ventricle fraction were aggregated, and outliers were identified using a ±3 SD criterion, consistent with large-scale pipelines such as UK Biobank and HCP. QC-passed datasets were retained for functional connectivity estimation and downstream modeling analyses.

Initial supervised models based on graph neural network architectures—including Graph Convolutional Networks (GCN), Graph Attention Networks (GAT), and Graph Isomorphism Networks (GIN)—demonstrate that ROI-based functional connectivity (FC), following stringent QC, retains biologically relevant information. In supervised benchmarking tasks, age prediction achieved a mean absolute error of 4.8 years compared with a baseline of 6.1 years, and sex prediction achieved an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.78 across multiple parcellation scales. Using QTIM repeat scans, subject-level embeddings derived from these models showed moderate test–retest reliability (ICC = 0.42), indicating consistent individual-level connectivity representations.

Self-supervised models, including contrastive graph learning and masked graph autoencoders, converged stably and produced latent embeddings that captured coherent global FC structure and were less sensitive to parcellation choice. Reconstruction performance in masked autoencoder settings indicated that learned representations encode distributed connectivity patterns rather than isolated local edges. Model architectures and hyperparameters are being actively refined, and updated quantitative results will be presented at the meeting.

Building on these completed analyses, ongoing analyses further extend the evaluation to benchmarking deep learning–derived imaging phenotypes (DL-IDPs) against classical FC features in terms of test–retest reliability, SNP-based heritability, and genetic association power using UKB data.

Conclusion

We present an end-to-end, scalable framework that integrates UK Biobank–style preprocessing, multi-scale functional connectivity estimation, and graph-based learning to derive deep functional connectivity phenotypes from large neuroimaging datasets. Completed analyses demonstrate that both supervised and self-supervised graph neural network models can extract structured and biologically relevant variation from resting-state functional connectivity matrices, yielding stable subject-level representations.

By combining reproducible HPC pipelines with twin imaging data and modern representation-learning approaches, this work establishes a practical foundation for evaluating refined functional connectivity phenotypes in imaging–genetics. Ongoing benchmarking against classical connectivity measures will determine their relative stability, heritability, and genetic association power. More broadly, deep learning–derived imaging phenotypes such as DL-IDPs may provide robust, low-dimensional representations suitable for large-scale genetic studies and, potentially, for downstream applications including disease-related brain network characterization in population-scale studies.
