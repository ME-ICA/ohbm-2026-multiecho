Introduction

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disease that leads to cognition and functional decline. Early detection of disease progression is crucial for treatment planning. The Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) is a widely used measure of AD severity. Models that accurately predict future CDR-SB could play an important role in monitoring AD progression and guiding clinical decision-making. Here, we benchmark three machine learning approaches to predict CDR-SB at year two using MRI images as well as demographic and clinical data.

Methods

Our dataset from the Artificial Intelligence for Alzheimer’s Disease (AI4AD) initiative consists of imaging and tabular data from three Alzheimer’s research cohorts: the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) Phase 1 and 2, the National Alzheimer’s Coordinating Center (NACC), and the Open Access Series of Imaging Studies (OASIS-3).1,2,3 Participants who met the following inclusion criteria were selected: availability of a T1-weighted MRI image at baseline, complete demographic information of age, sex, and body mass index (BMI), a baseline CDR-SB score, and a 2-year follow-up CDR-SB score (between 1.5 and 2.5 years). The final dataset included 2,327 participants: 322 Alzheimer’s disease (AD), 791 mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and 925 cognitively normal (CN) subjects. Not all subjects had diagnostic labels available.

We compare the performance of three models to predict CDR-SB scores at year two. The first model is a hybrid 2D ViT model (Figure 1). The model architecture consists of two parallel branches: a 2D vision transformer (ViT) for processing MRI images and an artificial neural network (ANN) for tabular inputs.  A slice selection step is applied: a 2D ViT is first trained on each slice individually, and slices with R² above the mean and separated by at least three slices are selected as inputs for the final model. Embeddings from each branch are used as inputs for a transformer to predict 2-year CDR-SB. The second is a hybrid 3D CNN model, consisting of a 3D CNN branch for processing MRI images and an ANN for tabular inputs. Embeddings from each model are concatenated into fully connected dense layers to predict CDR-SB at two years.4 The third model is AutoGluon, an automatic machine learning framework that uses multi-layer stack ensembling to predict 2-year CDR-SB.5 Here, AutoGluon is trained on the 2D axial and coronal middle slices instead of the full MRI image to reduce computational cost. 

Results

Data was split into 60% training data, 20% validation data, and 20% test data. Model performance was evaluated using R2. The 2D ViT hybrid model achieved an R2 of 0.74, showing strong correlation between predicted and actual AD progression. 

The Hybrid CNN model achieved an R2 of 0.70 and the AutoGluon model achieved an R2 of 0.77 when trained on the middle axial slice and an R2 of 0.76 when trained on the middle coronal slice.4

We performed ablation studies by training a separate 2D ViT-only model and a separate ANN model. We show that the 2D ViT model with slice selection achieves an R2 of 0.45, outperforming a 3D ViT model (R2=0.35) and a 3D CNN model (R2=0.22). We find that the ANN-branch has an R2 of 0.75, suggesting the predictive performance of the combined model is largely driven by demographic and clinical features.

Conclusion

We benchmarked three multimodal methods for predicting 2-year CDR-SB using MRI images combined with clinical and demographic data. The AutoGluon model achieved the highest R2, followed by the 2D ViT hybrid model and the 3D CNN hybrid model. Ablation studies show that the tabular data had a greater contribution than imaging data towards overall predictive performance.

The 2D ViT model with slice selection produced more informative imaging embeddings compared to CNN and 3D ViT models. These findings motivate future work on improved multimodal fusion methods to better leverage imaging data to increase predictive performance.
