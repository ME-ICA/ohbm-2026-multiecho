Introduction

In prior work,1 we found that metrics characterizing aperiodic signal variation in EEG timeseries data can be used to decode excitation-inhibition (E:I) balance mechanisms and that such metrics can be used to identify EEG autism neurosubtypes. However, signal variation in EEG data from periodic oscillatory components may also differ between these autism neurosubtypes. In this work, we test whether previously identified E:I autism neurosubtypes are different on periodic components of the EEG timeseries (e.g., θ, α, β, and γ oscillations).

Methods

Using a classifier trained to identify E:I autism neurosubtypes from the Child Mind Institute Healthy Brain Network (CMI-HBN) dataset1 based on the Hurst exponent (H), we predicted EEG E:I neurosubtypes within a new EEG dataset comprising n=65 autistic males (age range: 2–34 years) tested under naturalistic resting state conditions (i.e. ‘Inscapes’ movie viewing2). EEG data was also collected on n=51 age-matched typically-developing (TD) males. FOOOF spectral decomposition was applied to identify periodic (e.g., θ, α, β, and γ peak frequency and amplitude) and aperiodic (e.g., 1/f slope and offset) components from 20 second epochs of the first 2 minutes of EEG signal collected during Inscapes viewing. Hypothesis tests were conducted on each of the 93 EEG channels measured, using linear modeling testing for the effect of group, age and their interaction. Multiple comparison correction was achieved at FDR q<0.05 and significant differences are described using standardized effect size (Cohen’s d).

Results

Two neurosubtypes of relatively equal size were identified (i.e. A1 n = 34; A2 n = 31). The A1 subtype displayed H values that were comparable to TD (Cohen’s d  > -.47), while the subtype A2 exhibited significantly decreased H values (Cohen’s > .84). Unsurprisingly, given that H primarily captures variation in fractal aperiodic components of the signal, we also find that the subtype A2 has significantly flatter 1/f slope compared to TD (Cohen’s d > .97) and the other A1 subtype (Cohen’s d > .9). The flatter 1/f slope is congruent with the interpretation made from H regarding relatively higher excitability for the A2 subtype. For periodic components of the EEG signal (i.e. oscillatory power across θ, α, β, and γ bands), we find no differences in any of the bands except for α oscillations. Within the α band, we find significant ,yet relatively small, reductions in α power for A2 compared to TD (Cohen’s d > .37), but no differences between A1 vs TD (Cohen’s d > .03) or A1 vs A2 (Cohen’s d > -.04).

Conclusion

Identified E:I neurosubtypes based on aperiodic signal variation captured by H can be identified in new independent EEG datasets. Of these E:I-relevant neurosubtypes, the subtype with relatively increased excitation (e.g., A2) shows a reduction of α oscillatory power that may be of further relevance to E:I mechanisms (e.g., reduced inhibition).
