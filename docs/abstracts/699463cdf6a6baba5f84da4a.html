Introduction

Neuroimaging studies rely on a variety of templates and associated parcellations. While this diversity reflects different methodological priorities, it poses challenges for cross-study comparison, data aggregation, and reproducibility. Although these challenges may be less pronounced in non-human primates (NHPs) than in humans, given their simpler cortical anatomy, meaningful cross-study comparisons still require accurate transformations between templates. In practice, such transformations are often generated in-house using different registration strategies, introducing unquantified variability that can compromise reproducibility.

This issue is partly mitigated in volume space by RheMAP,1 which provides standardized nonlinear registrations between common rhesus macaque templates. However, volume warps do not directly define correspondences between cortical surfaces, and a community standard for inter-template surface transformations remains lacking. Moreover, surface registration is typically driven by geometric features such as cortical curvature, which differ from the intensity-based criteria used in volume registration. It therefore remains unclear to what extent surface alignments are consistent with, or diverge from, volume warps.

Here, we extend RheMAP into surface space by providing precomputed transformations among common macaque surface templates, including NMT v2.0, D99, MEBRAINS v2.0, and Yerkes19.2 We establish two surface alignments: (1) a conventional curvature-driven alignment and (2) a volume-informed alignment that approximates RheMAP volume warps. By comparing these two paths, we quantify geometric distance and distortion, feature preservation, and deviation from volume-based correspondences. The resulting transformations and maps of geometric variation are released at https://github.com/HumanBrainED/RheMAP-Surf, providing a benchmark for inter-template surface alignment and a reusable reference for cross-template analyses in macaque neuroimaging.

Methods

Surface-based registrations were performed on spherical representations of cortical surfaces using Multimodal Surface Matching (MSM).3 Among the templates, Yerkes19 includes well-established spherical surfaces. Spherical surfaces for NMT and MEBRAINS were generated using the standard FreeSurfer workflow. For D99, however, the original spheres required large deformations to align with other templates, exceeding typical MSM regularization limits and leading to unstable solutions. To mitigate this issue, we used smoothed midthickness surfaces in NMTv2.0-sym space to generate intermediate D99 spheres for the subsequent alignment calculation. This procedure reduced deformations and improved mesh quality and orientation consistency, yielding more stable and accurate MSM solutions.

To obtain harmonized medial wall (MW) masks, we mapped between surface and volume space to refine MW mask definitions across templates. The resulting masks serve as weighting maps in MSM (0 in MW, 1 in cortex), preventing MW variability from biasing alignment, and are also used to exclude MW vertices from quantitative evaluations; they are distributed as part of RheMAP-Surf and can be reused in other macaque surface analyses.

To obtain curvature-driven surface alignments (MSM-curv) while avoiding failures arising from mismatched initial sphere configurations, we adopted a two-step strategy. First, we constructed an anatomical prior based on the Markov parcellation4: a subset of cortical areas together with the MW were assigned discrete labels in Yerkes19 volume space and projected to native Yerkes19 surfaces and to surfaces of the other templates after warping them into Yerkes19 space using RheMAP. Using these labels as features, we computed an initial alignment using MSM. The resulting deformation spheres were then used as the initialization in a second MSM run driven by mean curvature, producing MSM-curv alignments. To account for differences in mesh resolution and smoothness, mean curvature maps were computed with template-specific smoothing to yield comparable visual sharpness across templates. Visual quality control was performed by overlaying transformed curvature maps on spheres and white matter surfaces and inspecting the correspondence of sulcal–gyral patterns.

To approximate RheMAP volume warps in surface space, we developed a volume-informed alignment strategy (MSM-aprx). Structured random volumetric features were generated, including spatially localized Gaussian-smoothed “dots” and directionally biased, low-pass filtered “stripes” at multiple spatial frequency levels. These volumetric features were projected to native white matter surface of the reference space and to surfaces of the other templates after warping them into that reference space using RheMAP. The maps were then merged into multiple composite feature maps. In the MSM-aprx optimization, MSM-curv solutions served as the initialization. For each composite feature map, we ran MSM to obtain a candidate alignment, then computed vertex-wise 3D distances between MSM-resampled and RheMAP-warped surfaces. We extracted the median, mean, and maximum distance (excluding MW vertices) and used their product as a composite score. The alignment with the lowest score was selected as the MSM-aprx solution and its deformation sphere was retained. Repeating this procedure once with the retained solution as the new initialization produced only minor improvements, suggesting convergence.

To compare MSM-curv and MSM-aprx, we computed vertex-wise distances and area distortion (log2 ratios, quantifying regional expansion or compression) between the corresponding resampled surfaces for each template pair and hemisphere. For both distance and area distortion, we summarized median, mean, and maximum values across the non-MW cortex. Finally, we evaluated feature preservation by computing the cosine similarity between resampled and native mean curvature in the target template space for each alignment method.

Results

Preprocessing and standardization: Our tests indicate that the template-specific smoothing strategy produces mean curvature maps with comparable visual sharpness across templates and improves the accuracy of curvature-driven alignment. For D99, generating spheres from smoothed midthickness surfaces warped into NMTv2.0-sym space yields more stable and accurate MSM solutions than the original D99 spheres. The cross-template MW consensus masks effectively avoid biases arising from inconsistent MW definitions across templates and provide consistent criteria for excluding MW vertices in subsequent evaluations.

Approximation of RheMAP volume registration by MSM-aprx: For the volume-informed alignment strategy (MSM-aprx), we quantified how closely the resulting surface transformations approximate RheMAP volume registrations. Vertex-wise distances between MSM-resampled and RheMAP-warped surfaces were computed for each template pair and hemisphere. Across most pairs, median distances in non-MW cortex are approximately 0.3–0.5 mm, and mean distances are approximately 0.3–0.6 mm, while maximum distances are on the order of several millimeters. Since RheMAP-warped surfaces are not guaranteed to lie exactly on the native surfaces, part of the observed discrepancy reflects the inherent geometric mismatch between the warped and native meshes themselves. We also evaluated this mismatch directly using signed distances and visualized the corresponding maps. Taken together, these findings indicate that the current MSM-based surface alignments provide a near-optimal approximation to the ANTs-derived volume registrations within the constraints of the underlying surface geometry.

Geometric differences between MSM-curv and MSM-aprx: To characterize geometric differences between the curvature-driven and volume-informed strategies, we compared the corresponding resampled surfaces directly. Vertex-wise distances between MSM-curv and MSM-aprx solutions show non-negligible but relatively modest deviations: for most template pairs, median distances in non-MW cortex are approximately 0.5–1 mm, mean distances approximately 0.7–1.3 mm, and maximum distances around 3–7 mm. Log2 area distortion (absolute values) reveals a similar pattern. For non-MW vertices, median values are typically around 0.2–0.3, mean values around 0.2–0.4, and maxima around 2–4, indicating that local expansion and compression are present but remain moderate over most of the cortex.

Feature preservation between MSM-curv and MSM-aprx: Feature preservation was assessed by computing the cosine similarity between resampled and native mean curvature maps in the target template space. The two alignment strategies are highly similar in this respect. For slightly more than half of the template pairs, MSM-curv achieves higher curvature similarity, indicating a modest advantage in preserving mean curvature features. MSM-aprx, while slightly lower in curvature similarity, achieves closer agreement with the RheMAP volume registration. These results support a complementary interpretation: MSM-curv tends to favor preservation of curvature-based surface features, whereas MSM-aprx prioritizes consistency with volume-based registrations.

Conclusion

RheMAP-Surf provides a systematically preprocessed and evaluated set of surface-to-surface transformations among commonly used rhesus macaque cortical templates. The resource includes standardized anatomical and spherical surfaces, cross-template MW consensus masks, and two sets of ready-to-use deformation spheres (MSM-curv and MSM-aprx), released together with documentation, demonstrations, reproducible code, and intermediate results through GitHub (https://github.com/HumanBrainED/RheMAP-Surf) and Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.15642801).

Methodologically, the project introduces a framework that projects information from volume-based registrations into surface space via structured random features and systematically compares this volume-informed alignment to a more conventional mean-curvature-based approach. The combination of distance measures, area distortion, and feature similarity offers a concrete example of how to design and evaluate surface registration strategies, and can be adapted to human and other NHP brain templates.

At the resource and community level,5 RheMAP-Surf acts as a bridge among macaque surface templates. It enables analyses developed in one template space to be transferred more smoothly to others and facilitates alignment, comparison, and integration of results across studies and datasets. The two alignment strategies explored here span a spectrum from conventional curvature-driven pipelines (MSM-curv) to alignments that more closely reflect established RheMAP volume registrations (MSM-aprx).

Overall, RheMAP-Surf extends the RheMAP volume registration framework into the cortical surface space and provides both methodological guidance and practical resources for more integrated, surface-based rhesus macaque neuroimaging.
