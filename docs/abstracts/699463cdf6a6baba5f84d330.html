Introduction

Cervical myelopathy (CM) is a common degenerative spinal cord disorder causing progressive motor dysfunction through corticospinal tract (CST) compression. While extensive research has characterized CST pathology in CM, compensatory mechanisms through alternative descending motor pathways remain poorly understood.

Classical studies by Lawrence and Kuypers (1968) demonstrated that following pyramidal tract lesions in primates, the rubrospinal tract (RST) and reticulospinal tract (ReST) can partially compensate for lost motor function. Recent evidence suggests these phylogenetically older motor pathways may contribute to functional recovery in human spinal cord pathology. The RST originates from the red nucleus and provides an alternative route for distal limb motor control, while the ReST from the pontine reticular formation influences proximal and axial muscle function.

Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) enables non-invasive assessment of white matter microstructure through metrics including fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), axial diffusivity (AD), and radial diffusivity (RD). Changes in these parameters reflect alterations in axonal integrity and myelination status.

This study investigated brain-level plasticity of descending motor tracts in CSM patients using DTI, with two primary objectives: (1) to identify compensatory microstructural changes in RST and ReST pathways compared to healthy controls, and (2) to characterize early post-surgical plasticity following cervical decompression surgery.

Methods

Participants Eighteen patients with CSM (14 males, 4 females; mean age 56.8±14.2 years) were prospectively recruited. Inclusion criteria were: (1) clinical diagnosis of CSM with myelopathic symptoms, (2) MRI-confirmed cervical spinal cord compression, and (3) scheduled for surgical decompression. Exclusion criteria included previous cervical spine surgery, other neurological disorders, and contraindications to MRI. Eighteen age-matched healthy controls (11 males, 7 females; mean age 50.0±21.7 years) without neurological symptoms were also enrolled. Clinical severity was assessed using the modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score (mean 14.6±2.9). Mean symptom duration was 17.6±19.8 months. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

MRI Acquisition All imaging was performed on a 3.0T Siemens scanner. DTI data were acquired using a single-shot echo-planar imaging sequence with the following parameters: TR=5000ms, TE=100ms, 45 diffusion-encoding directions, b-value=1000s/mm², voxel size=2×2×2mm³. CSM patients underwent MRI at two timepoints: pre-operatively and 2 weeks post-operatively.

Regions of Interest (ROIs) Four bilateral ROIs were selected based on their anatomical relevance to descending motor pathways: (1) Red Nucleus (RN) - origin of the rubrospinal tract, (2) Primary Motor Cortex (M1) - origin of the corticospinal tract, (3) Dorsal Pons (d_Pons) - CST pathway through the brainstem, and (4) Ventral Pons (v_Pons) - ReST pathway through the brainstem. ROIs were manually delineated on individual FA maps by an experienced investigator blinded to group assignment.

DTI Processing and Statistical Analysis DTI data were processed using FSL (FMRIB Software Library). Preprocessing included eddy current correction and motion correction. DTI metrics (FA, MD, AD, RD) were extracted from each ROI. Group differences between controls and pre-operative CSM patients were assessed using independent samples t-tests. Within-subject changes from pre- to post-operative timepoints were evaluated using paired t-tests. Pearson correlation analyses examined relationships between DTI metrics and clinical severity (mJOA), and structural stability by correlating pre- and post-operative DTI values. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05.

Results

Controls vs. Pre-operative CM: Significant differences were observed in two key regions. In the right red nucleus, CM patients showed higher FA (0.522 vs. 0.433, p=0.018) and lower RD (0.399 vs. 0.496×10⁻³mm²/s, p=0.003), suggesting enhanced axonal organization consistent with RST compensatory upregulation. In the left ventral pons, CM patients exhibited lower FA (0.531 vs. 0.587, p=0.002), indicating ReST microstructural changes under increased functional demand.

Pre-operative vs. Post-operative: Two weeks after surgery, right M1 FA decreased significantly (0.304 vs. 0.334, -8.8%, p=0.020), possibly reflecting normalization of CST hyperexcitability. Left vPons FA increased (0.558 vs. 0.531, +5.0%, p=0.026), suggesting early ReST recovery. No significant correlation was found between pre-operative DTI metrics and mJOA scores.

Pre-post structural stability analysis revealed distinct regional patterns: M1 showed the highest correlations (AD r=0.801, MD r=0.725, RD r=0.717, FA r=0.638, all p<0.01), indicating high structural predictability. In contrast, vPons showed the lowest correlation (FA r=0.518, p=0.028), suggesting high plasticity and variable recovery patterns.

Conclusion

This study provides novel evidence for brain-level plasticity of descending motor tracts in CM. Key findings include: (1) RST compensation at brain level, evidenced by increased FA and decreased RD in the red nucleus; (2) ReST pathway involvement, with decreased FA in ventral pons indicating microstructural reorganization; (3) Early post-surgical plasticity showing region-specific responses—M1 FA decrease suggesting CST normalization and vPons FA increase suggesting ReST recovery; (4) Differential plasticity profiles across motor tracts, with M1 showing high stability and vPons showing high plasticity. These findings suggest DTI metrics of brainstem motor nuclei may serve as biomarkers for monitoring compensatory mechanisms and predicting surgical outcomes. Limitations include small sample size (n=18) and short follow-up (2 weeks).
