Introduction

Diffusion MRI (dMRI) is the only non-invasive technique capable of inferring white matter microstructure by probing water molecule displacement. However, conventional clinical scanners with gradient strengths of 40-80 mT/m limit practical diffusion encoding to b-values of ~3,000/5000 s/mm², constraining angular resolution and the ability to resolve more complex fibre configurations.1

Ultra-high b-value imaging (>5,000 s/mm²) offers enhanced sensitivity to restricted intra-axonal diffusion, producing sharper fibre orientation distribution functions (fODFs) and improved angular contrast in crossing fibre regions.1 High performance systems, such as the GE MAGNUS 3T scanner, with 300 mT/m gradient amplitude and 750 T/m/s slew rate, provide the hardware necessary to implement ultra-high b-value protocols previously achievable only in preclinical settings.2

This study aimed to optimise acquisition parameters for ultra-high b-value imaging on a high performance system through comprehensive numerical simulations, determining: (1) the optimal b-value range balancing angular resolution and SNR; (2) minimum gradient directions required for stable spherical harmonic (SH) estimation; and (3) the smallest resolvable fibre crossing angle under realistic tissue conditions.

Methods

Diffusion signals were modelled using a single-compartment axonal stick model (axial diffusivity λ=1.7×10⁻³ mm²/s, radial diffusivity β=0.0 mm²/s) representing intra-axonal diffusion at ultra-high b-values where extra-axonal signal is suppressed.3 Simulations were performed under idealised (volume fraction VF=1.0) and more realistic partial volume conditions (VF=0.25-0.50).

SH Power Spectrum Analysis: Signals were generated over 752 gradient directions for b-values spanning 500-50,000 s/mm². SH basis functions up to order 22 were computed, and power spectra analysed under noise-free, noise-only, and noisy signal (SNR=20-30) conditions to characterise maximum recoverable angular frequency content.

Stability Analysis: Coefficient of variation (CV) was calculated across 200 realisations for SH orders 0-22, b-values 1,000-30,000 s/mm², and gradient schemes with 60-360 directions. CV thresholds of 5% and 10% defined ideal and acceptable reliability.

Fibre Crossing Simulations: Two-fibre crossing configurations were simulated with crossing angles from 5° to 90°, reconstructed using damped Richardson-Lucy spherical deconvolution.4 Equal (0.5/0.5) and unequal (0.7/0.3, 0.8/0.2, 0.9/0.1) volume fractions were tested to assess detection of minor fibre populations. The impact of gradient direction schemes (60-360 directions) on small-angle resolution was systematically evaluated.

Results

Optimal b-value range: SH power spectra demonstrated that angular information was maximised at b-values of 10,000–15,000 s/mm² under realistic partial volume conditions (VF = 0.25). The maximum reliably detectable SH order was 8, whereas orders 10–12 remained near noise levels. Beyond b = 15,000 s/mm², further increases in b-value yielded minimal gains in angular resolution (Figure 1A).

Gradient directions and b-value requirements: Low b-values, rather than the number of gradient directions, were the main source of instability and increased variance (Figure 1B). With low-direction schemes (60–90 directions) and low b-values (~3,000 s/mm²), CV was consistently above 30% under mixed partial volume conditions (VF = 0.5) for SH order 8. By contrast, schemes with 160–200 directions and b-values of 10,000–15,000 s/mm² produced more robust and reproducible SH estimates and fODF reconstructions, with CV below 10%.

Fibre crossing resolution: At b = 10,000–15,000 s/mm² with 160–200 directions, fibre crossings as small as 20° were reliably resolved with equal volume fractions (Figure 1C), a substantial improvement over the 30–40° limits typically reported for conventional systems.4 For substantially unequal volume fractions (0.9/0.1), secondary fibres remained detectable at crossing angles >50° when b ≥ 10,000 s/mm².

Stability: Overall, CV decreased consistently with increasing b-value, suggesting that higher diffusion weightings provide more stable estimates by confining signal to intra-axonal compartments and enhancing angular contrast.

Conclusion

This simulation study demonstrates that ultra-high b-value dMRI on high-performance gradient systems can substantially enhance angular resolution for white matter imaging. An acquisition protocol combining b-values of 10,000-15,000 s/mm² with 160-200 gradient directions optimises angular contrast while maintaining feasible SNR and low CV. Key advances include: (1) resolution of fibre crossings as small as 20°; (2) enhanced detection of minor fibre populations at smaller angles critical for accurate tractography; and (3) reduced reconstruction variability at ultra-high b-values.

These findings provide practical guidance for implementing next-generation dMRI tractography protocols, with direct implications for tractography accuracy in difficult regions and for microstructural characterisation. Future in vivo validation on the MAGNUS system will confirm these simulation-based predictions and refine protocol parameters.
