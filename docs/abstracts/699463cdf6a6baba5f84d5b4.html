Introduction

Inflammation is increasingly implicated in the pathophysiology of major depressive disorder (MDD), with elevated peripheral markers linked to altered neurobiology1,2. The glymphatic system supports waste clearance and neuroimmune regulation through perivascular pathways, and emerging work has tentatively implicated glymphatic dysfunction in processes potentially relevant to mood disorders3. However, whether peripheral inflammation relates to glymphatic function in MDD remains unclear.

Methods

Using data from the PReDICT (Predictors of Remission in Depression to Individual and Combined Treatments) study4, we analyzed diffusion-weighted MRI scans from 175 treatment-naïve individuals with MDD who also completed peripheral inflammatory marker sampling (CRP and IL-6). Glymphatic function was estimated using the DTI-alps metric, which automatically computes diffusion along perivascular spaces5. CRP and IL-6 concentrations were natural-log transformed to reduce skewness. Associations between DTI-alps and ln(CRP) or ln(IL-6), as well as depressive symptom severity (HDRS-17), were assessed in separate general linear models controlling for age, sex, and BMI.

Results

ln(CRP) and ln(IL-6) were moderately correlated (r=0.36, p<0.001). Lower DTI-alps values were associated with higher depressive symptom severity (β=-0.24, p<0.001). Higher ln(CRP) was significantly associated with lower DTI-alps index (β=-0.22, p=0.005), whereas ln(IL-6) showed no significant association with DTI-alps (β=-0.04, p=0.616).

Conclusion

These findings indicate that elevated peripheral CRP, but not IL-6, is linked to reduced glymphatic function in MDD. Moreover, lower DTI-alps values were associated with greater depressive symptom severity, suggesting a potential inflammatory–glymphatic system contributing to neurobiological alterations in depression. Incorporating glymphatic imaging metrics such as DTI-alps may provide novel mechanistic insights into CRP-related brain changes in mood disorders.
