Introduction

Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) is a neurodegenerative disorder that is associated with behavioral changes such as apathy and disinhibition1. FTD is also associated with patterns of brain atrophy, and several anatomical variants have been described. One such variant is right temporal variant FTD (rtvFTD), characterized by dominant right temporal lobe atrophy and associated with specific behavioral changes like facial recognition deficits, loss of empathy and problems recognizing emotions.2 Research on rtvFTD is limited and largely relies on case studies due to its rarity, so relatively little is known about the functional neural correlates of these deficits.

Difficulties in facial recognition and processing of negative emotions present particularly intriguing areas for investigation. Several case studies have reported prosopagnosia and difficulties recognizing familiar faces, while other studies document issues in processing emotional facial expressions.3 Understanding the functional neural correlates underlying these deficits could provide valuable insights into the pathophysiology of rtvFTD.

The current study investigates the functional neural basis of face and emotion processing in rtvFTD by comparing rtvFTD patients to healthy controls in a face-viewing fMRI experiment. We compare neutral and fearful faces to scrambled versions to isolate face-specific neural responses, and neutral to fearful faces to isolate emotion-specific responses. 

We used a univariate whole-brain group comparisons to investigate the impact of the disorder on face-selective activation as well as on emotion-selective activation. In addition, we used an innovative approach by comparing the expression of established neural signatures in the different conditions for the two groups.

Methods

Participants. 7 participants with rtvFTD and 19 healthy controls participated in our study. Patients were recruited from the memory clinic and Old Age Psychiatry Department of university hospitals Leuven (Leuven Belgium) and from the Neurology department at the regional Onze-Lieve-vrouw Ziekenhuis Aalst-Asse-Ninove (Aalst, Belgium). FTD diagnoses were made by experienced neurologists or old age psychiatrists after clinical assessment and assessment of atrophy patterns on structural MRI or FDG-PET. Further classification as rtvFTD patients was done with on structural fMRI data based on methods described in Potvin et al., 2017.4

 Healthy control subjects were recruited through a database of elderly volunteers and through advertisements in a local newspaper. Exclusion criteria were present or past neurological or psychiatric disorders including substance abuse as well as significant systemic comorbidities or use of medication susceptible to affect the central nervous system. 

fMRI task. The stimuli set consisted of six full color videos of six male actors displaying fearful facial expressions, and six videos of the same actors displaying neutral facial expressions while chewing. All videos were frontal views with averted eye-gaze. Face contours were removed and luminance was equalized. Spatiotemporally scrambled versions of stimuli were also created, removing facial shape and dynamic expression but retaining low-level motion information. 

The stimuli were employed in a two (emotion, Neutral/Fearful) by two (modality, Scrambled/Intact) factorial event-related fMRI design. We made use of an orthogonal task by means of oddball detection, which consisted of a white square appearing in one of the corners of the stimulus. Subjects were instructed to press a button whenever they detected the oddball. Every run included 12 oddball trials, equally divided over the four conditions and four locations. 

            Image acquisition. All subjects were scanned on a 3 Tesla Philips Achieva scanner using a 32-channel head coil. A high resolution anatomical scan (TR 9.6 msec, TE 4.6 msec, flip angle 8°, 182 slices, matrix size 256 × 256 and .98 × .98 × 1.20 mm³ voxel size) was acquired. A standard EPI sequence (TR 2 sec, TE 30 msec, flip angle 90°, 38 slices, matrix size 80 × 80 and 2.75 × 2.75 × 3.50 mm³ voxel size) was used. Functional scans were preceded by four dummy scans. 

Data processing. fMRIPrep version 24.0.0 was used for preprocessing including motion correction, slice timing correction, spatial normalization, and confound extraction. Frames exceeding 0.5 mm framewise displacement or 1.5 standardized DVARS were flagged as motion outliers. 

Data analysis. The univariate group comparison was performed using python with the nilearn toolbox. The data was smoothed using an 8mm kernel, then a first level glm was fitted for each subject. The second level analysis investigated group differences in the faces vs scrambled contrast, and the neutral vs fearful contrast. The overall effect of faces vs scrambled (both groups combined) was extracted to serve as a face-area ROI. 

For the signature expression analysis, we extracted beta maps to compare to established signatures of empathic care, empathic distress, negative emotion and subjective fear from the Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience lab (CANLAB) library at Dartmouth College.5 These signatures reflect constructs relevant to our task and associate with characteristic rtvFTD deficits. Signature expression was calculated within the face-area ROI derived from the univariate analysis and analyzed in MATLAB using a custom CANLAB toolbox applied with cosine similarity measure.

Results

Univariate analysis. The univariate analysis showed no clusters of significantly different voxels in the contrast faces > scrambled between the healthy controls and the rtvFTD patients. This suggests that in our sample there is no significant difference between the two groups when it comes to face-selective neural responses.

The analysis did show significant differences between the groups in the fearful faces greater than neutral faces contrast. Given the small sample size, we report clusters surviving an uncorrected threshold of z > 3.09 (p < 0.001) with a minimum cluster size of k ≥ 50 voxels. Controls showed greater activation than FTD patients specifically in the temporal pole (Peak MNI: (55, 11, -12), Z=4.70, k=169).

The extraction of the overall ‘face network’ was also thresholded at z>3.09 which resulted in a ROI of 955 voxels.

Signature expression. The expression of the signatures was analyzed using cosine similarity and group differences were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test.

The empathic care and empathic distress signatures showed no significant differences in expression between groups for either fearful faces (empathic care: U = 250.0, p = 0.657; empathic distress: U = 258.0, p = 0.477) or neutral faces (empathic care: U = 244.0, p = 0.774; empathic distress: U = 265.0, p = 0.322). The subjective fear signature also showed no significant differences in expression between the groups for fearful faces (U = 280.0, p = 0.092) or neutral faces (U = 262.0, p = 0.386).

The negative emotion signature showed significantly less expression in the face ROI for rtvFTD patients than controls when viewing fearful faces (U = 291.0, p = 0.025, r = 0.385), whilst there was no significant difference between the groups when viewing neutral faces (U = 281.0, p = 0.083). Conclusion

This study investigated the neural basis of face and emotion processing in rtvFTD using two complementary analytical approaches. Our univariate analysis revealed no significant group differences in the faces versus scrambled contrast, suggesting that basic face-selective neural responses may be preserved in rtvFTD. However, we observed significant group differences in the fearful versus neutral faces contrast, with controls showing stronger activation than rtvFTD patients in the right temporal pole. This finding aligns with the characteristic right temporal atrophy in rtvFTD and supports previous reports of emotion processing difficulties in this population.

The temporal pole has been implicated in social and emotional processing, particularly in integrating emotional information with semantic knowledge. The reduced activation in rtvFTD patients when viewing fearful faces may reflect compromised neural processing of emotionally salient facial expressions, potentially contributing to the empathy deficits and emotional recognition difficulties commonly reported in this population.

Beyond the traditional univariate approach, we employed an innovative signature expression analysis using established neural signatures from the CANLAB library. This exploratory analysis aimed to characterize activation patterns in terms of specific psychological constructs relevant to emotion processing and empathy. Of the four signatures examined, only the negative emotion signature showed a significant group difference, with rtvFTD patients exhibiting reduced expression compared to controls when viewing fearful faces, but not neutral faces. This finding complements our univariate results and suggests that the deficit in rtvFTD may be specifically related to processing negative emotional content rather than face processing more broadly.

The empathic care, empathic distress, and subjective fear signatures showed no significant group differences. 

The signature expression approach represents a methodologically innovative contribution to understanding neural processing in rtvFTD and other disorders. Rather than examining where in the brain activation differs between groups, this approach characterizes how activation patterns relate to specific psychological constructs, potentially providing a more mechanistic understanding of cognitive and emotional deficits in neurodegenerative disorders.

Several limitations warrant consideration. The small sample size (7 rtvFTD patients, 19 controls), while reflecting the rarity of rtvFTD, limits statistical power and generalizability. We used an uncorrected threshold to address this constraint. The signature expression analysis is exploratory and would benefit from validation with signatures trained on similar visual tasks, or direct behavioral validation of the constructs they reflect.

In conclusion, this study provides supporting evidence for emotion-specific neural processing deficits in rtvFTD, localized to the right temporal pole, and demonstrates the potential utility of signature expression analysis as a complementary approach to traditional univariate methods in characterizing neural processing differences in rare neurodegenerative conditions.
