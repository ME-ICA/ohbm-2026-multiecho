Introduction

【Objective】Social anxiety disorder (SAD) is characterized by excessive fear of negative evaluation. Beyond fear and avoidance, deficits in executive and social‐cognitive processes are increasingly recognized (Karaca et al., 2025; Pearcey et al., 2021). Resting‐state studies implicate large‐scale network abnormalities (Zhang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024; Langhammer et al., 2025), yet sex differences remain underexplored despite clinical indications of sex‐dependent symptom profiles (Asher et al., 2017). The Social Cognition Screening Questionnaire (SCSQ) offers a brief, multidimensional assessment of social cognition (Roberts et al., 2011; Kanie et al., 2014); however, its relationship to intrinsic connectivity in SAD—particularly potential sex-specific associations—has not been systematically tested, motivating the present study. To test sex differences in resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) and Social Cognition Screening Questionnaire (SCSQ) performance in SAD, and to examine whether rsFC–behavior associations are moderated by sex.

Methods

【Methods】A total of 30 patients with SAD (15 males, 15 females) and 30 healthy controls (HCs) (15 males, 15 females) matched for age, sex, and education underwent magnetic resonance imaging. The amplitude of low-frequency fluctuations (ALFF) and regional homogeneity (ReHo) were evaluated across the whole brain, and seed-based resting-state functional connectivity (rsFC) analyses were performed to characterize intrinsic cerebral activity. Social cognition was assessed using the Social Cognition Screening Questionnaire (SCSQ), which yields total and subscale scores (verbal memory, schematic inference, ToM, metacognition, and hostility bias). Group (SAD vs. HC) and sex effects were examined for imaging and SCSQ measures, and within-group associations between SCSQ scores and ALFF/ReHo/rsFC were analyzed.

Results

【Results】A diagnosis-by-sex interaction emerged in the right precentral gyrus (PreCG) for ALFF (FWE p<0.05). ReHo showed interactions in the right PreCG and left Rolandic Operculum (FWE p < 0.05). Initially, SAD females had lower PreCG ALFF/ReHo than SAD males. In HCs, males showed higher regional homogeneity (ReHo) in the left Rolandic Operculum than females. Seed-to-voxel analyses identified diagnosis-by-sex interactions in the right PreCG and cerebellar vermis (FWE p<0.05). Post hoc contrasts (uncorrected p<0.001) indicated greater PreCG–cerebellar connectivity in SAD males than females. In HCs, females exhibited higher intra-motor connectivity (right PreCG–right PreCG) than males.  SCSQ total scores were higher in HCs than SAD. HC females have the higher score than SAD females on schematic inference, theory of mind (ToM) and the total score. In HCs, right PreCG correlated with SCSQ theory of mind (ToM)and hostility bias; left Rolandic Operculum correlated with SCSQ total scores; and right PreCG–right PreCG connectivity correlated with SCSQ metacognition.

Conclusion

【Conclusion】Sex-specific alterations localize to the precentral gyrus, left Rolandic Operculum, and cerebello-cortical circuitry in SAD, alongside group and sex effects on SCSQ performance. Robust rsFC–SCSQ associations observed in healthy controls point to a disease-related uncoupling of intrinsic motor–cerebellar networks from social cognition. Sex should therefore be modeled as a biological variable when developing mechanistic accounts and intervention strategies.
