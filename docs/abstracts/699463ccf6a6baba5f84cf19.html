Introduction

Biophysical modeling of diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) can provide deeper insights into human brain microstructure, offering promising means to access MR-measurable parameters related to specific features of tissue and cellular architecture. Various biophysical models have been proposed to characterize the microstructure of white matter (WM) and gray matter (GM), and have been applied to study brain tissue integrity. Among these, several multicompartmental models, such as the Neurite Orientation Dispersion and Density Imaging (NODDI)1 model and the Soma And Neurite Density Imaging (SANDI)2 model have been proposed to overcome the limitations of the diffusion tensor (DTI)3 model. The availability of new ultra-strong gradient MRI systems has accelerated advancements in mapping microstructural parameters4, enabling measurements at shorter diffusion times and higher b-values, that could be achieved as well with ultra-high field MRI scanners coupled with strong gradients5.
However, few studies have thoroughly assessed the repeatability and reproducibility - both inter-scan and intra-scan - of these microstructural parameters, especially when comparing high field (7T) and strong (135 mT/m) and ultra-strong gradient systems (≥ 200 mT/m) to standard clinical systems (3T, up to 80 mT/m).

In this work, we computed microstructural metrics for WM and GM to evaluate inter- and intra-scan variability using Siemens Prisma (3T, 80 mT/m), Cima.X (3T, 200 mT/m) and Terra.X (7T, 135 mT/m) scanners. 

Methods

Subjects
A total of four healthy subjects (3 females/1 male, average age of 35  4 years) have been included in the study so far. Each subject underwent two sessions on Terra.X and Cima.X, and one session on the Prisma scanner (Fig.2a). 

Acquisitions
For each MRI scanner, we optimized the DWI acquisition to make the best use of the systems’ characteristics, while keeping the same acquisition scheme in terms of b-values and number of directions. For each acquisition protocol, we acquire also b0 volumes with reverse phase encoding and a 3D T1 volume for anatomical reference (Fig.1).

Preprocessing
DWI images have been preprocessed including denoising, correction for Gibbs artifacts, topup, eddy current correction. Then, fractional anisotropy (FA), mean, axial and radial diffusivity (MD, AD, RD) have been computed with Dmipy, orientation dispersion and neurite density indexes (ODI, NDI) and free water fraction (FWF) have been computed with AMICO toolbox and the soma radius (Rsoma), the fractions of soma and neurites (fsoma, fneurite) have been computed with the SANDI Matlab toolbox. All the computed metrics are reported in Fig.1. Harvard-Oxford atlas for cortical and subcortical grey matter regions and JHU label atlas for white matter tracts have beeen registered in the DWI space of each subject. We focused on different regions of interest (ROI): genu, body and splenium of the corpus callosum, bilateral cortico spinal tract and bilateral cingulate gyrus have been extracted to analyse WM metrics; while pre and postcentral gyri, superior/middle fronto/temporal giri, caudate nuclei, putamen, thalami, globus pallidum, hippocampi and bilateral amygdala have been extracted to analyse GM metrics.

Statistics
For all ROIs, mean values of each metric were computed. A Wilcoxon test was performed to compare the two sessions acquired on each scanner. Scatter plots were generated for each metric to qualitatively assess the values of each metric in each ROI across scanners.

Results

Intra-scanner variability

Results of intra-scanner variability on the Cima.X 3T showed that:

i) No significant differences were found respect to DTI and SANDI metrics in all the regions;
ii) Significantly different FWF and ODI values were detected in the genu and splenium of the corpus callosum and in the cingulate gyrus. No significant differences were found for NDI values in all the regions.

Results of intra-scanner variability on theTerra.X 7T showed that:

i) Significantly different RD in the splenium and FA in the bilateral cingulate gyrus were found. No significant differences were found for the other DTI metrics.
ii) Significantly different ODI and FWF were detedtec in the corpus callosum and in the cingulate gyrus.  No significant differences were found for NDI values in all the regions.
iii) No significant differences were found for SANDI metrics.

Inter-scanner variability

Plots of inter-scanner values for each metric showed for all subjects lower MD, RD and AD values and higher FA calculated on the Prisma scanner respect the the ones obtained with Cima.x and Terra.x. Regarding NODDI metrics calculated on Terra.x, ODI was higher in all the regions except for the corticospinal tract, FWF was higher in all the regions except for genu and body of the corpus callosum and NDI was higher in the corticospianl tract and lower in the cingulate gyrus respect to the ones calculated on Cima.x and PRISMA. Regarding SANDI metrics, Rsoma calculated on Prisma showed higher values respect to Cima.x and Terra.x, fsoma calculated on Cima.x showed the highest values and the distribution of fneurite values did not show a particular pattern.

Plots of a randomly chosen subject have been reported for reference in Fig.2. 

Conclusion

In this work, we evaluated inter- and intra-scan variability of microstructural diffusion metrics across field and gradient strength. 
Both Cima.X and Terra.X scanners showed a low intra-scanner variability between the two sessions, thanks to comparable signal-to-noise levels. Prisma showed greater inter-scanner variability with respect to the high-gradient systems (Terra.X and Cima.X), an the variability resulted to be smaller between Terra.X and Cima.X, which allow for shorter echo-time thanks to better gradient performance.
More subjects will be recruited for the study to increase the statistical power, and the metrics will be evaluated voxel-wise.
