Introduction

Brain charts across the lifespan using structural MRI are currently available with the goal to track, assess, and detect neurological abnormalities/diseases and changes over the lifespan.1 However, while structural MRI is limited to millimeter resolution and lacks pathological specificity, diffusion MRI (dMRI) can track microstructural changes and provide pathological specificity using biophysical modeling,2-4 which may enable both earlier detection and improved understanding of pathology. We created normative brain microstructure charts (μ-charts) with a large dataset of clinical dMRI using diffusion tensor imaging (DTI),5 diffusion kurtosis imaging (DKI),6 and standard model imaging (SMI) of white matter (WM).2-4 This study investigates the ability to assess MS disease severity through these μ-charts (as compared to the commonly used imaging biomarker, lesion load) to understand microstructural processes related to premature brain aging in multiple sclerosis (MS). Using the μ-charts, we compared the dMRI metrics of MS patients to the μ-charts as a function of the patient-determined disease steps (PDDS) score to evaluate disease severity.7 

Methods

Clinical Data: This retrospective study includes MS (N=865) and control (N=565) subjects from a cohort of 7984 patients who received routine clinical brain MRI in an outpatient center. Figure 1 contains patient demographics and inclusion criteria. Out of 865 MS subjects, 481 had sufficient data (PDDS score and lesion load) and were included in this analysis. dMRI, FLAIR, and MPRAGE were acquired on Siemens Magnetom Prisma (53.4%) or Skyra 3T (46.6%). The imaging protocol included A) dMRI –  4-5 b=0 images, b=250s/mm2 – 4 directions, b=1000s/mm2 – 20 directions, b=2000s/mm2 – 60 directions, and additional b=0 with reverse phase-encoding direction for distortion correction, TE=70-100ms, TR=3.5-5.0s, 50 slices, 1.7x1.7x3mm3, 6/8 partial Fourier, acquired within 7min, B) FLAIR – TE=325ms, TR=6s, TI=2.1s, flip angle=120°, 1mm3, C) MPRAGE – TE=2.7ms, TR=2.1s, TI=0.9s, flip angle=8°, 1mm3.

Image Processing: dMRI was preprocessed with the DESIGNER pipeline to remove noise, Gibbs ringing, and imaging artifacts, and DTI/DKI, and SMI maps were then derived.8 JHU WM atlas was warped to the subject space using FSL FNIRT, and mean values were extracted from 25 WM tract ROIs (left-right merged) and global WM (merged all ROIs).9 MS patients with FLAIR and MPRAGE images had lesions segmented using Samseg from FreeSurfer v7.3.2. Lesion load was calculated as total lesion volume/total intracranial volume (TIV).

Brain μ-charts: dMRI metrics of controls were plotted against age along with a quadratic fit. Z-score relative to the normative curves were calculated for each MS patient and plotted by PDDS scores to evaluate disease severity using dMRI metrics. Similarly, lesion load was plotted with respect to PDDS. We used Spearman correlation to determine p-value and strength/direction of association (⍴) with PDDS scores. The significant threshold for dMRI correlations was adjusted for multiple comparisons using Bonferroni correction. dMRI metrics and ROIs yielding strong associations (⍴) are reported here: 1. Fractional anisotropy (FA), 2. Radial kurtosis (RK), and 3. Axonal water fraction (f) in the body of corpus callosum (BCC) and whole WM.

Results

In Figure 2A, the μ-charts show age association of normative dMRI in WM ROIs relative to MS patients. MS patients show large heterogeneity but on average have noticeably lower FA, RK, and f compared to controls. This aligns with MS pathology where lower FA and RK may point to decreased WM integrity and demyelination and lower f indicates axonal loss.10 z-scores of MS dMRI data relative to the normative curve in Figure 2A are displayed in Figure 2B with respect to PDDS. Significant correlations (p-value << 1.2x10-4) between z-scores and PDDS were found in FA-BCC (⍴=-0.29), RK-BCC (⍴=-0.26), f-BCC (⍴=-0.23), FA-WM (⍴=-0.26), RK-WM (⍴=-0.27), and f-WM (⍴=-0.27). Here, we reveal with higher PDDS score, FA, RK, and f of MS patients deviate further from the normative data and have lower values compared to controls. Similarly, albeit less strong, lesion load correlates positively with PDDS (age as covariate, ⍴=0.14, p-value=0.002), demonstrating increased lesion load in patients with higher PDDS scores (Figure 2C). Altogether, with increased disease severity, we see dMRI metrics of MS patients worsen with increased PDDS and showing stronger correlations compared with just using lesion load.

Conclusion

Our work demonstrates the application of μ-charts generated from clinically feasible dMRI from controls to evaluate MS disease severity. We yield stronger correlations with PDDS using μ-charts with dMRI metrics compared to lesion load, which shows promise that μ-charts can provide insight to MS disease severity. Future work involves extracting age-of-onset as a covarying factor and including morphological features such as grey matter volumes to form a more accurate multi-dimensional brain-age framework. Additionally, μ-charts may be updated by including datasets from other studies.11,12
