Introduction

This study aimed to (1) compare plasma biomarker levels across three clinical groups—subjective cognitive decline (SCD), mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer’s disease (AD-MCI, stage 3 per 2024 NIA-AA criteria), and Alzheimer’s dementia (AD-DEM, stage 4)— within a tertiary memory clinic; (2) explore associations between resting-state EEG features and plasma biomarkers; and (3) evaluate the ability of EEG-based machine learning to classify pTau-217 positivity. 

Methods

We included 165 individuals: 128 patients (51 AD-MCI, 40 AD-DEM, 37 SCD) with plasma biomarkers and 32-channel resting-state EEG, and 37 healthy controls (HC) with EEG only. Plasma markers included pTau-217, pTau-181, and Aβ42/40 ratio. 

Plasma biomarkers were compared across diagnostic groups using ANCOVA (covariates: age, sex, creatinine). EEG features were extracted from 180 seconds of eyes-closed, clean data supervised by experts, and included spectral (FOOOF, peak frequency, peak amplitude, spectral slope; delta, theta, alpha and beta power across lobes and emispheres), complexity (multiscale entropy, Higuchi’s fractal dimension, Lempel-Ziv complexity), and cross-frequency (PAC) metrics. EEG–plasma associations were assessed using Spearman correlation with FDR correction. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on plasma data; EEG correlations were tested with all retained components (eigenvalue > 1). A random forest classifier with leave-one-out cross-validation and SHAP analysis was used to identify pTau-217+ patients (cut-off: 0.23 pg/mL). 

Results

pTau-217 and pTau-181 were elevated in AD-MCI and AD-DEM compared to SCD (p < 0.001), while Aβ42/40 was reduced. EEG showed progressive slowing along the AD continuum: increased theta power, reduced alpha power and peak frequency. No EEG feature correlated with individual plasma markers after FDR correction. However, theta power and PAC correlated with plasma Principal Component 1 (r = 0.31 and –0.17, respectively). Complexity measures did not differ across groups. The classifier achieved an AUC of 0.75 (sensitivity = 0.73, specificity = 0.62). Top EEG features for classification were frontal theta, PAC, left–right delta asymmetry, central beta, and multiscale entropy. HC were included for EEG comparisons only. 

Conclusion

EEG and plasma biomarkers reflect complementary aspects of AD. While direct EEG–plasma correlations were modest, classification performance supports EEG’s utility in detecting biological AD. Findings extend prior CSF-based results, suggesting that EEG slowing, PAC, and entropy may reflect early network dysfunction. 

Resting-state EEG captures functional brain changes linked to plasma-defined AD pathology. These findings support EEG as a fast, scalable, non-invasive biomarker for staging and stratifying patients in clinical and research settings.
