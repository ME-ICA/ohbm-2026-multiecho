Introduction

NeuroMark is a novel independent component analysis–based framework designed to delineate intrinsic connectivity networks (ICNs) and to extract network-specific neuroimaging features, thereby enabling systematic characterization of large-scale brain organization1. Methodologically, NeuroMark occupies a unique position at the interface of atlas-based and data-driven analytic strategies, the two dominant approaches to brain parcellation2. In standard implementations, NeuroMark employs direct spatial normalization of fMRI data to a canonical adult echo-planar imaging (EPI) template. However, the suitability of this approach for populations with marked anatomical differences from adults remains uncertain. In the present study, we systematically evaluate the performance of two widely used normalization strategies: (1) direct normalization to the adult EPI template (EPInorm)3, and (2) normalization via an age-specific anatomical T1 template followed by transformation to the adult EPI space (T1toEPInorm)4,5. These approaches are compared in terms of their impact on NeuroMark-derived functional network features across large-scale datasets spanning infancy, neurodevelopment, and aging.

Methods

Fig. 1 illustrates the analytical workflow to assess whether spatial normalization choices in fMRI preprocessing affect downstream outputs within the NeuroMark. Following standard preprocessing, fMRI data were normalized to MNI space using either EPInorm or T1toEPInorm, after which NeuroMark was applied to estimate individualized ICNs and their time courses and to derive functional network connectivity (FNC). Analyses were performed across three large cohorts spanning key stages of brain development and aging—infants (0–9 months), children and adolescents (5–21 years), and adults (36–100 years). Three core NeuroMark features were evaluated: spatial ICN maps, temporal dynamics of ICN time courses, and inter-network FNC. Comparisons across normalization strategies quantified the consistency of these features and evaluated whether spatial normalization systematically impacts NeuroMark’s characterization of functional brain organization.

Results

Across all cohorts, ICNs estimated using EPInorm and T1toEPInorm demonstrated exceptionally high spatial concordance (mean ± SD: infants = 0.9966 ± 0.0012; developmental cohort = 0.9947 ± 0.0019; aging cohort = 0.9963 ± 0.0012, Fig. 2A). At the individual level, cross-normalization similarity also remained high, with ICN time courses showing slightly greater consistency than spatial maps (average correlations: 0.7990–0.9931 for time courses; 0.6879–0.9131 for spatial maps). Importantly, no systematic alterations were detected in functional connectivity profiles, indicating that direct normalization of fMRI data to an adult EPI template does not introduce measurable distortions in FNC. The scatter plot in Fig. 2 reveals near-perfect correspondence between group-level FNC matrices derived from the two normalization strategies (r = 0.9974, p < 10⁻¹⁰). FNC estimates were highly preserved across cohorts (95% of connections with r > 0.9374 in infants; r > 0.8670 in the developmental cohort; and r > 0.9219 in aging adults, Fig. 2E), underscoring the robustness of NeuroMark-derived connectivity features to variation in spatial normalization procedures.

Conclusion

NeuroMark demonstrates strong robustness and cross-cohort generalizability across populations spanning the full lifespan, largely independent of the normalization strategy applied. Core NeuroMark-derived features remain highly stable across spatial registration procedures. This consistency holds whether fMRI data are mapped directly to the adult MNI template or normalized using age- or population-specific structural references, suggesting that NeuroMark reliably recovers intrinsic functional architecture even in cohorts whose neuroanatomical organization differs substantially from that of young adults.
