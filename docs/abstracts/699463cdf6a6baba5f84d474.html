Introduction

Patients with schizophrenia (SZ) generally have cognitive dysfunction, and the effect of drug treatment is limited1. Previous studies have shown that repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS), represented by intermittent theta burst stimulation (iTBS) mode, can effectively improve the cognitive function of SZ patients2. However, the neural mechanisms related to this improvement are not clear. To investigate the neural mechanism by which TMS improves SZ patients' cognition. This paper studies the microstate (MS) characteristics of electroencephalogram (EEG) data in SZ patients before and after accepting iTBS stimulation, which can reveal the impact of iTBS on the spatiodynamic characteristics of brain activity3, 4. 

Methods

This study recruited 20 patients with SZ (12 males and 8 females, aged 44.74±9.26 years) and 20 healthy controls (HC, 11 males and 9 females, aged 43.55±5.94 years). Among them, the inclusion criteria for patients are as follows: the total score of the Positive and Negative Symptom Scale (PANSS) ≥60 points, the score of the negative scale ≥20 points, and the score of the positive scale ≤24 points. Before and after the iTBS intervention, the speed of processing, working memory and attention/vigilance ability were assessed by the Trail Making Test (TMT), Digital Span Test (DST)and Continuous Performance Test-Identical pairs version (CPT-IP) test. And their resting-state EEG in the closed eye state were collected. Note that HC do not receive TMS intervention and only received one assessment.

In the experiment, SZ patients received 10 days of iTBS intervention, with the specific Settings: ①The stimulation target was set as the left dorsolateral prefrontal lobe (LDLPFC), ② the stimulation intensity was set as 80% of the resting motor threshold (RMT), ③1500 stimuli/session, 50 trains (2 s, 8 s inter-train interval), of 30- Hz bursts at theta frequency 3 pulses per burst, 200ms interval.

Based on finite impulse response digital filter and Independent Component Analysis methods, the EEG data was preprocessed to remove the noise, such as electromyography and eye movement. Then four features of MS, namely the average duration, coverage, occurrence and transition probabilities (TP) between states, were extracted from the EEG data. Finally, the difference of EEG features between groups were analyzed by the t-test method. In addition, the correlation between EEG features and cognitive abilities were analyzed with the Pearson algorithm.

Results

After iTBS intervention, the processing speed and working memory of the SZ patients were significantly improved. For example, the connection test time was significantly shortened from 57.50±21.43 seconds to 48.35±11.96 seconds (p=0.041), and the number span score was significantly increased from 18.95±4.07 to 21.45±4.33 (p=0.001).

Figure 1A shows that after the SZ patients received TMS intervention, the coverage value (from 0.176±0.053 to 0.192±0.046, p=0.002) and occurrence value (from3.483±0.746 to 3.752±0.618, p=0.002) of MS A significantly increased. The coverage (from 0.269±0.040 to 0.220±0.037, p<0.001), average duration (from 56.803±5.600 to 52.543±3.937, p=0.001) and occurrence value (from 4.724±0.318 to 4.168±0.429, p<0.001) of MS B significantly decreased. The coverage (from 0.297±0.052 to 0.341±0.075, p=0.003), the average duration (from 58.894±5.639 to 64.530±9.704, p=0.001), and the occurrence value (from 5.026±0.531 to 5.236±0.536, p=0.03) of MS C all significantly increased. 

Figure 1B shows that there are significant differences in TP SZ patients and HC. Specifically, the TP of MS A→B, B→C, and C→B are significantly higher than those in HC. The TP of MS from A to D and from B to D were significantly lower than HC. TMS intervention can significantly alter the TP in SZ patients. Specifically, after TMS intervention, the TP of MS B→A, C→A, C→D, D→A and D→C in SZ patients increase significantly. The TPs of MS A→B, B→D, C→B, and D→B were significant reduced. The detailed value are presented in the right side of Figure 1B.

Figure 1C showed that the TMS intervention significantly changed the correlations between the cognitive ability and the occurrence, average duration in SZ patients. Such as: (1) The coverage of MS C became positively correlated with the attention/alertness score, with the r value increased from -0.035 (p=0.885) to 0.405 (p=0.037). (2) The correlation between the average duration of MS B and the working memory score increased, and the r value increasing from 0.269 (p=0.252) to 0.434 (p=0.046).

Figure 1D showed that the TMS intervention significantly changed the correlations between the TP and attention/alertness ability in SZ patients. Such as: (1) The TP of MS B→A became negatively correlated with the attention/alertness score, and the r value increasing from -0.380 (p=0.099) to -0.461 (p=0.041). (2) The TP of MS D→A became negatively correlated with the attention/alertness score, and the r value increasing from -0.410 (p=0.065) to -0.453 (p=0.045). (3) The TP of MS D→C became positively correlated with the attention/alertness score, and the r value increased from -0.097 (p=0.684) to 0.553 (p=0.012).

Conclusion

This study employed MS of resting-state EEG and cognitive ability tests to investigate the intervention effect of iTBS on the cognitive function of SZpatients. The results show that 10 consecutive days of iTBS treatment significantly improved the patients' information processing speed and working memory ability. Meanwhile, the spatiotemporal dynamic characteristics of the MS of the SZ patients (including average duration, coverage, occurrence and TP) changed significantly. The correlation between these characteristics and the improvement of cognitive function were significantly enhanced. This indicates that iTBS could be used as an effective intervention method to improve cognitive dysfunction in schizophrenia. Moreover, the characteristic of MS can be used as the neural electrophysiological markers to characterize the improvement of cognitive function. In addition, it can providing a basis for revealing its therapeutic mechanism and clinical evaluation.
