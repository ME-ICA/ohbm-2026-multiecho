Introduction

Temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) is a common pharmaco-resistant focal epilepsy typically associated with pathology in the mesiotemporal lobe. Increasingly, TLE is conceptualized as a network disorder,1 with broad impacts on the brain beyond the primary substrate. Given the extensive literature showing marked differences in cerebral morphology and function between hemispheres, it is important to systematically characterize the effects of temporal lobe lesion laterality in TLE. This study aimed to investigate inter-hemispheric differences in MRI phenotypes in left and right TLE patients in one of the largest high-definition multi-modal datasets to date.

Methods

We leveraged a multisite, multimodal MRI (T1-weighted, diffusion) dataset of TLE patients and healthy controls, comprising data from the Montreal Neurological Institute-Hospital (MICA-MICs, NOEL), National Autonomous University of Mexico (EpiC), and the Jinling Hospital (Nanj), Nanjing University School of Medicine. We included 554 (147 left TLE; 135 right TLE; 272 healthy controls for imaging feature normalization) participants in our study. TLE diagnosis and lateralization were determined following ILAE criteria. MRI data were preprocessed uniformly using Micapipe,2 a robust open-access pipeline for processing multimodal MRI data (version 0.2.3) to extract cortical and subcortical morphological and microstructural feature maps (cortical thickness [CT], fractional anisotropy [FA], and apparent diffusion coefficient [ADC]) and connectivity matrices. Data were mapped to the conte69 (fsLR-32k) surface. Hippocampal surface-based data were extracted using Hippomaps.3

ComBat-harmonized structural feature maps were used for computing vertex-wise Mahalanobis distances in each patient by comparing values of CT, FA, and ADC against a normative distribution of 272 healthy controls with site representation proportional to patient site distribution. This measure of distance in feature space quantifies deviations from the multivariate normative value while accounting for covariance between metrics thus providing a vertex-wise multivariate structural abnormality score. Network measures were derived using normative structural and functional connectivity from the Human Connectome Project (HCP) connectivity data implemented in the ENIGMA toolbox.

In order to investigate the difference between left TLE, right TLE, and controls, we performed multiple linear regressions using Brainstat.4 For cortical and hippocampal surfaces, we applied cluster-correcting using Random Field Theory (RFT) with a cluster threshold ɑ=0.001 (unless otherwise specified) and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction to adjust for multiple comparisons.

Results

Consistent with prior findings, TLE was characterized by widespread structural alterations relative to controls, particularly in the ipsilateral temporal lobe and hippocampus, but also contralaterally. We performed a direct comparison of left and right TLE and found that left TLE showed more marked contralateral alterations, particularly in the temporal pole (pRFT < 0.01) and frontal lobes (pRFT < 0.01). These cortical differences were driven by differences in CT (dKS=0.146, pFDR = 0.13) and FA (dKS=0.162, pFDR = 0.093). Furthermore, we found that in the ipsilateral hippocampus, right TLE showed significantly more abnormality (pRFT < 0.01), driven by greater atrophy (dKS=0.160, pFDR = 0.093) and increased ADC (dKS=0.235, pFDR < 0.01). 

Having established both cortical and subcortical differences in structural alterations in TLE, we next sought to examine the relationship between these differences and connectivity. We compared the structural and functional disease epicenters between left and right TLE using a recently developed technique to map the brain regions whose connectivity profiles match global abnormality in the cortex. Connectivity was defined using normative data from an independent dataset of 200 young healthy adults from the HCP initiative. Here again, we found that disease epicenters reflected analogous differences to structural abnormality..

Conclusion

This study quantified hitherto uncharacterized cortical and subcortical structural differences between left and right TLE. Although previous literature has not shown a difference in cortical features between left and right TLE, these studies have often used few features, modest sample sizes, and lower field strength.5 We also found that the spatial distributions of the difference in alteration were explainable by looking at differences in inter- and intra-hemispheric normative structural connectivity using diffusion imaging. These data suggest that left and right TLE constitute a similar underlying pathology presenting differently as a function of diverging network organizations. These findings provide additional evidence supporting the understanding of TLE as a network disorder and contribute novel evidence suggesting that the side of pathology may determine patterns of disease propagation, with potential implications for individualized clinical management.
