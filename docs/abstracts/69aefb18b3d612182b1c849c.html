Introduction

Cognitive reserve (CR) has been proposed as a key factor supporting preserved cognitive performance in the presence of neuropathology. Although CR has been extensively studied in neurodegenerative disorders, its relevance in patients with brain tumors remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the contribution of CR, with a particular focus on leisure-related CR, to postoperative cognitive function and resilience against white matter disconnection following brain tumor resection.

Methods

Forty patients in the chronic postoperative phase following brain tumor surgery were evaluated. Current cognitive function was assessed using the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE), and premorbid cognitive ability was estimated using the Japanese Adult Reading Test (JART). Cognitive reserve was quantified using the Cognitive Reserve Index (CRI), developed for the Japanese population, including education, occupational attainment, and leisure activity subdomains. Postoperative structural MRI data were normalized to MNI152 space, and resection cavities were segmented and analyzed using the BCB Toolkit and TRACTOTRON to quantify tract-specific disconnection ratios across 68 white matter pathways. Correlation and multiple regression analyses were performed to examine associations among CRI components, cognitive measures, and white matter disconnection metrics.

Results

MMSE and JART scores showed a moderate positive correlation (r = 0.43). Among the CRI subdomains, only the leisure component was significantly associated with both MMSE and JART scores. Disconnection analysis revealed that involvement of the left inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus and the right posterior superior longitudinal fasciculus was significantly associated with lower JART scores (p = 0.0127 and p = 0.0051, respectively). Notably, patients with higher leisure-related CR demonstrated relatively preserved cognitive performance despite disruption of critical white matter pathways.

Conclusion

Higher cognitive reserve, particularly that derived from leisure activities, appears to confer resilience against postoperative cognitive decline in patients with brain tumors. These findings suggest that assessment of CR may be informative for preoperative planning and support the development of individualized surgical strategies aimed at preserving cognitive function.
