Introduction

The Choroid Plexus (ChP) is the primary site of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) production and plays a crucial role in the glymphatic system, a waste-clearance pathway of the central nervous system. Growing evidence suggests that disruptions in glymphatic clearance are closely linked to the development and progression of several age-related neurodegenerative diseases. In this context, the ChP has emerged as a structure of increasing scientific interest, not only due to its physiological function but also due to its potential role as an imaging biomarker for neurodegenerative processes. However, a major bottleneck in this line of research is the lack of accurate, automated, reproducible, and generalizable computational tools for ChP segmentation and quantification. While existing work1,2,3,4 has begun to explore automatic ChP segmentation, these tools still suffer from low performance and, in particular, poor generalization due to limited, homogeneous training datasets lacking sufficient diversity with respect to image resolutions, scanners, sites, demographics, and disorders. 

To address these gaps, we are developing FastPlex, an extension of our deep-learning-based FastSurfer neuroimaging pipeline for ChP segmentation. FastPlex will provide two main advantages over current methods: (1) an extensive dataset of manual ChP labels (700 3D images) that accounts for scanner type and image resolution and is balanced across age, sex, and brain disorders. These labels will be used for training, validation, and testing of the deep-learning tool; and (2) integration of our Voxel-Size Independent Neural Network (VINN)5, which enables segmentation of multi-resolution scans within a single model, supporting both standard 1 mm and high-resolutional (HiRes) sub-millimeter MR scans.

Methods

To accurately segment the lateral ventricle ChP, FastPlex employs the VINN architecture as its core. VINN is a resolution-independent 2.5D approach that aggregates predictions from three 2D Fully-Convolutional Neural Networks (one per anatomical view) with multi-slice input. This architecture enables training and inference on images of multiple resolutions within a single unified model. To train and test our tool, we utilized a preliminary subset of 463 manually segmented T1-weighted MRI scans (313 for training/validation and 150 for testing).  This subset comprises scans from approximately 20 different datasets (HCP, ABCD, EMBARC, Dementia, BANDA, BICEPS, PDC, etc.) and is balanced across sex, age group, scanner manufacturer, and image resolution, as shown in Table 1.  

To show a proof-of-concept for FastPlex, we benchmarked our method against all standalone open-source tools for ChP segmentation (Aschoplex1, Sorbonne2, CIBD3, GMM4), as well as whole-brain segmentation pipelines (FreeSurfer and FastSurfer). Furthermore, we also compared FastPlex with multiple nnU-Nets trained on our manual annotations. Based on performance on the hold-out validation sets in a 4-fold cross-validation setup, the best-performing nnU-Net configuration for this task was the 3D Residual Encoder L (3D-L-Res). Finally, segmentation performance was assessed in terms of segmentation accuracy, dice similarity coefficient (Dice), volume similarity (VS), 95% Hausdorff distance (HD95), on the unseen test-set. Statistical significance between FastPlex and comparative methods was evaluated using a non-parametric paired two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test with false-discovery-rate correction.

Results

The segmentation performance of all evaluated methods on the unseen test set is presented in Figure 1. FastPlex outperforms all other comparative methods with statistical significance across all evaluation metrics, both for the global ChP (Dice = 0.782, VS = 0.883, and HD95 = 2.111) and for each individual hemisphere (Right: Dice = 0.785, VS = 0.886, HD95 =  2.031; Left: Dice = 0.777, VS = 0.877, HD95 = 2.198). Additionally, we observed that current open-source tools lack generalization capabilities when applied to heterogeneous datasets (Global: Dice < 0.66, VS < 0.85, and HD95 > 2.66).

Conclusion

These preliminary results demonstrate that FastPlex can accurately segment the lateral ventricle ChP and substantially improves upon all available out-of-the-box tools, including deep-learning-based methods such as CIBD, Sorbonne, and Aschoplex. Moreover, FastPlex, with its VINN backbone, outperforms nnU-Net, a widely regarded state-of-the-art medical image segmentation framework.

Future work will focus on completing manual labeling for the full set of 700 scans, with the aim of further enhancing FastPlex performance. We will additionally expand the validation of FastPlex to assess other essential performance characteristics, including test–retest reliability and sensitivity to replicating known associations between ChP volume, age, and sex. Finally, FastPlex will be integrated into the FastSurfer neuroimaging software suite, providing a highly accurate, user-friendly tool for the reliable assessment of ChP imaging-derived phenotypes in the neuroimaging community.
