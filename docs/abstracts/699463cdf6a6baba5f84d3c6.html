Introduction

While Parkinson's disease (PD) is classically characterized by progressive motor dysfunction, cognitive impairment represents a frequent non-motor feature of the disorder, affecting up to 30 percent of patients with early PD.1 Clinical motor symptom severity in PD has traditionally been linked to the integrity of the striatal dopamine system, particularly the dopamine availability of the putamen. Cognitive impairment on the other hand has been associated with dopaminergic availability of the caudate nucleus,2 however, other structural changes have been discussed to relate to symptom expression. While the mechanistic pathways remain poorly understood, evidence suggests that pathological iron accumulation may influence cognition through multiple mechanisms that extend beyond striatal dopamine depletion.3 Pathological iron accumulation in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc), putamen, and caudate nucleus has recently been linked to cognitive abilities in PD. Therefore, iron imaging has been proposed as a supporting biomarker for cognitive impairment in PD. 4,5 This study therefore aimed to test whether iron accumulation in the SNc, putamen, and caudate nucleus is associated with motor and cognitive performance in early PD. Moreover, potential mediation effects via the striatal dopamine availability in putamen on motor and caudate nucleus on cognition were investigated. 

Methods

The study included 32 patients with early PD (11 prodromal / 21 clinical) and 26 healthy controls (HC) with available clinical, behavioral and neuroimaging data. For all participants part III of the Movement Disorder Society - Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS-III) was used as measure of clinical motor symptom severity. Additionally, participants performed a battery of cognitive tests, which were summarized into a standardized general cognitive performance (GCP) score. Imaging modalities comprised magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and dopamine transporter (DaT) single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT), which is an established measure for assessing dopamine degeneration. Quantitative susceptibility mapping (QSM) MRI was used as non-invasive in vivo measurement of iron accumulation. Mean QSM values were extracted for the bilateral SNc, putamen, and caudate nucleus. Similarly, the standardized mean DaT values were extracted for the putamen and caudate nucleus. Linear regressions were used to test the hypotheses that (1) PD patients show greater iron accumulation than healthy controls, and (2) within the PD group, higher iron accumulation within SNc, putamen, and caudate nucleus is associated with worse UPDRS-III values, and (3) lower GCP scores. All regressions were corrected for age and sex. Bootstrapping analyses with 5000 resamples were performed to test the hypotheses that (4) the association between iron accumulation and motor performance is mediated by the DaT availability of the putamen and (5) the association between iron accumulation and cognitive performance is mediated by the DaT availability of the caudate nucleus. 

Results

QSM values of the SNc were significantly different between patients with PD and HCs (p=.008), even after controlling for age and sex. Within the PD group, there was neither a significant relationship between the UPDRS-III and QSM values of the SNc (p=.222) nor the putamen (p=.323) nor caudate nucleus (p=.239). Strong associations emerged between the UPDRS-III and the DaT-SPECT values of the putamen (ß= -0.613, p<.001) and caudate nucleus (ß=-0.341, p=.003). Consistent with the absence of a direct effect between SNc QSM values and UPDRS-III scores, no mediation effect of putaminal dopamine availability was observed (b=0.85, p=.787, 95% CI [-7.64, 12.69]).

A different picture emerged for the association between GCP and QSM values, with the SNc (p=.003), the putamen (p=.014), and the caudate nucleus (p=.015) associating with cognitive performance. Moreover, no association between the GCP and DaT-SPECT values of putamen (p=.114) or caudate nucleus (p=.381) was found. While SNc QSM values showed a direct effect on GCP, caudate dopamine availability did not mediate this relationship (b=0.02, p=.956, 95% CI [-7.65, 8.98]).

Conclusion

The theorized functional dissociation between putamen and caudate nucleus, with the putamen relating to motor control while the caudate nucleus relates to cognitive functions, was not observed in our dataset. Instead, the results reveal two clearly distinct patterns: (1) motor impairment associates with dopamine availability in the putamen and caudate nucleus, while (2) cognitive impairment associates with pathological iron accumulation in the SNc, putamen and caudate nucleus. The effect of iron accumulation on cognition is not mediated by dopamine availability in the putamen or caudate nucleus. This suggests that iron accumulation may influence cognitive outcomes through mechanisms that extend beyond simple dopamine deficiency in the striatum.
