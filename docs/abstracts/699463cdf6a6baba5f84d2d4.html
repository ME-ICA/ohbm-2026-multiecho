Introduction

As cannabis use becomes increasingly normalized through liberalized social attitudes and policies, it has been the most widely used illicit substance worldwide since 20161. Rising recreational use and perceived therapeutic benefits have contributed to the belief among some women that cannabis poses minimal risk during pregnancy. Although accumulating evidence links prenatal cannabis exposure (PCE) to adverse cognitive and mental health outcomes in offspring2, the underlying neurobiological mechanisms—particularly at the level of large-scale functional brain networks—remain poorly understood. In this large-scale cross-sectional study, we applied a hybrid independent component analysis framework, NeuroMark3,4, to resting-state fMRI data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study5. This population-based cohort of over 11,000 children with extensive behavioral, psychiatric, and neurocognitive phenotyping provides a unique opportunity to characterize PCE-related alterations in whole-brain functional network connectivity (FNC) and their associations with cognitive and mental health outcomes.

Methods

Fig. 1 summarizes the analytical workflow for examining associations among FNC, PCE, and offspring mental health and cognitive outcomes. Individualized intrinsic connectivity networks were derived using the NeuroMark framework, and FNC matrices were computed as pairwise correlations among network time courses and averaged across scans to obtain subject-level profiles. Linear mixed-effects models were then implemented to assess (1) associations between PCE and offspring behavioral outcomes, and (2) associations among FNC, PCE, and behavioral measures. Shared neurofunctional substrates were identified by correlating t-statistic maps from the FNC–PCE and FNC–outcome models across cognitive and mental health domains, quantifying overlapping large-scale network architecture through which PCE may influence neurodevelopment.

Results

Associations among prenatal cannabis exposure, behavioral outcomes, and functional network connectivity were examined using linear mixed-effects models, adjusting for a comprehensive set of familial, pregnancy-related, and child-specific covariates. The analytic sample comprised 10,836 children (5,194 females and 5,642 males; mean age = 9.96 ± 0.62 years), of whom 754 (6.96%) had documented prenatal cannabis exposure. Compared with non-exposed peers, exposed children exhibited significantly elevated levels of psychopathology and poorer performance across multiple cognitive domains, except composite fluid cognition (Cohen’s d range = −0.1393 to 0.2451, false discovery rate [FDR]–corrected p < .05), consistent with prior evidence linking PCE to adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes. Notably, prenatal cannabis exposure was also associated with distributed alterations in functional network connectivity that significantly overlapped with the neurofunctional correlates of both mental health symptoms (positive correlations between t-statistic maps, r = 0.0641–0.5993, FDR-corrected p < .05; Fig. 2A–B) and cognitive performance (negative correlations, r = −0.5438 to −0.6665, FDR-corrected p < .05; Fig. 2C–D).

Conclusion

Interindividual differences in prenatal cannabis exposure, mental health symptoms, and cognitive performance were associated with distinct patterns of intrinsic functional connectivity, independent of confounders. The shared alterations across subcortical, sensory, cognitive control, and default mode networks indicate PCE disrupted subcortical–cortical integration, a core substrate for adaptive cognition and emotion regulation. Collectively, these findings provide evidence that PCE is linked to widespread reorganization of functional brain networks underlying variability in cognitive and behavioral development during late childhood.
