Introduction

Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is a prevalent and debilitating psychiatric condition affecting 15–18% of people worldwide. MDD was projected to rank second on a list of 15 major diseases in terms of disease burden in 2030. The burden and economic consequences of depression are high, especially because of its recurrent nature. Despite the availability of several treatments, the likelihood of recurrence increases with each episode. This emphasizes the critical need for effective preventive strategies and reliable markers of depression. Preventive Cognitive Therapy (PCT), based on Beck's cognitive model, has emerged as a promising treatment for recurrent depression. PCT has been shown to be effective increasing time to relapse in remitted recurrent MDD patients (rrMDD).Neuroimaging studies, particularly functional MRI, can provide insight into the neural changes that occur in Preventive Cognitive Therapy (PCT). Previously, changes in brain activity within the Executive Control Network [ECN] and the Salience Network [SN]have been related to the immediate effects of PCT, suggesting a role in relapse prevention. However, a comprehensive understanding of long-term prevention requires the integration of psychological and biological factors. The aim of this study is to investigate the clinical, cognitive and neural changes (post-pre) following PCT that relate to long-term lowering of relapse risk in patients with recurrent MDD individuals. 

Methods

The present study used longitudinal data from a randomised controlled trial: the Neurocognitive Working Mechanisms of the Prevention of Relapse in Depression (NEWPRIDE) study.  Data of 58 remitted recurrent MDD (rrMDD) participants was available, who were randomly assigned under two conditions (PCT /condition = 38 and WL condition = 20 ). Participants completed a detailed evaluation that included clinical tests and cognitive tasks designed to assess multiple cognitive-affective domains and cognitive performance at baseline and three-months follow-up. At 18-month follow up, burden and presence of relapse for each month following therapy was assessed with the SCID-I and the life-chart method. At baseline and follow-up, additional fMRI during the resting state and Emotion Regulation Task (ERT) were acquired (echo planar imaging (EPI) sequence: TR/TE = 2000/30 ms, flip angle = 70°, isotropic voxel size = 3.5 mm³). For anatomical images, a T1-weighted (T1w) sequence was acquired with the following MRI parameters: TR/TE: 9/3.5 ms; flip angle: 8°; isotropic voxel size: 1 mm³. 

A template-based dual regression approach was used to investigate neural changes, based on three network templates taken from the FIND lab template (DMN, EXN and SN). Regarding the ERT, we focused on downregulation vs. Attend (negative pictures) and upregulation vs. attend (of positive pictures) contrasts, then we performed an ROI analysis using regions based on the Brainetome Atlas (left lPFC, mPFC and dACC). The selection of regions of interest was based on those regions involved in emotional regulation. A stepwise regression was performed to identify the specific variables that contribute to the levels of burden. Prior to analysis, outliers were removed, and the data were normalized using. Finally, we used a non-parametric bootstrapping method to evaluate the stability of the coefficients (n = 2500).  

Results

The clinical model ( Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology (IDS –SR30), Positive and Negative Affect Scale (PANAS), Leiden Index of Depression Sensitivity (LEIDS),  Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (ERQ), Dysfunctional Attitude Scale form A (DAS-A), Domains and Dimensions of Pleasure Scale (DDOPS), Leuven Adaptation of the Rumination on Sadness Scale (LARSS), Responses on Positive Affect questionnaire (RPA) and Bermond-Vorst Alexithymia Questionnaire (BVAQ)) statistically predicted depressive burden ( R² adjusted = 0.5969; F(24, 32) = 4.455, p < 0.001). The PANAS-NEG exhibited a significant positive association with level of burden (β = -1.35, SE = 0.044, t = -4.836, 95% CI [-1.92, -0.78], p < 0.001), while the ERQ-ES showed a negative association with level of burden. Additionally, these effects were moderated by the interaction term ConditionPCT x PANAS-NEG (β = -1.35, SE = 0.044, t = -4.836, 95% CI [-1.92, -0.78], p < 0.001)) and ConditionPCT x ERQ-ES (β = -0.14, SE = 0.044, t = -4.836, 95% CI [0.03, 0.24], p < 0.003). The cognitive model (Attentional Response to Distal vs. Proximal Emotional Information task (ARDPEI), Implicit Association Task (IAT),Emotional Reasoning Task,34 and Digit span task (DS)) was also statistically predicted depressive burden, with an adjusted R² of 0.5969 (F(24, 32) = 4.455, p < 0.001). However, no significant predictors were identified in this model. Finally, the neural model also statistically predicted burden (F(8, 21) = 4.48, p < 0.002). Both the functional connectivity of the Executive Control Network  (β = 0.06, SE = 0.03, t =1.74, 95% CI [-0.05, 0.20], p < 0.09) and the brain activity observed in the medial prefrontal cortex during negative emotional regulation (β = 0.56, SE = 0.044, t = -1.46, 95% CI [0.13, 1.27], p < 0.01) were related to depressive burden and contributed to the model's overall significance. However, neither was an independent significant predictor. However, the latter effect was moderated in the PCT condition (β = -0.64, SE = 0.32, t = -2.00, 95% CI [-1.71, 0.13], p < 0.058), resulting in decreased levels of burden. 

Conclusion

This study provides an insight into how clinical, cognitive, and neural changes work together in PCT for recurrent MDD. Our results suggest that changes in mood regulation, as well as neural changes, are related to the stabilization of depressive burden over time. Specifically, larger changes in negative affect (PANAS-NEG) following PCT were related to lower burden levels. Also, changes in the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC) during down-regulation processes decreased burden levels in the PCT condition. These results indicate that changes in negative affective domains support lowering the long-term depressive burden and indicate a role for brain-regulatory efficacy of frontal networks in lowering relapse risk. Both maybe facilitated through PCT. Overall, predicting individual variation in long-term clinical outcome appears difficult to predict from a single marker or level of functioning.
