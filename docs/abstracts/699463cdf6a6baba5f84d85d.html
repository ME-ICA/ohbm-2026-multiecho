Introduction

The superficial white matter (SWM) immediately beneath the cortical mantle harbors short-range U-fibers that interconnect adjacent cortical regions, thereby supporting local information integration. Due to the key role of U-fibers in brain plasticity and aging, alterations in their density are observed in various disorders. Despite its importance, the SWM has been relatively understudied compared to deep white matter, in part due to its thin, heterogeneous structure and the challenges it poses for in-vivo imaging. Recent advances in high-resolution histology and surface-based analysis, such as those enabled by ultra-high field 7T magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and the BigBrain 3D histology dataset, have provided unprecedented opportunities to characterize SWM organization at multiple depths below the cortical interface. Nevertheless, reliably separating and quantifying short-range U-fibers from long-range fibers within the SWM remains technically challenging. Building on these advancements, we adapted a framework1,2 to separate and quantify short-range U-fibers and long-range fibers in the human SWM and to dissociate tangential and radial components using surface-based diffusion measures. We generated vertex-wise difference maps of fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) to capture microstructural asymmetries between these components and examined their distributions across cytoarchitectonic classes to characterize organizational differences within the SWM. 

Methods

Multimodal MRI dataset and processing. This study utilized data acquired at the Montreal Neurological Institute using Siemens MRI systems at 3T3 and 7T4 field strengths. The dataset included eleven healthy participants (4 females) with a mean age of 30.1 ± 5.52 years. The following scans were acquired: (i) 7T T1-weighted (T1w) images and (ii) 3T diffusion-weighted images. All MRI data were preprocessed using the micapipe5 pipeline. 

Warp Laplacian streamlines to DWI space. We characterized SWM fiber orientation by first solving the Laplacian equation across the white matter and shifting an existing surface along the resulting gradient using T1w images. Using this Laplacian filed, we computed streamlines extending from the white matter surface toward the subcortical regions and subsequently warped these streamlines into the diffusion MRI space (Fig. 1A).

Sample fixels and separate the streamlines. We performed diffusion fixel-based anaysis1 using multi-shell, multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution (CSD) model to separate and quantify U-fibers and long-range fibers in the SWM (Fig. 1B). Based on the orientation of fiber bundles relative to the Laplacian streamlines, fiber orientation distribution (FOD) functions aligned parallel were identified as radial fibers connecting the cortex and deep white matter (long-range fibers), whereas those oriented perpendicularly were classified as tangential to the cortex (i.e., putative U-fibers). We then extracted apparent fiber density (AFD) values for each fiber population across different SWM depths (Fig. 1C(i)). 

Map diffusion metrics using multi-tensors. In addition to fixel-based density estimates, we applied a multi-resolution discrete search (MRDS) approach2 to derive diffusion tensor metrics for each fiber component. Specifically, FA and MD maps were computed separately for radial (long-range) and tangential (shore-range) fiber components, sampled across multiple SWM depths to characterize depth-dependent microstructural differences within the SWM (Fig. 1C(ii)).

Results

We generated vertex-wise maps of radial-tangential differences in fiber density maps at 1mm SWM depth, as well as corresponding FA and MD contrasts, to capture microstructural asymmetry between radial and tangential SWM components (Fig.2A). 

To assess whether these microstructural asymmetries are systematically organized across cortical architecture, z-scored  △AFD(AFDradial - AFDtangential),  △FA(FAradial - FAtangential), and  △MD(MDradial - AFDtangential) values were grouped according to von Economo and Koskinas cytoarchitectonic class (Fig.2B). 

The limbic cortex and insula showed the largest negative △AFD values, indicating relatively higher U-fiber density in these regions, whereas the association cortex exhibited the largest positive values, reflecting relatively higher long-range fiber density. A similar trend was observed for △FA, with the limbic cortex showing the strongest radial-tangential microstructural separation, indicating more directional bias.

Conclusion

Our study applied a surface-based framework to dissociate and quantify radial and tangential components within the human SWM using 3T diffusion MRI and 7T structural MRI. Vertex-wise difference maps of FA, MD, and fiber density revealed systematic microstructural asymmetries between these components. Moreover, cytoarchitectonic class-level analysis demonstrated that tangential and radial components are differentially distributed across cortical regions. These findings indicates that SWM microstructure is closely aligned with cortical architectural organization and highlight the value of component-specific analyses for understanding SWM organization.
