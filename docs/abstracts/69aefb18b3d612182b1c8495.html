Introduction

Out-of-body experiences (OBEs) are characterized by a dissociation between self-location and physical body representation. The temporo-parietal junction (TPJ) has been implicated as a key cortical site of OBEs; however, the contribution of medial networks, particularly the posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), remains insufficiently understood. Awake brain surgery provides a unique opportunity to causally probe the neural substrates of bodily self-consciousness, although related reports are limited.

Methods

We report a right-handed male patient in his 30s with a right frontal oligodendroglioma who underwent awake craniotomy with intraoperative electrical stimulation mapping. During subcortical stimulation near the posterior cingulate cortex, the patient repeatedly experienced vivid OBE-like experiences, including a floating sensation originating in the lower limbs and a progressive, painless dissociation of bodily awareness. Postoperative semi-structured interviews were conducted to characterize the phenomenological features of these experiences.

Stimulation-positive sites were normalized to MNI space and integrated with diffusion MRI–based tractography using a one-ROI approach implemented in DSI Studio. Diffusion-weighted MRI data were acquired postoperatively using a 3.0-T MRI scanner (SIGNA Premier 3.0 T, GE Healthcare), with axial images obtained using diffusion-sensitizing gradients applied along 32 directions (b = 0 and 1,000 s/mm²). Deterministic tractography was performed using normalized quantitative anisotropy (NQA) as the tracking index (threshold = 0.10). Tracking parameters were as follows: angular threshold = 45°, step size = 1.0 mm, minimum length = 20 mm, and maximum length = 200 mm, to explore the associated white matter networks.

Results

OBE-positive stimulation sites clustered in the anterior portion of the PCC (Zone III), distinct from primary motor-positive regions. Tractography revealed short association fibers of the cingulum bundle connecting the PCC with the supplementary motor area and precuneus. These networks overlapped with regions previously implicated in self-referential processing and bodily awareness (Herbet et al. Neuropsychologia 2014). Integration with prior reports demonstrated spatial convergence between PCC, precuneus, supplementary motor area, and TPJ within a distributed medial–lateral network supporting bodily self-consciousness (Herbet et al. Brain 2019).

Conclusion

This case provides causal evidence that stimulation of the posterior cingulate network can induce OBE-like phenomena during awake surgery. Our findings extend the classical TPJ-centered model of OBEs, highlighting the PCC and its cingulum-mediated connections as critical nodes in bodily self-consciousness. Awake mapping combined with network-level analysis offers a powerful framework for elucidating the neural architecture underlying self-representation in the human brain.
