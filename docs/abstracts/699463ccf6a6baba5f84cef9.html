Introduction

Efficient cognition depends on functional segregation between the Default Mode Network (DMN) and Attentional Networks (DAN/VAN). Disruption of this balance has been implicated in neuropsychiatric risk, cognitive decline, and neurodegeneration. Building on two consecutive studies—adolescents (Lombardo & Kaufmann, 2023) and a sole-authored study in older adults (Lombardo, 2025)—I provide a lifespan perspective on DMN–DAN desegregation as a shared marker of cognitive vulnerability. We further outline a planned multimodal, multiscale longitudinal analysis to predict early preclinical disease dynamics.

Methods

Two large neuroimaging cohorts were analyzed to quantify DMN–DAN anti-correlation as a marker of network segregation:

  • Adolescents (ABCD, n=2,707): Resting-state fMRI and cognitive assessments were analyzed using cross-validated predictive models linking DMN–DAN anti-correlation with general intelligence and environmental risk factors.
  • Older adults (ADNI3, n=183; sole-author study): Group ICA and dual regression extracted network time courses. DMN–DAN anti-correlation, tau, and amyloid PET burden were evaluated as predictors of global cognitive performance (MoCA). Preprocessing included motion correction, scanner covariates, and global signal regression.
  • Planned analysis: Multimodal, multiscale integration combining network metrics, molecular markers, and environmental factors will test predictive power for early preclinical disease and identify intervention targets to slow cognitive and intellectual decline.

Results

•  Adolescents: Stronger DMN–DAN anti-correlation predicted higher general intelligence and lower environmental risk exposure, suggesting efficient network segregation supports cognitive performance during development.

•  Older adults: Reduced DMN–DAN anti-correlation correlated with lower global cognitive performance independently of tau and amyloid pathology, indicating large-scale network desegregation contributes to cognitive decline beyond molecular markers of Alzheimer’s disease.

Biological Interpretation:

Attenuation of DMN–Attention network segregation emerges as a shared biological marker of cognitive vulnerability across the lifespan. In youth, environmental and developmental factors shape network segregation, whereas in older adults, desegregation occurs alongside neurodegenerative processes. These findings suggest a convergent network-level mechanism linking functional, environmental, and molecular factors.

Conclusion

DMN–DAN desegregation represents a lifespan mechanism of cognitive vulnerability. Planned multimodal, multiscale analyses will aim to translate these network markers into predictive tools for early preclinical disease detection and intervention strategies, bridging developmental neuroscience and aging-related neurodegenerative research
