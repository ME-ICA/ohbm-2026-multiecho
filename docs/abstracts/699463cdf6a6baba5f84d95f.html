Introduction

Intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) leads to progressive accumulation and transformation of iron-containing blood breakdown products (BBPs), which contribute to secondary injury through oxidative stress, inflammation, and tissue toxicity1. No single MRI contrast mechanism can distinguish BBPs due to overlapping magnetic effects and the confound of iron concentration. While T2, T2*, and susceptibility-based MRI are commonly used to infer hemorrhage composition2, these contrasts are sensitive to multiple competing factors (including tissue susceptibility, water content, microstructure, and field effects) and do not directly quantify iron. Ferritin, a BBP, and the primary intracellular iron-storage protein, exhibits paramagnetic properties that influence longitudinal relaxation and may provide a complementary quantitative pathway for iron-sensitive MRI3. However, T1 relaxometry of ferritin has been underexplored, and appropriate acquisition parameters for reliable measurement remain unclear. We hypothesize that combining quantitative susceptibility mapping with imaging techniques sensitive to different chemical forms of iron (T1 and T2 and T2*-mapping) will allow simultaneous independent measurement of multiple BBPs. Here, we evaluate inversion recovery (IR) T1 mapping of ferritin phantoms at 3 Tesla as a proof of concept toward quantitative iron mapping in the context of ICH and as the first methodological step in developing a multimodal framework for iron characterization.

Methods

Ferritin dilution series phantoms were prepared using 0.9% saline with concentrations ranging from 0.0195 to 5.0 mg/mL. MRI was performed on a clinical 3T MRI scanner (MAGNETOM Prisma, Siemens) using an inversion recovery spin-echo sequence (TE = 10.0 ms, TR = 2550 ms). Initial acquisitions employed inversion times (TIs) up to 1100 ms (50,100,250,300,400,500,600,1100), which failed to capture full longitudinal recovery of the different ferritin concentrations. The protocol was subsequently amended to include 12 TIs spanning 50-5000 ms which showed adequate sampling of T1 recovery. Voxel wise T1 maps were estimated using a custom MATLAB pipeline with nonlinear least-squares fitting. Regions of interest were manually defined, and reproducibility was assessed across independent analyses.


Results

Results

IR acquisitions limited to TIs ≤1100 ms produced incomplete recovery curves and unstable T1 estimates, particularly at low ferritin concentrations. Extending the TI range to 5000 ms yielded well-conditioned fits and monotonic T1 shortening with increasing ferritin concentration, consistent with paramagnetic relaxation behavior. T1 estimates were reproducible across analysts, demonstrating robustness of the processing pipeline. These results establish the feasibility of quantitative ferritin T1 mapping and define acquisition requirements necessary for reliable measurement.


Conclusion

This study demonstrates that accurate T1 relaxometry of ferritin at 3T requires extended inversion recovery sampling and provides proof of concept for T1-based iron sensitivity relevant to intracranial hemorrhage. By establishing a reproducible acquisition and analysis framework, this work lays the methodological foundation for a multimodal MRI approach aimed at defining iron-related contributions to MRI contrast in hemorrhagic brain lesions. Quantitative T1 mapping may thus serve as a complementary tool to susceptibility-based methods for studying iron-driven tissue changes following ICH. The successful implementation of this multimodal MRI approach holds significant promise for enhancing the diagnostic precision, hemorrhagic risk stratification, non-invasive aging and monitoring of cerebrovascular lesions.
