Introduction

Myelin quantification enables early detection of neurological disease. Current MRI-based myelin quantification include myelin water fraction (MWF) mapping, magnetization transfer ratio (MTR). Recently, UTE/ZTE was proposed as a more direct myelin imaging method1, but it requires dedicated MRI systems and long measurement times. Recently, we proposed a multi-contrast multi-resolution UTE (multi-UTE) sequence2 for simultaneous quantitative and structural MRI to detect myelin in a clinical MRI system. However, multi-UTE required acquisition times of 20min, which is overdemanding in a clinical setting.

Recently, undersampling has been achieved with model-driven physics-guided deep learning (PG-DL). However, traditional supervised learning requires large, fully sampled training datasets, which are difficult to obtain in clinical settings. Self-supervised learning (SSL) via data undersampling (SSDU) is a strategy for training without fully sampled datasets that achieves results comparable to supervised deep learning and provides subject-specific zero-shot learning for reconstruction3.

Studies have integrated SSL in the PG-DL framework for 2D non-Cartesian data4, but with 3D data, the PG-DL method faces significant challenges due to hardware limitations5. In this study, we propose a dual-domain self-supervised learning-based reconstruction to accelerate 3D non-Cartesian multi-UTE acquisitions (DS3N) and evaluate its performance for up to 10-fold acceleration, comparing it with conventional reconstruction methods, including regridding, iterative nonuniform FFT (nuFFT), and iterative SENSE. 

Methods

  • 3D non-Cartesian multi-UTE

In multi-UTE (Fig. 1), T2* quantification and high-resolution anatomical imaging are combined. A dual-echo between 1-1 water-excitation pulses is acquired with variable echo times TE1 and TE2 for ultrashort T2* quantification (G1 = -G2 = 20 mT/m, FOV = 224 mm, ΔV = 1.7x1.7x1.7 mm3). Eleven different TEs with TE1 = {70, 90, 110, 140, 170, 250} µs and TE2 = {970, 990, 1010, 1040, 1070} µs and a high-resolution structural image (FOV = 224 mm, TE3 = 100 µs, G3 = 40 mT/m, ΔV = 0.5x0.5x0.5 mm3) were obtained in 20 min. Bayesian learning was used to estimate myelin fraction maps2.

  • Digital Brain Phantom

Digital brain phantoms at different relaxation rates and myelin fraction ratios were constructed via Bloch simulations using the multi-UTE signal equations. 

  • Image Reconstruction

Non-Cartesian reconstruction of the image data x was formulated as an optimization problem:

argminx { || E(x) - y ||2 + R(x) }

, where E(x) includes coil sensitivity maps, 3D nuFFT, the sampling k-space mask, density compensation, y is measured data, and R(x) is a neural network regularization term. Using variable splitting with quadratic trainable regularization parameter μ, this is separated and solved iteratively3:

 z(n) = argminz { μ ||x(n-1) - z ||22 + R(z) }

 x(n) = argminx { ||E(x) - y ||22 + μ ||x - z(n) ||22 }

The denoising network was designed based on 3D ResNet architecture. Variable splitting with a trainable regularization parameter μ was unrolled for 3 iterations, and data consistency (DC) was based on a conjugate gradient for 10 iterations. A dual-domain mix L1&L2 loss function with λ = 10-1 was used (Fig. 1 d): 

LDD = { ( ||yS(i) - ŷS(i)||1 / ||yS(i)||1 ) + ( ||yS(i) - ŷS(i)||2 / ||yS(i)||2 ) } + λ { ( ||xS(i) - x̂TS(i)||1 / ||xS(i)||1 ) + ( ||xS(i)- x̂TS(i)||2 / ||xS(i)||2 ) }

Training was performed with the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 10-3 for 300 epochs with early stop in an Nvidia H100 GPU. To limit memory consumption, mixed-precision training was applied only on the ResNet layers.

  • Performance evaluation

DS3N was evaluated against conventional reconstruction methods using structural similarity (SSIM), peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), and mean-squared error (MSE) with fully sampled reconstructed images. Reliability was assessed by performing inference reconstructions with the trained network across different TEs. Digital brain phantoms with different contrasts and 3T MRI (PRISMAFit, Siemens) data from 5 healthy volunteers were used. 

Results

Figure 2a shows high-resolution contrast-enhanced structural images of multi-UTE of an in vivo healthy volunteer measurement. High-resolution structural UTE of multi-UTE is more robust against motion compared to the conventional Cartesian T1w images, while offering a comparable CNR of 29.9, as the T1 MP-RAGE with a CNR of 32.7 between the gray and white matter. Automated segmentation of the high-resolution multi-UTE images was also successfully performed using the FSL (FMRIB Software Library) toolbox.

Figure 2b shows that DS3N provides reconstruction images that most accurately replicate the fully sampled images for both 2-fold and 10-fold acceleration. In three directions, DS3N achieves the highest mean SSIM values of {0.757, 0.741, 0.724}, the highest mean PSNR values in dB of {33.0, 33.3, 33.2}, and the lowest mean MSE values of {0.000503, 0.00047, 0.000484}, with a TE of 250 µs and an acceleration factor R of 10.

The subtraction image between TE = 70 μs and TE = 250 μs (Figure 2c) showed that our method maintained optimal retention of myelin ultra-short T2 component quantitative information and outperformed conventional methods. Figures 2d and 2e present comparisons between our proposed method for inference on a digital brain phantom at TE = 250 µs and conventional reconstruction methods. Our method, DS3N, achieves the highest SSIM of {0.864 ± 0.0171, 0.777 ± 0.0297} and PSNR in dB of {35.4 ± 1.8, 31.0 ± 1.8}, while having an MSE of {0.00032 ± 0.0001, 0.00087 ± 0.00032}, approximately half of the best conventional technique, both in 5-fold and 10-fold acceleration in realistic digital brain phantom simulations, in line with in vivo measurements.

Conclusion

Multi-UTE was accelerated 5-fold (i.e., TA < 5 min) while maintaining the information content for quantitative imaging. DS3N employs zero-shot training, requiring no additional database, and can be adapted to other 3D non-Cartesian sequences. DS3N not only reduces noise compared to conventional techniques but also preserves both edge and quantitative information more effectively. 

Reconstructing data from many receive channels is still challenging due to hardware limitations, which can be solved using Toeplitz approximation in DC layers with distributed training5. Sufficiently large datasets enable a dataset-driven supervised learning, replacing subject-specific training and inference with a single model training session. This reduces reconstruction time for a new subject, as only inference is required. However, due to internal differences within the acquired large datasets—such as variances in field of view (FOV) and orientation—the robustness of model training requires further evaluation and comparison3.

This study showed the feasibility of accelerating the multi-UTE using DS3N in 5 healthy volunteers. With the total acquisition of below 5 minutes enabled, clinical feasibility can now be tested on a patient cohort.
