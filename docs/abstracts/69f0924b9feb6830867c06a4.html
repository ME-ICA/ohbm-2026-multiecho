Introduction

Selective attention to working memory (WM) representations is essential for flexible, goal-directed behavior. The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) consistently activates during temporary retention and manipulation of information. Recent laminar fMRI studies have demonstrated preferential involvement of superficial dlPFC layers in WM maintenance and manipulation1–3, consistent with mechanistic models from invasive animal work proposing recurrent excitation in superficial layers as a WM buffer4. However, it remains unknown whether superficial dlPFC activity in humans is content-specific. Moreover, theoretical accounts propose that unattended WM items are preserved only via synaptic traces rather than sustained activity5, while deep dlPFC layers have been implicated in action selection and control of superficial layer maintenance processes4.

Here, we used ultra–high-resolution 7T laminar fMRI combined with multivariate pattern analysis (MVPA) and laminar connectivity analyses to test whether superficial and deep dlPFC layers differentially encode attended and unattended WM content in humans. We further examined whether deep-to-superficial layer connectivity changes between maintenance and non-maintenance periods. This preregistered study (https://osf.io/b5gz9) provides the first integrative test of dlPFC laminar mechanisms underlying WM content and attentional selection in humans.

Methods

Twenty-nine participants performed six runs of a retrocue WM task (Fig 1a) during 7T fMRI acquisition (GE-BOLD slab, 0.8 mm isotropic resolution, TR = 2 s). Two stimulus sets (faces or houses) were presented sequentially, followed by a retrospective cue indicating which set to prioritize for recognition. Probes required right-hand finger responses with randomized color-coded finger assignments. Structural (MP2RAGE, 0.75 mm isotropic resolution) and functional localizer (2 mm isotropic resolution) data were acquired in a separate session. The latter was used to guide slab placement.

Preprocessing included cortical segmentation, surface reconstruction, registration to HCP fs_LR space, voxel-wise depth estimation, and ROI definition using the HCP MMP1.0 atlas (Glasser et al. 2016). ROIs comprised left prefrontal regions of the frontoparietal network (FPN) and control regions within the left prefrontal cingulo-opercular network (COP). MVPA decoding (Fig 1b) targeted: (1) attended WM category (faces vs houses, delay period), (2) unattended category (delay period) and (3) motor responses (index vs middle finger, probe period). Generalized psychophysiological interaction (gPPI) analyses used deep-layer time courses as seeds to assess coupling with superficial layers across all trial periods (encoding, pre- and post-cue delay, probe).

Results

Attended WM content was reliably decodable from both superficial and deep dlPFC layers during the delay period (Fig 2b top; superficial p < 0.0009, deep p < 0.0089, permutation test), with significantly stronger decoding in superficial layers (p < 0.0374, paired t-test). In contrast, unattended WM content was not decodable from either layer in dlPFC. Control COP regions showed above-chance decoding in superficial layers (Fig 2b bottom, superficial p < 0.0339) but with lower accuracy than dlPFC. Motor responses were not decodable in dlPFC, whereas deep COP layers - primarily driven by the frontal eye fields - showed above-chance decoding. Laminar connectivity analyses revealed decreased coupling between deep and superficial layers in dlPFC during encoding and both pre- and post-cue delays, consistent with a gating role of deep layers. In COP regions, this decrease extended into the probe period.

Conclusion

These findings provide the first human evidence for laminar specificity of WM content representation in dlPFC. Superficial dlPFC layers preferentially encode attended WM information, supporting their proposed role as a WM buffer, while deep layers appear to contribute via modulatory interactions rather than distinct content coding. The absence of unattended WM representations supports synaptic-trace accounts of non-prioritized items and suggests selective maintenance of prioritized information5. At the same time, the presence of attended WM information across multiple prefrontal regions, together with laminar connectivity changes extending beyond the delay period, point to a more nuanced organization of prefrontal function. These results bridge animal microcircuit models with human neuroimaging evidence and advance our understanding of how attentional selection shapes WM at the laminar level.
