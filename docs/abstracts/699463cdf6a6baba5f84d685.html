Introduction

The human stress response is an adaptive mechanism that enables rapid reactions to acute challenges, such as threats. When stressors persist, the response can become chronic, increasing the risk of physical and mental disease. Both acute and chronic stress can reshape brain activity and connectivity, according to the Triple Network Hypothesis^1, particularly in the default mode (DMN), salience (SN), and frontoparietal network (FPN). To complement such network-level changes with an analysis of the brain’s broader macroscale functional organization, we here analyzed stress-related functional connectivity in cortical gradients.^2 After testing how acute and chronic stress alter regional connectivity along cortical gradient axes, we related them to the three cortical networks previously implicated in the stress literature, the DMN, FPN, and SN.

Methods

In secondary analyses of two datasets, we investigated changes in functional architecture in response to acute and chronic stress, respectively: 1) 67 healthy young males completed either an acute psychosocial stressor, the Trier Social Stress Test (TSST), or a control task, with 6 resting-state fMRI sessions (3T Siemens Verio MRI, 32-channel head coil) acquired from 60 minutes before to 105 minutes after intervention onset (T2*-weighted multiband EPI; TR = 1400 ms, TE = 30 ms, voxel size = 2.3 mm isotropic, 7 min 50 s per session).^3 2) 153 young adults (20-35 years) completed one resting-state fMRI session (3T Siemens Verio MRI, 32-channel head coil; T2*-weighted multiband EPI sequence with TR = 1400 ms, TE = 30 ms, voxel size = 2.3 mm isotropic, 15 min 30 s) and self-reported chronic stress questionnaire, assessed using the Trier Inventory of Chronic Stress (TICS), with an overall score derived from the 12-item screening scale reflecting the frequency of psychosocial stressors over the preceding three months.^4 From each participant’s resting-state functional connectivity matrix, we computed cortical functional gradients using principal component analysis and focused on the first three gradients, which capture the largest components of variance in large-scale cortical organization (Figure 1b). To capture how stress altered the position of cortical parcels along principal gradients, we used eccentricity (Figure 1c). Eccentricity measures the distance of one parcel to the centre point of the 3D gradient space, with this space being created by plotting one gradient per axis, with the origin at their intersection. Since all three gradients are zero-centered, the point where the axes intersect is the origin. Each parcel’s position in this space is determined by its scores on the three gradients, like coordinates in a 3D system. To quantify triple-network organization (DMN-FPN-SN) in 3D gradient space, we computed participant-level measures of within- and between-network dispersion (Figure 1c). Network centroids were defined as the mean 3D gradient coordinates of their constituent parcels. Within-network dispersion was calculated as the summed squared Euclidean distance of parcels from the centroid, and between-network separation as the Euclidean distance between network centroids. Acute-stress effects were tested using linear mixed-effects models with Group (TSST vs control), Session/Time (pre/post or block), and their interaction as fixed effects, and participant as a random effect. Models were fit separately for parcel eccentricity and for within- and between-network dispersion metrics. Chronic stress (TICS) associations were tested using general linear models relating TICS scores to eccentricity and dispersion measures.

Results

For acute stress, we observed selective changes in macroscale connectivity, expressed both as region-specific gradient reconfigurations and as targeted shifts in between-network interactions (Figure 2). We identified a significant session-by-group effect of acute stress in the right ventral prefrontal cortex (7Networks_RH_Default_PFCv_3; ROI 377, MNI centroid coordinates: 23, 55, 7; T = -4.84, pFDR = 0.003) and left insula (7Networks_LH_SalVentAttn_FrOperIns_4; ROI 100; MNI centroid coordinates: -43, 4, -1; T = -3.90, pFDR = 0.045). In the post-hoc analysis on individual gradients, we found that after the TSST, the left insula’s gradient connectivity profile shifted toward the somatomotor end of gradient 2 (pG2 Bonferroni = 0.002) and toward zero on gradient 3 (pG3 Bonferroni = 0.030), whereas the control group showed the opposite shift on gradient 2 and remained stable on gradient 3. In the right vPFC, the stress group shifted toward the transmodal end of gradient 1 (pG1 Bonferroni < 0.001) and the visual/DMN end of gradient 2 post-stressor (pG2 Bonferroni < 0.001), while the control group increased along both gradients. Within-network dispersion in the FPN, SN, and DMN showed no significant session-by-group interaction (all p > 0.056, Bonferroni-corrected). By contrast, we observed significant between-network dispersion interactions for SN–DMN (p = 0.029, Bonferroni-corrected) and SN–FPN (p = 0.002, Bonferroni-corrected). A post-hoc analysis showed that in the stress group along gradient 1 (pG1 SN-DMN Bonferroni = 0.027; pG1 SN-FPN Bonferroni = 0.024), the DMN and FPN decoupled from the SN, shifting toward the transmodal end of this gradient. Similarly, along gradient 2, the DMN and FPN decoupled from the SN (pG2 SN-DMN Bonferroni = 0.019; pG2 SN-FPN Bonferroni = 0.024), shifting toward the visual/DMN end, while the SN shifted toward the somatomotor end. In our data, differences in chronic stress (as measured by TICS) were not associated with differences in eccentricity (p FDR > 0.81 or with within- or between-network dispersion.

Conclusion

We showed that acute psychosocial stress reorganized cortical function via two coordinated processes: salience–left insula circuits shifted toward sensorimotor connectivity, suggesting heightened vigilance, while the right ventral prefrontal cortex, together with the DMN and FPN, shifted toward transmodal connectivity, indicative of potential stressor appraisal. Cross-sectionally, gradient and dispersion measures were not associated with self-reported chronic stress (TICS). Future longitudinal work should test whether repeated engagement of this configuration predicts maladaptive outcomes or stress-related symptoms. Overall, we identified a coordinated, network-level signature of acute stress in macroscale functional connectivity that linked salience-driven sensorimotor bias with transmodal reconfiguration.
