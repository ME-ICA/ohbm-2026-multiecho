Introduction

Ketogenic dietary therapies (KDTs) are an important option for pediatric drug-resistant epilepsies (e.g., Lennox–Gastaut syndrome, infantile spasms, Dravet syndrome, myoclonic–astatic epilepsy) and metabolic epilepsies including GLUT1 and pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) deficiency, yet the mechanisms by which metabolic change translates into brain-network stabilization remain incompletely specified. We developed a metabolically informed spectral Dynamic Causal Modeling (DCM) framework for resting-state EEG (rs-EEG) that extends conductance-based frequency-domain DCM1,2 inversion to capture metabolically driven excitability shifts. The extension introduces ATP-sensitive potassium (KATP) gating driven by a local ADP/ATP balance, enabling a principled link between bioenergetic state variables and membrane conductances.3,4 Given evidence that epileptic activity engages default mode network (DMN) regions,5 we focused on DMN effective connectivity as a target for quantifying KDT-related network modulation in children with drug-resistant epilepsy.

Methods

rs-EEG was acquired at baseline and at an early follow-up after KDT initiation in children with drug-resistant epilepsy. A four-node default mode network (DMN) was specified comprising the medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), precuneus (PrCu), and bilateral lateral parietal cortices (lLP, rLP). Cross-spectral densities (CSD) were modeled with a conductance-based spectral dynamic causal model (DCM) under physiologically constrained priors. The neural mass model included AMPA and GABA_A synaptic conductances and a voltage-dependent NMDA conductance. We extended the generative model with an ATP-sensitive potassium (KATP) conductance whose open probability was governed by a local ADP/ATP state variable representing perimembrane metabolic microdomains.3,4 Subject- and session-specific parameters were estimated via variational Bayesian inversion to reproduce empirical CSD. Longitudinal treatment effects were assessed using a hierarchical Parametric Empirical Bayes (PEB) framework: a second-level within-subject model captured the mean and the follow-up–baseline difference for each child (X1=[1 1; 1 −1]); a third-level group model then explained individual difference terms using age, KDT duration (days on diet at follow-up), and clinical improvement status (improved vs not improved), with continuous covariates Z-scored and centered and Bayesian model reduction used to identify parameters with strong posterior evidence.

Results

The metabolic-DCM reproduced key CSD features across sessions and yielded robust parameter estimates, indicating good model adequacy. Relative to pre-therapy fits, post-KDT inversions indicated a shift in the inferred metabolic state consistent with increased ADP-driven KATP opening, accompanied by changes in DMN effective connectivity. Compared with pre-therapy conditions, metabolic constraints such as the ketogenic diet were associated with elevated ADP levels that increased KATP channel open probability, providing a mechanistic link between channel-level alterations and large-scale brain network dynamics.

Conclusion

Incorporating ADP/ATP-dependent KATP gating into conductance-based spectral DCM provides a mechanistic pathway from cellular bioenergetics to large-scale effective connectivity measured with rs-EEG. This metabolically constrained DCM framework may support individualized forecasting and optimization of ketogenic interventions in drug-resistant childhood epilepsy, potentially reducing diet-related burden by improving early identification of likely responders.
