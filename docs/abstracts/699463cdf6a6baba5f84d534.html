Introduction

Accumulating evidence suggests that stronger acupuncture stimulation leads to enhanced clinical responses; however, the network-level neural basis of acupuncture dose–response effects remain unclear. Our previous work introduced somatic acupuncture (SA) and visual acupuncture (VA) paradigms to dissociate somatic and visual components of acupuncture stimulation and demonstrated graded psychophysical and regional brain activation patterns with increasing stimulation intensity.^1 Building on these findings, the present study aimed to examine whether graded SA and VA stimulation modulate functional connectivity (FC) at the brain network level.

Methods

Twenty-four healthy participants underwent somatic and visual acupuncture at three graded dosage levels (control, low-dose, and high-dose) during functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). Stimulation intensity was manipulated by varying rotation angle and frequency parameters, with identical dose levels applied across SA and VA conditions. SA involved needle insertion and manual rotation at the acupuncture point on the leg, whereas VA consisted of viewing prerecorded videos of the same acupuncture stimulation without somatosensory input. A resting-state fMRI scan preceded the event-related task fMRI. Task-based functional connectivity was assessed using a trial-wise general linear modeling framework, with regions of interest derived from group independent component analysis of resting-state fMRI data and organized at the brain network level. Functional connectivity between regions was then examined across graded stimulation conditions for both acupuncture modalities.

Results

Across both somatic and visual acupuncture, graded stimulation elicited widespread, network-level functional connectivity changes involving sensorimotor, salience, visual, and default mode networks. Stimulation effects (control vs. stimulation) and dose-dependent effects (high vs. low) showed partially overlapping but modality-specific connectivity patterns, with SA preferentially engaging sensorimotor- and salience-related network interactions, whereas VA predominantly modulated visual- and default mode–related network connectivity.

Conclusion

Together, these findings indicate that network-level functional connectivity analyses provide complementary insight into graded and modality-dependent acupuncture-related brain responses that are not fully captured by regional activation measures alone.
