Introduction

A transient increment of thermal nociceptive stimulus causes a disproportionate reduction of pain perception. This phenomenon is known as offset analgesia (OA) and was attributed to activation of the descending pain modulatory system by an earlier functional magnetic resonance (fMRI) study.

Methods

After finding that OA was decreased in patients with chronic pain in comparison with healthy controls,1 we went on to perform whole-brain fMRI during OA stimulation on the forearm, as well as multimodal MRI including resting-state fMRI, diffusion, and 3-dimensional anatomical MRI.

Results

A between-group comparison of OA-related activation in 17 patients with chronic pain and 17 healthy controls revealed significant suppression of both the descending pain modulatory and reward systems in the patients.2 Anterior insular volume loss in the patients was associated with its weakened functional connectivity with the reward systems in the nucleus accumbens and medial prefrontal cortex.3 In addition to those systems, attenuation of 2 different intracerebral networks was found associated with pain chronicity. Fractional anisotropy of the prefrontal corpus callosum, as well as that of the anterior thalamic radiation, was smaller in the patients implicating suppressed interhemispheric inhibition of pain-related activity.4 Furthermore, after-pain activity at the middle temporal gyrus, associated with pain rumination, was enhanced in the patients, while it was suppressed by top-down network activity from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex.5

Conclusion

From the above findings, we suggest that novel pain modulatory circuits might exist in the interhemispheric and prefrontal-temporal networks over the traditional descending and reward networks. Attenuation of all those 4 systems might contribute to nociplastic pain that lacks peripheral pathology. That is, cessation of pain might depend on those novel intracerebral pain modulatory networks: "the brain inhibits brain-originated pain," disorder of which might potentially be associated with pathogenesis of nociplastic pain.
