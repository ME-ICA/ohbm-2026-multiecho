Introduction

The Graph Laplacian has shown promise in network neuroscience, providing a mathematically elegant way to describe diffusion, synchronization, and large-scale patterns of neural activity across the brain’s structural connectome1,2. Connectome’s intrinsic harmonic modes are modelled as eigensystem, offering a compact basis for understanding how functional signals propagate through anatomical pathways. Yet, the Laplacian approach remains fundamentally abstract: it describes activity in terms of smoothness, spectral modes, and algebraic operators, but offers limited intuition about the physical processes that give rise to these patterns. 

We propose a reinterpretation of the Graph Laplacian through the lens of electrical Nodal Analysis; a framework grounded in physical laws of current flow and conservation3. By mapping the structural connectome onto a resistive–capacitive network, the Laplacian matrix naturally emerges as the conductance Laplacian governing node voltages, while its eigenvalues correspond to the effective “stiffness” of independent current-flow modes. This transformation reveals that connectome harmonics are not merely mathematical abstractions, but physically meaningful patterns shaped by principles analogous to Kirchhoff’s laws, where gradients in activity drive fluxes along structural pathways.

This perspective provides a mechanistic foundation for Laplacian-based approaches, enabling intuitive interpretations of functional connectivity, mode dominance, and oscillatory propagation. Ultimately, recasting the Graph Laplacian as a Nodal Analysis problem bridges analytical tools with biophysically interpretable processes: the Graph Laplacian tells what patterns are mathematically possible whereas the Nodal Analysis tells which patterns are physically realizable.

Methods

In nodal analysis, structural brain regions are modelled as nodes with temporal integration, coupled by structural connectivity, and driven by stochastic inputs whereas functional correlations are predicted as the covariance structure emerging from noise-driven network dynamics. The Graph Laplacian1,2 emerges naturally as the operator governing interactions between nodes linking this approach naturally to previous works on the Spectral Graph Model4 of the human brain.

Three subjects from Human Connectome Project5 were selected for the preliminary investigation of this proposal. The minimally preprocessed diffusion-weighted and resting-state functional MRI data were used 1) to obtain the Default Mode Network to investigate method’s applicability on structural brain analyses on streamline level (Fig. 1) and 2) to calculate structural and functional connectomes based on the Desikan-Killiany parcellations to investigate the predictive properties of the proposed approach on whole-brain level(Fig. 2).

Figure 1 demonstrates how the structural analysis of the Default Mode Network could be improved with the proposed approach by pruning the full tractogram that does not propagate functional correlations in the network. A) The full tractogram of the network and B) pruned tractogram.

The open source Python code used in this study is freely available online and link to it will be released in the poster. The code is written so it can take functional and structural connectomes (whole-brain or network specific) as well as streamlines as inputs to compute the proposed nodal analysis.

Results

The analysis of streamlines in the Default Mode Network revealed that many of the streamlines purely based on diffusion information were not necessary for the functional information and therefore could be pruned away (Fig. 1). These pruned streamlines might be parts of other networks, or they could be false positive findings that are typical to tractography.

The predictive capability (Fig. 2) of the proposed method was on average 0.73 measured as Pearson’s correlation coefficient between the predicted and measured functional correlations across the whole-brain network of the three HCP subjects. While this is not very high correlation, it is slightly higher or on the same level with many of the recent deep learning-based brain network graph analyses5.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that the nodal analysis, typically applied to analysis of electrical circuits, can be used to predict functional correlations from structural connectome and used on tractography streamline basis to prune non-necessary streamlines from a tractogram. The streamline-wise information could be used in the future to study how different diseases or conditions might affect the functional information propagation in the brain white matter. While modelling the human brain as an analogical circuit might sound far-fetched, mathematically nodal analysis is based on the same Graph Laplacian principles that have been proposed earlier1,2.

The benefit of the nodal analysis is that it is more interpretable than many of the proposed Laplacian operators: structure models the conductance, function models the potential, and the propagation of the function in the structural networks models the current of a RC-circuit. While the Laplacian captures the geometric structure of connectivity and defines the space of possible network modes, nodal analysis reveals how activity actually propagates through that space by explicitly accounting for signal flow, temporal integration, and energetic constraints.

By modeling brain regions as nodes that integrate and redistribute inputs, this framework explains why specific network modes dominate observed functional activity and how correlations emerge from shared pathways under stochastic drive. Importantly, nodal analysis enables interpretations of functional connectivity in terms of currents, impedances, and dissipation, offering insights that are inaccessible to static spectral descriptions alone. These results show that nodal analysis does not replace Laplacian theory, but enriches it by transforming an abstract operator into a mechanistic model of brain dynamics, thereby providing a deeper understanding of structure–function relationships in large-scale neural systems.
