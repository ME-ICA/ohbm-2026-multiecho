Introduction

Voxel-based morphometry (VBM) and voxel-based physiology (VBP) measure structural atrophy and functional hypometabolism, respectively. In Alzheimer’s disease (AD), VBM and VBP changes have signature, but distinct, patterns, with VBM changes predominantly occurring in the bilateral hippocampus and temporal gyrus and VBP changes occurring in the posterior cingulate and bilateral parietal lobes. However, whether these alterations occur in a network-based pattern remains unclear. The Network Degeneration Hypothesis states that patterns of neurodegeneration are not random and instead follow healthy functional networks. Here, we hypothesize that both VBM and VBP alteration patterns in AD occur in a network-based manner.

Methods

BrainMap databases (VBM & VBP) and PubMed were used to identify AD studies for meta-analysis. Inclusion/exclusion criteria were applied. Activation likelihood estimation (ALE) was performed separately for VBM and VBP to identify cross-study convergence of alteration patterns. Local maxima were used to define regions of interest (ROIs). Meta-analytic connectivity modeling (MACM) was used to identify functional connections between ROIs (nodes). Specifically, each ROI was seeded into the BrainMap Task-Activation database (>100,000 healthy subjects) to determine regions of co-activation. ALE analysis and thresholding were applied to determine significant connections between nodes.

Results

A total of 95 VBM and 68 VBP AD < controls contrasts were included. VBM analysis identified 5 clusters comprising 19 local maxima. VBP analysis identified 5 clusters with 14 maxima. MACM of VBM-derived nodes resulted in 24 edges at a stringent threshold (p < 0.01), with an additional 33 edges at p < 0.05. All VBM nodes were connected to at least one other node at p < 0.05. MACM of VBP-derived nodes resulted in 19 edges at p < 0.01 and 21 additional edges at p < 0.05. Three nodes were disconnected, while all other nodes showed robust connectivity. Conclusion

Both VBM and VBP alterations in AD exhibit network-based organization consistent with the Network Degeneration Hypothesis. These findings suggest strong potential of VBM and VBP alterations to serve as network-based biomarkers for AD diagnosis and staging.
