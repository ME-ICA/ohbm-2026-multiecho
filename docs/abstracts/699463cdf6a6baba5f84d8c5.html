Introduction

The use of parallel transmit (pTx) imaging1,2 has been rising with the increased availability and usability of pTx solutions for research, as well as with recent FDA clearance for 7T pTx for clinical imaging. pTx  maging helps to mitigate excitation inhomogeneities and SAR limitations that are more prominent at ultrahigh fields. While there are robust, standardized solutions for daily and weekly quality assurance (QA)  imaging for the receiver side3, there remains little if any guidance for QA on the multi-channel transmit of the pTx imaging system, including from scanner manufacturers themselves. Robust pTx QA is a prerequisite for high quality brain imaging research based on this new generation of scanner technology, as any systemic instabilities in the transmit chain may confound results. To help address this gap, we outline procedures for collecting multi-channel pTx transmit data and basic stability analysis.

Methods

Daily QA scans were performed on a 7T Siemens Terra (Erlangen, Germany) in pTx mode using an 8 channel transmit/32 channel receive pTx Nova head coil (Nova Medical, Wilmington, MA) on a standard functional Biomarker Imaging Research Network (fBIRN) phantom from January 2023 to July 2025. The scanner was upgraded to the Terra.X platform and QA scans resumed from August 2025 to October 2025. A new Nova head coil was included with this upgrade, but was virtually identical in design to the pre-upgrade coil. The standard QA protocol3 consisted of a localizer, a serial BOLD sequence (ep2d_bold, 68 volumes, SMS-MB=3, 81 slices, FOV 192 mm2, resolution 1.2x1.2x1.5 mm3, TE/TR=22/1700ms, 55° FA, no partial Fourier), and a product GRE-based receive channel QA sequence (gre, 0.5x0.5x5.0 mm3 resolution, TE/TR=6/30ms). The coil position, phantom placement within the coil, shimming volume prescription, and imaging prescription were held constant across all daily QA scans. Prior to imaging, the scanner performs an automated calibration to adjust the voltage magnitude and phase of the transmit channels to optimize B1+ homogeneity4. This procedure creates two key internal files: a text-based transmit log and a Matlab data file containing the transmit calibration imaging data. These internal files are generated once per imaging session and were collected for pTx QA analysis. Amplitude (as a scale factor of transmit voltage) and phase for each transmit channel was extracted from the log (figure 1), and calibration images were reconstructed for each transmit channel from the data file. The peak intensity locations for each channel were extracted, and the mean signal intensities of a local ROI centered on those locations were calculated. All analyses were performed with MATLAB (Mathworks, Natick, MA). This procedure was repeated with each QA to monitor the day-to-day stability of the transmit chain.

Results

Other than the new coil provided with the upgrade to the Terra.X platform, no significant events affecting coil performance occurred from January 2023 to October 2025. For visualization purposes, data prior to July 2024 is not shown in figures. Figure 1 shows the daily variation in amplitude and phase related to B1 shimming for each channel, which shows increased variability on the Terra.X platform. Prior to the upgrade, the overall standard deviation across all eight transmit channels was 0.046 and 14.8° for the amplitude scale factor and phase, respectively. After the Terra.X upgrade, these values increased to 0.075 and 20.4°, respectively. Figures 2a and 2b show the extracted peak intensity location of each channel’s B1+ adjustment data (only X and Y coordinates over time are shown since the Z coordinate did not vary due to the physical arrangement of transmit coils). These peak locations were stable over time, both before and after the scanner upgrade. The peak locations on the Terra had a mean displacement of 0.6±0.2 voxels, while those on the Terra.X had a mean displacement of 0.4±0.2 voxels. Note that higher resolution B1+ adjustment data was collected after the upgrade to Terra.X, resulting in a different set of peak voxel coordinates. Figure 2c shows the mean image intensity per transmit channel of the B1+ adjustment data for a small ROI around each peak. These ROI means show a small systematic downward drift of 3.74±0.8% across all channels in the 3 months prior to upgrade but otherwise remained stable. On the receive side QA, there was a corresponding downward drift of 10.8% in SNR and 2.8% in the radius of decorrelation5 during the same 3 months.

Conclusion

We present a simple procedure to extract and analyze internal scanner data generated during B1+ pTx shimming, requiring no additions or changes in standard daily QA protocols. This data can be collected, stored, and tracked as a proxy for QA on the multi-channel transmit side of the imaging chain. With the rising prevalence of pTx imaging for both research and clinical purposes, transmit stability should be monitored to mirror receive side QA. Monitoring and ensuring transmit stability allows for more robust and consistent brain imaging and can facilitate benchmarking over long term and multisite studies utilizing pTx scanners.
