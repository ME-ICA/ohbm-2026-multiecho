Introduction

Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) has become an ideal tool for studying the neural mechanisms of social interaction in realistic settings because of its high ecological validity. Understanding these neural mechanisms depends heavily on accurately identifying key brain regions, making the precise placement of fNIRS optodes crucial for ensuring data accuracy and reproducibility. However, current localization methods face significant challenges: the 10-20 system provides only rough localization, which is insufficient for targeting core brain regions precisely; individual structural image registration is more accurate but requires expensive equipment and complex procedures, limiting its practicality for large-scale studies; and the photon measurement density function (PMDF) model has not been tailored for social interaction-specific brain networks, reducing its adaptability and targeting accuracy. To overcome these limitations, this study introduces an fNIRS targeting method for social interaction research based on the transcranial functional brain atlas framework and PMDF, aiming to develop a social interaction PMDF-based transcranial brain atlas (PMDF-TBA) for precise localization of key brain regions involved in social interaction using fNIRS. 

Methods

The approach involves two main steps: first, extracting coordinates related to social interaction from existing imaging databases and relevant studies through large-scale meta-analysis, then creating a probabilistic brain atlas by integrating brain activation data with maximum likelihood values to map the spatial distribution and probabilistic features of core social interaction regions; second, using this probabilistic atlas as a target, constructing a continuous standard fNIRS channel space based on structural MRI data from 48 healthy adults, and accurately estimating each channel’s sensitivity to social interaction-related brain regions to generate the social interaction-specific PMDF-TBA.

Results

Results show that under some social interaction paradigms, brain activation detection using this atlas is significantly better than with traditional localization methods.

Conclusion

This atlas offers an innovative approach for precisely placing fNIRS optodes in social interaction studies and provides an important tool for understanding the neural basis of social behavior and social cognitive impairments in autism spectrum disorder (ASD), thus holding considerable scientific and clinical value.
