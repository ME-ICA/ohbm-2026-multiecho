Introduction

Classic psychedelics profoundly alter conscious experience and provide a powerful tool for perturbing large-scale brain dynamics. Electrophysiological studies consistently show broadband α, β desynchronization under serotonergic psychedelics [1], yet LSD may additionally accelerate rhythmic activity [2]—a phenomenon previously hinted at but never systematically mapped across cortex.

The vast majority of the previous studies analyze raw power spectra [1], which conflate periodic and aperiodic activity [3], obscuring whether apparent “desynchronization” reflects true oscillatory weakening or changes in the 1/f background.  More recent work has begun to parameterize the spectrum and explicitly analyze oscillatory modes [4]. However, prior work has neither systematically characterized LSD-induced peak-frequency shifts nor analyzed oscillatory power while accounting for such shifts, increasing the risk of conflating frequency displacement with true desynchronization. 

Here, we combine spectral parameterization, peak-resolved oscillatory analysis, and complexity metrics with MEG recordings acquired under LSD and placebo. This framework enables us to (i) identify spatially resolved shifts in α and β peak frequencies, (ii) isolate true oscillatory damping from changes in 1/f structure, and (iii) characterize how LSD alters temporal irregularity and fractality.

Our goal is to provide the first unified electrophysiological account of how LSD reorganizes cortical oscillations across spectral, aperiodic, and temporal dimensions.

Methods

Data, Preprocessing and Source Localization: We analyzed MEG recordings from 17 participants in a within-subject, single-blind LSD versus placebo study [2], acquired approximately 4 hours post-injection using a 275-channel CTF system. MEG data were band-pass (1-120 Hz) and notch filtered, segmented into 2-s epochs, and cleaned using a multi-step artefact rejection pipeline combining visual inspection, Autoreject, and ICA. Approximately 200 high-quality epochs per run were retained, with no significant difference in data quality between LSD and placebo conditions. Source localization was performed using individual MRI-based forward models and dSPM, with source signals morphed to a common template, parcellated into 360 cortical regions defined by the HCP-MMP atlas [5]. The regional time-series were used to probe for LSD-induced changes on spectral, and complexity measures.

Spectral parameterization. To dissociate periodic and aperiodic components, PSDs were modeled using the Fitting Oscillations and One-Over-F toolbox [3] over the 1–120 Hz range (fixed aperiodic mode; up to 5 peaks). From each model, we extracted (i) peak frequency and peak power for α, β, and high-γ oscillations; (ii) band-limited high-γ oscillatory power after 1/f removal; and (iii) aperiodic offset and exponent. Robustness was confirmed using a test-retest analysis by fitting spectral parametrization models on a narrower 1–45 Hz.

Complexity metrics. Lempel–Ziv complexity (LZC) was computed on concatenated epochs after binarization and normalized by sequence length. Higuchi fractal dimension (HFD) was computed per epoch, using Koptimal=18 selected via knee-detection algorithm. HFD values were averaged across epochs.

Machine-learning decoding. Random Forest classifiers were used to decode LSD versus placebo using (i) single-feature families (e.g., α peak power, β peak shift, exponent, LZC, HFD) and (ii) a multifeature model concatenating all metrics across regions. Model selection used GroupKFold cross-validation (K = 4). Statistical significance was assessed using 1000 label permutations to generate null distributions of decoding accuracy and feature importance.

Statistical analysis. For global PSDs, drug effects were tested using non-parametric cluster-based statistics across frequencies. For regional contrasts (LSD–PLA), paired permutation t-tests (50,000 permutations) were used, controlling the false-positive rate at p < 0.05.

Results

Figure 1 summarizes the primary electrophysiological effects of LSD on cortical dynamics (main effect of drug).

Global and regional spectral effects (Fig. 1A–D). Region-averaged spectra revealed a broadband reduction of low-frequency power under LSD. After removal of the aperiodic component, oscillatory activity showed robust attenuation spanning the θ–β range, alongside a relative increase at higher frequencies (Fig. 1A). Importantly, inspection of the oscillatory component revealed systematic α and β peak-frequency shifts. Consequently, part of the apparent band-limited power reduction may reflect peak displacement rather than genuine amplitude loss.

At the regional level, α and β power reductions became widespread when oscillatory power was quantified at individual spectral peaks after removing the 1/f background (Fig. 1B–C). Alpha peak-power suppression localized predominantly to visual, temporal and associative cortices, whereas β peak-power suppression was more widespread, extending across frontal, parietal, and temporal regions. 

High-γ activity showed a distinct profile (Fig. 1D). While raw spectra suggested increased high-frequency power under LSD, isolating the oscillatory component revealed suppressed high-γ band power, particularly in posterior cortices. This inversion indicates that apparent γ increases are driven primarily by changes in the aperiodic background rather than enhanced oscillatory synchronization.

Peak-frequency shifts and aperiodic structure (Fig. 1E). Both α and β oscillations were significantly accelerated under LSD. For interpretation, we display grand-averaged peak-frequency changes projected onto the corresponding significant LSD–placebo contrast masks. Alpha peak shifts were widespread, with an average increase of ~2 Hz, whereas β peak shifts were larger (~5 Hz) but more focal, primarily involving frontal and associative cortices. Analyses comparing the magnitude of peak-frequency shifts and peak-power suppression revealed no significant correlation, indicating that oscillatory acceleration and damping reflect partially independent processes.

LSD also produced pronounced flattening of the aperiodic exponent and reductions in offset (Fig. 1E), with strongest effects in posterior and associative regions. In parallel, temporal complexity increased, as indexed by elevated HFD and LZC. Regions exhibiting the strongest aperiodic flattening also showed the largest increases in complexity, linking broadband spectral rebalancing to increased temporal irregularity.

Machine-learning decoding and feature importance. Random Forest classifiers reliably discriminated LSD from placebo using both spectral and temporal features. Decoding accuracy was strong above chance (p<0.01, permutation-corrected) across feature families, with particularly strong performance for β peak power (Accuracy: 84%), aperiodic exponent (Acc: 88%), HFD (Acc: 83%), and LZC (Acc: 78%). Feature-importance analyses converged with classical statistics, highlighting the robustness of the findings.

Conclusion

In this study, we provide a characterization of how LSD reorganizes human cortical dynamics measured with MEG, demonstrating that its acute effects arise from multiple dissociable mechanisms. By combining spectral parameterization, peak-resolved oscillatory analysis, and aperiodic modeling we identify three principal axes of modulation. First, LSD induces strong alpha and beta power damping in auditory, visual, language, and emotion-related cortical regions, while largely sparing motor cortex. Second, LSD produces shifts in alpha and beta peak frequencies that follows a distinct functional gradient, independent of power suppression. Third, LSD flattens the aperiodic (1/f) spectrum and increases temporal complexity (HFD, LZC), with the largest changes in sensory and imagery domains, consistent with a shift toward a desynchronized, high-entropy cortical regime. Overall, LSD induces a structured reorganization of cortical dynamics across multiple spectral dimensions rather than a uniform global perturbation. Future work aimed at disentangling the neuropharmacological mechanisms underlying oscillatory damping and peak shifts will be essential for clarifying how these effects underlie the LSD state.
