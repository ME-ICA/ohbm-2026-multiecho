Introduction

Alpha-range (8-12Hz) neural oscillations have been associated with a role in gating for selective attention and cognitive control1, with concurrent EEG-fMRI studies and source localization supporting its association with engagement of fronto-parietal attention networks. However, alpha modulations have been observed in contexts other than selective attention, such as free recall2 or resting state3, suggesting a more dynamic role in cognition. To test this hypothesis, we identified dynamic coactivation patterns (CAPs) of blood-oxygen level dependent (BOLD) network activity associated with modulations of alpha power during resting state. This allowed us to evaluate if alpha power modulations are related exclusively with fronto-parietal network engagement or whether they reflect more dynamic changes in BOLD activity.

Methods

We recorded, concurrently, EEG and fMRI signals while participants (n=94) rested with their eyes open. Alpha power was extracted for a-priori occipital electrodes and averaged in contiguous non-overlapping two-second time windows, then convolved with a hemodynamic response function. We identified static alpha-related FC using voxel wise linear regression between convolved alpha power and BOLD time series to replicate previous research showing an inverse relationship between alpha and resting-state BOLD visual activity. We then identified dynamic patterns of BOLD activity using alpha-power constrained CAP analysis4. Namely, across subjects, we identified TRs associated with the top and bottom 15% of alpha power time series peaks, then performed k-means clustering (5000 iterations & replications) on the corresponding BOLD spatial patterns to establish the presence of underlying states. Phase scrambling null modeling was used to validate that CAPs represented neural activity and not random noise, while Dice coefficients were used to associate CAPs with canonical BOLD networks.

Results

Static FC analysis replicated previous finding of a negative association between alpha power and BOLD signal within visual cortex. However, CAP analysis revealed that alpha power is also associated with dynamic states anchored in five configurations: somatosensory & dorsal/ventral attention (CAP1), default mode network (CAP2), visual/dorsal attention (CAP3), control-default mode (CAP4) and visual/somatosensory & dorsal/visual attention (CAP5). The contribution of these networks to peaks versus throughs of alpha power differed, as did their composition. In particular, whereas peaks of alpha power were associated with two instantiations of CAP4 (control-default mode network coupling), troughs but not peaks were associated with CAP5 (visual/somatosensory & dorsal/visual attention coupling). States shared between peaks and troughs (CAP1, 2 and 3), also showed subtle differences in regions, cortical and sub-cortical, contributing to the overall patterns.

Conclusion

The results replicate that alpha power modulations at rest are associated with previously known static BOLD activity in visual cortex, while also demonstrating dynamic BOLD activity anchored around canonical networks. These dynamic CAPs vary between troughs and peaks of alpha power and vary in composition. The data are consistent with a more dynamic association of alpha oscillations with cortical activity that includes fronto-parietal networks, but not exclusively, and is thus more reflective of a global brain state (network interaction configuration) than a single functional network.
