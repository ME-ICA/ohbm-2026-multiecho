Introduction

Epilepsy is a neurological disorder affecting over 65 million people worldwide. It is a network disorder characterised by recurrent seizures due to excessive neuron firing. The epileptic discharge of 30% of patients could not be controlled by antiepileptic medications and many of them are considered for surgical resection of epileptogenic tissue. Intracranial Electroencephalography (iEEG) is gold standard pre-surgical tool to localize the regions responsible for seizure generation.  Although iEEG provides excellent temporal resolution and a high signal-to-noise ratio, the identification of seizure onset zone remains challenging due to its different onset patterns, rapid propagation, and network-level seizure dynamics. Earlier studies reported that a considerable proportion of patients continue to experience seizures despite surgical resection due to the issues associated with poor localization. In this work, an attempt has been made to differentiate the seizure onset zone (SOZ) and non-SOZ using dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) based connectivity features of iEEG. DMD captures the inherent nonlinear and nonstationary characteristics associated with the brain dynamics.

Methods

The iEEG of fifty-nine epileptic patients who have gone for presurgical evaluation was considered for this study. The surgical outcome was classified as Engel 1 for those whose seizures were stopped and considered as Engel 2+ for the patients who continued to have seizures. The signals associated with the SOZ and non-SOZ are preprocessed to remove artefacts and subjected to DMD for the representation of neural signals into different spatiotemporal modes and sub-band frequency modes. This approach captures the dynamic patterns of epileptic discharge that propagate faster at the seizure onset. The modes represent the coherent recurring pattern of brain activity with a fixed oscillation frequency. The temporal change of each mode is governed by an exponential function, allowing reconstruction of the EEG signal as a sum of these modes. Functional connectivity measures such as magnitude squared coherence (MSC) and phase locking value(PLV), were evaluated from the modes.  Graphical network features such as clustering coefficient and betweenness centrality were extracted from the resultant connectivity matrix to build functional brain networks (FBN). Further, we analyse how these networks differ in SOZ and non-SOZ. 

Results

Magnitude Squared Coherence (MSC) was constructed from the dynamic modes to quantify the linear relationship between brain regions in the frequency domain, measuring how well one signal predicts another. MSC values range from 0 to 1, where higher values indicate stronger connectivity. An MSC of 1 signifies perfect linear coupling at a given frequency.  From the Fig. 1 a, it can be observed that there is a strong connectivity network in SOZ whereas, no such connectivity framework is observed in the case of non-SOZ. The connectivity patterns of non-SOZ is shown in Fig. 1 b. Similar results were found in all patients with Engel-1 surgical outcome. In the case of Engel 2+ patients, strong connections are also found in non-SOZ which could be a possible reason for the presence of epileptic tissue. The efficacy of PLV is analysed for both SOZ and non-SOZ. It is observed that MSC-based graph features, along with random forest classification algorithms, detect the EEG of SOZ with an accuracy of 81%. The PLV is found to detect with an accuracy of 76% in classifying SOZ and non-SOZ.

Conclusion

The proposed study demonstrates that DMD is an effective framework in analyzing the intracranial EEG signals with DMD based approach. Functional connectivity measures derived from DMD-reconstructed signals, namely Magnitude Squared Coherence (MSC) and Phase Locking Value (PLV), revealed significantly stronger synchronization within seizure onset regions. Further, the graph features extracted from the network is found to localize the SOZ with a maximum accuracy of 81%. The proposed approach can be readily implemented in the iEEG system to aid neurosurgeons for the accurate demarcation of SOZ. This eventually improves the surgical outcome of epilepsy patients and improve their quality of life.
