Introduction

Our brains are constantly bombarded with a vast and ever-changing stream of sensory information. Predictive Coding (PC) hypothesizes that our brains construct a generative hierarchical model of the world around us that allows us to predict the information that will come into our senses. This predictive account of perception has been investigated extensively in the auditory cortex using oddball-like paradigms.1,2

In previous research, we have probed the understanding of hierarchical information exchange in the auditory cortex using a cascading oddball paradigm.3 Our results highlight the role of deep cortical layers in processing model updates and the role of superficial cortical layers in detecting deviation from the expectations elicited by the current context. However, in our first paradigm, local and global regularity violations always occurred simultaneously, preventing us from disambiguating local from global error signatures. The ‘local-global’ paradigm has been previously introduced to study both automatic and conscious processing of oddball stimuli, dissociating two kinds of error (e.g. local [short term] and global [longer term]) processing across the auditory hierarchy.4

Here, we adapt our previous cascading paradigm to include both local and global components. Leveraging the higher signal-to-noise ratio, available at 10.5 Tesla, we collected 0.65 mm isotropic gradient echo blood oxygenation level-dependent (GE-BOLD) functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) data. To allow the investigation of layer-specific responses to both prediction error signals and model updating signals across the hierarchy, we extended the coverage to include the auditory cortex bilaterally and the frontal regions without compromising temporal resolution. 

Methods

Design

We adapted the classical local-global paradigm to investigate the violation of local and global regularity on 'error’ responses,3 using cascading sequences.4 Sequences of four tones (100 ms tones, interleaved with a 100 ms gap) were presented with the first three tones in either an ascending or descending acoustic frequency order. The fourth tone in a sequence was either following the ascending or descending pattern (local standard) or violating it (local deviant). Sequences were arranged in groups of five (a quintet, with 500 ms gap between sequences), and to manipulate the long term context we presented quintets in two types of blocks. The first type presented 78% of quintets composed by only local standard sequences (local standard global standard [LSGS]) while in 22% of the quintets the fifth sequence was a local deviant (local deviant global deviant [LDGD]). The second type of block quintets were composed in 78% of cases by only local deviant sequences (local deviant global standard [LDGS]), while in the remaining 22% of quintets the last sequence was a local standard (local standard global deviant [LSGD]). To strengthen the block context, each block started with three habituation quintets in which we presented only global standards (e.g. either composed by only local standards or only local deviants - see Fig. 1 for a depiction of sequences and quintets and the relevant experimental conditions). 

To make sure that in the context of local deviants the occurrence of a local standard but global deviant would not result in the presentation of a rare acoustic frequency (one that had never been presented before), we shifted that acoustic frequency of the first tone in a sequence across the sequences composing a quintet (and did the equivalent in the context that consisted of local standards). During scanning quintets were presented using a slow event-related design with an ITI of [16 - 18 s]. We collected 6 runs in one participant in which we counterbalanced the global context across runs.

Scanning parameters

The data were collected using a 10.5T Siemens Magnetom System with an in-house built 16 transmit and 80 receive-channel head coil. We collected 0.65 mm isotropic voxels with GE-BOLD with a TR of 2.3 seconds (FOV: 190 mm x 182 mm, TE: 20.6 ms; iPAT: 4; partial Fourier: 5/8; Multiband factor: 2, 60 slices; phase encoding = posterior to anterior). In addition to the functional data, we collected MPRAGE data for anatomical reference (0.75 mm isotropic, TR = 5000 ms, TI = 1500 ms, TE = 2.69 ms, 224 slices). MP2RAGE anatomical data from the same participant were collected in a previous session (0.7 mm isotropic, 192 slices) in which we tested the functional protocol.

Data Analysis

All anatomical and functional data were processed using BrainVoyager (BV - v24.1). Functional data were preprocessed using slice scan timing corrections (sinc interpolation) and 3D rigid body motion correction (sinc interpolation - aligning all volumes of all runs to the first volume of the first run). High pass filtering (removing slow drifts) was performed using a discrete cosine transform using 4 basis functions and low pass temporal filtering was applied using a kernel over 2 consecutive datapoints. Finally, the data were corrected for geometric distortions using Topup (FSL, version 6.0). We aligned the functional time series to the MP2RAGE data collected in a different session by combining: 1) intra session alignment of the fMRI data to the anatomical MPRAGE and 2) between-session alignment of the anatomical data. When creating the functional time series co-registered with the anatomy the functional resolution was not changed (i.e. 0.65 mm isotropic). 

BOLD activation maps were generated using a general linear model with task predictors (convolved with an HRF response) accounting for: 1) all experimental conditions separated in ascending and descending order sequences (e.g. LSGS_ascending; LSGS_descending etc.) and 2) the habituation phase. 

Results

We acquired high-resolution functional images with a voxels size of 0.65 mm isotropic at 10.5T covering not only primary auditory and association areas bilaterally, regions that are generally difficult to image, but also frontal areas such as the inferior frontal gyrus (Figure 2A). The inclusion of frontal areas allows the investigation of their involvement in hierarchical predictive processing, as the generative model of the world is thought to be stored here. Figure 2B showcases the EPI image quality (mean-EPI image).

The 0.65 mm isotropic data had sufficient temporal signal-to-noise ratio to reliably detect activation in relation to the auditory stimuli. An omnibus F-test thresholded at qFDR<0.05 demonstrated increased BOLD responses in auditory areas, with significant voxels localized within the gray matter ribbon (see Figure 2C). This demonstrates the ability of our paradigm to investigate of local and global error signatures separately. Figures 2D and E present example event-related averages of the local deviancy effect in Heschl’s Gyrus and global deviancy in the frontal cortex.

Conclusion

We leveraged the increased SNR of 10.5T to collect 0.65 mm isotropic functional data (as opposed to 0.8 mm isotropic voxels) covering temporal and frontal regions. Using a local-global paradigm, we aimed at distinguishing the cortical contribution of local (short-term) and global (long-term) contextual processing in the soundscape.4 Our initial results demonstrate sufficient data quality to reliably detect cortical activation in response to sound presentation. In addition, the large coverage of our acquisition allowed us to have first indicative results of the hierarchical nature of the processing of both local and global errors at a resolution that also allows the investigation of laminar responses. These results highlight the promise of using 10.5T for high resolution mapping of hierarchical cortical responses while not compromising temporal efficiency. That is, we collected the highest resolution auditory data to date with a large coverage in just 2.3 seconds, which affords good temporal sampling of event-related responses necessary to separately analyze standard sequences and oddball sequences. Extending these analyses to the laminar level will allow testing the hypothesis that deep cortical layers carry information related to global model update.3
