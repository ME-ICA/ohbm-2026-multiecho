Introduction

Cortical folding variability is increasingly recognized as a meaningful neuroanatomical signal,particularly in psychiatry and neurodevelopmental research.While classical neuroimaging pipelines tend to minimize this variability through template alignment,recent approaches instead treat folding patterns as biologically informative features.This motivates the development of learning frameworks that capture local sulcal geometry while also integrating global anatomical context.

This global-to-local perspective is supported by fundamental principles of cortical morphometry.In particular, cortical surface area, hull surface area, and mean cortical thickness have been shownto follow a logarithmic–linear scaling law across individuals and species,indicating that global morphometric organization constrains local folding geometry.At the same time, recent self-supervised foundation models of cortical folding have demonstratedstrong potential for learning fine-grained sulcal representations across large populations.However, global morphometric descriptors are rarely used to guide local cortical representation learning.

In our work, we address this gap by proposing a global-to-local cortical representation learning framework that integrates subject-level morphometric descriptors into contrastive learning.Building on the Champollion foundation model, we incorporate a Y-aware contrastive formulation in which pairwise similarities between sulcal embeddings are weighted according to morphometric metadata.By leveraging biologically grounded descriptors inspired by cortical scaling laws,our approach explicitly links global morphometric organization with local sulcal geometry.We evaluate whether this integration improves generalization across environmental and clinical prediction tasks,such as maternal smoking exposure and schizophrenia classification,and whether it yields interpretable morphological signatures associated with psychiatric disorders.

Methods

Structural MRI scans were preprocessed using BrainVISA~\cite{cointepas2001brainvisa} to extract cortical sulcal skeletons.

After region selection, cropping, and spatial normalization, sulcal segments were encoded using Champollion~\cite{laval2024self}, a self-supervised foundation model for regional cortical folding based on a 3D convolutional neural network.

Champollion learns robust latent representations of local sulcal geometry through contrastive learning, combining strong data augmentations with an objective that aligns multiple views of the same subject while separating different,The proposed framework is illustrated in Fig.~1.

We extend Champollion using a Y-aware contrastive learning framework in which pairwise similarities between embeddings are modulated by subject-level morphometric metadata.

Rather than relying on a single scalar proxy, we introduce a multivariate formulation based on morphometric vectors comprising log-transformed cortical surface area, hull surface area, and mean cortical thickness.

These descriptors are motivated by the logarithmic–linear scaling law of cortical folding, which links global morphometric organization to local folding geometry.Pairwise similarities are weighted using a Gaussian kernel applied to distances in morphometric space, thereby guiding local sulcal embeddings with biologically grounded global information.

We evaluate two distance formulations to compare how morphometric similarity is modeled:

a weighted Euclidean distance with inverse-variance normalization of each descriptor, and a

Mahalanobis distance that explicitly accounts for correlations between morphometric features.

A critical hyperparameter of the Y-aware formulation is the kernel bandwidth, which controls the

sensitivity of metadata-based weighting.

This bandwidth was initialized using Scott’s rule and optimized through validation across multiple

downstream tasks to balance local sensitivity and global smoothness in the latent space.

Models were pretrained and evaluated on the UK Biobank cohort~\cite{sudlow2015ukbiobank} (>40,000 subjects) and further tested

for generalization on an aggregated multi-site schizophrenia dataset (SCHIZCONNECT-VIP, PRAGUE,BSNIP1, CNP, CANDI).

Residualization analyses were performed to ensure that observed performance gains were not drivenby direct encoding of morphometric or demographic confounds.

Results

The results in Table.~1 highlight the importance of 

incorporating morphometric vectors to inform local sulcal skeleton representations. 

Using single scalar descriptors as metadata (e.g., volume, surface, thickness) 

did not yield improvements over the baselines (Champollion V1, SimCLR), and in several 

cases even underperformed.

By contrast, aggregating multiple morphometric descriptors into a vector and 

comparing subjects through a Euclidean distance led to consistent and statistically 

significant improvements. On the maternal smoking task (OCCIPITAL\_left), the Euclidean 

formulation achieved an AUC of $0.620$ versus $0.592$ for SimCLR, corresponding to an 

absolute gain of $+0.028$ ($\sim$4.7\% relative).

Statistical significance also improved substantially, with $-\log_{10}(p)$ increasing from $129 \pm 15$ to $160 \pm 8$ ($\Delta =+31$). To ensure these improvements were not driven by direct overfitting 

to the metadata, residualization of the latent space was performed on the same descriptors (volume, surfaces, 

thickness). Residualized scores remained unchanged, confirming that the embeddings 

capture information beyond these confounds. Statistical robustness was further confirmed 

using the Nadeau--Bengio corrected paired test on the 

10$\times$10 repeated cross-validation protocol.  

A similar trend was observed on schizophrenia (FCLP\_left), where the Euclidean 

vector achieved an AUC of $0.665$ compared to $0.652$ for SimCLR 

($+0.013$ absolute, $\sim$2\% relative gain). The association signal improved from 

$4.371 \pm 0.345$ to $6.014 \pm 0.429$ ($\Delta = +1.64$). Here, residualization was applied 

with respect to sex and site of acquisition, ensuring that predictive performance was not 

confounded by demographic or scanner effects. Since this evaluation was conducted on an 

independent test set, statistical significance was assessed using a Wilcoxon signed-rank 

test across seeds, confirming the consistent superiority of the Euclidean formulation.  

Overall, these findings emphasize that while scalar proxies provide limited benefit, explicitly informing local cortical geometry with global morphometric context through a simple Euclidean combination of descriptors yields a significant and reproducible advantage with respect to the SimCLR or Barlow Twins baseline. Despite its simplicity and independence assumption, the Euclidean distance proved more stable and discriminative than Mahalanobis, enabling robust generalization across both environmental (maternal smoking) and clinical (schizophrenia) prediction tasks.

Conclusion

We introduce a global-to-local cortical representation learning framework that integrates biologically grounded morphometric vectors into contrastive learning of sulcal geometry. By explicitly linking global anatomical organization with local cortical folding patterns, the proposed approach improves generalization across environmental and clinical prediction tasks while yielding interpretable neuroanatomical signatures.

These results highlight the importance of embedding global anatomical context into local representation learning and support the development of biologically informed foundation models for neuroimaging.
