Introduction

Vorasidenib, a dual isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH)1/2 inhibitor, has been shown to prolong progression-free survival in patients with IDH-mutant low-grade gliomas throught suppression of 2-hydroxyglutarate (2HG)1. However, conventional MRI remain insensitive to early treatment effects and often cannot distinguish true progression from treatment-related changes. Diffusion MRI (dMRI) and MR spectroscopy (MRS) provide noninvasive insighs into tumor microstructure and metabolism. Microscopic fractional anisotropy (µFA) and kurtosis-derived measures (MKt, MKa, MKi) capture axonal organization, tissue complexity, and microstructural heterogeneity beyond conventional FA. Tumor metabolites such as choline (Cho), N-acetyl-aspartate (NAA), creatine (Cr) and myo-inositol (Ins) respectively reflect membrane turnover and cellular proliferation, neuronal integrity, energetic balance, and glial or inflammatory patterns in glioma.

This study investigates anomalies in tract-specific diffusion metrics and tumor-localized MRS measurements across multiple visits in glioma patients receiving vorasidenib, aiming to characterize treament response and longitudinal tumor growth dynamics.

Methods

Thirteen patients with IDH-mutant gliomas initiating vorasidenib therapy (40 mg daily) were scanned on a 3T Siemens Prisma at baseline (V1), 2 weeks (V2), 3 months (V3), and one year (V4).

Edited MRS data were acquired using MEGA-PRESS2 (TR/TE: 2000/68 ms; 128 pairs of transients) with VAPOR water suppression, and voxel size was adapted to tumor dimensions. AutoAlign was used for reproducible region of interest (ROI) placement across visits. Frequency and phase corrections were applied to individual transients in MATLAB, and metabolite quantification was performed on edit-off spectra using LCModel3 with water scaling.

Diffusion encoding was performed at b-values of 100, 700, 1200, and 2000 s/mm², with 6, 6, 10, and 21 directions for linear tensor encoding and 6, 6, 10, and 15 averages for spherical b-tensor encoding, respectively (TR/TE: 7700/90 ms; voxel size: 2×2×2 mm³). After preprocessing, the multidimensional diffusion MRI toolbox [https://github.com/markus-nilsson/md-dmri] was used to compute DIVIDE4 parameters : total kurtosis (MKt), anisotropic and isotropic components (MKa, MKi), and microscopic fractional anisotropy (µFA). Classical mean diffusivity (MD) was also computed.

Sixty white-matter bundles were segmented using TracSeg5 and were coregistered to the previous DIVIDE parametric maps. Figure 1 shows T2-FLAIR, µFA and MD maps for a representative case (Patient 3), with the tumor ROI voxel and arcuate fasciculus (AF) bundle masks for both hemispheres.

For each subject, bundle, visit and metric, weighted means of diffusion histograms were extracted for ipsilesional and contralesional hemispheres.

An asymmetry index (AI) was computed for lateralized bundles :

AI = (Ipsi - Contra) / ((Ipsi + Contra)/2).

Longitudinal changes were quantified as : ΔAI = AIVx - AIV1, relative to baseline (V1).

Pearson correlations between tumor metabolites (tCho, tNAA, tCr and Ins) and diffusion-derived metrics were computed across visits V1-V3. 

Finally, the longitudinal tCho/tCr ratios were plotted against subject-level trajectories of µFA and MD infiltration gradients (mean ipsi-contra values across bundles) to assess multimodal coupling between structural infiltration and tumor metabolic alterations.  

Results

Data from five patients who completed at least V1 and V2 were analyzed so far; five underwent V3 (Patients 3-5), and two reached V4 (Patients 1-2). Patient 2 showed radiological progression during follow-up and was switched from vorasidenib to temozolomide between V2 and V3, whereas all other patients remained on vorasidenib.

Tumors locations were : left temporo-insular (Patient 1), right fronto-temporal (Patient 2), left insular (Patient 3), right fronto-temporal with corpus callosum extension (Patient 4), and right temporo-insular (Patient 5).

Figure 2A shows longitudinal ΔAI for µFA (left) and MD (right). The striato-fronto-orbital (ST_FO) bundle AI could not be computed for Patient 5 due to complete disruption of the right ST_FO at tumor site. µFA ΔAI maps showed overall stability for Patients 1, 3 and 4. Marked negative ΔAI values were observed for Patient 2 at V4 across most bundles. Patient 5 displayed a progressive negative trend in the uncinate fascicle (UF) at V2 and V3 relative to baseline.

MD ΔAI maps generally showed patterns opposite to µFA. Patients 1, 3 and 4 remained stable across visits, while Patient 5 exhibited increased MD in the UF at V2 with partial reduction at V3. Patient 2 showed a pronounced shift between V3 and V4, transitioning from low or negative ΔAI to strongly positive values in the same bundles altered in µFA.

As expected, 2HG levels decreased following treatment intitiation acorss patients. Because 2HG primarily reflects IDH inhibition rather than tract-specific microstructural alterations, it was not included in diffusion-metabolic coupling analyses.

Figure 2B displays Pearson correlation matrices between tumor metabolites and bundle-specific dMRI metrics across V1-V3. tCr, tCho and Ins negatively correlated with MD and MKi, while tNAA positively correlated with µFA, MKa and MKt across all visits.

Figures 2C-D illustrate individual trajectories relating tCho/tCr ratios to µFA (C) and MD (D) infiltration gradients. Patients 1, 3 and 4 exhibited minimal gradient changes across visits. In contrast, Patients 2 and 5 showed substantial shifts beginning at V2, characterized by decreasing µFA gradients and increasing MD gradients over time, with  Patient 2 showing the largest changes by V4. Patients 1, 4 and 5 maintained tCho/tCr ratios below 0.4, whereas Patient 3 and Patient 2 (V3-V4) exhibited elevated ratios.  

Conclusion

Baseline-normalized ΔAI indices reflected individual responses to vorasidenib. Stable ΔAI patterns were consistent with treatment responsiveness, while large derivations from baseline, particularly in Patient 2,  indicated limited therapeutic benefit. Bundle-specific alterations highlighted spatially localized white-matter vulnerability around the tumor. Decreased µFA gradients suggested impaired microstructural anisotropy and axonal integrity, whereas increased MD reflected higher free-water content and diminished tissue complexity.

Negative correlations between MD and glial metabolites (tCho and Ins) may reflect reactive gliosis or edema, while positive associations between tNAA and anisotropy measures (µFA, MKa) suggest relative preservation of neuronal integrity.

Highly variable infiltration gradients captured the divergent trajectories of responders and non-responders. The tCho/tCr ratio, typically elevated in higher grade tumors, further differenciated metabolically active cases (Patient 2-3) from metabolically stable ones (Patients 1, 4, 5).

Together, these findings support the use of combined MRS and dMRI as complementary non-invasive biomarkers of tumor evolution and treatment response under vorasidenib. Future work will evaluate whether baseline and longitudinal metabolic-microstructural features can predict tumor growth direction and clinical progression, enabling more personalized monitoring and surgical planning.
