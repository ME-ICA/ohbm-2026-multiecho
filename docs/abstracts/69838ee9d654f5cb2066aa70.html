Introduction

Early childhood is a critical period for the emergence of behavioral regulation and emotional control, processes supported by coordinated subcortical-cortical systems. Disruptions in these networks can manifest as externalizing behaviors (e.g., impulsivity, aggression) or as internalizing symptoms (e.g., anxiety, withdrawal). These early difficulties are distressing and predict later psychopathology, highlighting the need to understand their neurobiological underpinnings.

The amygdala is central to emotion processing, learning, and behavioral regulation, and is therefore key to understanding individual differences in children’s behavioral traits. Its influence relies on dynamic connectivity with major cortical networks involved in cognitive control, decision-making, and social functioning. For example, weaker amygdala-prefrontal connectivity may reduce top-down regulation, leading to greater reactivity and externalizing tendencies.

Although often studied as a single structure, the amygdala comprises three subnuclei groups: centromedial, basolateral, and superficial, each with distinct connectivity and behavioral relevance. Hence, examining each subnucleus is essential for identifying neurobiological pathways underlying behavioral regulation, especially in terms of their differential connectivity profiles to major cortical networks.

This study investigates associations between amygdala subnuclei connectivity and behavioral problems in early childhood across two independent cohorts: BRAin COnnectivity in Learning Difficulties (BRACO-LD) and Singapore PREconception Study of long-Term maternal and child Outcomes (S-PRESTO). Using high-resolution subnuclei segmentation and a network-based connectivity framework, we aimed to identify amygdala-cortical pathways linked to externalizing and internalizing behaviors and assess their reproducibility across behavioral instruments. Node-level analyses were also conducted to isolate cortical regions driving network-level effects.

Methods

Overall study design is delineated in Figure 1.

Participants

Participants were drawn from two neuroimaging cohorts: the BRAin COnnectivity in Learning Difficulties (BRACO-LD) study and the Singapore PREconception Study of long-Term maternal and child Outcomes (S-PRESTO). After applying inclusion and exclusion criteria and quality control for imaging data, the final analytical sample included 361 children aged between 4.0-7.9 years. The BRACO-LD cohort comprised preschoolers with functional learning difficulties, while S-PRESTO included typically developing children from a population-based birth cohort. Written informed consent was obtained from parents, and study protocols were approved by institutional review boards.

 

Behavioral Measures

Child behavioral problems were assessed using two standardized parent-report instruments. The Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) provided total, internalizing, and externalizing scores reflecting broad psychopathology. The Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) offered complementary indices of externalizing (conduct and hyperactivity) and internalizing (emotional and peer) problems. 

 

MRI Acquisition and Preprocessing

Structural T1-weighted and resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data were acquired using a Siemens 3T Magnetom Prisma scanner. Functional data underwent preprocessing in the CONN toolbox (v20b) using standard pipelines, including realignment and unwarp, slice-timing correction, motion outlier detection, normalization to MNI space, segmentation, and spatial smoothing (6 mm FWHM). Denoising included regression of motion parameters, white matter and CSF signals, and outlier scans, followed by temporal band-pass filtering (0.008-0.09 Hz). 

 

ROI Definition and Functional Connectivity Analysis

The bilateral amygdala was segmented using FreeSurfer’s automated subcortical parcellation pipeline1 and subdivided into three subnuclei, basolateral (BLA), centromedial (CMA), and superficial (SFA), based on probabilistic atlases2. Cortical regions were defined using the Yeo7-network functional atlas3: Visual (VN), Somatomotor (SMN), Dorsal Attention (DAN), Ventral Attention/Salience (VAN), Limbic (LN), Frontoparietal (FPN), and Default Mode (DMN) networks. Seed-to-network resting-state functional connectivity (RSFC) was computed between each amygdala subnucleus and cortical network using Fisher z-transformed bivariate correlations from the BOLD signal.

 

Statistical Analyses

Analyses were performed in MATLAB (v2020b). Covariates included age, sex, mean framewise displacement, maternal education, and household income. To identify amygdala subnuclei-cortical network RSFC measures associated with overall behavioral difficulties, we first applied LASSO regression across 42 bilateral amygdala subnuclei-Yeo7 network RSFC pairs. To determine whether this effect was driven by specific behavioral domains, we conducted follow-up linear regression analyses with both CBCL Externalizing and Internalizing scores. Next, to evaluate the robustness and convergent validity of the CBCL findings, we conducted parallel linear regression analyses using SDQ Externalizing and Internalizing scores. Finally, we performed node-level analyses to explore specific cortical nodes driving network-level effects.

Results

361 children were included in this study (60.4% male; mean age ≈ 5.6 years, SD = 0.87). LASSO regression across 42 amygdala subnuclei-network RSFC pairs identified reduced right centromedial amygdala (CMA)-Limbic Network (LN) connectivity as the sole significant predictor of CBCL Total Problems (β = -6.202, p = .020). Follow-up linear regressions revealed that this effect was significant for both CBCL Externalizing (β = -13.600, p < .001) and Internalizing (β = -7.443, p = .038) domains, with a stronger association for externalizing symptoms. To establish convergent validity, analogous models were conducted using SDQ scores. Reduced right CMA-LN RSFC also significantly predicted higher SDQ Externalizing symptoms (β = -2.374, p = .026), replicating the CBCL findings. No significant association emerged for SDQ Internalizing (β = 0.371, p = .661). The consistency of effects across behavioral instruments underscores the robustness of CMA-LN hypoconnectivity as a neural correlate of externalizing behaviors. Within the Limbic Network, reduced connectivity between the right CMA and the temporal pole (LimbicA) (β = -13.785, p < .001) and between the right CMA and the orbitofrontal cortex (LimbicB) (β = -10.231, p = .038) were associated with higher CBCL Externalizing scores. These findings suggest that disrupted CMA coupling with regions responsible for social-emotional evaluation and reward processing contributes to behavioral dysregulation. 

Conclusion

This study provides novel evidence that functional hypoconnectivity between the right centromedial amygdala (CMA) and the Limbic Network (LN) plays a pivotal role in the emergence of early behavioral problems. Across two complementary behavioral assessments, reduced right CMA-LN resting-state functional connectivity consistently predicted higher levels of externalizing symptoms. The specificity of the effect to externalizing behaviors highlights the CMA’s unique role as the amygdala’s output hub, mediating rapid emotional and action-oriented responses through projections to subcortical and cortical limbic structures. When CMA-limbic coupling is reduced, emotional impulses may be less effectively modulated by regions involved in contextual evaluation and social-emotional regulation, such as the orbitofrontal cortex and temporal pole. Collectively, these findings advance understanding of the neurodevelopmental mechanisms underlying externalizing behavior in early childhood and identify the right CMA-limbic pathway as a promising neurobiological target for early identification and intervention efforts. Future longitudinal work should clarify whether CMA-limbic hypoconnectivity reflects a developmental delay, a stable vulnerability marker, or a modifiable target for prevention and treatment of behavioral dysregulation.
