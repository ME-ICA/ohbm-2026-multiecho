Introduction

Sleep disturbance has been linked to altered intrinsic brain functional organization, including disruptions in both local and network-level coherence [1-3]. Acupuncture at GV20 (Baihui) is commonly used in clinical practice to alleviate sleep-related symptoms and has been suggested to modulate central neural regulation [4, 5]. However, the neural mechanisms underlying its therapeutic effect remain unexplored. Leveraging voxel-wise local correlation (LCOR) measures to assess focal neural modulation [6], this study investigated whether GV20 acupuncture is associated with immediate changes in LCOR in individuals with sleep disturbance.

Methods

Adults aged ≥18 years with sleep disturbance (Insomnia Severity Index > 7) underwent resting-state fMRI before and after a single GV20 acupuncture stimulation using a 3T clinical MRI scanner (GE Discovery 750W). Functional data were acquired using T2*-weighted echo-planar images (TR/TE = 2000/30 ms, voxel size 3.44×3.44×4 mm³) and preprocessed using a default pipeline in the CONN toolbox. LCOR maps were computed by estimating short-range correlations within a Gaussian neighborhood (25-mm FWHM). To identify acupuncture-related effects, post- versus pre-acupuncture LCOR difference was evaluated using voxel-wise GLM and corrected for cluster-wise significance level at p < 0.05 using the topological FDR procedure.

Results

Eight participants were included (mean age = 29.88±8.92; mean ISI = 15.5 ± 4.63; mean PSQI = 10.88 ± 2.85). Following GV20 acupuncture, a significant cluster of increased LCOR was observed in a right posterior temporal–parietal (cluster size = 205; corrected p = 0.009). This cluster primarily encompassed the temporo-occipital middle temporal gyrus (42%), inferior lateral occipital cortex (27%), and angular gyrus (11%). Notably, the acupuncture-modulated regions mainly located within default mode network, including the right angular gyrus, which have been previously implicated in insomnia-related connectivity alterations [2, 3].

Conclusion

This pilot study demonstrated that a single session of GV20 acupuncture was associated with localized increases in LCOR within right posterior temporal–parietal regions in sleep-disturbed adults. These findings indicate that GV20 acupuncture modulates local functional coherence within regions associated with the default mode network, which are known to be disrupted in insomnia. Further studies with larger samples are warranted to elucidate the inter-individual variability and clinical relevance of these neural effects.
