Introduction

Background: Cognitive impairment is a frequent and heterogeneous feature of Parkinson’s disease (PD), even at early non-demented stages. The multifactorial mechanisms linking β-amyloid accumulation, cerebral metabolism, gray matter integrity and cognitive reserve remain poorly characterized from an integrative, predictive perspective. Objective: To determine how multimodal neuroimaging biomarkers account for cognitive heterogeneity in non-demented PD and to test whether multivariate brain–cognition patterns predict trajectories of cognitive decline over three years.

Methods

Fifty-three patients with PD underwent baseline multimodal imaging including FDG-PET, β-amyloid PET, and structural MRI, together with an extensive neuropsychological battery covering attention, executive function, language, visuospatial abilities, and memory. Cognitive status was defined according to MDS criteria, and cognitive reserve was quantified using a validated questionnaire. Partial Least Squares (PLS) cross-correlation analysis with joint singular value decomposition (SVD) was applied to identify latent variables (LVs) linking imaging features with domain-specific cognitive performance. Analyses were repeated including cognitive reserve as a covariate. Longitudinal neuropsychological follow-up at three years was used to assess predictive validity.

Results

PLS identified two significant latent variables explaining complementary aspects of cognitive heterogeneity. LV1 was associated with worse cognitive performance and characterized by increased β-amyloid burden predominantly in frontal and parietal regions, whereas LV2 showed the opposite profile, driven by preserved cortical metabolism across distributed regions. Both LVs survived family-wise error correction (P_FWE < 0.02). Importantly, baseline multimodal brain scores significantly predicted cognitive evolution at three years, explaining a substantial proportion of inter-individual variability in longitudinal cognitive change. Higher cognitive reserve moderated the strength of brain–cognition associations, attenuating the impact of amyloid-related patterns on cognitive outcomes.

Conclusion

Multivariate PLS–SVD modeling reveals distributed multimodal imaging signatures that explain and predict cognitive decline in early-stage PD. These findings highlight the role of large-scale cortical–subcortical networks and cognitive reserve in shaping cognitive trajectories, supporting the use of integrative, data-driven approaches for individualized risk stratification in Parkinson’s disease.
