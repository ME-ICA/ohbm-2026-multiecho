Introduction

Trauma exposure, educational level, premorbid adjustment, and cannabis use are critical factors influencing both the onset and progression of psychotic disorders. Lower levels of education have been consistently associated with an increased risk of developing psychosis, whereas higher educational attainment in individuals experiencing a first episode of psychosis is linked to better baseline functioning and less severe psychotic symptoms, as measured by SAPS, SANS, and BPRS1. The general premorbid adjustment questionnaire is a structured interview that assesses an individual’s level of social, educational, occupational, and personal functioning prior to the onset of psychiatric illness. General premorbid adjustment is implicitly related to years of education and, consequently, is also associated with these clinical and functional outcomes. On the other hand, persistent cannabis use further contributes to more severe symptomatology and poorer long-term outcomes, although symptom severity may improve following cessation2. Importantly, a history of non-consensual sexual experiences predicts an increased risk of psychosis, and this relationship is significantly moderated by cannabis use: specifically, individuals with both sexual trauma and cannabis use are more than seven times as likely to receive a psychosis diagnosis as those with neither exposure3.

This study aims to clarify the relationships among these variables in patients with first-episode psychosis (FEP) by modeling trauma both as a predictor and as a moderator, and to explore how trauma relates to brain structure, which prior evidence suggests may mediate the association between trauma and other phenotypic variables like general intelligence4. To this end, normative models have been used as tools to quantify individual deviation from typical brain trajectories and Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) have been used to perform path analysis and mediation moderated analysis.

Methods

Subjects

MRI data from individuals who had experienced a FEP, confirming a Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorder -SSD- (nSSD = 333, age = 29.81 ± 8.79), along with their healthy controls (nHC = 193, age = 29.04 ± 7.66), were obtained from Programa de Atención a las Fases Iniciales de Psicosis (PAFIP). SSD individuals were antipsychotic-naïve or minimally medicated. Structural MRI data (3D T1-weighted) were acquired using a 3T Philips Medical Systems.

MRI acquisition and processing

MRI images underwent quality assurance (Q/A). Using FreeSurfer v7.4.1, volumes were extracted at whole brain-level (Cortical Thickness -CT-, cortical Grey Matter Volume -cGMV-, Subcortical Grey Matter Volume -sGMV- and White Matter Volume -WMV-), and for subcortical structures (cerebellum, thalamus, pallidum, accumbens, hippocampus, caudate, amygdala, third and fourth ventricle). Whole-brain and subcortical volumes were referenced against normative data derived from Dorfschmidt et al. (2025)5. Thus, individual centile scores for these structures were obtained for each FEP patient, providing a measure of how these regions deviate from typical values for their sex and age.

Phenotypic assessments

A total of 323 FEP participants were evaluated using the Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale (BPRS, 24 items), the Scale for the Assessment of Positive Symptoms (SAPS, 30 items), and the Scale for the Assessment of Negative Symptoms (SANS, 21 items). Participants also completed two trauma questionnaires -one assessing childhood trauma and the other recent trauma- in which trauma severity was rated on a 7-point Likert scale. For each questionnaire, trauma severity (i.e., intensity) was analyzed using an independent principal component analysis (PCA) implemented with the Python scikit-learn library. 

SEM Analyses: Trauma as a predictor, premorbid adjustment as mediator and cannabis as moderator

Based on the hypothesis that trauma may exert a direct influence on clinical outcomes, we conducted three separate multi-predictor SEM mediation analyses with full moderation, one for each outcome. In these models, binary cannabis use (1 = use, 0 = no use) was specified as the moderator, the four trauma principal components (PCs) as predictors, general premorbid adjustment as the mediator, and SANS, SAPS, or BPRS as the respective outcome. All P-values were corrected using FDR.

SEM Analyses: Education as a predictor, trauma as moderator and brain volumes as mediator

Under the hypothesis that trauma may not only act as a predictor but also as a moderator of the relationship between education level and symptoms, we conducted a series of moderated mediation SEM analyses with multi-parallel mediators. Trauma PC scores were used as moderators, years of education as the predictor, brain centiles as mediators, and SANS, SAPS and BPRS as outcomes. All SEM analyses were performed using the Python library semopy, with age, sex, and socioeconomic status (binary variable: 1 = low, 0 = other) included as covariates. Building on this approach, a total of 12 models were estimated: for each outcome (SANS, SAPS and BPRS), 4 analyses were run for each trauma PC across the two trauma questionnaires. All models included full moderation and incorporated the four whole-brain centiles (CT, cGMV, sGMV and WMV) as multi parallel mediators. After this analysis, we subsequently tested the same model considering the centiles of the 18 subcortical regions plus sGMV as parallel mediators. All P-values were corrected using FDR. 

Visualization

The graphical representation of the subcortical plots of the aseg atlas (Desikan Killinay, FreeSurfer) was carried out using the Python library subcortex_visualisation.

Results

Principal Components of trauma

The PCA of trauma intensity yielded two major components for each trauma questionnaire (Figure 1A). For the childhood trauma questionnaire, the first component (PC1_trauma_Childhood; 30.2% explained variance) showed broadly positive loadings across all trauma domains, indicating a general childhood trauma factor. The second component (PC2_trauma_Childhood; 17.4%) loaded primarily on illness-related trauma and negatively on sexual violence.

Similarly, two PCA components were extracted for recent trauma in adult life. The first component (PC1_trauma_recent; 24.9%) also showed high loadings on general trauma, encompassing both violent and environmental exposures. In contrast, the second component (PC2_trauma_recent; 18.2%) loaded positively on violent trauma and negatively on environmental trauma, reflecting a dissociation between these trauma types.

SEM

We first examined whether premorbid adjustment mediated the association between trauma exposure and clinical outcomes, with cannabis consumption as moderator (Figure 1B). A significant mediation effect was observed only for PC2_trauma_recent (primarily loaded on violent trauma). Specifically, premorbid adjustment significantly mediated the effects of PC2_trauma_recent on SANS, SAPS, and BPRS scores. Interestingly, the mediation effect was stronger among cannabis consumers (indirect_moderated: β > 0.06, p < 0.03) compared with no-consumers (indirect_no_moderated: β > 0.03, p < 0.022).

Next, we examined how brain volumes mediated the association between education and symptomatology, with trauma acting as moderator. Both sGMV and WMV mediated the effect of education on negative symptoms (SANS) (Figure 1C, left). This mediation was significantly moderated by recent trauma (PC1), such that individuals with greater trauma showed stronger mediation effects. In addition, sGMV mediated the association between education and positive symptoms (SAPS), with this mediation again being moderated by recent trauma (Figure 1C, middle). Furthermore, childhood trauma (PC2) emerged as a moderator in the relationship between education and both SAPS and BPRS scores through sGMV and cGMV (β > Figure 1C, right). Importantly, both positive and negative mediation effects were observed, underscoring the complex and bidirectional interplay between education, trauma, brain structure, and clinical outcomes.

Finally, we performed SEM using 18 subcortical regions as parallel mediators. For negative symptoms, several regions -including the left amygdala, right thalamus, right caudate, left putamen, and left thalamus- emerged as significant mediators of the association between education and symptom severity, again showing significant moderation by trauma exposure (Figure 1D, left). On the other hand, when positive symptoms were considered as the outcome, five regions -the left amygdala, right pallidum, left putamen, and right putamen- significantly mediated the relationship between education and symptom severity (Figure 1D, right).

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that SEM combined with normative modeling approaches provides a powerful framework to integrate traumatic experiences, educational attainment, brain structural deviations, and psychotic symptomatology in patients with FEP. 

Our findings indicate when trauma is modeled as a predictor, recent trauma -particularly experiences involving violence and sexual assault- emerges as a significant determinant of poorer general premorbid adjustment and more psychotic symptoms (SANS, SAPS and BPRS). Within this framework, cannabis use acts as an important moderating factor, amplifying the indirect effects of trauma on symptom severity among users, consistent with multi-hit vulnerability models of early psychosis. 

Our findings also indicate that the relationship between years of education and both positive and negative symptoms is partially mediated by deviations in sGMV, suggesting a pathway through which educational factors relate to early clinical expression of psychosis. When recent trauma is modeled as a moderator, higher levels of trauma significantly strengthen these indirect pathways. Region-specific analyses further reveal that distinct subcortical structures contribute differentially to the mediation of positive and negative symptoms.
