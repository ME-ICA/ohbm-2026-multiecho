Introduction

Structural covariance networks (SCN)—defined as inter-individual covariation in cortical morphology—capture long-term coordinated maturational processes across distributed cortical systems and have been widely used to characterize large-scale brain organization relevant for social cognition. Valk et al. (2016)1 reported partially dissociable SCN patterns associated with empathy and Theory of Mind (ToM), reflecting interactions among salience-related, mentalizing-related, and perceptual systems. Using seeds in the dorsal anterior insula, temporoparietal junction, and additional prefrontal and temporal regions, they identified distinct large-scale structural covariance networks.

However, the reproducibility of SCN architectures across independent datasets and contemporary preprocessing frameworks remains largely untested. SCN estimates are known to be sensitive to factors such as cortical surface reconstruction, vertex resolution, and ROI definition—yet these dependencies have rarely been quantified. At the same time, increasing attention to replicability in neuroimaging highlights the need to validate previously reported associations, particularly those involving individual differences in empathy and mentalizing traits.

In this study, we address these gaps by (i) replicating the seed-based SCN analyses of Valk et al. (2016)1 in an independent cohort using updated FreeSurfer and SurfStat pipelines; (ii) assessing how alternative anatomical and functional ROI-definition strategies influence SCN outcomes; and (iii) testing whether empathy and ToM scores modulate covariance strength among regions. Beyond replication, this work provides insight into how analytic and ROI-definition choices shape observed SCN patterns, thereby informing the robustness and interpretability of structural covariance approaches in social-cognitive neuroscience.

Methods

Participants and MRI Processing

A sample of 137 healthy adults underwent T1-weighted structural MRI. All participants gave informed consent in accordance with institutional guidelines. Structural preprocessing was performed with FreeSurfer 7.4.1, including skull stripping, intensity normalization, white/gray matter boundary extraction, topology correction, and cortical thickness estimation. Cortical surfaces were registered to the fsaverage5 template for group analysis.

Manual quality control followed a parallel double-rater procedure: Each participant's reconstruction was independently reviewed by two trained raters; Surface boundaries, pial/white matter segmentation, and topology errors were examined; Discrepancies were resolved through consensus review by a third rater; Manual edits (e.g., control points, white matter edits) were applied when necessary. This procedure ensured high reliability across subjects and harmonization of surface reconstructions.

ROI Definition

Because the original study used heterogeneous functional and anatomical criteria for defining empathy- and ToM-related ROIs, we systematically compared alternative ROI-construction strategies:

1）Atlas-based ROIs: HCP-MMP1 parcels corresponding to the anatomical labels reported by Valk et al. (2016)1 were used to approximate original seeds within a standardized cortical parcellation.

2）Functional overlap masks: Functional overlap masks derived from EmpaToM task contrasts and meta-analytic conjunction maps of empathy and mentalizing.

3）Task-activation–derived clusters: EmpaToM activation peaks reported in the literature were reconstructed from volume space to the cortical surface using mri_vol2surf. These exploratory seeds enabled testing for behavior-related modulation in task-relevant regions not contained in the HCP atlas.

The EmpaToM paradigm2 reliably activates empathy- and ToM-related cortical systems by presenting emotional video clips followed by questions probing mental-state inference. Regions consistently engaged across EmpaToM contrasts and meta-analytic maps were used to define functionally grounded ROIs.

For ROI-based analyses, cortical thickness was extracted using a surface-area–weighted averaging procedure to minimize biases arising from differences in ROI size or vertex density.

For each seed ROI, we modeled cortical thickness at each vertex using SurfStat GLMs, controlling for age and sex. Cluster-level significance was evaluated using random field theory (RFT) with FWE correction (p<0.025), following Valk et al. (2016).

Behavioral Measures and Modulation Analyses

Behavioral empathy and ToM scores were obtained from the EmpaToM task, which provides separate indices of affective empathy (emotional ratings) and Theory of Mind (mental-state inference accuracy). These scores served as individual-difference measures in behavior-modulated SCN analyses.

Interaction models of the form were used to assess whether empathy or ToM scores alter covariance strength. Empathy and ToM behavioral scores were included as moderators in interaction models:

CTj∼CTi+ age+ sex+ Behavior+ CTi×Behavior.

Interaction significance was evaluated using PFWE < 0.05. Exploratory analyses were additionally conducted using task-derived EmpaToM ROIs.

Results

System-level SCN architecture replicates key components of Valk et al. (2016)1: Across 13 seeds, seed-based SCN recovered three major systems consistent with the original report.

dmPFC and TPJ seeds covaried with medial/lateral prefrontal, temporo-parietal, and anterior–middle temporal regions, reproducing the canonical mentalizing/DMN subsystem. dAI seeds covaried with anterior/middle insula, orbitofrontal cortex, and parietal operculum, consistent with a salience/interoceptive network. STS/STG seeds covaried with bilateral STS, lateral temporal, and auditory belt areas, forming a social-perception/speech-processing pathway.

Despite variability in cluster extent, the network-level organization showed clear replication.

Behavioral modulation: no significant effects for HCP-defined seeds: For the 13 predefined HCP seeds, no empathy × covariance or mentalizing × covariance interactions survived FWE correction. Trend-level findings varied across ROI definitions, consistent with the instability of brain–behavior interaction effects in modest samples.

Exploratory EmpaToM-derived seeds show localized modulation effects: Task-activation–based seeds (left IPL, dmPFC, precuneus, right IPL) yielded small, spatially restricted PFWE-significant clusters modulated by ToM or empathy scores. These effects were highly dependent on ROI definition and did not generalize to the HCP-based seeds, indicating preliminary, hypothesis-consistent but non-robust behavior modulation.

Methodological insights: SCN outcomes varied across ROI-definition strategies and analytic choices, including surface registration, vertex resolution, and area-weighted thickness extraction. These dependencies help explain discrepancies between studies and underscore the need for standardized, transparently specified pipelines.

Conclusion

This replication study provides an updated assessment of structural covariance networks supporting social cognition. By applying contemporary preprocessing pipelines and multiple ROI-definition strategies, we show that large-scale SCN organization is broadly stable across datasets, whereas finer-grained covariance patterns and behavior-related modulation effects are more sensitive to methodological choices and sample size. These findings underscore the importance of transparent analytic specification, standardized ROI frameworks, and adequately powered designs for future investigations of individual differences in social-cognition networks. More broadly, the present work highlights how replication-oriented analyses can clarify which aspects of SCN architecture are robust and which require further methodological refinement.
