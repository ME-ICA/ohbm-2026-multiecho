Introduction

For neuroimaging research, accurate brain MRI registration, encompassing both global affine alignment and subsequent non-linear refinement, is fundamental to most group analyses, longitudinal studies, and multi-site neuroimaging projects. Well-established registration toolboxes such as FSL (FNIRT), ANTs,1 and SPM perform non-linear registration by optimizing smooth and parameterized deformation fields, providing stable transformations but sometimes struggling to capture fine-scale anatomical correspondence, particularly in regions with subtle structural variability; moreover, their iterative optimization procedures are computationally expensive. In parallel, deep learning-based non-linear registration approaches such as VoxelMorph (VXM) and vector field attention (VFA) achieve fast inference with competitive accuracy,2,3 yet their performance may degrade under domain shift. In this work, we focus on the non-linear registration stage and aim to systematically evaluate how established and learned methods generalize across datasets.

Methods

In this work, 500 T1-weighted MRI scans from the IXI dataset were used to train the deep learning models. A held-out set of 77 IXI subjects and an additional 50 locally acquired T1-weighted scans (hereafter referred to as L50) were reserved for evaluation, representing in-distribution and out-of-distribution test settings, respectively. All L50 participants were scanned under a protocol reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of National Yang Ming Chiao Tung University, and written informed consent was obtained from each individual prior to data collection. All images underwent an identical preprocessing pipeline: voxels were resampled to 1 mm³ isotropic resolution, image intensities were normalized to the [0, 1] range, images were cropped to 160 × 224 × 192, and scans were aligned to the MNI152 template using affine registration in ANTs.4

VXM and VFA were trained under an unsupervised framework using randomly selected paired training volumes, with normalized cross-correlation (NCC) as the similarity metric and a deformation regularization weight of λ = 1. Both models were optimized using the Adam optimizer for 5000 epochs with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁴ and a batch size of 1. In addition, we implemented a method for instance-wise optimization, pursuing finer-grained refinement than the zero-shot VFA model. We term this method VFA-Refine (Fig. 1). The VFA-Refine procedure consists of three stages. First, for each image pair, we compute the initial deformation field from VFA and extract the highest-resolution encoder feature map. Second, we perform localized gradient-based optimization using the mean squared error between the warped moving and fixed feature maps, updating primarily locations with strong feature gradients and stopping after 50 iterations. Finally, the resulting adjustment field was composed with the original VFA deformation to produce the refined output. All models were implemented in Python using the PyTorch framework, and training and inference were conducted on a workstation equipped with an Intel i7-14700K CPU and an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU. For quantitative comparison, structural segmentations were obtained using the TigerBx toolkit with FreeSurfer-compatible Aseg labels.5 We then calculated the Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) for key anatomical regions. For intensity alignment, we employed locally-computed, globally-averaged metrics: NCC, mutual information (MI), and structural similarity (SSIM).

Results

Table 1 list the performance metrics of the registration methods. Across both datasets (i.e., IXI and L50), deformable registration methods consistently outperformed affine alignment by a substantial margin. Among established methods, SyN scores noticeably below those of the deep learning-based approaches. Comparing within deep learning methods on the IXI test set, VFA and VFA-Refine shows a small yet consistent advantage over VXM. When evaluated on the L50 dataset, which was never used during training, deep learning models showed a modest decline in performance, reflecting domain shift. VXM’s mean DSC decreased from 0.774 on IXI to 0.761 on L50, accompanied by a notable increase in standard deviation, suggesting that its predictions became less stable under distribution shift. VFA from 0.791 to 0.786, and VFA-Refine from 0.791 to 0.788, while VFA-Refine also retained the highest NCC and SSIM on L50. Taken together, these results indicate that VFA is more robust to distribution shift than VXM, and that the additional VFA-Refine step further stabilizes performance by strengthening local anatomical correspondence across datasets. The refinement continued to yield the highest scores on the external dataset. These findings indicate that the refinement process, although lightweight, effectively strengthens local correspondence in a way that transfers beyond the training distribution.

Conclusion

Overall, this study highlights a clear performance gap between established toolbox-based and deep learning–based MRI registration methods. Deep learning models consistently outperformed SyN and affine alignment. Although all methods showed a moderate performance decline under domain shift, VFA demonstrated better resilience, and the proposed VFA-Refine variant achieved the smallest degradation (0.791 to 0.788), suggesting that its refinement step effectively corrects fine-scale local misalignments at the voxel level without disturbing the macroscopic anatomical alignment already achieved by the base VFA model. These findings suggest that lightweight, feature-guided refinement can enhance the robustness of existing registration networks and may offer practical benefits when applying learned models to real-world multi-site neuroimaging data.
