Introduction

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) are two common neurodevelopmental disorders. Although they have partially distinct clinical features, they often co-occur and exhibit overlapping symptoms1. Recent studies have increasingly highlighted immune system dysregulation as a potential contributing factor for both ASD and ADHD2–4. This study aimed to identify the shared basis of immune dysregulation in both ASD and ADHD and to explore commonalities and distinctions in inflammatory cytokines.

Methods

We systematically searched the PubMed, Web of Science, and Embase databases for relevant literature published up to January 1, 2025. The search strategy combined terms related to ASD, ADHD, and inflammatory cytokines. For all inflammatory markers, a network meta-analysis was performed when at least one study provided data for both ADHD and ASD, and the total number of included studies was ≥3. This analysis integrated both direct and indirect evidence to enable multiple comparisons, with p-values adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg method. A random-effects model was used, and Hedges’ g was used as the effect size measure for blood inflammatory factor levels. Additionally, meta-regression analysis was employed to assess the influence of age and gender on effect size.

Results

A total of 74 studies were included (58 on ASD and 16 on ADHD), comprising 990 participants with ADHD, 4,331 participants with ASD, and 5,037 healthy controls. Compared to controls, individuals with ASD showed higher levels of CPR (Hedges's g=0.57, 95% CI [0.26, 0.89], p=0.001), IL-6 (Hedges's g=0.61, 95% CI [0.28, 0.93], p<0.001), IL-8 (Hedges's g=0.48, 95% CI [0.31, 0.64], p<0.001), IL-1β (Hedges's g=0.54, 95% CI [0.26, 0.81], p<0.001), and BDNF (Hedges's g=0.53, 95% CI [0.16, 0.90], p=0.016). In contrast, individuals with ADHD showed lower levels of TNF-α (Hedges's g=-0.91, 95% CI [-1.61, -0.21], p=0.016), IL-2 (Hedges's g=-0.49, 95% CI [-0.85, -0.12], p=0.013), and MCP-1 (Hedges's g=-0.54, 95% CI [-0.86, -0.23], p=0.001). Comparisons across disorders based on network meta-analysis indicated that individuals with ASD had higher levels of TNF-α (Hedges's g=-0.20, 95% CI [-2.00, -0.41], p=0.009), IL-2 (Hedges's g=-0.66, 95% CI [-1.07, -0.25], p=0.013), IL-8 (Hedges's g=-0.67, 95% CI [-1.13, -0.21], p=0.006), IL-1β (Hedges's g=-0.55, 95% CI [-1.04, -0.05], p=0.045), and MCP-1 (Hedges's g=-0.72, 95% CI [-1.09, -0.34], p=0.001) compared to those with ADHD. Meta-regression showed no significant moderating effects of age or gender on inflammatory cytokine concentrations.

Conclusion

Our findings revealed distinct inflammatory cytokine patterns in peripheral blood between ASD and ADHD. Individuals with ASD display broader-spectrum immune dysregulation, while ADHD exhibits a decrease in the levels of certain specific inflammatory factors, particularly TNF-α. These profiles hold promise as diagnostic biomarkers and may facilitate advance in subtyping, disorder progression monitoring, and individualized intervention.
