Introduction

Non-invasive neurostimulations such as repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (rTMS) or transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS) have been shown to be able to alleviate pain in about half of the patients suffering from chronic neuropathic pain [1]. A main challenge in clinical practice is to understand why some patients are responding and others are not. Hypothesis related to stimulation conditions or related to personal biological characteristics are searched for. Recent publications suggest that cerebral perfusion could be modified in relation with pain. For instance, in patients with diabetic neuropathic pain [2], a significant increased perfusion in cerebral blood flow was found in the right anterior cingulate cortex and a decrease in the left ventral striatum. In another study, researchers suggest that visual distortions could result from reduced perfusion of the non-dominant posterior parietal lobe during migraine episodes [3]. It is thus of interest to explore the involvement of the cerebral perfusion in chronic neuropathic pain. Here, we present an ancillary study of a clinical trial (NCT04936646) aiming at comparing the analgesic effectiveness of three modes of rTMS treatment. These three conditions involve (1) a superficial rTMS stimulation with a target over the primary motor cortex, (2) a deeper rTMS stimulation with a target over the hand knob of the primary motor cortex and (3) a sham rTMS stimulation.

The aim of the present study is to investigate: (aim 1) whether the cerebral perfusion differs at baseline between hemispheres corresponding to painful vs. non painful sides, (aim 2) whether the perfusion is modified after a rTMS treatment in true stimulation vs. sham control and (aim 3) whether the cerebral perfusion could be used as prognostic factor for responsiveness to rTMS treatment.

Methods

Patients, protocol and data acquisition: Forty-five patients with pharmaco-resistant chronic neuropathic pain were included in this longitudinal mono-centric double-blinded study. Each patient was randomly allocated to one of the three groups. At baseline, each patient had an MRI examination including: a structural MRI, a functional mapping of the motor cortex on the hemisphere corresponding to the painful and to the non-painful sides, a pCASL cerebral perfusion, and a resting-state fMRI to assess the functional connectivity. The main parameters of the pCASL sequence were: TR=4s, Tdelay=1.8s, TE=11ms, 14 slices with voxel size of 3.75x3.75x6 mm3. The rTMS protocol consists of one session per day for five days during the first week, then three sessions the second week and then one session the third week, followed by two monthly sessions. During treatment, the patient scored daily his/her pain level using a visual numeric scale (VNS). Finally, a second MRI examination, identical to the initial one was performed after the end of treatment.

Subject’s level analysis: The perfusion images were pre-processed with the SPM25 software in the following way. First, the anatomical and perfusion images were converted into BIDS format. Second, a visual quality control of each anatomical and perfusion images were done to reject images with poor quality or with large deformations. Third, perfusion images post and pre-treatment were both co-registered to the anatomical MRI of the post-treatment session. Fourth, the grey matter of the anatomical MRI was segmented and used to compute the deformation field that permits to fit to a symmetrical template in MNI space, using the DARTEL technique of SPM. Fifth, the deformation field was further applied to the anatomical image and to the perfusion images. Sixth, the perfusion images were slightly smoothed with a 3-mm Gaussian kernel. Finally, in order that the painful side is represented in the same side of the images, the MRI of patients with left-sided pain were left-right flipped. Note that the superimposition of the two sides is ensured because the template used during pre-processing is symmetrical. Doing so, the painful hemisphere is located on the left side of the images and the non-painful hemisphere on the right side. 

Statistical analysis at group level: At baseline, the comparison of the perfusion from painful hemisphere and the non-painful hemisphere is statistically done by a paired-t test. This was done with the whole group of patients and in subgroups according to the pain location (aim 1). A two-sample t-test was then performed between responders and non-responders subgroups to explore whether there was a prognostic factor to responsiveness (aim 3). After treatment, the differences in perfusion images for each group were entered in an ANOVA analysis to compare the treatment related perfusion changes between the 3 groups (aim 2). Post-hoc tests were performed using Tukey’s test. 

Results

Among all the data, the images of six subjects were not considered (3 patients retired from the study after 1st session, two presented too large deformations and one had a corpus callosum agenesia). 

Aim 1: Are painful perceptions related to a perfusion modification? At baseline, the comparison between the hemispheres corresponding to painful vs. non painful sides using paired-t test, elicited a small region identified as the Nucleus Accumbens (NAcc), with a hypoperfusion in the hemisphere corresponding to the painful side and a second small region within the inferior frontal gyrus (iFG) (Figure 1) also presenting a hypoperfusion.  

Given the known somatotopy of the primary motor cortex, we tested whether there exists a difference in perfusion between the hemisphere corresponding to painful vs. non-painful side according to the location of pain (superior member or inferior member). This was tested using a paired t-test in each subgroup of patients. Only a tendency for a hypoperfusion was found in the ventro-posterior insula in the painful vs non painful side. After correction for multiple comparisons, this was not significant.

Aim 2: After rTMS treatment, in the whole group of patients, we did not identified a change in cerebral perfusion nor in each separated group of patients. However, when comparing the perfusion variations in responders vs. non-responders sub-groups, we found an increased perfusion in the NAcc region and a hypoperfusion in the hippocampal gyrus (HG) and in the middle temporal gyrus (mTG).

Aim 3: At baseline, the comparison of perfusion images before treatment between responders and non-responders elicit a small region within the dorsal part of the anterior cingulate cortex with hyperperfusion, although it is not significant after correction for multiple comparisons. This region is also known to be involved in the cognitive control of pain [4].

Conclusion

The present study is one of the very first exploring perfusion related to chronic neuropathic pain. The results found here correspond to regions that are located in the pain matrix [5], in areas known to be related to emotional component of pain (NAcc), cognitive control of pain (dACC), to a stress model of chronic pain or pain anticipation (HG). The involvement of the NAcc seems new in this field of research. However, it is known to be involved not only in reward system but also in chronic pain [4]. It is also the target of deep brain stimulation to alleviate pain.

These results were achieved in small groups of patients and need replication since some of the results do not remain when correcting for multiple comparisons.

At time of submission, we remain blind of the 3 groups. Additional analysis results will thus be presented at time of the conference.
