Introduction

Anterior temporal lobectomy (ATL) and selective amygdalohippocampectomy (SAH) achieve seizure freedom in 60-80% of patients with drug-resistant temporal lobe epilepsy. However, 30-40% experience persistent postoperative seizures despite appropriate selection, representing a significant clinical challenge5. Presurgical evaluation has emphasized particularly hippocampal sclerosis (HS) identification and volumetric measurements1,yet hippocampal structural abnormalities are prevalent across surgical patients regardless of outcome. When hippocampal pathology is present in patients with divergent surgical outcomes, conventional hippocampal-focused assessment provides limited prognostic discrimination2. Identifying reliable outcome predictors requires systematic evaluation of structural integrity across distributed brain regions.

Normative modeling enables such systematic assessment by quantifying individualized structural deviations as standardized z-scores from age-, sex-, and brain size-adjusted population norms. Unlike conventional approaches relying on population-derived thresholds, this method provides patient-specific evaluation of structural abnormalities across distributed brain regions4. While normative modeling has been applied to hippocampal structures in temporal lobe epilepsy, systematic whole-brain evaluation remains unexplored. Whether extrahippocampal structural patterns provide prognostic information independent of hippocampal measures has not been systematically investigated. We established comprehensive normative models spanning 110 cortical and subcortical regions and generated individualized deviation maps for a surgical cohort to identify which brain regions predict postoperative seizure freedom. We hypothesized that extrahippocampal structural integrity would provide prognostic value independent of hippocampal pathology, enabling more accurate outcome prediction than hippocampal-focused assessment alone.

Methods

We established normative reference standards using structural MRI data from 1,690 neurologically healthy individuals. T1-weighted images were processed using the Computational Anatomy Toolbox (CAT12, r2170) in SPM12, employing standardized preprocessing, tissue segmentation, and spatial normalization to MNI152 space with modulation to preserve absolute gray matter volumes. Regional gray matter volumes were included 110 cortical and subcortical regions of interest (ROIs) defined by the Harvard-Oxford atlas, which provide comprehensive whole-brain coverage, enabling systematic assessment of both traditional mesial temporal structures and distributed extrahippocampal regions known to be implicated in TLE networks. Normative models were constructed using the Hierarchical Bayesian Regression algorithm as implemented in the Predictive Clinical Neuroscience toolkit (PCNtoolkit, v0.29). For each ROI, regional gray matter volume was modeled as a function of age, sex, and ICV. The Hierarchical Bayesian approach accounts for site-specific variance while estimating population-level parameters, generating robust age-, sex-, and ICV-adjusted normative references. This sample size provided sufficient statistical power to construct robust normative models predicting expected regional brain volumes as a function of demographic and anatomical covariates. 

The normative models were then applied to our surgical cohort of 88 TLE patients (32 males, mean age: 34.32 ± 11.26) who underwent ATL or SAH. For each patient, whole-brain deviation maps were generated by comparing observed regional volumes to normative predictions, yielding subject-specific standardized deviation scores (z-scores) for each ROI. The independent sample T test was applied to see if the abnormality degree can be distinguished between seizure-free and seizure persistent groups for all ROIs. Regional volumes exhibiting z-scores < -1.96, corresponding to the lower 2.5th percentile of the normative distribution, were classified as statistical outliers, representing extreme negative deviations from normative expectations. For each ROI, we quantified the outlier proportion as the percentage of patients demonstrating z-scores below this threshold, thereby characterizing the prevalence and spatial distribution of severe structural abnormalities across the surgical cohort3. 

To identify brain regions whose structural integrity was associated with surgical prognosis, we performed Point-Biserial correlation analyses between preoperative regional z-scores and postoperative seizure outcome. Surgical outcome was assessed using the Engel classification system and dichotomized as favorable outcome (Engel Class I: complete seizure freedom) versus unfavorable outcome (Engel Class II-IV: persistent seizures). Correlation analyses were conducted for all ROIs, and significance was assessed at α = 0.05 for exploratory whole-brain screening. Brain regions showing significant correlations with outcome were identified as potential prognostic markers of surgical success.

Results

At the exploratory screening level, preoperative regional volumes revealed no significant volumetric differences in mesial temporal structures between surgical outcome groups. Both seizure-free and persistent seizure patients exhibited comparable degrees of atrophy in ipsilateral hippocampus (seizure-free: -3.118 ± 1.082; persistent seizures: -2.172 ± 0.918; p = 0.157), ipsilateral amygdala (seizure-free: -1.179 ± 1.104; persistent seizures: -0.916 ± 1.142; p = 0.600), contralateral hippocampus (seizure-free: 0.335 ± 1.629; persistent seizures: -0.551 ± 1.707; p = 0.068), and contralateral amygdala (seizure-free: -0.105 ± 1.115; persistent seizures: 0.318 ± 1.344; p = 0.272) (Figure 1A). This absence of group differences in traditional epilepsy biomarkers suggests that mesial temporal pathology alone does not distinguish surgical outcome.

Analysis of extreme deviations (z-scores < -1.96) across all 110 brain regions revealed divergent spatial patterns between outcome groups. Both groups showed high rates of marked ipsilateral hippocampal atrophy (seizure-free: 72%; persistent seizures: 57%), with lower rates of contralateral hippocampal involvement (seizure-free: 6%; persistent seizures: 14%). However, the groups differed substantially in extrahippocampal regions. Patients with persistent seizures demonstrated elevated abnormality rates across 70 extrahippocampal regions, predominantly involving insular, frontal, temporal, and occipital cortices (Figure 1B). In contrast, seizure-free patients showed relatively preserved extrahippocampal volumes despite comparable hippocampal pathology. This divergent spatial pattern indicates that widespread structural involvement beyond mesial temporal structures distinguishes poor from favorable surgical outcomes.

Correlation analyses between preoperative regional volumes and postoperative seizure freedom confirmed the dissociation between mesial temporal and extrahippocampal predictors. Hippocampal volumes in either hemisphere showed no significant relationship with surgical outcome (ipsilateral: r =0.043, p = 0.691; contralateral: r = -0.199, p = 0.066), consistent with the absence of group differences in these structures. In contrast, multiple extrahippocampal regions demonstrated significant associations with seizure freedom (Figure 1C). The insula showed bilateral correlations (ipsilateral: r = -0.281, p = 0.009; contralateral: r = -0.247, p = 0.022). The lateral occipital cortex exhibited the strongest correlations, with nearly symmetric bilateral associations (ipsilateral: r = -0.292, p = 0.006; contralateral: r = -0.300, p = 0.005). Frontal regions showed lateralized patterns, with ipsilateral orbitofrontal cortex (r = -0.234, p = 0.030) and contralateral precentral gyrus (r = -0.213, p = 0.039) both predicting outcome. Additionally, contralateral inferior temporal cortex demonstrated a correlation (r = 0.223, p = 0.039). The strongest single predictor of surgical success was contralateral lateral occipital cortex volume (r = -0.300), a region not included in standard presurgical evaluations.

Conclusion

This exploratory study demonstrates that hippocampal volume fails to predict surgical outcome in temporal lobe epilepsy despite being the primary focus of presurgical evaluation. Mesial temporal volumes showed neither group differences between outcome groups nor correlation with seizure freedom, indicating that conventional hippocampal-focused assessment provides limited prognostic information. In contrast, extrahippocampal regions not routinely assessed in surgical evaluation, including insula, lateral occipital cortex, orbitofrontal cortex, and precentral gyrus, demonstrated significant associations with surgical outcome. Patients achieving seizure freedom showed preserved extrahippocampal volumes despite comparable hippocampal pathology, whereas those with persistent seizures exhibited widespread cortical atrophy beyond resection boundaries. This pattern suggests that beyond a critical threshold of mesial temporal involvement, surgical success depends less on mesial temporal pathology severity than on structural preservation of distributed cortical systems. These findings are consistent with epilepsy as a network-based disorder where widespread structural involvement limits surgical remediability. Whole-brain normative modeling offers a practical approach to presurgical evaluation by generating individualized structural deviation maps. Such maps can identify patients with preserved extrahippocampal integrity who may be optimal surgical candidates and those with widespread abnormalities who may benefit from alternative treatment approaches. Systematic assessment of extrahippocampal structural integrity, particularly in insular, occipital, and frontal regions, may improve outcome prediction beyond conventional hippocampal-focused evaluation. These exploratory findings require independent validation before clinical implementation.
