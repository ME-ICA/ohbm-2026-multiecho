Introduction

Dementia with Lewy bodies (DLB), a progressive neurological condition, is characterized by a multidomain cognitive decline along with noncognitive symptoms, such as parkinsonism, visual hallucinations, isolated REM sleep behavioral disorder, and fluctuating alertness and cognition. It belongs, together with Parkinson's disease (PD), to the category of Lewy body diseases (LBD) that are distinguished by early synaptic disruption that affects structural and functional connectivity.

This multimodal study uses mapping whole-brain aperiodic bursts of activities, measured by EEG, a thorough neuropsychological evaluation, and two types of structural acquisitions—neuromelanin sequence for locus coeruleus (LC) and free-water diffusion imaging for substantia nigra pars compacta (SNpc)—to capture brain and body changes in participants with progressed PD and in participants showing signs of very early LBD.

Methods

On the sample of 30 healthy controls (HC), 56 cognitively normal subjects with Lewy body core symptoms (CN-CCF), 34 subjects with mild cognitive impairment and Lewy body core symptoms (MCI-LB)^1, and 30 subjects with diagnosed PD, bursts of heightened (supra-threshold) neuronal activity^2 were captured by EEG and analyzed. Flexibility, defined as the number of unique supra-threshold spatial patterns, was statistically compared across groups, using PERMANOVA, based on Mahalanobis distance and 20,000 iterations, with age, sex, and Levodopa equivalent daily dose regressed out. Next, flexibility was correlated (Spearman's partial correlation) with Lewy body core features and signal intensities from caudal LC and SNpc.

Results

The groups significantly differed in flexibility (F = 5.30, p = .002; Fig 1). The flexibility was found higher in the prodromal LBD groups than in HC and PD groups. Concentrating further on the prodromal groups and their differences from HC, the flexibility was significantly related to fluctuating attention (quantified by Mayo Fluctuation Scale (MFS); rho = .29, p = .002) and sleep disturbances (quantified by REM Sleep Behavior Disorder questionnaire (RBDq); rho = .24, p = .010) across all subjects and also specifically within the MCI-LB group (rho = .39, p = .029 for MFS, rho = .38, p = .032 for RBDq).

Right caudal LC intensity was found significantly different across groups (F = 4.13, p = .019), being lower in MCI-LB, and negatively correlated with flexibility across all subjects (rho = −.21, p = .036). Groups did not differ in SNpc.

Conclusion

We conclude that subjects with prodromal LBD have a broader functional repertoire (= flexibility). This positively relates to fluctuating attention and sleep disturbances and negatively to the right caudal locus coeruleus intensity. Flexibility was not related to motor symptoms' severity. We pose that flexibility is a marker linked to the non-motor LBD core features, and could thus be used to differentiate between subtypes of LBD^3.
