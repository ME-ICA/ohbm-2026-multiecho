Introduction

Cortical folds, known as sulci, provide a stable signature of human brain morphology and neurodevelopment (1). The Champollion encoder, a self-supervised deep learning framework (2, 3), compresses sulcal regions extracted with Morphologist from BrainVISA (4) into extremely compact latent vectors. While previous work has shown that Champollion embeddings capture interruptions, central sulcus shapes, and some combination of patterns in the orbitofrontal region (3), the specific morphological details preserved in these embeddings remain unclear for many other brain regions. Here, we introduce Noillopmahc, a decoder designed to reconstruct the sulci from those representations, allowing us to assess how much structural information Champollion preserves across regions.

Methods

We use T1-weighted MRI from 42,430 UK Biobank participants (47% male, ages 44–82), as described in (3). Latent vectors are obtained for each sulcal region from a pretrained and frozen self-supervised Champollion model (3) (Fig. 1a). A sulcal region is defined as a part of a sulcal skeleton and is obtained using Deepfolding (5). There are 28 regions per hemisphere, which together form a negative cast of the cortex. Noillopmahc is an architecture for reconstructing the original binary sulcal volumes from the latent vectors, with 1 assigned to voxels labeled as sulci and 0 assigned to background voxels. Noillopmahc has the decoder architecture of the β-VAE (6) previously used for sulcal reconstruction (Fig. 1b). With a binary cross-entropy loss, one decoder per region is trained on the same 38,190 subjects used for Champollion’s training (with 2,120 for validation and 2,120 for testing). The hyperparameters used are a learning rate of 5 × 10-4, a batch size of 32, a dropout of 0.05, and training is stopped after 10 epochs because convergence was reached. No hyperparameter optimization was carried out.

Results

Reconstruction performance is consistent across training, validation, and test subjects, and generalizes to the Human Connectome Project (n=1,200), with an average BCE loss between 0.038 ± 0.005 and 0.096 ± 0.009, depending on the regions. An example of a reconstructed HCP subject is given in Fig. 2. 

However, we identify several limitations: (i) small or sparse sulcal regions are reconstructed more accurately than larger ones ; (ii) out-of-distribution subjects yield reconstructions biased toward average shapes ; (iii) the relative position of sulci within the mask of the region under study can sometimes hinder reconstruction.

Conclusion

Some limitations arise from decoder capacity or from the embeddings used. Nevertheless, the shapes of the main sulci remain recoverable from 256-dimension embeddings, except in the occipital, inferior temporal, and frontal regions. As can be seen in the Fig. 2, Noillopmahc shows that Champollion captures a rich cortical representation despite its compactness.
