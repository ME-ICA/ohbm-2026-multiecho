Introduction

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) is a debilitating condition triggered by trauma, characterized by intrusive memories, avoidance, and hyperarousal. Predicting PTSD risk early remains challenging due to a lack of objective neurobiological markers. Resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) reveals large-scale network disruptions but often overlooks fine-grained spatial features critical for early diagnosis. Complementary research has also examined temporal dynamics of brain networks, such as dynamic functional network connectivity (dFNC), showing that altered brain state occupancy soon after trauma can predict subsequent PTSD symptom severity.^1 However, even these dynamic approaches often rely on spatially coarse network representations. Recent advances in spatial-frequency–aware modeling suggest that principled modifications to early convolutional layers can yield substantial improvements in feature extraction. For instance, NeuroMark-HiFi incorporates 3D high-pass filtering in conjunction with frequency-informed independent component analysis (ICA), demonstrating that enhanced representation of high–spatial-frequency components improves the detection of fine-scale yet clinically meaningful variations in intrinsic connectivity networks (ICNs).^2 High-spatial-frequency components reflect fine-grained connectivity patterns but are often neglected in standard analyses. Meanwhile, deep learning approaches, particularly 3D CNNs, can capture hierarchical spatial features from neuroimaging data. Innovations such as high-pass kernel initialization and dilated convolutions have enhanced sensitivity to fine-scale patterns in other disorders, suggesting high-frequency spatial variations contain clinically meaningful information.^3 Building on these insights, this study introduces a 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) optimized to detect high-frequency spatial features in ICNs derived via NeuroMark ICA, aiming to improve early PTSD prediction.

Methods

We used early post-traumatic resting-state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) data acquired from participants enrolled in the multisite AURORA study, a large longitudinal cohort designed to investigate trauma recovery. This study included over 4000 participants, with clinical data at various intervals: 2 weeks(WK2), 4 weeks, 3, 6, and 12 months post-trauma. PTSD symptom severity was measured at each timepoint using the PTSD Checklist for DSM‑5 (PCL‑5), with a binary PTSD classification derived using a clinical cutoff of PCL‑5 ≥ 31. In the present analysis, we focused on neuroimaging and symptom data collected at WK2. rs-fMRI data were obtained from approximately 600 participants scanned at approximately two weeks (WK2) post-trauma across five imaging sites in the United States.  For model training and evaluation, we used the WK2 neuroimaging scans together with the corresponding WK2 symptom-based binary labels (WK2_PCL5) as the prediction target. Preprocessing of rs-fMRI data included slice-timing correction, realignment, spatial normalization, and spatial smoothing using a standard pipeline. For network extraction, we applied the NeuroMark framework, a fully automated, spatially constrained independent component analysis (ICA) pipeline. Using the NeuroMark_fMRI_1.0 template, we derived 53 intrinsic connectivity networks (ICNs) per participant, organized into seven functional domains: Subcortical, Auditory, Sensorimotor, Visual, Cognitive Control, Default Mode, and Cerebellar.

A custom 3D Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) was developed to analyze the extracted ICNs. The model consists of three hierarchical convolutional blocks. The first block employed dilated convolutions with kernels initialized using a discrete 3D Laplacian filter to enhance sensitivity to high-frequency spatial features and edge information. The second and third blocks utilized standard 3D convolutions to progressively abstract higher-level spatial representations. Following feature extraction, activation maps were flattened and processed through a series of fully connected layers with nonlinear activations to perform classification. The model was trained to predict PTSD severity using the 53 ICNs as input, optimizing for binary classification between PTSD and non-PTSD cases based on clinical assessments

Results

Quantitative evaluation of the proposed model demonstrated a notable improvement in classification performance when employing Laplacian kernel initialization combined with a dilation factor of 3. Under this configuration, the model achieved an overall accuracy of 65% in distinguishing between PTSD and non-PTSD cases. In contrast, a baseline model using random kernel initialization and standard convolutions (dilation factor of 1) attained only 55% accuracy. Analysis of the corresponding confusion matrices revealed that the Laplacian-enhanced model significantly improved sensitivity for PTSD detection, correctly identifying more true positive cases, while also reducing false negatives, thereby enhancing the reliability of positive predictions. Qualitative analysis of the feature maps revealed sharper, more localized activations in PTSD subjects under Laplacian weighting, particularly in early convolutional layers. These patterns highlighted discriminative neural signatures, such as enhanced edge and structure details, which were less apparent under random initialization. Together, these quantitative and qualitative findings underscore that principled, frequency-aware kernel design coupled with dilated convolutions can markedly improve the extraction of clinically informative spatial features from resting-state fMRI data.

Conclusion

Our study establishes that a 3D convolutional neural network, when initialized with a 3D Laplacian kernel and enhanced through dilated convolutions, can significantly improve the early prediction of PTSD from resting-state fMRI data. By applying this architecture to NeuroMark-derived intrinsic connectivity networks, the model is specifically tuned to capture high-frequency spatial components that reflect fine-grained, clinically relevant neural alterations. This methodological innovation led to an overall classification accuracy of 65%, outperforming conventional approaches, which achieved only 55% accuracy. Moreover, the model produced feature maps that were sharper, more localized, and more interpretable in PTSD-positive participants, particularly in early convolutional layers, underscoring its ability to isolate discriminative neurobiological signatures. These findings highlight the value of principled kernel design and dilation in neuroimaging-based deep learning, positioning the framework as a viable tool for early mental health risk stratification with translational potential in clinical diagnostics and longitudinal research.
