Introduction

Reading disability (RD) is a common neurodevelopmental disorder with a prevalence of 5-17% across language systems, which is characterized by difficulties in accurate and fluent reading, despite appropriate cognitive ability and instruction. Previous neuroimaging studies have reported altered activation and functional connectivity in language and reading neural networks in individuals with RD compared to controls. The pairwise maximum entropy model (pMEM, also called the Ising model) integrates both activation and co-activation between brain regions to capture a comprehensive picture of neural dynamics. In this study, we employed the pMEM to fMRI data obtained while participants were performing a reading fluency task to analyze the dynamic changes of individuals’ brain during reading.

Methods

In this study, 24 typical adults (TD) and 17 adults with RD were recruited from local associate degree colleges. Their reading skills were measured using three standardized tests: a Character Naming Test, a 3-minute Sentence Reading Fluency Test, and a 1-minute Character Naming Test. The fMRI task in this study was a sentence reading task where participants were asked to read sentences with a length of 5, 9, or 13 characters and judge whether each sentence makes sense. 

We extract the time series from 10 networks adapted from Gordon2017-17 atlas (visual, salience, language, fronto-parietal, DMN, premotor, medial temporal, dorsal attention, contextual association and somatomotor). Averaged activation in each network was binarized using baseline activation as a threshold. The binarized network states was then fitted to pMEM to construct an energy landscape. A disconnectivity graph was used for searching the local minimums and the basin size of local minimum. We then numerically simulated dynamical trajectories of brain activity on the energy landscape for each participant by calculating the frequency and transitions among the local minimums.

Results

We found three local minimums for the TD group and two local minimums for the RD group. ﻿Based on spatial distributions of active regions in each activity pattern, the three major local minimums for TD group were labelled language-area local min (state 1), co-activate local min (state 2) and intermediate local min (state 3), respectively. The two major local minimums for RD group were labelled premotor-area local min (state 1) and co-activate local min (state 2). In contrast to the TD group, the RD group showed a lower mean energy in State 2 and a higher mean energy in State 1. The basin size for state 2 was larger than the other states in both groups. The energy barrier between state 1 and state 2 of the RD group was smaller than that of the TD group. For frequency and transitions among the local minimums, the RD group exhibited a longer mean duration and a higher direct transition frequency between States 1 and 2 than the TD group.

Conclusion

The present study investigated the neural dynamics underlying reading disability (RD) by applying energy landscape analysis to task-based fMRI data. We identified two stable brain states in the RD group compared to three in the TD group. Our results suggest that individuals with RD fail to establish a distinct language-dominated state, characterized instead by a prolonged duration in a co-activated state.
