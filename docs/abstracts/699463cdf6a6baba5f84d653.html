Introduction

Chronic musculoskeletal (MSK) pain is a global health issue for adolescents, affecting between 5% and 46% of youth (Miró et al., 2023). This condition interferes with normal growth and daily functioning, leading to less physical activity and social withdrawal that significantly lowers quality of life (QoL) (Kashikar-Zuck et al., 2013). While multidisciplinary treatments are the standard of care, approximately 40% to 60% of adolescents do not fully recover (Simons et al., 2022).

Brain networks exhibit systematic relationships between structural (SC) and functional connectivity (FC). Structure-function coupling (SFC) quantifies this relationship by measuring the similarity between the strength of structural connections and the magnitude of functional correlations at the local level (Esfahlani et al., 2022). Regions with high SFC show FC patterns that closely match their underlying structural wiring, while low SFC indicates functional communication that extends beyond direct anatomical pathways (Foriadis et al., 2024). This local alignment varies across cortical hierarchies: unimodal sensorimotor regions typically exhibit higher SFC, whereas transmodal association cortices show lower coupling.

In chronic pain, alterations to SFC may reflect maladaptive functional reorganization. While anatomical connections form the physical substrate for neural communication, FC can deviate from these structural pathways through activity-dependent plasticity processes implicated in central sensitization. Changes in SFC may therefore index how chronic pain modulates the neural activity within fixed anatomical constraints analogous to how traffic patterns can shift across a city's road network despite unchanged infrastructure. 

We hypothesized that regional variations in SFC would associate with baseline QoL and predict 12-month outcomes. We expected that alterations in regions supporting pain processing, sensory integration, and affective regulation would relate to worse QoL.

Methods

Data was drawn from the SPRINT cohort (Simons et al., 2022), a multisite prospective study enrolling adolescents with chronic MSK pain. Participants were recruited from Stanford, Cincinnati, and Toronto. Neuroimaging data included 225 participants with complete baseline scans with resting-state, multisensory task, and DWI. QoL was assessed using the Pediatric QoL Inventory (PedsQL), yielding physical and psychosocial functioning scores. All participants received a 3-week intensive interdisciplinary pain treatment program incorporating physical therapy, occupational therapy, psychological interventions, and pharmacological management as clinically indicated. The 12-month follow-up captured longer-term outcomes following this treatment. 

MRI was acquired on 3T scanners at three sites: a GE Premier system at Stanford, a Philips Ingenia Elition at Cincinnati, and a Siemens Prisma at Toronto. Acquisition parameters were harmonized across sites. Structural MRI consisted of T1-weighted 3D MPRAGE or FSPGR sequences with 1 mm³ isotropic voxels and 160 to 200 slices. DWI used 60 to 64 directions with b = 1000 s/mm², 2 mm³ isotropic resolution, TR 3600 to 4154 ms, TE 71 to 80 ms, and multiband factor 2. fMRI was acquired using simultaneous multislice EPI. rsfMRI scans included 257 volumes (TR 1500 ms, TE 30–35 ms, 2.5 mm³ isotropic resolution, multiband factor 3) and lasted 6.4 minutes (eyes open, fixation cross). The multisensory task comprised 180 volumes with identical parameters and a duration of 4.5 minutes, consisting of four 30-second blocks of simultaneous visual (flickering checkerboard), auditory (tones), and tactile-motor (finger opposition) stimulation, alternating with approximately 22.5-second rest periods.

DWI data was preprocessed using mrtrix3. Preprocessing steps included denoising, deGibbs and topup corrections. Whole-brain fiber orientation distributions were estimated using constrained spherical deconvolution. Resting state and task fMRI data were preprocessed using the CONN toolbox. Denoising included regression of motion parameters, outlier regressors, WM signal, CSF signal and band-pass filtering. For the task data, an additional task regressor was included to remove task-evoked activation.

Whole brain tractography was performed using the iFOD2 algorithm, generating 5 million streamlines. Anatomically constrained streamline filtering (SIFT) produced a tractogram of 1 million biologically plausible streamlines. SC matrices were weighted using streamline count normalized by ROI volumes. For the FC matrices, local time series were extracted from each Brainnetome region, and weights were computed as Fisher z-transformed Pearson correlations.

We implemented 35 communication models to derive model-based predictions of structural communication between all ROI pairs. These models included distance-based metrics, search information, path transitivity, cosine similarity, weighted communicability and flow-based models using flow graphs.

To calculate SFC, FC was predicted from the full set of 35 communication model estimates using ridge regression with nested cross-validation. For each ROI, the 35 predicted connectivity values for all its edges formed the design matrix Xi, and the FC vectors served as the target yi. Then, a 5-fold cross-validation procedure was run across 100 alpha values log-spaced from 0.001 to 1000. The alpha that maximized cross-validated R² was selected, and final SFC coupling was defined using the out-of-sample prediction accuracy R² (Fig. 1). ComBat harmonization was applied to the SFC measures to control for site differences.

ROI-wise univariate regression models were used to test associations between SFC and QoL. For the 12-month prospective analyses, we controlled for baseline clinical measures. Family-wise error correction was used to control for multiple comparisons. Significance was defined as FWE-corrected p < 0.05. We also examined findings at an uncorrected p < 0.01 deemed exploratory.

Results

Descriptive:

The final sample included 225 adolescents with chronic MSK pain (mean age 15.6 ± 1.6 years, range 10-18; 84.8% female). Mean pain duration was 46.4 ± 39.6 months (3.9 ± 3.3 years). Baseline clinical measures were: physical QoL 35.0 ± 22.0 and psychosocial QoL 173.2 ± 55.0. At 12-month follow-up: physical QoL increased to 50.3 ± 26.0 and psychosocial QoL 189.2 ± 60.

Physical QoL:

SFC showed significant associations with physical QoL at baseline (Fig. 2a). Three regions reached family-wise error correction (FWE-corrected q<0.05) during the multisensory task: left caudal angular gyrus (A39c_L, q=0.024), left pregenual anterior cingulate cortex (A32p_L, q=0.014), and right ventral caudate (vCa_R, q=0.036). In these regions, lower SFC was associated with worse physical QoL. At the exploratory threshold (uncorrected p<0.01), 24 additional regions showed associations with physical functioning across resting-state and multisensory-task conditions. During the multisensory task, exploratory findings (p<0.01) included bilateral anterior cingulate cortex (subgenual A32sg), bilateral caudate (dorsal dCa, ventral vCa_L), parietal regions (supramarginal gyrus A40c/A40rd/A40rv), prefrontal cortex (A12/47l_R, A46_R, A6dl_R), temporal regions (superior temporal sulcus aSTS/cpSTS, auditory cortex A41/42, middle/inferior temporal gyri A21c/A37vl/A20il), visual areas (middle occipital gyrus mOccG, motion area V5/MT+), thalamus (rTtha_R), somatosensory cortex (A1/2/3tru, A1/2/3ulhf), and insula (vIa_R during rest). For prospective prediction of 12-month physical QoL when controlling for baseline measures, one region reached FWE correction during multisensory task: left caudal inferior frontal gyrus (A45c_L, q=0.014), where higher baseline SFC predicted worse 12-month physical QoL. At the exploratory threshold (uncorrected p<0.01), three additional regions showed prospective associations where lower baseline SFC coupling predicted worse 12-month physical QoL: left postcentral superior parietal lobule (A7pc_L) during rest and left medial prefrontal cortex (A8m_L) and right medial frontopolar cortex (A10m_R) during multisensory task.

Psychosocial QoL:

No regions reached FWE correction for baseline psychosocial QoL. At the exploratory threshold (uncorrected p<0.01), one region showed association where higher baseline SFC predicted worse psychosocial QoL: right dorsolateral premotor cortex (A6dl_R) during multisensory task. For prospective prediction of 12-month psychosocial QoL when controlling for baseline measures, one region reached FWE correction during resting-state: right rostral hippocampus (rHipp_R, q=0.046), where lower baseline SFC coupling predicted worse 12-month psychosocial functioning. At the exploratory threshold (uncorrected p<0.01), two additional regions showed prospective associations: left lateral temporal pole (A38l_L) during resting-state where higher baseline SFC predicted worse 12-month psychosocial QoL, and left opercular inferior frontal gyrus (A44op_L) during multisensory task where lower baseline SFC predicted worse 12-month psychosocial QoL (Fig. 2b).

Conclusion

SFC coupling showed significant cross-sectional associations with physical QoL in adolescents with chronic MSK pain. Three regions reached family-wise error correction at baseline during multisensory task: left caudal angular gyrus, left pregenual anterior cingulate cortex, and right ventral caudate. In these regions, lower SFC was associated with worse physical QoL.

To interpret these findings: SFC quantifies the similarity between structural connectivity (the fixed anatomical scaffold) and functional connectivity (dynamic neural communication). When SFC is lower, functional networks deviate more from their structural substrate. Since structural connections remain relatively stable, reductions in SFC reflect functional reorganization, in which the brain's functional activity increasingly diverges from its anatomical wiring. Our findings suggest that this functional reorganization is maladaptive in these regions: adolescents with worse physical QoL show greater functional reconfiguration beyond structural constraints in networks supporting sensory integration, emotion regulation, and sensorimotor control. This pattern suggests that pain-driven functional changes that extend beyond the anatomical scaffold may represent inefficient processing that fails to support physical functioning.

Prospective analyses revealed contrasting patterns. For 12-month physical QoL, left caudal inferior frontal gyrus showed that higher baseline SFC predicted worse outcomes. This opposite pattern suggests reduced functional flexibility. Hence, adolescents with worse QoL outcomes show less functional reorganization at baseline, indicating that their functional networks remain rigidly constrained by structural connections. This lack of functional adaptation may limit the brain's capacity to reorganize in beneficial ways.

For psychosocial QoL, right rostral hippocampus showed that lower baseline SFC predicted worse 12-month psychosocial QoL. This finding indicates that excessive functional divergence from structural constraints in hippocampus, a region supporting memory, emotion regulation, and stress response, may undermine psychosocial adaptation. Similarly, left lateral temporal pole showed that lower baseline SFC coupling predicted worse outcomes, suggesting that maladaptive reorganization in regions supporting social-emotional processing contributes to psychosocial difficulties.

These findings suggest region- and outcome-specific patterns in which both excessive functional reorganization and insufficient functional flexibility may contribute to worse QoL in adolescents with chronic MSK pain.
