Introduction

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is a rare inborn error of metabolism caused by a deficiency of the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase, which converts the amino acid phenylalanine (Phe) into tyrosine (Tyr). This enzymatic defect leads to elevated blood and brain Phe levels, resulting in disrupted neurotransmitter synthesis, brain structural alterations, and severe cognitive impairments if not adequately treated during childhood through low-Phe dietary management and amino acid supplementation. 

In early-treated adults with PKU, cognitive performance remains a matter of debate. Meta-analytic findings indicate that attention and executive functions tend to be the most consistently affected domains, while other cognitive functions often remain comparable to healthy controls1,2. These cognitive alterations may point toward a dysregulation of specific large-scale brain networks.

While structural brain alterations in PKU are well documented, the functional connectivity has not been characterized in comparable detail. A four-week oral Phe load in adults with early-treated PKU, from the same cohort as the present study, was reported to cause temporary alterations in cerebral white matter microstructure, reductions in cortical gray matter, and increases in white matter volume, all of which returned to baseline levels3,4 

Given that attention and executive domains have been reported to be affected in PKU, we implemented resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) in this study to focus on brain networks associated with these two cognitive domains. The present study aimed to investigate whether functional connectivity differs between adults with classical PKU and healthy controls, and whether functional connectivity metrics are associated with metabolic parameters and cognitive functioning.

Methods

In this cross-sectional observational study, we used key nodes of the dorsal attention (frontal eye fields and intraparietal sulcus) and frontoparietal (lateral prefrontal cortex and posterior parietal cortex) networks as seeds to investigate resting-state functional connectivity. For metabolic assessments, venous blood samples were drawn immediately before MRI scans, and neuropsychological assessments were performed after MRI using validated instruments. Resting-state fMRI was acquired on a 3T Siemens Prisma scanner with a 64-channel head coil using a multiband gradient-echo EPI sequence (TR = 1300 ms, TE = 37 ms, flip angle = 52°). Images were collected with 2.2-mm isotropic voxels, 60 interleaved slices, multiband factor 4, A≫P phase encoding, and FOV = 230 mm. A total of 300 volumes were acquired over 6:39 min with participants resting with eyes closed.

All fMRI analyses were performed using CONN 22.a5. Preprocessing included realignment, slice-timing correction, normalization to Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space, and spatial smoothing (6 mm full-width-at-half-maximum). Functional data were denoised using CompCor-derived white matter and cerebrospinal fluid signals, motion parameters, outlier scans, and linear trends, followed by temporal band-pass filtering (0.008–0.09 Hz). Each voxel-level general linear model evaluated differences in functional connectivity between individuals with PKU and healthy controls separately for each seed, including age and sex as covariates. Statistical inferences were based on Gaussian Random Field theory, with significance determined at the cluster-level, applying an initial voxel-level cluster-forming threshold of p < 0.001 (uncorrected), followed by a Family-Wise Error (FWE) corrected threshold of p < 0.05 at the cluster-level. 

A principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the functional connectivity values extracted from all significant clusters identified in the group comparison to characterize patient-specific alterations further. The first three components were retained for subsequent analyses based on eigenvalues > 1 and visual inspection of the scree plot. These components were entered as predictors in robust linear regression models to examine their association with concurrent blood Phe levels and cognitive outcomes. 

Results

We compared 28 individuals with PKU (median age = 35.47 years, interquartile range [IQR] = 11.70 years; 12 females) to 58 healthy controls (median age = 30.28 years, IQR = 10.51 years; 26 females). Patients showed significantly lower performance than healthy controls in working memory (n-back accuracy: 94.5% [IQR = 7%] vs 98% [IQR = 3%], W = 1162, p = 0.001), sustained attention (response time standard deviation: 169 ms [IQR = 63.5 ms] vs 139 ms [IQR = 48 ms], W = 471.5, p = 0.004), and cognitive flexibility (completion time: 59 s [IQR = 11.25 s] vs 51 s [IQR = 12 s], W = 459, p = 0.002).

The seed-based connectivity analysis using the left intraparietal sulcus seed of the dorsal attention network revealed significantly higher connectivity in patients than healthy controls, specifically in the left middle and superior temporal gyri (t = 5.28, pFWE = 0.010, voxels = 154, 95% CI [0.11, 0.27], peak coordinate MNI = -56, -14, -6) and in the right superior and middle temporal poles (t = 6.35, pFWE = 0.001, voxels = 218, 95% CI [0.12, 0.25], peak-level MNI = 52, 14, -20). The right intraparietal sulcus seed showed significant higher functional connectivity in patients compared to healthy controls in the left middle and superior temporal gyri (t = 5.29, pFWE = 0.006, voxels = 173, 95% CI [0.11, 0.25], peak-level MNI = -54, -14, -4), the right middle and superior temporal poles, and the right superior temporal gyrus (t = 4.99, pFWE = 0.031, voxels = 124, 95% CI [0.10, 0.24], peak-level MNI = 52, 14, -18). In addition, higher functional connectivity was presented in patients in the bilateral cuneus, left calcarine cortex, and right precuneus (t = 4.89, pFWE < 0.001, voxels = 348, 95% CI [0.10, 0.26], peak-level MNI = -2, -68, 20).

Using the left lateral prefrontal cortex seed of the frontoparietal network, patients showed significantly higher connectivity relative to healthy controls in the right superior frontal gyrus and supplementary motor area (t = 5.16, pFWE = 0.006, voxels = 168, 95% CI [0.12, 0.29], peak-level MNI = 18, 8, 62). Functional connectivity was higher in a second cluster including the right middle and superior frontal gyri and medial segment of the superior frontal gyrus (t = 5.56, pFWE < 0.001, voxels = 363, 95% CI [0.14, 0.30], peak-level MNI = 30, 56, 8). In contrast, the right lateral prefrontal cortex seed showed significantly lower functional connectivity in patients compared to healthy controls in the right putamen, pallidum, and insula (t = -6.51, pFWE < 0.001, voxels = 266, 95% CI [-0.19, -0.10], peak-level MNI = 32, -12, -06). 

The bilateral frontal eye fields and posterior parietal cortices seeds showed no significant group differences in functional connectivity. 

PCA of the functional connectivity values extracted from all significant clusters revealed three principal components with eigenvalues > 1, explaining 77.50% of the total variance (PC1 = 47.16%, PC2 = 16.38%, PC3 = 13.96%). PC1 strongly loaded on clusters within the bilateral dorsal attention network and bilateral temporal areas. PC2 exhibited a more heterogeneous pattern, with strong negative loadings in the frontoparietal network. PC3 most strongly loaded negatively on subcortical connectivity of the right putamen, pallidum, and insula.

Robust linear regression showed that PC1 was positively associated with Phe concentrations (β = 0.22, p = 0.006), whereas PC2 was negatively associated with Phe concentrations (β = −0.26, p = 0.048). The model explained 52.46% of the variance in Phe concentrations (adjusted R2 = 0.525). This indicates that patients with higher Phe had stronger connectivity within the clusters captured by PC1, but weaker connectivity within those represented by PC2. The association patterns remained consistent when tested with ordinary least squares regression. No significant association was found for PC3 (p > 0.05). None of the components were significantly associated with any of the cognitive function measures (all p > 0.05).

Conclusion

Our study revealed altered functional connectivity in patients with early-treated classical PKU across dorsal attention and frontoparietal networks. Within the PKU cohort, the magnitude of these functional connectivity alterations was significantly correlated with concurrent blood Phe levels but not with cognitive performance. This finding of altered connectivity could represent a compensatory mechanism in response to inefficiencies of attention or working memory processes. The higher coupling may indicate an over-recruitment of control resources during resting-state conditions, consistent with observations in conditions involving elevated cognitive load or neural inefficiency. The lower cortico-basal-ganglia connectivity might be due to metabolic imbalances. Elevated Phe competitively inhibits Tyr transport across the blood-brain barrier, thereby lowering brain Tyr, the precursor of dopamine, and potentially reducing dopaminergic signaling within this circuit. Future intervention studies that experimentally modulate Phe levels and combine multimodal imaging approaches with metabolic and cognitive assessments are necessary. They would allow to disentangle the dynamic relationships among metabolic imbalances, network characteristics and cognitive functions.
