Introduction

Adolescence is a sensitive developmental period marked by substantial reorganization of large-scale brain networks that support cognitive control, affect regulation, and adaptive behavior (Dahl et al., 2018). Normative age-related increases in functional connectivity reflect heightened neuroplasticity but also increased vulnerability to environmental exposures during this period. Substance use commonly emerges during adolescence, raising concern that alcohol and cannabis exposure may alter typical trajectories of brain maturation. Prior neuroimaging studies have linked adolescent substance use to differences in functional connectivity; however, most evidence is cross-sectional, limiting the ability to distinguish pre-existing differences from substance-related developmental disruption (Bourque et al., 2016). Longitudinal approaches that separate within-person fluctuations in substance use from stable between-person differences are critical for identifying substance-specific effects on neurodevelopment.

Methods

We analyzed longitudinal resting-state fMRI data from the Neuroventure cohort ( Conrod, 2016), comprising 150 adolescents assessed across multiple time points ( 410 observations). Functional connectivity strength was examined using the Yeo’s seven network parcellation. Linear mixed-effects models were used to characterize age-related changes in functional connectivity, controlling for quadratic age effects, head motion, sex, and network identity, with random intercepts for participants. Cannabis and alcohol use were decomposed into within-person (time-varying deviations from an individual’s mean use) and between-person (stable individual differences) components. Primary models tested include, age × substance interactions to determine whether substance use altered normative developmental trajectories, while additional models examined age × network and substance × network effects.

Results

Across models, functional connectivity strength increased with age, with a significant negative quadratic age term indicating nonlinear maturation consistent with accelerated change during early adolescence followed by stabilization. Significant age × network interactions confirmed heterogeneity in normative developmental trajectories across large-scale brain systems.

Critically, cannabis use significantly moderated age-related changes in functional connectivity. Within-person increases in cannabis use were associated with a robust attenuation of age-related connectivity strengthening (age × cannabis_within: β ≈ −0.0063, p ≈ 0.002), indicating that periods of elevated cannabis use within an individual corresponded to weaker developmental gains in connectivity. A similar negative interaction was observed for between-person cannabis use (age × cannabis_between: β ≈ −0.0057, p < 0.001), suggesting that adolescents with chronically higher cannabis exposure followed divergent developmental trajectories relative to lighter users. These effects remained significant after controlling for alcohol use and covariates, supporting the specificity and robustness of cannabis-related developmental disruption.

In contrast, alcohol exhibited a distinct pattern. Between-person alcohol use significantly interacted with age in the opposite direction (age × alcohol_between: β ≈ 0.0059, p < 0.001), whereas within-person fluctuations in alcohol use did not significantly modify age-related connectivity changes (p ≈ 0.73). Substance × network interaction models revealed limited evidence for strong network-specific cannabis effects, indicating that cannabis primarily altered global developmental trajectories rather than selectively targeting individual networks.

Conclusion

These findings demonstrate that cannabis and alcohol exert substance-specific influences on adolescent brain development. Cannabis use—particularly within-person increases—consistently attenuates normative age-related strengthening of functional connectivity, suggesting disruption of maturational processes during a sensitive developmental window. Alcohol, in contrast, shows a different and partially opposing developmental signature that appears driven by stable between-person differences rather than acute fluctuations. By leveraging longitudinal data and decomposing substance use into within- and between-person components, this study provides compelling evidence that adolescent cannabis use alters normative functional brain development.
