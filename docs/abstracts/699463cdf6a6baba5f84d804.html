Introduction

The apolipoprotein E ε4 (APOE4) allele is the strongest genetic risk factor for Alzheimer’s disease, associated with earlier onset and accelerated pathophysiological progression. Evidence indicates that ApoE4 carriers exhibit faster tau propagation at lower amyloid levels compared to non-ApoE4 carriers (Steward et al., 2023). However, the mechanisms by which amyloid burden accelerates tau aggregation and spread remain incompletely understood.

Methods

Using multimodal biomarker data pooled across two large cohorts, ADNI (n=368) and A4 (n = 218), we investigated whether amyloid–tau colocalisation dynamics vary according to APOE genotype, a potential mechanism for increased amyloid effects on tau. Specifically, we quantified the spatial coupling between baseline amyloid and annual rate of tau accumulation, comparing Schaefer 200 defined regional similarities and hemispheric lateralisation across ApoE e2/e3, e3/e3, e3/e4, and e4/e4 carriers. Furthermore, using fMRI derived template connectivity we examined the functional connectivity profiles of individualised tau epicentres and amyloid hotspots i.e. 5% of regions with the strongest base line amyloid or tau burden, to understand how these both contribute to tau spreading patterns across genotypes.

Results

ANCOVA revealed a significant ApoE4 allele-dose effect on amyloid-predicted tau accumulation across 200 Schaefer regions, with the strongest colocalisation in ApoE4 homozygotes (Figure 1A). Linear regression indicated an APOE genotype–dependent interaction in interhemispheric asymmetries: hemispheric differences in amyloid deposition predicted corresponding differences in tau accumulation (b=0.28, p<0.001; Figure 1B). Next, functional connectivity patterns of tau epicentres and amyloid hotspots showed progressively greater overlap with increasing ApoE4 allele (e2/e3: r=–0.1, SD=0.4; e3/e3: r=0.12, SD=0.3; e3/e4: r=0.21, SD=0.3, e4e4:  r=0.36, SD=0.38; Figure 2). Furthermore, tau-epicentre connectivity predicted subsequent tau accumulation across all genotypes, increasing stepwise with ApoE4 allele dosage. Interestingly, amyloid-hotspot connectivity also contributed to tau spreading increasing stepwise with ApoE4 allele dosage, but showing minimal effects in low-risk genotypes (e2e3 and e3e3).

Conclusion

Overall, our findings demonstrate an ApoE4 allele–dose increase in the colocalisation of tau accumulation with regions of higher amyloid burden, indicating greater amyloid involvement in shaping tau accumulation in ApoE4 carriers. Consistent with this, tau preferentially spread along connections associated with both tau epicentres and amyloid hotspots in ApoE4 carriers, whereas in non-carriers it followed only tau-epicentre connectivity patterns. This suggests a potential ApoE4-specific amplifying role of amyloid in driving tau spread via amyloid-altered activity dynamics.
