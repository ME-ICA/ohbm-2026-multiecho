Introduction

ADHD is a highly prevalent neurodevelopmental condition in children and adolescents, that can persist into adulthood. Why symptoms persist in some individuals and not in others remains unclear. Though neuroimaging studies suggest these differences stem from variations in brain development, which can be shaped by genetics and the environment.  

Epigenetic mechanisms, like DNA methylation, that regulate gene expression in response to varying genetics and environments have gained in popularity as a biomarker of ADHD. Numerous epigenome wide studies have identified differential methylation in individuals with ADHD, relative to controls, though it remains unclear as to how they associate with brain development. We examined whether the association between DNA methylation and change in brain development differs between children and adolescents with or without ADHD, or based on diagnostic outcomes (ADHD remittance and persistence). 

Methods

Children and adolescents with or without ADHD completed repeated T1-weighted MRI scans (3T Siemens scanner) across three waves of the Neuroimaging of the Children’s Attention Project (NICAP)1. Participants were 9-11 years of age at the first scan, and two additional visits were conducted at 18-month intervals. ADHD diagnoses were confirmed at the first and last wave of assessment, and used to subgroup diagnoses as remitted (no longer meeting ADHD criteria by the final wave) or persistent (meet criteria at the final wave) ADHD. Controls were participants who did not meet criteria for ADHD across any wave. 

Anatomical images were acquired using a multi-echo magnetization-prepared rapid gradient-echo (MEMPRAGE) sequence (TR = 2530 ms; TE = 1.77, 3.51, 5.32, and 7.2 ms; FOV = 230 x 230 mm; 176 slices; flip angle = 7; matrix size = 256 x 232, voxel size = 0.9 mm; acquisition time = 6 min 52 s). Preprocessing was performed using the longitudinal FreeSurfer (v6.0) pipeline, which included non-parametric non-uniform intensity normalization (N3), intensity normalization, and skull stripping (http://surfer.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/). A robust, unbiased within-subject template space from all available scans initialized segmentation at each time point. A total of 18 participants were excluded due to excessive artefacts or movements, following visual and automatic MRI quality controls (MRIQC v0.14.2)2. Additional region-by-region quality control and exclusions were conducted according to the ENIGMA pipeline. Missing regions were imputed using the missForest package on R3. We focused this investigation on brain regions previously associated with ADHD by the ENIGMA Consortium4, which include the left-right average surface area and cortical thickness of 24 and 4 brain regions, respectively. 

DNA methylation was quantified from saliva samples using the Illumina EPIC array, version 1.0 (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). This study focused on first wave methylation data of 12 sites previously identified as being differentially methylated between ADHD (remitted or persistent) and controls (8 CpGs), or by ADHD outcomes (remitted vs. persistent; 4 CpGs)5. Beta values of DNA methylation were converted to M-values (log2 [methylated/unmethylated]) for subsequent analyses.

Associations between baseline methylation and longitudinal change in cortical thickness and surface area were modelled for each CpG site and brain regions using linear mixed models including a three-way interaction between follow-up diagnosis (comparisons include remitted and persistent ADHD vs. controls; remitted vs. persistent ADHD), methylation and time (defined by age at timepoint subtracted from the samples grand-mean centered age), with each participant as the random intercept. Models were adjusted for sex, baseline cell-proportion and batch, and scanner. Multiple comparisons were corrected at a false discovery rate of 5%.

Results

A total of 79 participants had epigenetic and MRI data at the first wave (aged between 9.5-11.9 years; 34 had ADHD, and 73% were male), and had completed two (n = 58) or three (n = 21) MRI scans. All 8 candidate sites for ADHD (remitted or persistent) and control differences had a statistically significant three-way interaction with surface area or cortical thickness. Among these findings, methylation of cg17657037 was associated with the surface area of 8 brain regions, suggesting higher levels at baseline methylation in remitted ADHD was associated with a greater increase in surface area overtime, compared to controls (Figure 1). In contrast to controls, a higher methylation at 3 CpGs in persistent ADHD was linked to a statistically significant greater increased (cg19892195 and 26901352) or decreased (cg17657037) change in cortical thickness of the fusiform gyrus. However, none of these findings survived multiple correction. 

Of the four candidate sites delineating remitted from persistent ADHD, methylation at cg1760216 was associated with increased change in surface area of multiple brain regions (inferior parietal and temporal, lateral occipital, medial orbitofrontal, pars triangularis, rostral middle frontal, superior frontal and parietal). Conversely, none of these four sites were statistically significantly associated with change in cortical thickness (unadjusted p > 0.05), nor had any of the findings with surface area survived multiple correction. 

Conclusion

We investigated the potential role of peripheral DNA methylation in longitudinal brain development in ADHD. Whilst our findings did not strongly support an association with surface area or cortical thickness, they highlighted methylation patterns at several sites which may represent differential brain development in ADHD. Larger neuroimaging epigenetic studies are needed to clarify these findings, most warranted are studies into diagnostic subgroups, including remittance and persistence ADHD.
