Introduction

White matter hyper-intensities (WMH) are observed on Fluid Attenuated Inversion Recovery (FLAIR) Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), increase with age, and are prominent in neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD), Lewy body diseases (LBD), and frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD). The etiology of WMH are multifactorial, but the most common is cerebral small vessel disease due to various risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes and smoking. Nevertheless, recent studies also suggest that non-vascular mechanisms (e.g. neuropathological processes) are involved, especially in the above mentioned neurodegenerative diseases.^1 The aim of the current study is to assess the cross-disease differences in (regional) WMH volume, and their association with neuropathological stages and regional burden of amyloid-beta (Aβ), hyperphosphorylated (p-)tau neurofibrillary tangles (NFT), and α-synuclein (α-syn).

Methods

See figure 1 for the data collection pipeline. 126 clinically defined and pathologically confirmed brain donors were included in the study: 45 AD, 26 LBD, 13 mixed AD_LBD, 12 FTLD and 25 controls. Clinical information on risk factors and cognition (Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) scale scores) were retrospectively collected from patient records. 

Postmortem in-situ (brain in cranium) 3T-MRI (GE Signa-MR750) was performed with the following sequences: i) a sagittal 3D T1-weighted fast spoiled gradient echo sequence (repetition time (TR) = 7 ms, echo time (TE) = 3 ms, flip angle = 15°, 1-mm-thick axial slices, in-plane resolution= 1.0 x 1.0 mm2); ii) and a sagittal 3D fluid attenuation inversion recovery (FLAIR) sequence (TR = 8000 ms, TE = 130 ms, inversion time (TI) = 2000-2500 ms (adjusted for post-mortem delay), 1.2-mm-thick axial slices, in-plane resolution= 1.11 x 1.11 mm2). 

From FLAIR images the Fazekas score was obtained, from T1 images the medial temporal atrophy (MTA) score. Image processing and quantification of WMH volume was performed using the Apptainer-containerized MIAC Automated Region Segmentation (MARS) tool.^2 MARS was used for affine co-registration, brain mask generation, and automated WMH segmentation. To characterize lobar WMH distribution (frontal, temporal, parietal, occipital, insula and cingulate), the USC-Lobes atlas was employed.^3  

Aside from the brain tissue used for neuropathological diagnosis, for a subset of the donors (31 AD, 20 LBD, 10 mixed AD_LBD, 9 FTLD and 19 controls), regional brain tissue (from the frontal, temporal, parietal, and occipital cortex) was collected. These were subsequently immunostained and evaluated for quantitative regional area percent (%) Aβ, pTau and α-syn. 

WMH volume was normalized using the Box-Cox transformation. Group differences were assessed with linear models (corrected for age, gender, post-mortem interval and intracranial volume), and FDR corrected for multiple comparisons. Effect sizes are notated as Cohen’s d, in which <0.2 is considered small, 0.5 moderate, and >0.8 large. Associations between WMH and pathological stages were performed with Spearman partial correlations with age and ICV as covariates. 

Results

As shown in figure 2A, the cross-disease comparison shows that AD, mixed AD_LBD and FTLD groups have significantly higher lesion volume than controls (AD: d=1.36, p<0.001, mixed AD_LBD: d=0.84, p=0.03, FTLD: d=1.60, p<0.001). The difference between controls and PD did not reach significance although the effect size is moderate (d=0.55, p=0.10). In addition, AD and FTLD had higher lesion volume than LBD (AD: d=0.84, p=0.009; FTLD: d=1.04, p=0.014). Across all groups, the frontal cortex was most affected, followed by the parietal, temporal and occipital cortex, cingulate and insula, and cross-disease differences were present, especially in the frontal cortex (figure 2B). 

            Age at death was positively associated with WMH volume (rp=0.20, p=0.024). This remained significant only within the control (rp=0.59, p=0.003) and mixed AD_LBD (rp=0.64, p=0.044) groups. Only the AD, LBD and mixed AD_LBD group has sufficient information on smoking, alcohol consumption, diabetes mellitus, hypertension and hypercholesteremia, for these measures, only presence of hypertension had borderline significant higher WMH volume in the mixed AD_LBD group (d=1.49, p=0.065). Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) scale scores were positively associated with WMH volume across the clinical-pathological disease groups (rs=0.45, p<0.001). 

As expected, Fazekas score and WMH volume showed a strong positive correlation (rs=0.65, p<0.001; figure 2C). Medial temporal lobe atrophy (MTA) was moderately positively correlated with WMH volume (rs=0.56, p<0.001; figure 2D). 

Figure 2E-G shows that WMH volume associated positively with Thal Aβ phase (rs=0.19, p=0.031) and Braak NFT stage (rs=0.22, p=0.014). In contrast, Braak α-syn stage did not correlate with WMH volume (rs=0.02, p=0.83). In addition, cases with cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA; build-up of Aβ in the walls of arteries) had higher WMH volume (d=0.42, p=0.039). For the available regional data, the occipital cortex WMH volume associated with Aβ (r=0.31, p=0.016). 

Conclusion

Although the order of most to least affected regions (frontal to insula) is the same across all groups, neurodegenerative diseases are differentially affected by WMH volume. AD and FTLD have the highest total WMH volume, while LBD show a similar volume to aged controls. This is also reflected by the neuropathological stages; AD pathological hallmarks of Aβ and NFTs associating with a higher WMH volume, while the LBD pathological hallmark of α-syn did not. Regionally, we only see an effect for Aβ in the occipital cortex, a hotspot for CAA accumulation (a vascular risk factor). In summary, there seems to be a role for global AD related neuropathology in WMH burden, but this cannot be seen in isolation from regional cerebral small vessel disease.
