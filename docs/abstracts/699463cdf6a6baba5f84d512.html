Introduction

Over 10 % of infants are born prematurely during the third trimester of pregnancy, disrupting a time of critical wiring of the major brain connections¹ and the maturation of functional networks². Preterm infants are exposed to adversities in structural and functional development, which can ultimately lead to long-lasting neurodevelopmental compromise, markedly affecting the quality of life. However, little is known about how changes in structural connectivity (SC) and functional connectivity (FC) relate to each other during early neurodevelopment. Recent work has targeted this question by comparing diffusion-weighted magnetic resonance imaging (dMRI) with functional MRI (fMRI)³. However, fMRI is unable to disentangle the richness of the frequency content of brain activity that drives coupling at different time scales, particularly the multifrequency spontaneous activity bursts that are the hallmark of neonatal brain activity. Therefore, it is essential to obtain insights from electroencephalography (EEG), whose high temporal resolution allows for capturing connectivity at subsecond timescales. In this work we leveraged a unique dataset of dMRI and high-density EEG recordings in the same preterm infants to study the relation between SC and FC at term age. We hypothesized that maturational changes in long-range cortico-cortical pathways would be associated with frequency-specific FC measures following their known maturational trajectories.  

Methods

MRI (T2-weighted MRI and dMRI, Ingenia 3T scanner, Philips Healthcare) and high-density EEG (N=128 channels, EGI system, Magstim) recordings were obtained in 31 very preterm-born infants (mean±standard deviation: 27.2±1.7w of gestational age) at term-equivalent age (38-42w of post-menstrual age, PMA) in the Pediatric University Hospital Robert-Debré (APHP, Paris, France). The study protocol was approved by the Comité de Protection des Personnes Ile de France 3 (protocol DEVine, CEA 100 054). Study procedures conformed to the Declaration of Helsinki and local data protection regulations. The analysis pipeline is presented in Fig. 1. 

dMRI data (42 directions, b=1000s/mm2) were pre-processed for denoising and correction for motion, geometric distortions, and susceptibility-induced artefacts. Major white matter tracts were identified using a single-shell model of fiber orientation distributions with Bedpostx and probabilistic tractography using babyXTRACT⁴. In the present work, we selected five major inter- and intrahemispheric cortico-cortical tracts: forceps minor (FMI), forceps major (FMA), anterior commissure (AC), and inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus (IFO right and left). We computed mean diffusivity (MD) and fractional anisotropy (FA) maps and considered their averaged values over each tract. EEG was preprocessed by selecting 3-minute epochs of active sleep (AS), prefiltering from 0.4-40 Hz, downsampling from 1000 Hz to 100 Hz, removal of artefacts and bad channels, and bandpass filtering into 24 narrow frequency bands. The preprocessed signals were source reconstructed using a realistic infant head model² ⁵, and the source signals were collated into 58 cortical parcels. FC was calculated by amplitude-amplitude correlations (AAC) using the orthogonalized correlation coefficient (oCC) between each pair of parcels, yielding whole-cortex FC matrices for each subject at all frequency bands.   

First, in the absence of a well-defined atlas that conforms with both dMRI and EEG data, we opted for a data-driven approach to compare DTI metrics (MD and FA) in the five tracts of interest to all edges of the AAC matrices (Spearman correlations). No significant age correlations were found in MD (p=0.12, rho=-0.28), FA (p=0.38, r=0.16) or AAC (p=0.14-0.96, rho=-0.27-0.13; ranges across frequency bands) measures in the current term-age cohort, and age was therefore not added as a covariate. The proportion of significant structural-functional correlations (K) was plotted as a function of frequency, and multiple comparisons were corrected by rejecting the number of significant connections corresponding to the positive false discovery rate (FDR, q=0.05). Frequencies showing significant structural-functional connections were identified as peaks in the K plots. Next, a spatially constrained analysis was performed on these frequencies of interest. Here, oCC was averaged over regions-of-interest (ROIs) related to each tract (FMI: interhemispheric frontal regions; FMA: interhemispheric occipital regions; AC: interhemispheric temporal regions; IFO: intrahemispheric frontal and occipital regions) and correlated (Spearman) with DTI metrics (MD and FA; only MD presented in the current abstract) in the corresponding tract (multiple comparisons correction: Benjamini-Hochberg). 

Results

We found significant positive correlations (p < 0.05, K > 5 %) between SC (measured by MD) and FC (measured as AAC using oCC) for FMI and IFO, particularly in the right hemisphere (Fig. 2A). The most prominent frequencies were high-delta (2.6 Hz, K up to 12.5 %) and beta (23 Hz, K up to 12.0 %). The same tracts showed coherent negative correlations between FA and AAC at the same frequencies. Negative correlations between FA in FMA and AAC were found at high-delta (3.7 Hz, K = 8.3 %) and alpha (9.2 Hz, K = 9.0 %) frequencies. AC showed no significant correlations surpassing the FDR level. To assess the biological plausibility of these networks, we performed a spatially constrained analysis limiting FC to regions known to be associated with each tract. Here, we found a prominent correlation between MD in the FMI tract and interhemispheric frontal AAC at the frequencies of interest (Fig. 2B; 2.6 Hz, p = 0.03, rho = 0.52; 23 Hz, p = 0.07, rho = 0.33). Correlations for the other tracts did not reach statistical significance; however, the IFO tracts showed the same positive trends as found in the data-driven analysis. 

Conclusion

In the present work, we show evidence of structural-functional coupling in the brain at term-equivalent age of infants born preterm, most prominently in the FMI and IFO tracts at high delta and beta EEG frequencies. To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate structural-functional coupling with dMRI and EEG in the same infants. It is striking that association of structure and function are detectable even on a coarse spatial level given the large variability of both structural and functional networks in preterm infants.  

From a neurobiological standpoint, the directions and spatial signatures of the correlations are biologically plausible and in line with the known trends of brain development¹ ². Specifically, MD and AAC are known to decrease with PMA and hence a positive correlation between the metrics is expected, while FA is known to increase with PMA and thus a negative correlation with AAC is anticipated. While these directions may feel counterintuitive from the perspective that mature pathways (i.e. low MD and high FA) could be expected to have more efficient functional connections (i.e. high AAC), there is increasing evidence pointing that frequency-specific hyperconnectivity in EEG rather reflects immature synchronous bursting activity and is a sign of maturational delay. Furthermore, mature structural connections coupled with EEG hypoconnectivity fit well with recent insights showing that brain organization develops from a highly redundant to a synergistic system during this period⁵. 

Taken together, the findings indicate exciting new avenues in understanding the complex interplay between functional and structural development in the newborn brain, and future work should include more accurate mapping using individual source reconstruction and inclusion of all cortico-cortical tracts.
