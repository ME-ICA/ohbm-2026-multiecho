Introduction

The brain constantly switches between functional states to adapt to the environment, yet the mechanisms governing this global reorganization—specifically the "re-integration" of neural activity—remain elusive. Traditional linear methods (e.g., ALFF, functional connectivity) often overlook the complex, nonlinear interactions driving these transitions, failing to capture the emergent properties where the whole exceeds the sum of its parts 1-3. To bridge this gap, we developed a multi-level deep learning framework grounded in General Integratics 3 to decode the hierarchical architecture of information integration during the transition between eyes-open and eyes-closed states 4. By leveraging Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) to extract nonlinear spatiotemporal features directly from raw fMRI signals 5, this study moves beyond linear assumptions to systematically quantify integration across three scales: the nonlinear dynamics of individual regions, the emergent synergy within functional networks, and the global integration patterns between networks. This approach aims to reveal how discriminative information originates in primary sensory regions and evolves into global synergy within higher-order cognitive systems, providing a novel data-driven paradigm for understanding the multilevel information integration processes in complex biological systems.

Methods

Data Preprocessing. We utilized resting-state fMRI data from 46 healthy participants (EO and EC conditions). Data were preprocessed using the Deepprep pipeline and parcellated into 400 ROIs (Schaefer atlas) assigned to 15 functional networks (DU15NET-Consensus).

Deep Learning vs. Linear Modeling. To isolate nonlinear discriminative information, we designed a comparative benchmark using four modeling strategies:

  1. Linear Baselines: Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) and Support Vector Machines (SVM, linear kernel) trained on both handcrafted features (ALFF/fALFF and seed-based FC) and raw BOLD time series.
  2. End-to-End Deep Learning: We implemented classic 1D-Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and InceptionV2 architectures. These models take raw BOLD time series as input to automatically extract hierarchical nonlinear spatiotemporal features.

Multi-Level Information Integration Framework. We developed a three-level framework to quantify information integration across scales (see Fig.1):

Level 1: Nonlinear Temporal Integration (Component Level). We defined "Nonlinear Integrative Gain" as the classification accuracy improvement of deep learning models over linear models at the single-ROI level. This metric isolates the contribution of nonlinear temporal dynamics within local regions.

Level 2: Intra-network Spatiotemporal Integration (Subsystem Level). To measure informational emergence (synergy), we calculated "Intra-network Integrative Gain." This compares the performance of a model trained on all ROIs within a network against the performance of that network's single best-performing ROI. A positive gain indicates that the network as a whole provides information greater than the sum of its parts.

Level 3: Inter-network Spatiotemporal Integration (Global Level). We assessed "Inter-network Integrative Gain" by systematically pairing functional networks. This metric quantifies the synergistic performance boost achieved by combining two networks relative to their individual capabilities, allowing us to identify "integration hubs" that drive global brain state transitions.

Results

Our multi-level decoding framework revealed a hierarchical architecture of information integration, demonstrating that state-discriminative information evolves from local nonlinear dynamics to network-level emergence and global synergy.
Conventional group-level contrasts replicated classic EO–EC effects (e.g., ALFF/fALFF and seed-based FC differences), but these linear metrics did not fully capture subject-level discriminative information. In individual-level decoding benchmarks, end-to-end nonlinear models trained on raw BOLD signals consistently achieved the best performance: CNN (62.1%) and InceptionV2 (64.2%) significantly outperformed linear classifiers trained on handcrafted features (LDA: 56.9%; SVM: 58.9%; paired tests, P<0.001) and linear models applied to raw signals (lda: 58.7%; svm: 58.9%; paired tests, P<0.001). Whole-cortex accuracy maps further showed that deep learning extended high-accuracy decoding beyond classical sensory areas to broader association cortices, indicating that substantial state-relevant information is embedded in nonlinear temporal dynamics that conventional pipelines likely discard (Fig.2A).

Level 1 (single ROI / temporal integration): ROI-wise decoding revealed a highly heterogeneous informational landscape. The highest "Nonlinear Integrative Gain" was concentrated in primary sensory cortices (e.g. visual and somatomotor cortices) (Fig.2B), suggesting these regions encode rich nonlinear temporal dynamics.

Level 2 (within-network integration / emergence): Scaling from single regions to network-level multichannel inputs reshaped the hierarchy of informational importance (Fig.2C). In higher-order cognitive networks—Action/Cingulo-Opercular (CG-OP), Default (DN-A/B), Dorsal Attention (dATN-A/B), and Fronto-Parietal control (FPN-A)—network-level models significantly outperformed the best single ROI within the same network, yielding positive “Intra-network Integrative Gain” consistent with emergent/synergistic information (i.e., the whole exceeds its best part). In contrast, most primary networks exhibited redundancy: integration provided little improvement (e.g., VIS-P, SMOT-B) or even reduced accuracy relative to the best ROI (e.g., SMOT-A, AUD), consistent with added noise from less informative regions without compensatory synergy.

Level 3 (between-network integration / global synergy): Across all 105 network pairs, combinations involving dorsal attention or visual systems often achieved high accuracy (>85%). However, isolating true synergy via “Inter-network Integrative Gain” revealed structured, non-homogeneous integration fingerprints (Fig.2D and 2E). Visual networks showed substantial gains when paired with other primary sensory systems and CG-OP, while somatomotor and auditory networks emerged as prominent global “integration hubs,” displaying high gain with many higher-order networks (attention/action/control). Conversely, DN-A/B—despite strong intra-network emergence—showed weak or negative inter-network gains, indicating relative functional insulation during EO–EC transitions. Notably, integration gain only weakly correlated with absolute FC changes (Fig.2F), and large gains frequently occurred in pairs with minimal FC differences, suggesting that the learned discriminative interactions go beyond linear second-order connectivity. Finally, whole-brain models using all 400 ROIs achieved 91.25% (CNN) and 89.54% (InceptionV2), exceeding the best single-network (84.4%) and best network-pair performance (86.1%), providing direct evidence for a whole-brain integrative effect (~6% gain) in which global state information surpasses any local subsystem.

Conclusion

Our results demonstrate that EO–EC state transitions are supported by a hierarchical, cross-scale architecture of information integration. At the component level, state-discriminative information is densely embedded in the nonlinear temporal dynamics of primary sensory cortices, revealing critical signals that are systematically underestimated by linear features and correlation-based connectivity. At the subsystem level, emergent “whole-greater-than-best-part” effects arise predominantly within higher-order cognitive networks (attention, control, and default systems), indicating that network-level representations contain synergistic information not reducible to single nodes. At the global level, inter-network integration is highly structured rather than homogeneous: primary sensory systems—especially somatomotor and auditory networks—act as major integration hubs that broadcast and amplify state-relevant information across the brain, whereas the default network shows relative insulation despite strong within-network emergence. Together, these findings support a bottom-up progression from local nonlinear encoding to network-level emergence and whole-brain synergy, providing a data-driven framework to quantify integrative effects across scales. This multilevel deep learning paradigm offers mechanistic insight into how macroscopic brain states emerge and may inform practical neuroengineering applications such as robust brain-state decoding for BCIs.
