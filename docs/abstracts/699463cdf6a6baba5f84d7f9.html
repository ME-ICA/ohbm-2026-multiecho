Introduction

Monoaminergic neurotransmitters help regulate the interplay between the nervous system and immune system.^1 Aberrant monoaminergic functional connectivity is presumably involved in MS-related fatigue, but mechanisms remain unclear.^2,3,4 Here, we aimed to investigate how static and dynamic functional interactions of monoaminergic circuits relate to fatigue in MS.

Methods

Participants completed the Fatigue Scale for Motor and Cognitive Functions (FSMC) and underwent functional MRI. Regions were assigned to resting-state networks (RSNs). PET-derived receptor/transporter densities determined assignment to monoaminergic circuits (5-HT1a/5-HT2a/D1/D2/5-HTT/DAT/NAT). Per circuit, we compared how often regions switched between RSNs (flexibility), and the total number of RSNs switched to (promiscuity), between healthy controls (HC; FSMC<53; n=76), not-to-mildly fatigued people with MS (PwMS; FSMC<53; n=120), and moderately-to-severely fatigued PwMS (FSMC≥53; n=109). For circuits showing flexibility differences, we also compared rates of synchronous (cohesion) and independent switches (disjointedness).

Results

Flexibility and cohesion were higher in moderately-to-severely fatigued PwMS than in HC globally (pflex=0.037; pcoh=0.048) and for the 5-HT1a circuit (pflex=0.027; pcoh=0.025). Similar patterns were found for the D2/DAT circuit, although contrasts were non-significant.

Conclusion

Fatigued PwMS show more dynamic global functional connectivity and of regions expressing the inhibitory serotonergic receptor. These more unstable dynamics likely raise energy expenditure in PwMS and therefore may underlie fatigue.
