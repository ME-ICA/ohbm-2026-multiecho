Introduction

Bariatric surgery remains the most effective treatment for severe obesity, however, up to 40% of patients fail to adopt healthy eating behaviors. We examined whether transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) targeting the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) could enhance cognitive control in patients preparing for bariatric surgery and improve regulation of food-related impulses.

Methods

This preliminary analysis includes 17 participants (3 male) enrolled in a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial. Participants received either active (n = 8) or sham (n = 9) home-based anodal tDCS (2 mA, 20 minutes, 20 sessions). All participants completed pre- and post-intervention assessments, including EEG recordings during a food-related Go/No-Go task.

 The obtained EEG data were filtered to a 0.1-100Hz frequency band. Concentrating on 15 regions of interest (ROIs), consisting of reward/food-related regions and regions involved in the inhibitory network, mean event-related spectral perturbations (ERSP) were computed and statistically compared between pre- and post-intervention conditions. The average differential ERSPs of time window from 100ms to 1000ms after stimulus were computed and compared by PERMANOVA between sham and active stimulation subjects.

 We used IBM SPSS 29 to analyze Go/NoGo task behavioral data (i.e., reaction times, misses and error rates), applying the Mann-Whitney U test for non-parametric and the t-test for parametric data to compare changes between real and sham stimulation groups.

Results

Across all ROIs, correcting for the effects of age and sex, the sham and active groups significantly differed (F=2.5, p=.048). Post-hoc tests identified significant differences in the left frontal middle gyrus (F=12.4, p=.005) and left insula (F=10.9, p=.006). In both regions, the active tDCS stimulation group showed positive post-intervention synchronization during the No-Go tasks, while the sham group showed none or slightly negative post-intervention synchronization (Fig 1).

Behaviorally, the active stimulation group showed slight improvement in Go reaction time pre and post stimulation (p=0.061) and demonstrated this faster reaction times relative to the sham group (p=0.039).

Conclusion

These preliminary findings suggest that repeated tDCS over the left DLPFC may enhance cognitive control by modulating fronto-limbic circuits, particularly in response to emotionally salient food cues. The effects in the active stimulation group supports the potential of tDCS as an adjunctive intervention before bariatric surgery. Further investigation with larger samples is ongoing to assess efficacy and individual variability in treatment response.
