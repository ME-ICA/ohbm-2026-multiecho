Introduction

Alterations in large-scale brain networks have been thought to be related to cognitive impairment in epilepsy. Cognitive impairment is present in patients with newly diagnosed epilepsy (NDE)1, yet limited attention has been given to functional network-level alterations at diagnosis and their relationship to cognition. Understanding baseline functional connectivity patterns could provide crucial insights into mechanisms underlying the cognitive impairment in patients with NDE. This study sought to determine whether altered functional brain networks are related to distinct cognitive impairment phenotypes in patients with NDE using resting-state functional MRI (rsfMRI).

Methods

We examined 104 patients with focal NDE and 45 healthy age, sex and education matched controls (16–65 years; 45% female). Each participant underwent multi-sequence MRI including resting-state fMRI (de Bezenac et al., 2019). MRI data was pre-processed using FreeSurfer followed by fMRIprep. Functional connectivity analysis was performed using the CONN Functional Connectivity Toolbox. Data was denoised using a standard pipeline including noise regression of white matter and CSF components, motion parameters and derivatives, outlier scan regression and finally bandpass filtering (0.008-0.09 Hz). Resting-state functional ROI-to-ROI connectivity analysis examined 71 features for within-network connectivity, cross-network connectivity, and subcortical-to-cortical network connectivity. These were derived from a manually constructed hybrid atlas consisting of 100 cortical parcels made up from 7 canonical networks (Schaefer Atlas), 32 subcortical regions (Tian atlas), and 7 cerebellar parcels (Buckner atlas). Connectivity strength represented by Fisher-transformed bivariate correlations from weighted general linear models. Group-level analyses employed separate GLMs for each connection. fMRI data was compared between groups of patients stratified according to cognitive phenotypes and healthy controls. All participants were examined using a neuropsychological battery that assessed memory, executive function, motor, and mood domains2. Cognitive phenotypes were derived from a data-driven approach employing unsupervised machine learning K-means clustering of neuropsychological assessments scores. Group differences were assessed using univariate and multivariate approaches with correction for multiple comparisons in RStudio. 

Results

K-means clustering analysis identified four distinct cognitive phenotypes; a selective memory impaired group (n=12), a subtly overall impaired group (n=41), a globally impaired group (n=30) and a non-impaired group (n=21). Univariate analyses showed that no individual connectivity feature differed significantly between patients and controls or between phenotypes after FDR correction. ANOVA revealed that the global impairment group had the lowest within-network connectivity across many key networks, reflecting widespread network insufficiency. Multivariate analysis however revealed robust phenotype group and control separation despite weak univariate effects. MANOVA demonstrated significant differentiation between groups (Wilks’ λ=0.565, p=0.0002), and linear discriminant analysis achieved 79.9% five group (four phenotypes and control) classification accuracy. Network-pair correlation analysis identified a single FDR-corrected effect: the non-impaired/cognitively resilient group had a reduced hippocampal–default mode network coupling compared with controls (pFDR=0.029) and the greatest connectivity between all other regions and networks. Exploratory analysis of subcortical hub connectivity revealed several group differences at the uncorrected level. Thalamus–salience network connectivity differed across groups (F=2.97, p=0.021), driven by lower connectivity in the globally impaired group compared with controls (p=0.010). Thalamus–dorsal attention connectivity also showed a group effect (F=2.63, p=0.037), with reduced connectivity in the memory-impaired group relative to controls (p=0.025). Finally, the non-impaired group revealed increased hippocampal–visual network connectivity compared with the memory impaired group (p=0.014) and greater hippocampal within-network connectivity than the globally impaired group (p=0.034). 

Conclusion

Patients with NDE have distinct cognitive phenotypes that are associated with differential resting-state functional connectivity patterns. Cognitively resilient/non-impaired patients exhibit a distinctive functional profile marked by elevated connectivity across multiple networks alongside reduced hippocampus–default mode network coupling. This may suggest adaptive or compensatory network reorganization rather than simple preservation of canonical memory circuits. Exploratory hub-level analyses further implicated thalamic and hippocampal connections in phenotype-specific domains, therefore highlighting that alterations in key subcortical hubs may underlie distinct cognitive profiles observed in patients with NDE.
