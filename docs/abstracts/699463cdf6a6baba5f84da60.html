Introduction

Chronic alcohol consumption induces alterations in brain regions classically linked to addiction, such as the nucleus accumbens and prefrontal cortex, but also affects less explored areas. The cerebellum has recently emerged as a modulator of cognitive, emotional, and reward-related processes, with growing evidence showing its involvement in drug reinforcement and conditioned responses, supporting a role in addiction-like behaviors. At the cellular level, ethanol exposure can disrupt Purkinje neuron integrity while producing limited microglial alterations under certain developmental conditions, suggesting selective sensitivity among cerebellar cell types. Despite this evidence, the longitudinal impact of chronic alcohol intake on cerebellar structure remains poorly characterized, particularly regarding alterations in Purkinje neurons, astrocytes, and microglia. Here, we evaluated cerebellar volumetric changes and cellular alterations induced by chronic alcohol exposure in Wistar rats.

Methods

Male and female Wistar rats were habituated from P21 to P45 and subsequently exposed to 20% ethanol in an intermittent two-bottle choice (IA2BC) paradigm administered every other day for a total of 20 drinking sessions. Longitudinal MRI was acquired at three stages aligned with the experimental timeline: baseline (T1, P45), early exposure (T2, P67), and after completing the alcohol protocol (T3, P90). Imaging was conducted using a 7 T Bruker Pharmascan 70/16 US scanner equipped with a 2×2 surface-array rat head coil and a Paravision 6.0.1 console. Animals were anesthetized with 5% isoflurane for induction and ~1% for maintenance, with physiological parameters continuously monitored. High-resolution T2-weighted structural images were acquired with a FLASH 3D sequence (TR = 30.76 ms, TE = 5 ms, flip angle = 10°, FOV = 28.2 × 19 × 25.6 mm, isotropic voxel size = 160 µm, two repetitions, sagittal orientation). These datasets were used to evaluate brain volumetric changes across time using deformation-based morphometry. For histology, independent cohorts were perfused at T1, T2, or T3; brains were cryoprotected, coronally sectioned at 30 µm, and processed for immunofluorescence using primary antibodies against calbindin (Purkinje neurons), GFAP (astrocytes), and Iba1 (microglia). Confocal microscopy was performed using identical laser intensity, gain, and pinhole settings across animals. Regions of interest were delimited in cerebellar lobules VIII and IX, and cell densities for each marker were quantified.

Results

Alcohol intake during the IA2BC protocol remained stable across sessions, with males consuming approximately 2.3-2.5 g/kg per 24 h and females averaging around 1.1–1.3 g/kg per 24 h. Intake classification revealed two consumption profiles: a high-intake subgroup composed predominantly of males (92.07% males vs. 7.93% females) and a low-intake subgroup with a larger proportion of females (42%). Chronic alcohol exposure produced volumetric increases in cerebellar lobule IX (p < 0.05), while lobule VIII showed no consistent changes across time. At the cellular level, alcohol-exposed rats exhibited a progressive reduction in Purkinje neuron density in lobule IX, with females showing marked decreases at both T2 and T3 compared with controls (p < 0.01), whereas males displayed only modest reductions. Microglial density decreased in exposed females at T2 (p < 0.05), while males showed no substantial alterations. Astrocyte density increased significantly in alcohol-exposed females at T3 (p < 0.01), whereas males again showed minimal changes.

Conclusion

Together, these findings reveal a sex-dependent pattern in which females exhibit earlier and stronger cellular alterations, including Purkinje neuron loss, reduced microglial counts, and later astrocytic activation, which may help explain the volumetric expansion observed in cerebellar lobule IX. This region emerges as a key target for understanding alcohol-related neuroadaptations.
