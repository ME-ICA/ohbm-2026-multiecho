Introduction

Mesial Temporal Sclerosis (MTS) is the most common drug-refractory epilepsy. MTS patients are affected by severe white matter abnormalities in and outside the temporal lobe. Those patients refractory to antiepileptic drugs are potential candidates for surgical resection.  Seizure activity in MTS may bring widespread propagation of neuronal firing through cortical and subcortical regions and white matter networks1. Analysing the white matter tracts and connectivity may reveal the diffuse changes in the brain. MTS is considered a network-level disorder that primarily affects the hippocampus, the surrounding medial temporal lobe, and extra-temporal white matter tracts2. Analyzing the white matter structural reorganization and connectivity pre- and postoperatively may assist in predicting the prognosis of surgery, including seizure recurrence and other complications. The longitudinal changes in MTS patients before and after surgery are not fully explored. Multiple fiber tracts, ipsilateral and contralateral to the epileptogenic zone, are affected due to MTS. In the present study, we aim to assess the longitudinal structural reorganization pre- and postoperatively by quantifying the nodal and global network measures, Quantitative Anisotropy (QA) and Fractional Anisotropy. We extracted the quantitative anisotropy from ipsilateral and contralateral limbic tracts to calculate the asymmetry index.

Methods

The study is a longitudinal prospective analysis on eight MTS patients age range (20-50 yrs) before surgery and 3 months after surgery.   The diffusion images were acquired on a GE DiscoveryMR750w scanner using a diffusion sequence. TE=82.7 ms, and TR=9000 ms. The slice thickness was 3 mm. The in-plane resolution was 1.0156 mm x 1.0156 mm. The flip angle was 90 degrees. The pixel bandwidth was 1953.12 Hz. A DTI diffusion scheme was used, and a total of 27 diffusion sampling directions were acquired. The b-value was 1000s/mm². The eddy current distortions of 30 DWI were corrected using FSL eddy. The diffusion data were reconstructed in the MNI space using q-space diffeomorphic reconstruction3 to obtain the spin distribution function. We conducted a whole brain connectivity analysis using Freesurfer seg atlas. We extracted the connectivity matrix and extracted the global and nodal graph measures. The global measures we evaluated was global efficiency, clustering coefficient, small world network and average path length. The Nodal measures assessed was Degree (binary), local efficiency, eccentricity and betweeness centrality. The global efficiency measures how information is communicated across entire network. Clustering coefficient is the ability of a node to connect with nearest neighbours. Average path length measure the shortest path between nodes, Small world network analyses quantifies the balance between clustering coefficient and average path length. The nodal measure like degree measures the total connections of a specific node. Local efficiency analyses the how efficiently a node communicate with their nearest neighbours. Eccentricity evaluates the shortest path from node to neighbouring nodes. Finally the betweeness centrality measures whether a particular node act as bridge between two separated clusters4. Quantitative Anisotropy (QA) is more sensitive measure of the extent of diffusing water and estimates the differences in the density of diffusion spins. Moreover the isotropic diffusion component is removed to eliminate the free water effect. Hence, QA is more robust to the free water effect and the partial volume of crossing fibers5. We quantified the Diffusion Tensor Imaging metrics like FA and QA. Paired t-tests was used to assess the PRE–POST differences (α=0.05) in each patient. Diffusion tensor metrics (FA) and (QA) were quantified from fiber tracts like cingulum, uncinate fasciculus, inferior fronto occipital fasciculus, middle longitudinal fasciculus and fornix, across patients. PRE–POST nodal statistics for local efficiency and eccentricity were extracted from paired samples analyses (192 nodes from freesurfer seg atlas). We calculated the QA from the ipsilateral and contralateral side of limbic network tracts such as cingulum parahippocampus, inferior longitudinal fasciculus, uncinate fasciculus and fornix.

Results

All global network measures were insignificant between pre and post DTI of all patients. Local efficiency was significantly increased in seven patients, pre to post surgery. For eccentricity, all patient’s pre and post pair demonstrated highly significant changes. We observed a significant change in QA rather than FA in all patients from pre to post. The one sampled t test done on Asymmetry Index calculated from QA of cingulum, inferior longitudinal fasciculus, uncinate fasciculus and fornix revealed significant negative Asymmetry indices in the evaluated tracts. Conclusion

The study observed significant white matter degeneration in the ipsilateral side of Cingulum, Inferior longitudinal fasciculus, uncinate fasciculus and fornix tracts in pre and post MTS patients. The present study observed that white matter abnormalities are more marked in ipsilateral hemisphere of MTS rather than contralateral hemisphere. The study demonstrated considerable change in nodal network properties after surgery. There were negligible changes in the global graph metrics in all subjects. The nodal measures showed robust pre-post reorganization than global graph metrics. The QA values showed sensitive microstructural sensitivity than FA in the pre to post assessment of MTS patients. The present study is a preliminary analysis done on a limited dataset involving 8 MTS patients. Huge sample size including both seizure free group and non- seizure free group may enable the analysis on seizure outcome in MTS patients.
