Introduction

Depression is a leading cause of disability worldwide, with a lifetime prevalence of 15-20%. Yet approximately one-third of patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) do not respond to pharmacotherapy. Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) remains the most effective treatment for treatment-resistant cases, with response rates of 60-80%. However, ECT's neurobiological mechanisms remain poorly understood, and reliable biomarkers predicting treatment response are lacking.

Depression involves dysconnectivity across large-scale brain networks. The ENIGMA consortium and PsyMRI mega-analysis reported widespread abnormal white matter integrity from diffusion MRI and reduced functional connectivity within somatosensory motor, salience networks and default mode from resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) studies .

Neuroimaging studies also investigated ECT-related connectivity changes. Rs-fMRI studies identified changes across default mode, frontoparietal, and fronto-limbic networks. Additionally, diffusion MRI studies reported increased fractional anisotropy in multiple white matter tracts, including corpus callosum, and dorsal fronto-limbic circuits.

Graph theory provides a framework for characterizing brain connectome through metrics like global efficiency, local efficiency, and small-worldness. In MDD, graph analyses have identified reduced network efficiency correlating with symptom severity. Of note, if few studies have examined ECT-related changes using graph theory [1,2], none have investigated structural network modifications.

In this context, we conducted a prospective study examining brain networks in treatment-resistant depression patients undergoing ECT, using graph theory applied to structural and functional connectivity at baseline, after five sessions, and post-treatment. Our objectives were to characterize longitudinal changes in global graph metrics and examine whether baseline metrics predict treatment response.

Methods

Patients and protocol description

This prospective longitudinal monocentric study (PI: M. Plaze, GHU Paris, France) included patients with MDD above 18 years old at GHU-Paris University Hospital (France) between June 2018 and April 2022. Inclusion criteria were: (1) DSM-5 criteria for uni- or bipolar major depressive disorder, and (2) requirement for ECT treatment. Exclusion criteria were: co-morbid psychiatric disorders including schizophrenia, pervasive developmental disorder, substance abuse other than tobacco, dementia or neurological illness, ECT in the previous 6 months, pregnancy, and MR contraindications. The protocol was approved by a national ethics committee (Comité de Protection des Personnes - CPP17-046). The protocol is shown on Figure 1.

Bitemporal ECT (5000M MECTA spECTrum) was delivered twice weekly using propofol or etomidate for anesthesia and succinylcholine for paralysis. Treatment continued until remission or when improvement ceased over three consecutive sessions. Patients were assessed at baseline (V1), after 5 ECT sessions (V2), and 14 days post-treatment (V3) using Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) and Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HDRS-21). Response was defined as 50% MADRS reduction; remission as MADRS < 11. 

Patients were scanned at each timepoint on a 3T MR scanner (MR750; GE Healthcare) equipped with a 16-channel head coil. Healthy participants were scanned once at inclusion (V1). Sequences included: structural 3D T1-weighted at 1mm isotropic resolution (TE/TR=3.2/8.2); diffusion-weighted multiple-shell at 2mm isotropic resolution (TE/TR=80/440ms) with three shells (b=200/1500/2500 s/mm² with 30/45/60 diffusion directions respectively); and resting-state functional acquisition (TE/TR=26/2000ms) at 3mm isotropic resolution.

Structural connectivity

White matter structural connectivity was estimated using a pipeline combining MRtrix3, FreeSurfer, FSL, and ANTs. T1-weighted images underwent FreeSurfer reconstruction with the Destrieux atlas. DWI preprocessing included MP-PCA denoising, Gibbs artifact correction, susceptibility distortion correction using TOPUP and EDDY, and bias field correction. Fiber orientation distributions were estimated using multi-shell multi-tissue constrained spherical deconvolution, and streamlines were seeded at the WM-GM interface using the deterministic SD Stream algorithm with 10 million seeds, then filtered using SIFT. The NODDI model was applied to compute Neurite Density Index (NDI), Free Water Fraction (FWF), and Orientation Dispersion Index (ODI), while the DTI model extracted standard diffusion maps including radial diffusivity (RD) and fractional anisotropy (FA). Structural connectivity matrices were constructed from region-to-region streamline counts and microstructural information.

Functional connectivity

Resting-state functional MRI and structural MRI were preprocessed using fMRIPrep 23, followed by confound regression using Nilearn 0.10.3 and time series extraction from Destrieux atlas regions. Functional connectivity matrices were constructed using z-score transformed Pearson correlation between regional time series, retaining only positive values.

Graph analysis

Graphs were first density-thresholded to ensure metric comparability across subjects (structural: 0.095; functional: 1-40% in 1% increments, with area under the curve computed for functional networks). Global and local efficiency were computed for structural and functional networks, quantifying network integration and segregation/robustness, respectively [3]. Linear mixed-effects models assessed time effects (V1, V2, V3) on global metrics, with time, age, and sex as fixed effects and subject-specific random intercepts. Baseline metrics were compared between remitters (MADRS<11) and non-remitters using ANOVAs, with FDR correction applied to control for multiple comparisons across edge weights and metrics. R 4.3.2 was used for analysis.

Results

Demographic and Clinical Characteristics

The final sample included 41 patients at baseline (27 women, 53.7±18.4 years old) and 24 age- and sex-matched healthy controls (HC; 13 women, 49.3±20 years old). At V2, 31 patients were assessed, and 29 patients completed treatment at V3, with an average of 70 days (±25 days) between V1 and V3. At baseline, patients presented with severe depression (MADRS=35.6±8.1; range:12-51). Among the 29 patients completing treatment, 23 (79%) responded (≥50% MADRS reduction) and 20 (68%) achieved remission (MADRS<11).

Brain connectivity in MDD versus controls at baseline

Comparison of MDD and HC networks at baseline revealed differences in FA-weighted networks, with higher local efficiency in HC compared to MDD (+4.8%, pFDR=0.0008). Global and local efficiency networks based on other edge weights (AD, MD, RD, ODI, NDI, FWF, SC) did not differ between patients and controls (all pFDR>0.5). Analysis of functional networks at baseline revealed no significant difference between MDD and HC after correction for multiple comparisons (all pFDR>0.3).

Brain connectivity changes in MDD following ECT

Analysis of structural and functional networks revealed no significant changes after correction for multiple comparisons (all pFDR>0.5). 

Brain connectivity at baseline and clinical outcome

Analysis of structural networks at baseline in HC, remitters (MADRS<11 at V3) and non-remitters revealed significant main effects of group in FA-weighted networks for global efficiency (pFDR=0.024; Figure 2-A) and local efficiency (pFDR=0.012). Post-hoc analyses revealed lower global efficiency in FA-weighted networks in remitters than non-remitters (p=0.0432). Future remitters had lower global efficiency compared to controls (p=0.0211); whereas non-remitters showed no differences (p=0.9113). Future remitters showed lower local efficiency in FA-weighted networks compared to HC (p=0.0022). The NDI-weighted network revealed a significant global efficiency difference between future non-remitters and remitters (p=0.055). Structural networks at baseline did not reveal differences in efficiency between responders (≥50% MADRS reduction) and non-responders after correction for multiple comparisons (all pFDR>0.5).

Baseline functional network analysis comparing HC, remitters, and non-remitters revealed significant differences for local efficiency (pFDR=0.0484; Figure 2-B). Post-hoc comparisons revealed lower local efficiency in functional correlation-weighted networks among non-remitters compared to remitters (p=0.0567). Non-remitters exhibited lower local efficiency relative to HC (p=0.0206), whereas future remitters showed no significant differences from HC. Functional networks at baseline did not reveal differences between responders and non-responders after correction for multiple comparisons (all pFDR>0.5).

Finally, binomial Firth regressions investigated prognostic power of baseline graph metrics for predicting clinical outcomes. Remission (MADRS<11 at V3) was predicted by global efficiency in NDI-weighted networks (Likelihood ratio test=9.19417 on 3 df, p=0.026). No other metrics predicted clinical remission (all pFDR>0.1). None of the metrics predicted clinical response (MADRS≥50% reduction).

Conclusion

This study examined brain structural and functional graph-theory metrics as predictive biomarkers for ECT response in treatment-resistant depression. At baseline, MDD patients showed reduced structural network local efficiency compared to healthy controls. At baseline, future remitters exhibited lower structural local and global efficiency than non-remitters in FA and NDI-weighted networks, while non-remitters showed reduced functional local efficiency compared to controls.

Baseline connectivity analysis revealed complementary patterns across modalities. In structural networks, remitters showed lower efficiency than non-remitters in FA-weighted and NDI-weighted networks, and lower efficiency versus controls, while non-remitters showed no differences from controls. In functional networks, non-remitters exhibited lower baseline local efficiency versus controls, with a trend toward lower efficiency compared to remitters, while remitters showed no differences from controls. These divergent patterns suggest that non-remission is characterized by relatively preserved structural connectivity coupled with reduced functional connectivity, whereas remission is associated with reduced structural efficiency but preserved functional organization.

Precision psychiatry aims to tailor treatments to biologically defined patient subgroups, yet this approach cannot succeed without validated biomarkers, "a characteristic that is objectively measured and evaluated as an indicator of normal biological processes, pathogenic processes, or pharmacologic responses to therapeutic intervention". The FDA-NIH framework distinguishes predictive biomarkers, which prospectively identify individuals likely to respond to specific treatments, from monitoring biomarkers, which track treatment response. Recent frameworks emphasize that effective biomarkers should also integrate clinical information [4]. While neuroimaging biomarkers are actively explored in research, their implementation in clinical practice remains limited [5].

Graph-theory metrics offer a practical approach to biomarker development by compressing high-dimensional connectivity data into interpretable summary statistics. Rather than analyzing thousands of individual structural or functional connections, global metrics such as global and local efficiency can capture fundamental organizational principles: network-wide integration and regional clustering, respectively. 

Of note, graph metrics are sensitive to processing decisions including parcellation schemes, tractography algorithms, and connectivity thresholding approaches. While we employed multi-threshold density approaches to enhance robustness, our findings should be confirmed using alternative parcellation atlases and tractography methods to establish their generalizability across different processing pipelines.

Our findings emphasize group-level differences but clinical application requires individual-level classification with sensitivity, specificity, and predictive values. Our sample (N=29; 20 remitters, 9 non-remitters) was suitable to investigate group differences but insufficient to pinpoint robust individual prediction. Larger samples and machine learning with cross-validation and external validation are necessary to translate these findings into clinical practice.

In conclusion, this study provides preliminary evidence that graph-theory metrics from structural and functional networks may serve as trait markers for predicting ECT remission. Translation will require multi-site replication, validated individual classification models, and demonstration that biomarker-guided decisions improve outcomes.
