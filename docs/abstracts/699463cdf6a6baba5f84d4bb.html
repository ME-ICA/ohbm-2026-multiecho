Introduction

Transcranial electrical stimulation (tES) is a promising non-invasive technique for modulating brain activity.1 However, its efficacy remains limited by diffuse current flow, coarse electrode montages, and substantial inter-individual variability.2 While recent advances in high-density tES and electric-field modeling have improved targeting, a principled framework for optimizing whole-brain stimulation patterns is still lacking. Here, we introduce a unified computational pipeline that integrates multichannel E-field simulations, whole-brain dynamical modeling informed by human structural connectivity, and maximum-entropy energy-landscape estimation. The framework is designed to systematically evaluate how different multichannel tES patterns may reshape large-scale brain dynamics and stability, laying the groundwork for algorithmical optimization of the brain’s energy landscape toward desired dynamical regimes (e.g., MDD to HC).

Methods

Personalized E-field simulations are generated using the SimNIBS package based on individual structural MRI data, with electrode placements following the international 10-20 system using a 32-channel EEG-tES Neuroelectrics Starstim montage. These E-field maps are incorporated into a whole-brain dynamical model that simulates large-scale neural activity and corresponding BOLD signals.3 Because empirical stimulation data for model calibration are not yet available, we explore multiple hypothesis-driven coupling schemes between the E-field and neural populations (e.g., modulation proportional to E-field magnitude or orientation relative to cortical columns). Simulated activity is binarized to estimate population activation statistics, and a pairwise Maximum Entropy Model is fitted to recover the corresponding energy landscape. This approach enables quantitative characterization of how different stimulation patterns and coupling assumptions influence the stability structure of whole-brain dynamical states.

Results

Using a publicly available subject with structural and functional magnetic resonance imaging4, we demonstrate the feasibility and internal consistency of the whole-brain model. It reproduces large-scale functional organization with empirically consistent accuracy (functional connectivity correlation > 0.6). The Maximum Entropy Model accurately captures activation statistics (pairwise marginal similarity > 0.9). Systematic variation of stimulation patterns and E-field coupling assumptions produces robust and interpretable shifts in the estimated energy landscape, indicating that the framework is sensitive to multichannel tES parameters and suitable for hypothesis testing and optimization in silico.

Conclusion

We introduce a unified, mechanistic framework for investigating and optimizing multichannel tES at the whole-brain level by integrating E-field simulations, large-scale brain dynamics, and energy-landscape analysis. While empirical calibration of the E-field to neural coupling is ongoing, the present work establishes a proof-of-concept and a flexible framework for comparing stimulation hypotheses and guiding data-driven optimization. Ongoing work aims to fit the model to stimulation data and apply the framework to disorder-specific targets, such as reducing energy-landscape differences between Major Depressive Disorder and healthy controls5, paving the way for personalized, model-based neuromodulation strategies.2
