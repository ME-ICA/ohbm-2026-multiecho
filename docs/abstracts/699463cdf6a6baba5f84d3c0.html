Introduction

Information processing speed (IPS) is a core cognitive deficit in people with multiple sclerosis (PwMS)1, and is typically assessed with the Symbol Digit Modalities Test (SDMT). fMRI studies of SDMT performance in PwMS report increased frontal recruitment and enhanced network connectivity2, but lack the temporal resolution to capture fast-evolving neural dynamics. EEG and MEG capture brain activity on a millisecond timescale, yet IPS-related work in MS has primarily examined isolated regions or specific frequency bands.3,4 However, cognition relies on dynamic interactions across distributed, large-scale brain networks. Consequently, network-based, time-resolved analyses of IPS-focused tasks, such as the SDMT, are critical for understanding the underlying neural dynamics.

Methods

MEG data were recorded while 37 healthy controls and 73 PwMS completed a computerised SDMT task. After source reconstruction, we employed a data-driven method, the time delay embedded-hidden Markov model (TDE-HMM), to identify transient, spectrally defined networks engaged during task execution. Network activation dynamics were extracted post hoc from the state time courses using an event-related (ER) approach. Group-level effects of both experimental and subject-specific factors were assessed using a second-level generalised linear model (GLM). For networks showing significant disease-related differences (maximum t-statistic permutation), we extracted peak amplitudes and latencies and compared groups using permutation statistics. Relationships between these peak features and behavioural performance on the clinical SDMT (z-scores) and the MEG-SDMT (reaction time and accuracy) were assessed using Pearson correlations (FDR-corrected). Spatio-spectral network properties were extracted using multitaper spectral estimation, summarised via data-driven spectral components, and used to derive spatial power maps and coherence networks for each state.

Results

Using a 6-state TDE-HMM, five states showed task-related activity (Figure 1c). These included prefrontal theta and broadband frontoparietal cognitive networks (Figure 2), as well as early and late occipital alpha, and sensorimotor beta networks. Prefrontal peak amplitude during Match trials was reduced in PwMS (p = 0.01, FDR-corrected) and was negatively associated with reaction time in both Match (r = −0.396, p = 6 x 10-4) and Non-Match trials (r = −0.299, p = 0.018), while also positively correlating with clinical SDMT performance in Non-Match trials (r = 0.263, p = 0.047). Late frontoparietal peak latency was positively associated with RT in Match trials (r = 0.395, p = 4 x 10-4). In addition, the latency of late occipital alpha deactivation was positively associated with reaction time in both task conditions and negatively associated with clinical SDMT performance in Non-Match trials (r = −0.288, p = 0.023).

Conclusion

This study provides a detailed description of large-scale brain network dynamics underlying an IPS task, with distinct temporal, spectral, and spatial characteristics. Using the TDE-HMM, we identified five task-relevant states, supporting a tri-factor contribution to IPS5: sensory speed (occipital visual detection and processing), cognitive speed (prefrontal executive and frontoparietal attentional processes), and motor speed (sensorimotor). Reduced prefrontal activation was observed in PwMS, while peak features across prefrontal, frontoparietal, and occipital networks were associated with task reaction times and clinical SDMT performance. Together, these network-level impairments and their behavioural relevance can drive future research for interventions aimed at improving IPS in MS.
