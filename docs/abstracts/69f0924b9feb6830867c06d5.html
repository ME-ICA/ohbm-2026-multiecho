Introduction

Recent advances have revealed that the brain is organized along continuous functional gradients, offering a low-dimensional framework for understanding organization of large-scale brain connectivity. Prior studies consistently demonstrate that the brain is structured around two fundamental connectivity gradients: one spanning sensorimotor to visual areas, and another going from primary areas to transmodal regions. These gradients are detectable at birth, indicating that the scaffold for large-scale organization is established prenatally and refined postnatally. Developmental studies further demonstrate that the sensorimotor-to-visual gradient is dominant in newborns and children. However, the normative development of connectivity gradients across the first year of life remains unknown.

The developing brain is also sensitive to environmental exposures including prenatal substance exposure (PSE), which can disrupt the developing connectome with cascading effects on cognition and behavior. Although PSE is related to atypical brain connectivity at birth, no studies have examined how PSE may alter development of connectivity gradients. Moreover, animal models show that PSE timing and magnitude differentially shape outcomes, but human fMRI studies typically rely on binary exposure classifications, limiting the ability to detect nuanced effects.

 Here, we aim to map longitudinal development of connectivity gradients across the first year of life, examine how PSE impacts gradient growth trajectories, and identify differential effects related to exposure timing and magnitude. Characterizing these normative and altered developmental pathways will advance our understanding of neural mechanisms that drive functional connectome maturation. Identifying how prenatal exposures shape gradient development is critical for identifying sensitive periods of vulnerability and resilience, ultimately informing early intervention strategies that optimize developmental outcomes.

Methods

Participants included substance-free controls (CTR; n=43) and infants with prenatal substance exposure (PSE; n=132). A comprehensive review of self-report, urine toxicology screen, and medical records was conducted to determine PSE classification. Categorical data on the timing and magnitude of PSE by trimester (indexed by the Timeline Follow Back, TLFB) were available for a subset of participants. PSE infants were then separated into a low-magnitude subgroup (PSE-L; n=34) with shorter exposure to fewer substances during only the first and/or second trimester, and high-magnitude subgroup (PSE-H; n=76) with sustained exposure to more substances across all three trimesters. Resting-state fMRI scans were acquired during natural sleep at 2 weeks, 3 months, 6 months, and 12 months. All groups were matched on sex, race, and gestational age at birth, rsfMRI motion parameters (residual framewise displacement), and scan length. Birthweight, maternal education, neighborhood adversity, and maternal depression were included as covariates of no interest in all analyses to control for group differences in these variables.  

Connectome gradient analysis: We computed macroscale connectome gradients for each participant using BrainSpace.1 For each subject, we calculated a voxelwise functional connectivity matrix by extracting the residual timeseries for each voxel (n=13,819) and correlating that with every other voxel (i.e., pairwise correlations between all voxels were calculated to form a 13,819 x 13,819 connectivity matrix for each subject). Correlation values were Fisher-Z transformed for subsequent statistical analyses. Diffusion map embedding, a nonlinear manifold learning approach, was applied to identify principal gradient components accounting for connectome variance in descending order. Briefly, the algorithm estimates a low-dimensional embedding using a high-dimensional connectome matrix; in this space, cortical nodes that are strongly interconnected by either many connections or a few very strong connections are positioned closer together, whereas nodes with little or no interconnectivity are positioned farther apart. We set alpha, a parameter that controls the influence of sampling density, to the recommended value of 0.5 (which retains global relations between plotted data points). We estimated the group-level gradient component template, which is generated from an average of all subjects, and performed Procrustes rotation to align components of each subject to the template. After assessing the variance explained by the generated gradients, we selected the first two gradients (accounting for the majority of the variance) for further analyses. These gradients were projected onto a cortical mesh using nilearn’s plotting module. Within each group, global measures of gradient range and gradient variance were calculated. Within-group, between-timepoint comparisons were conducted using Welch’s t-test. At each timepoint, within-network gradient scores were calculated within seven canonical resting-state networks2 (visual, sensorimotor, dorsal attention, ventral attention, limbic, frontoparietal, default mode networks) as well as a subcortical network. 

Nonlinear longitudinal analysis: Multivariate sparse functional principal components analysis (mSFPCA)3 was used to estimate nonlinear individual smooth trajectories to characterize longitudinal growth of gradient scores for the primary and secondary gradient. Next, linear mixed-effect models were conducted to detect significant between-group differences in the resulting functional principal components (FPCs) for each network. We first examined whether there were effects associated with PSE (i.e., CTR vs PSE). We then examined whether there were differential effects associated with PSE timing and magnitude (i.e., CTR vs PSE-L vs PSE-H). Permutation testing with random group assignments (n=100,000 permutations) was conducted for each regression model to assess significance.

Results

Sensorimotor-to-visual gradient dominates across the first year: We first mapped normative connectome gradients in substance-free controls (Figure 1a). At all four timepoints, the primary gradient showed a gradual axis defined by the sensorimotor network at one end and the visual network at the other (sensorimotor-to-visual gradient; Figure 1b, top), and the secondary gradient extended from primary regions to transmodal areas (primary-to-transmodal gradient; Figure 1b, bottom). Since these accounted for the majority of connectome variance, we focused on the development of these two gradients. 

Dynamic gradient development across the first year: To assess global changes in each gradient, we calculated the gradient range (i.e., absolute difference between positive and negative extremes of the gradient axis) and gradient variance (i.e., variance of the gradient axis). From 2 weeks to 3 months, the primary gradient showed significant increases in gradient range and gradient variance that was maintained through 6 months (Figure 1c). The secondary gradient showed a similar qualitative pattern, though effects were trending. Network-based profiles validated the organizational axis observed in each gradient (Figure 1d). For the primary gradient, the visual and sensorimotor networks occupied the extremes (i.e., highest and lowest primary gradient scores, respectively), with an expansion of gradient scores from 2 weeks to 3 months that was maintained through 6 months, followed by a reduction by 12 months. The secondary gradient showed sensorimotor and default mode networks at the extremes, with a similar developmental pattern. Nonlinear longitudinal growth trajectories revealed dynamic changes in the primary gradient across the first year of life, but flatter slopes for the secondary gradient (Figure 1e).  

Prenatal substance exposure alters macroscale gradient development: In PSE, the sensorimotor-to-visual gradient was also dominant across the first year, with the primary-to-transmodal gradient serving as the secondary gradient. However, primary gradient scores were suppressed at 3 months, with both extremes (sensory and visual networks) contracted relative to the control range. Global gradient measures followed a similar developmental pattern in PSE as that observed in CTR (Figure 2a). For PSE infants, the primary gradient showed dramatic growth of gradient range and gradient variance from 2 weeks to 3 months that was maintained through 6 months, followed by a significant decrease by 12 months (Figure 2a, left). A similar pattern was observed in both PSE-L (Figure 2a, middle) and PSE-H subgroups (Figure 2a, right). For PSE-L infants, no significant differences were observed in gradient range and gradient variance of the secondary gradient, but PSE-H infants showed a peak at 6 months followed by a significant decline by 12 months. Lastly, we examined how PSE impacts growth trajectories of these connectivity gradients. Compared with CTR, PSE infants showed divergent primary gradient growth trajectories in the limbic and default mode networks, with trending effects in the sensorimotor and dorsal attention networks (Figure 2b). When we split the PSE infants into two timing-based subgroups, we found differential effects on primary gradient growth trajectories localized to the sensorimotor, dorsal attention, limbic, and subcortical networks (Figure 2c). In the sensorimotor network, PSE-L showed normalized growth trajectories with no significant differences compared with CTR, whereas PSE-H showed significant differences versus CTR. For the dorsal attention network, PSE-L showed intermediary effects compared to both CTR and PSE-H. PSE-L showed significant differences compared with CTR in the limbic and subcortical networks. We did not observe any difference between PSE and CTR or between PSE subgroups for the secondary gradient, indicating that PSE effects are specific to the primary gradient.

Conclusion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to map the development of connectivity gradients across the first year of life and to demonstrate how PSE shapes early growth of the functional connectome, revealing potential mechanisms underlying cognitive development. In substance-free infants, the sensorimotor-to-visual gradient was dominant, as evidenced by the maximum separation of sensorimotor and visual regions along the first gradient axis. This replicates previous findings in newborns4 and children5 and extends prior work showing the emergence of this gradient during the late preterm period.4 Our results indicate that the primary sensorimotor-to-visual gradient experiences dramatic postnatal development from 2 weeks to 3 months, followed by consolidation and refinement through 12 months. This rapid early expansion and subsequent contraction of gradient extremes may reflect synaptic pruning and other maturational processes that actively shape large-scale functional organization in the first year. The secondary primary-to-transmodal gradient displayed limited growth across early infancy, consistent with hierarchical maturation patterns in which primary regions mature earlier and higher-order regions develop more gradually across the first few years of life. 

Although the sensorimotor-to-visual gradient remained dominant in PSE infants, development of this primary gradient was significantly altered, with differential effects associated with the timing and magnitude of PSE. Divergent gradient growth trajectories were localized to networks that support attention, limbic function, and subcortical processing, providing potential mechanistic links to later deficits in cognition, attention, and emotion regulation. Notably, these effects were specific to the primary gradient, indicating that PSE differentially impacts sensorimotor-to-visual organization across the first year. 

Together, these findings point to critical windows in the first postnatal year when the developing connectome is both rapidly maturing and sensitive to cascading effects from prenatal environmental exposures. In addition to providing a framework for understanding how early neurodevelopmental disruptions may result in later functional impairments, these results highlight opportunities for targeted intervention before the onset of observable symptoms to optimize long-term outcomes for affected children.
