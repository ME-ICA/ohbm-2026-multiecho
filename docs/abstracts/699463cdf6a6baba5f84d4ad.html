Introduction

The fetal period represents a critical window for brain development, with rapid neural development establishing essential neural circuits that underpin the key functions of the human brain .1 Resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) most recently has proved to open a measureless window on functional neurodevelopment in utero. Investigating the developmental characteristics of fetal brain activity and functional connectivity (FC) is crucial for understanding normal developmental trajectories and enabling early detection and prevention of potential disorders .2 However, previous studies have largely focused on investigating the FC characteristic, less is known about regional brain activity and FC in utero simultaneously. 

The amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation (ALFF) has been proven to be an effective and reliable parameter for evaluating regional intrinsic brain activity .3 Seed-based FC reveals functional interactions between specific regions (e.g., regions identified by ALFF) and the rest of the brain, allowing us to targetedly identify connectivity patterns of important brain regions in early development. These two indicators have been used in normally developing fetuses or infants, children, as well as those with various neurodevelopmental disorders .4-5 Thus, the aim of this study is to explore the intrinsic brain activity and connectivity developmental characteristics in normal fetuses.

Methods

Fetal rs-fMRI acquisitions were conducted on a 1.5 T MR system at West China Second University Hospital using a standardized EPI protocol. Key imaging parameters included: time repetition = 2000ms, time echo = 30ms, flip angle=75°, 3 mm slice thickness (no interslice gap), FOV = 224 mm× 224 mm, voxel size = 2.25 × 2.25 × 3.0 mm³, 60 volumes in each run and repeated four times.

We preprocessed the rs-fMRI data and performed nuisance regression, detrend and filter (0.01-0.08 Hz). Then, we computed ALFF within gray matter, standardized it using the global mean ALFF derived from a gray matter mask, and assessed its correlation with gestational age (controlled for maternal age) under a voxel-level threshold of puncorr < 0.001 and cluster-level FWE correction pcorr < 0.05.

Significantly correlated regions between ALFF and gestational age were defined as seeds for FC analysis. We calculated Pearson correlations between each seed’s mean time series and all gray matter voxels, applied Fisher’s Z-transformation, and evaluated correlations between FC maps and gestational age (maternal age as covariate) using the same statistical thresholds.

Results

This study finally included 82 fetal subjects. The fetal gestational age was 31.42 ± 2.71 weeks, and the maternal age was 29.93± 3.47 years.

We observed fetal gestational age positively correlated with ALFF in the bilateral ACC and negatively correlated with ALFF in bilateral thalamus (Figure 1A).

For the bilateral ACC seed, no significant correlations were found between ACC-based FC and fetal gestational age.

For the bilateral thalamus seed, we found that fetal gestational age was negatively correlated with FC between the bilateral thalamus and the left precuneus, superior parietal gyrus, and postcentral gyrus; the left cuneus and superior occipital gyrus); and the left supplementary motor area  (Figure 1B).

Conclusion

To our knowledge, this is the first study to simultaneously investigate the development trajectory of intrinsic brain activity and functional connectivity of fetuses. We found fetal gestational age positively correlated with ALFF in the bilateral ACC and negatively correlated with ALFF in the bilateral thalamus. In addition, FC between the bilateral thalamus and regions associated with the default mode, attention, sensorimotor, and visual networks decreased. This study discovered the developmental patterns of spontaneous brain activity and network connectivity in a normal fetal thalamus, underscoring the critical importance of thalamic development during the fetal period. 

Our findings provide substantial evidence for understanding typical fetal brain functional development and provide a theoretical basis for the early identification of developmental disorders and reveal their neural mechanisms.
