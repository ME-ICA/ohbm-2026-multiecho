Introduction

Value-based decision making involves the initial assignment of value to positive experiences and the retrieval of value at the time of decision. During hedonic experience, brain regions such as the ventromedial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum integrate sensory, emotional, and contextual information into a value signal.^1 Although much is known about where value is encoded in the brain, far less is known about how value representations evolve over time and how neural patterns formed during pleasurable experiences influence subsequent decisions. 

Understanding these dynamics is particularly important for disorders characterized by pleasure and motivation deficits, such as schizophrenia. Patients often report intact momentary pleasure but reduced motivation to pursue rewarding activities, suggesting a disruption in how value is represented rather than a deficit in hedonic experience itself.^2 Prior work has shown blunted striatal and prefrontal responses to reward, altered prediction-error signaling, and weaker coupling between reward-related signals and behavior.^3 Despite extensive work on reward anticipation and learning in schizophrenia, most studies rely on univariate signal changes and do not test whether the structure of value representations is disrupted. Likewise, prior paradigms rarely link hedonic experience directly to subsequent decisions, limiting insight into whether motivational impairments reflect failures of value formation, retrieval, or translation into choice

Here we developed a novel fMRI task that is a hybrid between conventional naturalistic viewing and task-based paradigms and collects 2 hours of fMRI per subject. This task links hedonic experience with economic decision-making to quantify the emergence and reinstatement of neural value patterns tracking willingness-to-pay. We aim to clarify the mechanisms through which value representations support motivated behavior, and how their disruption contributes to anhedonia in schizophrenia.

Methods

Participants: 27 healthy controls (n=6 female, aged 38±10) and 27 individuals with schizophrenia (n=7 female, aged 38±9) with moderate-to-severe negative symptoms were recruited (eligibility: score >3 for avolition and anhedonia/asociality on the Scale for the Assessment of the Negative Symptoms (SANS).

fMRI task : Participants performed the Movie Trailer Auction Game (Figure 1). On each trial participants watched a movie trailer and then reported how much they enjoyed watching the trailer (pleasure rating). They received a $5 endowment to bid on the movie advertised with high bids increasing the odds of winning a copy of the movie (willingness-to-pay, WTP), following the Becker-Degroot-Marschak (BDM)^5 auction procedure. Participants then had another chance to win the movie by making repeated key presses (willingness-to-work, WTW). There were 90 trials divided into two 70-minute scan sessions. Following each session, two trials were randomly selected for prize payout (1 for auction, 1 for effort).

Data collection: A clinical assessment including psychiatric diagnosis and severity of negative symptoms of schizophrenia was performed. Participants underwent MRI scanning using a Siemens Biograph mMR 3T PET/MRI located at The Royal Ottawa Mental Health Centre. For each participant, we acquired 10 fMRI runs using a gradient-echo EPI sequence (50 slices; TR = 1.6 s; TE = 26 ms; Voxel size = 2.5 x 2.5 x 2.75 mm3; multiband acceleration factor = 2; slices oriented 30° from the AC-PC axis) each lasting ~12 mins and consisting of 9 trials of the task (total duration of fMRI scanning was 2 hours per participant, divided over 2 sessions). Structural and distortion correction scans were also collected.

fMRI data analysis : Standard fMRI preprocessing was performed using Fmriprep:20.0.2. For each participant a support vector regression (SVR) was performed using Scikit-learn^5 relating brain activity to participant responses using a cross validation approach (K=5 folds). For this, the brain activity patterns during the trailer viewing were decoded to predict participant willingness-to-pay. This analysis was performed within value-encoding regions of ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) and Ventral Striatum (VSt).^1 Prediction accuracy was determined as the Fisher's Z-transformed correlation coefficient between the predicted bid responses from the model and the observed bid responses (WTP). The expression of the value (WTP) pattern was calculated using the dot product of weights yielded from the model and fMRI data for each time point throughout the whole experiment and then averaged across trials and participants to observe the dynamics of their emergence during trailer viewing and reinstatement during financial decision-making. Value representation was defined as the correlation of the WTP pattern expression to the bid amounts and this was calculated at each time point of the trial. This metric of value representation was correlated to anhedonia severity in schizophrenia.

To examine the value representation outside of the canonical value regions a brain-wide searchlight analysis (sphere radius=10 mm, SVR with k=5 folds) during trailer viewing was performed to predict WTP.

Results

In the healthy group, participants bid on average $1.75±0.26 for movies and the average correlation between pleasure ratings and WTP was r=0.77±0.13. Brain activity patterns in canonical brain value regions during trailer viewing predicted WTP during the subsequent auction procedure (SVR prediction accuracy, t24=4.70, p=0.0001, permutation test) (Figure 2A). Even though these WTP patterns were trained on the trailer period, they returned during the auction period (t156=4.93, p=0.000002, linear mixed effects model correlating WTP to WTP patternauction) (Figure 2B). 

The schizophrenia group showed similar pleasure ratings (t52=0.43, p=0.67) and agreement between pleasure and WTP (t52=0.31, p=0.76) as the control group, but WTP was higher in patients (t52=2.29, p=0.026, 2-sample t-tests). Brain activity in schizophrenia patients was less predictive of WTP during trailer viewing compared to controls (t50=2.94, p=0.0051, 2-sample t-test on SVR prediction accuracy) (Figure 2A) and WTP patterns were retrieved less effectively during the auction period (t304=-2.08, p=0.038, group*bid value interaction in linear mixed effects model predicting WTP patternauction; Figure 2B). Impaired value representation (correlation of WTP to WTP patternauction) during this period was related to worse symptoms of anhedonia (SANS Anhedonia global) in the schizophrenia group (r=-0.41, p=0.036; Figure 2C). 

Given that value representation appeared to be impaired in canonical value regions in schizophrenia, we also performed a searchlight analysis throughout the whole brain to examine if value representation was intact or impaired in other regions in schizophrenia. Figure 2D presents the results of a whole-brain searchlight analysis decoding WTP from brain activity during trailer viewing. Predictive maps in the healthy control group (red overlay) suggest that value-related patterns are distributed across the brain. A minority of these value regions show deficient value representation in schizophrenia (pink overlay; two-sample t-test), while many other regions may be intact in schizophrenia. 

Finally, to validate the relevance of our value representation metric to an external standard, we tested whether WTP patterns predicted the financial success of the movies advertised. Across trailers, the average neural WTP patternauction in the control group significantly correlated with gross box office revenue (inflation adjusted, t86=2.44, p=0.017, linear regression controlling for production budget and release year). This prediction was numerically stronger than that of any of the behavioral metrics from the experiment (e.g. pleasure ratings, Figure 2E). 

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that value representation occurs during hedonic experience, it resembles that seen during subsequent financial decisions, and can predict these decisions. Schizophrenia patients showed impaired formation and retrieval of value patterns and this impairment correlated to the severity of their anhedonia, consistent with evidence of a value representation deficit in schizophrenia. Our experimental paradigm may help elucidate the link between hedonic experience and reward seeking, for instance in the context of advertising and psychopathology.
