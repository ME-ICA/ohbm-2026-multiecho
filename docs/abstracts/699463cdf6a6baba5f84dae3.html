Introduction

Mood symptoms fluctuate over short timescales, yet clinical monitoring is typically intermittent and subjective. Neural power spectra exhibit broadband power-law structure in intracranial field potentials,1 which can be parameterized into oscillatory peaks and a broadband 1/f-like component.2 The power-law exponent (slope) is widely studied as an interpretable marker of brain state and has been proposed as an indirect indicator of excitation–inhibition balance in humans.3 In patients with treatment-resistant depression (TRD) undergoing deep brain stimulation (DBS), a 1/f-like slope in the ventromedial prefrontal cortex (vmPFC) reliably tracked changes in depressive symptom severity, motivating its use as a candidate biomarker of mood state.4 Here, we test whether this relationship generalizes beyond TRD and whether nodes in an affective salience network (ASN) that includes vmPFC, insula, and amygdala show dissociable coupling to composite mood versus affective valence, using a band-limited slope measure (20–45 Hz) selected to minimize low-frequency contamination while remaining below the 60 Hz line-noise region and to align with prior biomarker work.

Methods

Participants completed two validated mood measures near resting-state stereo-EEG (sEEG) recordings collected during clinically indicated monitoring: the Computerized Adaptive Test for Depression Inventory (CAT-DI) and the Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS).5 Analyses included subjects with adequate ROI coverage; therefore, the number of contributing subjects varied by ROI (13 subjects for vmPFC×PANAS Ratio; 10 for vmPFC×CAT-DI; 9 for insula×PANAS Positive; 8 for amygdala×PANAS Negative). Rest data were downsampled to 500 Hz, notch-filtered, and common-average referenced. Depth contacts were localized and assigned to clinically predefined ROIs. Power spectral density was estimated using Welch’s method (1s Hamming windows, 50% overlap). Band-limited power-law slope was quantified by a log–log linear fit over 20–45 Hz, selected a priori to align with the motivating TRD biomarker framework and reduce contamination from prominent low-frequency rhythms while remaining below the 60 Hz line-noise region. Slopes were averaged across contacts within each ROI to yield one value per run per ROI. Run-level Pearson correlations tested associations between ROI slope and CAT-DI, PANAS Positive, PANAS Negative, and PANAS Ratio. To address within-subject clustering (repeated runs per subject) and multiple testing, PANAS associations additionally included sensitivity tests using linear mixed-effects models with subject as a random intercept, Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) correction across ROI×outcome tests, and leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) robustness analyses. CAT-DI runs were linked to the session CATDI score (constant within session), so multiple runs could share the same CAT-DI value;  primary CAT-Di associations were testing using run-level Pearson correlations.

Results

vmPFC slope tracked both depression severity (CAT-DI) and affective balance (PANAS Ratio). Flatter slopes were associated with higher PANAS Ratio (r = 0.46, p = 0.0005; N = 54 runs across 13 subjects), whereas steeper slopes were associated with higher CAT-DI (r = −0.55, p = 0.0009; N = 33 runs across 10 subjects), supporting generalization of a TRD-derived vmPFC biomarker to a non-TRD cohort.4 Valence dissociated across network nodes: in insula, steeper slopes were associated with higher PANAS Positive (r = −0.42, p = 0.0050; N = 43 runs across 9 subjects), whereas in amygdala, flatter slopes were associated with higher PANAS Negative (r = 0.35, p = 0.018; N = 44 runs across 8 subjects). Other tested ROIs did not show significant associations (all p > 0.05). Sensitivity analyses confirmed the stability of run-level PANAS correlations under LOSO and their significance after FDR correction. However, vmPFC and insula effects weakened and did not remain significant in mixed-effects models that accounted for within-subject clustering. In contrast, the amygdala–negative affect association remained significant after FDR correction in mixed-effects analyses, providing the most robust evidence for a within-subject–accounted relationship at this stage.

Conclusion

Band-limited power-law slope (20–45 Hz) from brief resting sEEG recordings provides a low-burden, interpretable marker of affective state with network-specific meaning. vmPFC slope tracked composite mood across CAT-DI and PANAS, while insula and amygdala showed valence-selective relationships. Panas run-level effects were robust to LOSO and FDR control, but mixed-effects modeling highlighted amygdala as the most robust ROI after accounting for repeated runs. These findings extend a TRD-inspired vmPFC biomarker to a distinct population and broaden mood mapping across salience–limbic circuitry, potentially informing closed-loop neuromodulation strategies for mood disorders. Next, we will epoch neural data around individual PANAS adjectives and apply Morlet wavelet time–frequency analyses to test trial-level dynamics and links to affective arousal descriptors.
