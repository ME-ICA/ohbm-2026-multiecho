Introduction

Using graph theory, the brain is represented through nodes (anatomical brain regions) and edges (connections between nodes), creating a functional connectome to study the connections between different regions1.  After parcellating data to reduce dimensionality, we can use centrality measures to understand a nodes significance through measures of interaction strength, integrative capacity, and impact on network stability2. Nodes that demonstrate high centrality measures within a brain connectome are hubs, key candidates of investigation which are crucial for long-range and inter-network communication3.  While some studies have questioned both the concordance between centrality measures4 and the resulting effect of parcellation5, there has been no consensus regarding their formulation or optimal spatial scale. We propose to address this important question, when comparing networks of patients with temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE) and healthy controls, for whom disruption of key hub regions is expected to be found in patients.

Methods

53 TLE patients and 126 healthy controls underwent 3T resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging. Data was parcellated according to four different parcellation schemes (100, 300, 600, 900 Schaefer parcellations).  The patient rs-fMRI timeseries was z-scored relative to controls, smoothed with a 10mm kernel along the cortical surface, and sorted according to lateralization of the epilepsy focus. Connectivity matrices were computed by computing the Pearson’s Correlation between every node and were subsequently thresholded at 10% for all participants. The functional connectomes were then analyzed using the Brain Connectivity Toolbox2. We computed centrality measures at a nodal level for strength (degree centrality, eigenvector centrality) and efficiency (betweenness centrality, participation coefficient) metrics at each parcellation scheme. Cohen’s d indexed the TLE-vs-control differences for each node in the parcellation, and significant (positive or negative) differences were mapped back at the vertex level for each parcellation. To assess the consistency of measures at the parcel level, the Dice coefficient was used to compute the cortex-wide spatial overlap occurring between positive and negative Cohen’s d parcels. Finally, we used Pearson’s correlation to assess the inter-parcellation consistency between Cohen’s d values for each metric projected back at a vertex level.

Results

Positive and negative effects were evenly distributed when comparing TLE and controls, indicating large-scale network reorganization, but no clear significant decrease in centrality measures were found in the ipsilateral temporal regions across measures. When assessing the consistency between metrics at a parcel level, strength nodes displayed more consistent patterns (average spatial correlation <ρ>= 0.88) across the four parcellations than efficiency nodes (average spatial correlation <ρ>=0.58).  At a vertex level, the 100-node parcellation was the least correlated with other parcellations across both strength and efficiency nodes. Strength and efficiency nodes were strongly coupled within each parcellation (<ρ> = 0.73), with the 300 and 600 parcellations showing the strongest between-parcellation coupling across metrics. When computing the Dice spatial overlaps between metrics within the same parcellation, nodes exhibiting a positive effect size (Cohen’s d > 0.2) demonstrated higher Dice overlap (max Dice = 0.85, 100 and 900 strength parcellation) than the negative ones (Cohen’s d < -0.2, max Dice = 0.73, 600 strength parcellation).

Conclusion

TLE is characterized by large-scale hub reorganization relative to healthy controls, with important variations across hub metrics and scales, but we did not retrieve the expected results of significant centrality decrease in pathological regions. Overall, strength nodes are more consistently reorganized than efficiency nodes. Stronger Dice coefficients for positive Cohen’s d indicate more reliability in regions with increased connectivity in epilepsy. These findings highlight the importance of parcellation schemes and hub metric selection when interpreting hubs in TLE.
