Introduction

Although major depressive disorder (MDD) and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) frequently co-occur and share overlapping symptoms such as sleep disturbances, poor concentration, and affective dysregulation1,2, neuroimaging studies suggest that each disorder is associated with distinct and heterogeneous alterations in white matter microstructure3,4. Specifically, previous diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) studies have consistently reported reduced white matter integrity in MDD, particularly in the corpus callosum, corona radiata, and superior longitudinal fasciculus, whereas findings in PTSD have been more variable3, with reports of both increased and decreased fractional anisotropy (FA) in different tracts. The high comorbidity between MDD and PTSD2, coupled with overlapping symptomatology, raises the question of whether these disorders interact additively in the brain or whether comorbid PTSD represents a neurobiologically distinct condition with unique structural features. The present study aimed to investigate this question by comparing white matter integrity across three groups: patients with MDD, patients with MDD and comorbid PTSD (MDD+PTSD), and healthy controls (HC), with the goal of elucidating structural differences specifically associated with PTSD comorbidity.

Methods

Data from 1,591 participants of the Marburg-Münster Affective Disorders Cohort Study (MACS) were included, comprising 999 healthy controls, 521 patients with MDD, and 71 patients with comorbid MDD+PTSD. Diffusion tensor imaging data were processed using Tract-Based Spatial Statistics (TBSS) to examine voxel-wise differences in FA across major white matter tracts. Group differences were assessed using non-parametric permutation tests within a generalized linear model controlling for age, sex, total intracranial volume, and scanner site, with statistical significance set at p < .05, corrected for multiple comparisons using threshold-free cluster enhancement and family-wise error correction. Additional analyses were conducted to control for potential confounding variables, including depressive symptom severity, hospitalization history, medication load, and comorbid anxiety.

Results

The results revealed a significant main effect of diagnostic group on FA, primarily localized in the corpus callosum, corona radiata, and superior longitudinal fasciculus. Post-hoc comparisons indicated that FA was significantly lower in MDD patients compared to healthy controls, whereas patients with MDD+PTSD exhibited higher FA than MDD alone and did not differ from healthy controls. These effects remained robust after exclusion of other anxiety comorbidities, matching for depressive symptom severity, and controlling for clinical covariates, suggesting that the observed preservation of white matter integrity was specifically associated with PTSD comorbidity rather than other clinical factors.

Conclusion

In conclusion, these findings indicate that comorbid PTSD moderates the white matter alterations typically observed in MDD, resulting in comparatively preserved fiber integrity. This pattern suggests that MDD with comorbid PTSD may constitute a neurobiologically distinct subtype rather than a simple additive manifestation of both disorders. Understanding these structural differences may help refine diagnostic conceptualizations and inform targeted interventions for individuals with this comorbid presentation.
