Introduction

White Matter Hyperintensities (WMH) of vascular origin are brain lesions visible on FLAIR or T2-weighted magnetic resonance imaging. Given their strong association with age, WMH’s their role in cognitive decline, increased risk of dementia, and stroke is well established^1.

Among white matter tracts, the Corpus Callosum (CC) is the largest interhemispheric commissure and shows high propensity for WMH-related damage. Previous studies have shown that regions affected by WMH are characterized by fractional anisotropy and fiber density reductions, particularly in the anterior and posterior portions of the CC^2.

Here, we sought to investigate the impact of WMHs on fiber orientation peaks within CC projections, as derived from fiber orientation distributions (FOD)^3. 

Methods

The dataset comprised 300 subjects (ages 22-78) acquired as part of a longitudinal study in the community-dwelling adult population. White Matter (WM) and WMH masks were derived from FLAIR images (1 mm isotropic). Multi-shell diffusion MRI was acquired using a 4-shell protocol (b-values: 0, 650, 1000, 2000 s/mm²) at 2 mm isotropic resolution and preprocessed using MRtrix3 and FSL tools. Fiber orientation distributions (FODs) were estimated using multi-shell multi-tissue Constrained Spherical Deconvolution (CSD), extracting the three principal peaks per voxel. Each orientation peak represents a three-dimensional vector with spatial components describing the principal fiber direction at that voxel, thus yielding nine values per voxel: three vector components for each of the three principal peaks. This representation enables the capture of complex fiber configurations, including crossings and branching patterns.

To enable voxel-wise analysis across subjects, a population template was constructed from WM FODs using MRtrix^4. First, whole-brain masks were generated for each subject with the dwi2mask command, based on diffusion MRI gradient information. A random subset of 150 subjects, representative of the full age range, was then selected to create the population template, excluding those with extreme WMH volumes, i.e., outliers based on the interquartile range, to avoid bias.

Subjects not included in the template construction were registered individually using mrregister, which computed non-linear transformation fields (warps)  to transform any image from the native space to the common template space. These transformations were subsequently applied to each subject’s peak data.

TractSeg^5, a deep learning based method, was used to automatically segment the masks of the corpus callosum and its seven anatomical subsegments in each subject’s native space. Whole-brain WM and WMH masks were resampled from 1 mm isotropic to 2 mm isotropic to match the spatial resolution of the diffusion data. Both the segmented tracts of the CC and the WM and WMH masks were transformed to the common template space using the warps generated during registration to the population template.

Only the WM portion of each tract was selected using its corresponding mask to perform the initial filtering, excluding Gray Matter (GM) to minimize partial volume effects. Subsequently, the overlap with the WMH mask was computed to estimate the affected volume and the percentage of tract involvement.

Given that corpus callosum subsegments exhibit variable anatomical volumes—rostrum (7.8 cm³), genu (66.2 cm³), rostral body (17.4 cm³), anterior midbody (27.2 cm³), posterior midbody (25.4 cm³), isthmus (79.9 cm³), and splenium (25.2 cm³)—WMH burden was quantified as a percentage of each subsegment's volume to normalize for size differences and enable direct comparisons across regions (Figure 1).

From these binary masks, all values of the three main peaks were extracted for WMH and NAWM voxels within each tract. Once voxels within each tract were classified as NAWM or WMH, an exploratory analysis of magnitude was performed by computing the mean magnitude within each region. Additionally, local angular coherence was assessed by calculating the mean angular dispersion between each voxel and its six immediate neighbors for each of the three principal orientation peaks. High angular dispersion values indicate greater local fiber disorganization and loss of directional continuity between adjacent voxels.

For group-level analysis, mean magnitude and angular coherence values were computed for NAWM and WMH across all subjects, along with their corresponding standard deviations, to assess inter-individual variability. These values were analyzed both for each corpus callosum subsegment individually and for the entire tract, and visualized through plots showing the distribution of values and the comparison between regions as a function of age and lesion burden.

Results

WMH lesions were present in the CC tracts of 138 out of 300 subjects, with estimated volumes between 0.08 and 0.16 cm³. The volume was estimated based on voxel counts, taking into account the spatial resolution of the data.

Based on these observations, the subsegments with the highest WMH frequency were the genu, isthmus, and splenium (Figure 1.b). This pattern was consistent when considering only subjects with WMH volumes exceeding 0.1 cm³ per subsegment.

Mean magnitudes of the three peaks were compared between native and template spaces for both NAWM and WMH regions. Peak 1 magnitudes showed minimal changes after registration in both NAWM (native: 0.45, template: 0.38) and WMH (native: 0.47, template: 0.45). Peak 2 exhibited similar stability, with NAWM decreasing slightly from 0.16 to 0.13 and WMH remaining nearly unchanged (native: 0.13, template: 0.13). Peak 3 magnitudes, which were substantially lower overall, also showed modest changes across spaces, NAWM (native:  0.054, template: 0.046) and WMH (native: 0.04, template: 0.05). Magnitude values progressively decreased from Peak 1 to Peak 3, consistent with the expected hierarchy of principal fiber orientations. Figure 2.a and 2.b illustrate these magnitude comparisons as well as standard deviation differences between native and template spaces for both NAWM and WMH regions.

In the template aligned data, all three peaks across CC subsegments exhibited a clear and consistent trend in NAWM mean magnitudes across subjects, in marked contrast to the behavior observed in WMH. This effect was preserved regardless of WMH presence within the tract and independent of subject age, although mean magnitude values progressively decreased from the first to the third peak.

Notably, the rostrum and rostral body tracts, corresponding to the smallest subsegments, exhibited substantially greater dispersion in third-peak magnitudes, resembling the pattern observed in WMH. 

To assess the spatial extent of WMH effects, the analysis of mean magnitudes in voxels neighboring WMH (up to three voxels away) revealed values systematically intermediate between those observed in WMH and NAWM. This pattern suggests the presence of a local gradient in microstructural properties surrounding the lesions.

Regarding directional coherence using angular dispersion revealed that the isthmus and splenium, which presented the highest WMH burden, showed similar mean angular coherence values between NAWM and WMH regions. However, a substantial difference in variability was observed. For example, in the isthmus tract, the mean and standard deviation of the angular coherence of the first peak was 29.4° ± 2.3° in NAWM (n=300 subjects), while it was 30.3° ± 10.4° in WMH (n=85 subjects with detectable WMH). This pattern of greater dispersion in WMH was consistently observed across all tracts, where the standard deviation of angular coherence in NAWM ranged between 1.5° and 2.3°, in contrast with values between 9.0° and 11.7° in WMH (Figure 2.c). 

Conclusion

Non-linear registration to a common space adequately preserved the microstructural characteristics of WMH, with minimal volume changes. In cases where registered volume within the tract varied, WMH were located predominantly in peripheral regions, where variations are attributable to minor effects inherent to the spatial normalization process. Principal peak magnitudes showed no substantial differences between native and template spaces (Δ < 0.07), confirming preservation of microstructural values.

The main finding of this study is the marked microstructural heterogeneity observed within WMH. Although mean angular coherence values were similar between WMH and NAWM, variability was consistently higher in lesions (standard deviation: 9.0-11.7° in WMH vs. 1.5-2.3° in NAWM). This high dispersion indicates that WMH do not constitute a homogeneous entity, but rather present different degrees of microstructural alteration across subjects and within lesions themselves. This variability pattern contrasts with the remarkable consistency observed in NAWM, where angular coherence values showed a narrow distribution across all 300 subjects, regardless of the tract analyzed, suggesting that the directional microstructure of NAWM remains preserved even in the presence of nearby lesions.

Analysis of magnitudes in voxels neighboring WMH revealed intermediate values between NAWM and WMH, suggesting the existence of a microstructural damage gradient extending beyond the visible boundaries of lesions on FLAIR. This finding has important implications for understanding the true extent of WM damage and could explain cognitive deficits that do not fully correlate with lesion volume.

These findings open several avenues for future research. Now that we have established that WMH exhibit substantial microstructural heterogeneity rather than uniform pathology, this variability can be leveraged for clinical prognostication. Longitudinal follow-up could reveal whether high angular dispersion serves as an early biomarker of white matter vulnerability, predicting accelerated WMH progression or cognitive decline before substantial lesion expansion occurs. Moreover, investigating the relationship between FOD-derived metrics and tract-specific cognitive functions could clarify the functional consequences of microstructural heterogeneity within WMH, potentially explaining individual differences in clinical presentation despite similar lesion volumes.
