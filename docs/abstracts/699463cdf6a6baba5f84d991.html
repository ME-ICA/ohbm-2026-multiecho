Introduction

Essential tremor is the most common adult movement disorder and is increasingly recognized as a disorder of distributed brain networks rather than a focal pathology. Abnormal interactions within cerebellothalamocortical circuits are thought to underlie tremor generation, and neuromodulatory interventions targeting this system have proven highly effective for symptom control. MR-guided focused ultrasound thalamotomy of the ventral intermediate nucleus offers durable tremor reduction without implanted hardware, yet a subset of patients experience imbalance and sensory disturbances that can persist beyond the acute postoperative period. These adverse outcomes represent a major clinical concern, as they may limit functional benefit despite successful tremor suppression.

From a neuroscience perspective, postural and sensory side effects following focal thalamic lesions provide a unique opportunity to probe how disruption of a circumscribed node within the cerebellothalamic system influences large-scale brain networks. Lesion network mapping offers a framework to interpret focal interventions in terms of their distributed functional connectivity, enabling inferences about the network architecture supporting motor coordination, sensory integration, and balance. Understanding these network-level effects is particularly relevant for essential tremor, where cerebellar, thalamic, and cortical systems interact dynamically to support movement and posture.

In this work, we apply a lesion network mapping framework to examine the brain networks associated with imbalance and sensory symptoms following MR-guided focused ultrasound thalamotomy in a large essential tremor cohort. By linking clinical outcomes to distributed functional network organization, this study aims to advance a systems-level understanding of focused ultrasound side effects and to inform future efforts to improve precision, safety, and circuit-level targeting in noninvasive neuromodulation.

Methods

Participants and Clinical Data

We analyzed a large cohort of patients with essential tremor treated with unilateral MR-guided focused ultrasound thalamotomy at Brigham and Women’s Hospital. By December 2025, the cohort comprised approximately 711 patients with available clinical follow-up. Tremor outcomes and adverse effects were assessed longitudinally using structured clinical evaluations, with data collected predominantly via telehealth. Tremor severity was measured using the Fahn–Tolosa–Marin Clinical Rating Scale for Tremor. Adverse effects, including imbalance and sensory symptoms, were assessed using a standardized questionnaire with binary (yes/no) responses. Post-operative assessments were performed at 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and annually up to 5 years.

Lesion Segmentation and Spatial Processing

Post-procedural MRI scans were used to segment thalamic lesions for all available patients. Lesions were spatially normalized to standard space and, when necessary, flipped to the left hemisphere to ensure consistent anatomical alignment across subjects. This approach enabled group-level analyses while preserving the relative spatial organization of lesion locations within the cerebellothalamic system.

Lesion Network Mapping

Lesion network mapping was used to identify distributed brain networks associated with post-operative imbalance and sensory symptoms. For each lesion, whole-brain functional connectivity maps were generated using a normative resting-state connectome parcellated with the Yeo 1000 atlas. Individual lesion connectivity maps were transformed to z-scores and averaged across subjects to derive group-level connectivity profiles.

Statistical Analysis

Associations between lesion-derived functional connectivity and clinical outcomes were assessed using permutation-based general linear models implemented with Permutation Analysis of Linear Models (PALM) in FSL. Side effects at 3 months post-procedure served as the primary variables of interest. Continuous covariates were mean-centered, and categorical variables were demeaned. Models included age, sex, lesion volume, and lesion laterality as covariates. Statistical significance was evaluated using 10,000 permutations with Threshold-Free Cluster Enhancement and corrected for multiple comparisons using false discovery rate and family-wise error correction.

Results

A total of 711 patients were included, with 69% receiving left-sided lesions and the remainder right-sided lesions. Tremor control at 3 months was achieved in 91% of patients. The most common adverse effect was imbalance (35%), followed by fatigue (20%), sensory symptoms (20%), dysmetria (19%), and weakness (7%). Among all adverse effects examined, only imbalance and sensory symptoms demonstrated significant network-level associations after correction for multiple comparisons using both false discovery rate and family-wise error correction.

Imbalance was significantly associated with functional connectivity to cerebellar regions involved in motor and action-related processing, including lobules I–IV, V, and the inferior cerebellar peduncle. Increased imbalance was also associated with connectivity to regions within the default mode network, including medial temporal and hippocampal areas. In contrast, reduced imbalance was associated with connectivity to cerebellar lobules VIIIa, VIIIb, and VIIb, as well as cortical regions implicated in supplementary motor control and visuomotor integration. Notably, the M4 functional area1 of the cerebellum within lobules I–IV showed an anticorrelated relationship with the M4 functional area in lobule VIIIb, suggesting opposing roles of distinct cerebellar motor representations in postural stability.

Lesions associated with sensory disturbances demonstrated significant functional connectivity to somatosensory-related thalamocortical networks. These effects survived stringent multiple-comparison correction and were consistent with established sensory processing pathways. Compared to imbalance, sensory symptoms exhibited more spatially circumscribed network involvement and weaker associations with higher-order cortical networks.

Conclusion

These findings suggest that post-operative imbalance and sensory symptoms following MR-guided focused ultrasound thalamotomy are associated with distinct patterns of cerebellothalamic and large-scale functional network connectivity. In particular, imbalance appears to relate to differential engagement of cerebellar motor subregions and higher-order networks implicated in postural control, whereas sensory symptoms show more circumscribed associations with somatosensory pathways. Together, these results support the view that adverse outcomes following focal thalamic intervention may reflect distributed network effects rather than lesion location alone.

Future work will focus on integrating functional findings with structural connectivity to further clarify underlying circuit mechanisms. Independent replication, longitudinal validation, and assessment across alternative connectomes will be critical to determine the generalizability and robustness of these network associations. Ultimately, this line of investigation may contribute to more precise, network-informed approaches for risk stratification and target refinement in noninvasive neuromodulation.
