Introduction

Regular physical exercise (PE) increases peak oxygen uptake (VO₂peak), which is an index of cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF)1. Although PE has been linked to structural plasticity in the brain, the current evidence regarding PE-related changes in white matter (WM) metrics is still highly limited in cohorts of young adults compared to elderly cohorts2. Of the two PE interventions that have examined WM changes in young adult cohorts specifically, one reported significant increases in WM integrity in frontal lobe fibres after six months3, while the other reported increased WM integrity in the right corticospinal tract after nine weeks4, compared with the control group. Findings thus highlight a gap in understanding where, when and to what extent PE interventions influence WM organization.

Methods

Young individuals with sedentary life style were recruited and randomly assigned to an intervention group (IG; aerobic exercise training, 3 x per week; N = 26, age = 24.73 ± 4.40, and sex (M:F) = 7:19) or an active control group (CG; stretching and toning, 3 x per week; N = 26, age = 25.08 ± 4.42, and sex (M:F) = 7:19). The study design allowed to assess Vo2peak and diffusion measures (fractional anisotropy (FA), mean diffusivity (MD), axial diffusivity (AD), and radial diffusivity (RD)) at 3 time points (baseline (T0), 3 months (T3) and after 6 months (T6); see study design in figure1). Study was approved by ethics committee of University Hospital Bonn (146/21).

Diffusion MRI data were acquired on a 3T Elition Philips scanner (TR = 3.441 ms, TE = 89 ms, matrix = 112 x 112 mm2, slice thickness = 2mm, one b = 0 image, while rest was 30 b = 1000 and 2000 each). Additionally, an opposite phase encoded b = 0 image was acquired for distortion correction. Diffusion images were preprocessed to remove motion, susceptibility distortions and eddy current artefacts (https://github.com/MASILab/PreQual). Next, the four diffusion measures were extracted by implementing weighted least square fitting of the tensors. FA, MD, RD and AD maps were registered and transformed to the MNI template space using advanced diffeomorphic registration based on the ANTS-SyN algorithm (https://github.com/ANTsX/ANTs) in order to assess voxel-wise changes in response to the exercise intervention. A repeated measure ANOVA was applied to assess 2 (group) x time (3 timepoints) interactions for both VO2peak and the diffusion measures. Additionally, Pearson correlations were performed to examine the relationship between the changes in CRF and the changes in the diffusion measures.

Results

A significant group-by-time interaction effect was observed for VO₂peak (p < 0.001). Further post hoc comparisons suggested that VO₂peak increased only in the IG at T3 and T6, relative to T0 (see Figure 2a). No significant group-by-time interaction was observed for the diffusion metrics. However, MD, RD and AD showed significant changes over time (p < 0.001 at the voxel level; cluster-level p < 0.05, family-wise error corrected). Exploratory post hoc comparisons revealed that the effects of MD, AD and RD in the precentral and postcentral gyri were driven by significant increases (p < 0.001 at the voxel level, p < 0.05 family-wise error corrected) at T6 compared to T0, but only in the IG (See figure 2b, c, d). No significant associations were found between changes in either diffusion metric and increases in CRF.

Conclusion

Our study reports significant white matter changes in the precentral and postcentral gyri (leg representation) after regular PE in sedentary young adults, strengthening the preliminary evidence of increased WM integrity in the right corticospinal tract after nine weeks of PE in young adults reported previously2. A possible mechanism underlying these WM changes could be the structural reorganization in fiber tracts especially in leg area which were the focus of fitness training. Earlier exercise studies suggest that factors like Brain derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and myokines increase the cross talk between muscle and brain5. Hence, we can speculate that regular muscle contractions during exercise training might have triggered the fiber reorganizations leading to increase in diffusivity in brain.
