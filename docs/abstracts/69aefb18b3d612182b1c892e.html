Introduction

Brain tumours pose a significant challenge to the integrity of human brain networks, yet they simultaneously trigger adaptive neuroplastic responses (Cho et al., 2022; Duffau, 2021; Ellis et al., 2025; Martínez Lozada et al., 2025). In the current study, we aimed to track signs of adaptation in the global organisation of resting-state networks (RSNs) and their temporal evolution in a cohort of patients with left-hemisphere brain tumours, assessed both before and after surgical resection. In this pathological context, neuroplasticity is considered fundamental for preserving cognitive functions across the pre- and postoperative stages (Cargnelutti et al., 2020). A growing body of evidence suggests that resting-state functional connectivity (rs-FC), which reflects patterns of spontaneously synchronised neural activity, offers a powerful window into network-level adaptations (Sighinolfi et al., 2022). The use of rs-FC in clinical practice is a promising tool for assessing the organisation of large-scale brain systems in clinical populations, including patients with brain tumours (Moretto et al., 2023; Pasquini et al., 2022; Silvestri et al., 2022; Voets et al., 2019).

Methods

In the current study, we investigate how neuroplasticity extends beyond specific functions to influence the overall organisation of RSNs over time. Specifically, network-level alterations in rs-FC patterns in patients with left-hemisphere lesions, both before and after surgery using a group of healthy participants as a reference. To describe the evolution of these connectivity patterns in relation to cognition and whether they are compensatory, we combined two complementary approaches: a multivariate contrast of estimated ROI-to-ROI network changes controlling for demographic and clinical factors, and an analysis of global graph metrics, focusing on efficiency and segregation. Clinical variables and demographic data are included as moderators of the possible relationship between preoperative changes at the network level caused by the presence of the tumour and the cognitive progress of patients. Further understanding of long-term changes in connectivity patterns caused by the presence of brain tumours can help us move towards more personalised strategies that include resting-state data into clinical practice. 

Participants. This study included 72 patients with primary brain tumours without metastases and a cohort of 68 healthy participants used as a reference group. All patients were evaluated between one and three weeks after the episode that prompted their first consultation with the Neurosurgery Department at Cruces University Hospital (Bilbao, Spain). Both patients and controls underwent a combination of advanced functional and structural neuroimaging techniques, along with a comprehensive cognitive assessment covering different cognitive domains. From the initial patient group, those meeting the following inclusion criteria were selected: (1) Presence of a left-hemisphere lesion affecting one of the critical language nodes and (2) FLAIR imaging suggestive of low-grade glioma with diffuse lesion, without hyperintensities indicative of high-grade. Applying these criteria resulted in 35 patients (mean age = 45.34 years; SD = 13.65), 13 of whom were women. All patients had normal hearing and normal or corrected-to-normal vision. Patients were followed longitudinally for up to one year after surgery. However, due to the clinical complexity of oncology patients, only a subgroup of 24 underwent both postoperative rs-fMRI and behavioural assessments, conducted approximately three months after surgery (mean interval between assessments = 132 days; SD = 48.47). 

Cognitive assessment. All participants underwent a series of standardised neuropsychological and behavioural evaluations. For patients, cognitive assessments were conducted twice: approximately one week before surgery and again three months post-intervention. This allowed for a direct comparison of their cognitive performance pre- and post-surgery. Healthy participants completed this evaluation only once. Our cognitive evaluations focused on three domains: language, working memory, and executive functions. To quantify performance within each domain, we created composite variables.

MRI Data Acquisition. All MRI7fMRI data were acquired using a 3T Siemens Magnetom Prisma Fit scanner (Siemens AG, Erlangen, Germany). To obtain high-resolution images, a 3D ultra-fast gradient echo (MPRAGE) pulse sequence was employed, using a 64-channel head coil.

MRI Data Preprocessing and Analysis. Functional resting-state data images were preprocessed using SPM12 (Welcome Department of Cognitive Neurology, London, UK) using in-house Matlab (2021b release, Mathworks, Inc, Natick, MA) codes. As a first-level analysis, ROI-to-ROI connectivity matrices were estimated, characterising the FC between each pair of regions among 32 HPC-ICA network ROIs focused on the sensorimotor network, language network, visual network, executive control network, lateralized frontoparietal network, salience network, dorsal attention network, and default mode network (DMN) (Whitfield-Gabrieli & Nieto-Castanon, 2012).

Results

In this study, we evaluated rs-FC changes in patients with left-hemispheric brain tumours across eight canonical networks. Four main findings were observed: 1) FC is altered both inter- and intra-hemispherically in presurgical patients compared with healthy controls, providing evidence to consider brain tumors as a whole-brain disease; 2) these connectivity alterations remain largely stable four months after surgery, with efficiency levels comparable to those of healthy participants; 3) significant longitudinal changes in global network topology were observed, indicating that both efficiency and segregation tend to recover towards a neurotypical pattern four months post-surgery; and 4) network segregation and age could determine cognitive recovery  (see Figure 2).

Taken together, our results confirm that the presence of a brain tumour affects the connectivity of RSNs (Derks et al., 2017; Stoecklein et al., 2020). We provide evidence that brain tumours, contrary to traditional views, induce functional changes beyond lesion location. Specifically, we identified changes in the connectivity patterns of the sensorimotor, salience, default mode, frontoparietal, and cerebellar networks, as well as among the nodes that constitute these networks. While alterations in those same networks have been previously documented for patients with brain tumours (Sparacia et al., 2020), their direction and potential implications as protective neuroplastic mechanisms remain unclear (Sighinolfi et al., 2022). We found those connections to occur bidirectionally: some connections were stronger while others were weaker in comparison to healthy participants. The existence of evidence pointing towards increased or decreased FC in the same sample of patients reinforces the view that neuroplasticity encompasses a wide range of processes that go beyond any simplistic biological notions of “good” or “bad” (Diniz & Crestani, 2023), ultimately progressing towards balance. Moreover, as revealed by the mediation analysis, this pattern reflects an alteration in the efficiency of information flow, which may in turn stem from a reconfiguration of the neuronal hierarchy within domain-general networks.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that brain tumours have a widespread impact on RSNs, supporting the conceptualisation of brain tumours as a whole-brain disease. At the network level, tumour-related alterations affected both local and global topological organisation preoperatively: patients exhibited increased segregation while maintaining global efficiency relative to healthy controls, along with changes in the synchronisation of domain-general networks. Moreover, significant longitudinal changes were observed between the pre- and postoperative phases, characterised by postoperative increases in both segregation and efficiency that approximated the neurotypical pattern. Preoperative network segregation was found to be a significant predictor of postoperative cognitive recovery, with potential as a marker of resilience. This perspective underscores the need to reconsider compensatory mechanisms and refine strategies for targeted therapeutic intervention. Future investigations involving larger and more diverse cohorts, together with advanced network modelling, are needed to clarify the mechanisms of neural plasticity underlying long-term cognitive and functional outcomes in patients with brain tumours.
