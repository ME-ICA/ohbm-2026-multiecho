Introduction

Early-onset Alzheimer's disease (EOAD) is characterized by widespread disruptions in large-scale brain networks, making network-based approaches essential for understanding its atypical clinical presentation. However, the way in which different neuroimaging modalities jointly shape the large-scale organization of brain networks is essential for characterizing individual variability in EOAD. Computational neuroscience has shown that functional and metabolic interactions are complementary dimensions of brain organization¹; however, integrating them into a unified multiscale framework remains a methodological challenge. Multilayer network models provide a rational means of capturing interactions across modalities2, but few studies have exploited them to make individualized inferences at the patient level.

Methods

We analyzed data of 37 young Alzheimer’s disease patients from the COMAJ cohort, a multicenter, longitudinal, observational study including clinical, neuropsychological, and multimodal neuroimaging assessments collected over the course of follow-up, who underwent time-matched resting-state fMRI and FDG-PET scans. PIHAM (Probabilistic Inference in Heterogeneous and Attributed Multilayer Networks3), a probabilistic method, was applied to detect multilayer communities that integrate heterogeneous edges and node-level attributes. The model developed by Crossley et al. was used to divide each participant's whole brain into 638 cortical and subcortical regions of interest, each representing a node in the network. For each participant, we constructed a four-layer network composed of (i) resting-state fMRI functional connectivity, (ii) metabolic FDG-PET values as node attributes, and (iii–iv) two spatial attribute layers encoding hemisphere and anatomical region labels. PIHAM jointly deduces latent community structure across layers, thereby capturing the integrated functional and metabolic architecture of each participant.

To compare participants, multilayer community profiles were integrated into a low-dimensional space using t-SNE (t-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding) method and clustered using k-means and hierarchical clustering. Consensus clustering and cumulative distribution functions (CDF)  were used to assess the robustness of the clusters and the stability of patient-specific community models. 

Results

The cohort included 37 young Alzheimer’s disease patients (24 females; mean age 58.8 ± 4.6 years; mean MMSE 16.7 ± 4.6). Multilayer community detection revealed reproducible functional and metabolic patterns specific to each subject. The identified communities corresponded to anatomically and functionally known networks, including a limbic–orbitofrontal network (cingulum, hippocampus, amygdala, orbitofrontal cortex), an occipito-parietal visual network (cuneus, angular gyrus, occipital cortex), fronto-temporal networks (inferior frontal and superior temporal regions), dorsolateral prefrontal, temporal mid, sensorimotor, and cingulum–inferior temporal networks. The t-SNE method highlighted distinct inter-individual variability,with an average pairwise euclidean distance of 617 ± 334 across subjects in the low-dimensional space, reflecting subject-specific community organization. Consensus clustering identified 4 stable patient subgroups, supported by the CDF of consensus values (Fig. 1A), which showed that most values were higher than 0.8 indicating strong within-cluster agreement and low between-cluster similarity (Fig. 1B)

Conclusion

Our results demonstrate that multilayer probabilistic inference combined with consensus clustering can uncover significant heterogeneity in multi-modal brain imaging. By integrating fMRI and FDG-PET into a unified multilayer framework, these methods captures complementary aspects of functional and metabolic organization that can provide additional information compared to traditional uni-modal analyses. Future work will evaluate whether the differences observed between the subgroups of patients can be explained by clinical or cognitive variables, such as disease severity (e.g., MMSE) or phenotypic presentation, potentially linking multilayer network phenotypes to distinct clinical profiles in EOAD.

Probabilistic detection of multilayer communities, combined with t-SNE and consensus clustering, offers a promising theoretical framework to identify robust multi-modal connectivity profiles in neurodegenerative diseases. This approach provides a promising approach for the individualized characterization of functional/metabolic network disorders.
