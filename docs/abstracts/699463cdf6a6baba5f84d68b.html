Introduction

The Inferior Parietal Lobule (IPL) is a heterogeneous region involved in diverse cognitive functions, including attention, language, and sensorimotor integration. While the anatomical and functional subdivisions of the IPL are well-established in adults, the organization of this region in the neonatal brain remains poorly understood. The rapid development of cortical microstructure and functional networks during the perinatal period suggests that adult atlases may not be directly applicable to newborns. Furthermore, relying on a single modality may fail to capture the complex architectural boundaries of the developing cortex. To address this, we utilized high-quality data from the Developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP) to delineate the subregions of the neonatal IPL. By integrating structural connectivity (SC) and resting-state functional connectivity (FC) through a consensus approach, this study aims to establish a robust, multimodal parcellation of the neonatal IPL and characterize the specific connectivity fingerprints of its subregions.

Methods

Data were obtained from the Developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP). We analyzed 40 healthy term neonates (20 males; mean gestational age [GA]: 39.6 weeks; range: 39.4–40.4 weeks). Inclusion was strictly limited to subjects with a radiology score of 1 to ensure the absence of clinically significant incidental findings.

The Inferior Parietal Lobule was defined as the Region of Interest on a standard template and registered to individual native surfaces. We employed a dual-modality approach to partition the IPL:

Structural Modality: Probabilistic tractography was performed using FSL on T2w and diffusion MRI data. Streamlines were tracked from IPL surface vertices to the whole brain to generate connectivity matrices. A vertex-wise similarity matrix was constructed via cross-correlation of these connectivity profiles. We computed a group-average similarity matrix and applied spectral clustering to partition the IPL into k = 2-10 clusters.

Functional Modality: Resting-state fMRI data underwent the standard dHCP preprocessing pipeline, including motion/distortion correction and ICA-FIX denoising. Data were mapped to individual cortical surfaces using ribbon-constrained mapping. Functional connectivity was calculated between IPL vertices and the whole brain (excluding the ROI). Pearson correlation matrices were Fisher-z transformed to construct a functional similarity matrix, which was similarly partitioned using spectral clustering for k = 2–10.

To determine the optimal number of clusters, we quantitatively evaluated the Dice coefficient and Symmetry Index across all k solutions for both modalities. A neighborhood consensus method was subsequently employed to fuse the independent structural and functional parcellations into a unified, final multimodal atlas.

Connectivity Characterization and Fingerprinting The connectivity profiles of the derived subregions were characterized as follows:

Structural Connectivity: Whole-brain probabilistic tractography was performed from the fused subregions. Individual results were normalized, averaged, and thresholded at 0.05 to generate Maximum Probability Maps (MPM).

Functional Connectivity: One-sample t-tests were performed on subregion-specific seed maps (FWE-corrected) to generate masked T-maps.

Network Fingerprinting: To quantify functional specialization, we utilized 10 canonical Independent Component Analysis (ICA) networks (threshold > 4) as masks. By overlaying these network masks onto the structural and functional maps, we calculated the average connectivity strength between each IPL subregion and the 10 networks to generate distinct connectivity fingerprints.

Results

Parcellation Topology: Quantitative evaluation of the clustering solutions using Dice coefficients and Symmetry Indices indicated that a 4-cluster solution provided the highest inter-hemispheric consistency and stability for both structural and functional modalities. Consequently, we generated a final 4-region multimodal parcellation of the neonatal IPL using the neighborhood consensus approach.

Connectivity Profiles: The derived subregions demonstrated distinct structural and functional connectivity patterns.

Structural Profiles: The Maximum Probability Maps revealed that the four subregions possess unique white matter projection pathways to the rest of the brain.

Functional Profiles: The masked T-maps confirmed that each subregion maintains specific intrinsic functional connectivity with distributed cortical areas.

Network Fingerprints: Analysis of the overlap with the 10 ICA-derived networks revealed heterogeneous connectivity fingerprints. Each of the four IPL subregions exhibited a unique magnitude of overlap with the defined resting-state networks, quantitatively demonstrating functional specialization within the IPL at the neonatal stage.

Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive, data-driven parcellation of the neonatal IPL based on high-resolution dHCP data. By fusing structural and functional information, we identified four distinct subregions with unique connectivity profiles, evidenced by their specific white matter tracts and functional network coupling. These findings suggest that the functional specialization of the IPL is already established at term birth. This multimodal atlas provides a critical reference for future studies investigating neuro developmental trajectories and potential alterations in preterm or pathological cohorts.
