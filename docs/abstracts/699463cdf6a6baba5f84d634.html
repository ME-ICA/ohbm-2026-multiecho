Introduction

Emotion dysregulation is a critical risk factor to the development and maintenance of mental health problems in both neurotypical and autistic populations. It is increasingly recognized that elucidating underlying mental health mechanisms requires exploring the shared neural mechanisms across both typical and clinical populations. 

Prior studies of the neural correlates of ER have implicated the role of prefrontal (PFC) regions in modulating activity in limbic regions responsible for emotional experiences. Specifically, atypical resting-state functional connectivity (FC) in corticolimbic circuitry have been reported in individuals with emotion dysregulation1 and autistic individuals2,3. 

Given the transdiagnostic nature of emotion dysregulation and the significant challenges that it poses for many autistic individuals, this study aimed to adopt a dimensional approach to examine whether disturbances in corticolimbic circuitry is associated with heightened emotion dysregulation in a combined sample of young adults with and without autism.

Methods

Fifty neurotypical and 51 autistic young adults (48.5% males; mean age = 20.0, SD = 2.2) completed the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale short form4 and a 5-minute resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) scan. Higher scores on the DERS-SF indicated greater emotion dysregulation. The fMRI data (TR/TE = 1500/32 ms, flip angle = 79°, 54 axial 2mm slices) were acquired using a Siemens 3T Prisma MR scanner. The fMRI data were preprocessed and analyzed using CONN5. FC was measured using seed regions representative of the corticolimbic network, which included: bilateral amygdala, insula and the dorsolateral PFC (dlPFC). Group-level general linear models (GLM) that included age, gender and IQ as control variables were performed to evaluate whether DERS-SF scores were associated with the seed regions and the rest of the brain. Cluster-level corrections for multiple comparisons were carried out using Gaussian random field theory (p < .05, FDR-corrected).

Results

FC analysis revealed that higher emotion dysregulation is associated with stronger connectivity between the dlPFC and several brain regions, including the frontal pole, precuneous, insula, superior parietal lobule, postcentral gyrus, and the central opercular cortex. Emotion dysregulation scores were also associated with stronger connectivity between the bilateral insula and frontal orbital cortex, but no significant associations were found when the amygdala was used as a seed. 

Conclusion

The current results show that individual differences in emotion dysregulation are related to brain connectivity at rest in a combined sample of young adults with and without autism. Corticolimbic circuitry related to emotional processing such as the dlPFC and insula show stronger FC with areas implicated in autism symptomatology, e.g., frontal pole, precuneous, superior parietal lobule. These findings support the view that emotional vulnerability exists as a continuum across typical and neurodiverse populations. Future studies should examine whether FC is a mediator of autistic symptomatology and emotion dysregulation, which will elucidate whether atypical FC may be a potential mechanism underlying ER problems in autistic individuals.
