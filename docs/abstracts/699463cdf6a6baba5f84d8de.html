Introduction

Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) tear is the most common ligament injury of the knee joint, and arthroscopic ACL reconstruction (ACLR) remains the main operative treatment1. More than half of patients develop post-traumatic osteoarthritis (PTOA) within 10-20 years after ACLR. The pain course associated with ACL tear, ACLR, postoperative rehabilitation, and PTOA and the related brain adaptations are poorly understood, which prevents development of novel therapeutics and optimization of current management for these patients. Prior neuroimaging studies have implicated various brain structures and networks involved with knee OA-induced pain at lower field strengths2. We present an initial exploratory analysis examining morphological and connectivity-based changes associated with ACL tear and subsequent surgical treatment using ultrahigh field (≥7T) MRI.

Methods

9 subjects from the MOON cohort1 with more than 10 years after ACL reconstruction (ACLR: 6 female, age 34.1 +/- 3.2) and 9 subjects with recent, unrepaired ACL tear (ACLU: 3 female, 40.7 +/- 10.2) participated in this study. The Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Score3 for pain subscale (KOOS pain) for ACLR and ACLU subjects were 88.3 +/- 9.2 and 74.7 +/- 8.7, respectively. Subjects were scanned on a 7T Siemens Terra (Erlangen, Germany) using an 8Tx/32Rx Nova head coil (Massachusetts, USA). Whole-brain, resting state fMRI (rsfMRI) (SMS multi-band=3, 81 1.5mm slices, 260 volumes, TE/TR=22/1700 ms, 63° flip angle, FOV=192 mm, resolution=1.2x1.2x1.5 mm) and DTI (single-shot, 72 non-collinear directions, b-value=1000 s/mm, resolution=2x2x2mm) sequences were acquired. High resolution T1w images were acquired for anatomical context as well as dual-echo B0 field maps. rsfMRI data were corrected for B0 distortions, slice timing, motion, and physiologically-based nuisances. DTI data were corrected for B0 distortions and eddy currents. rsfMRI and DTI data were then linearly registered to their corresponding anatomical data. Anatomical data were non-linearly registered to the Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) template, and the combined registrations were used to transform the DTI and rsfMRI data to MNI space in a single warp. Additionally, morphological features, including volume and cortical thickness, were extracted from anatomical data with the FreeSurfer pipeline.

The fractional amplitude of low frequency fluctuations4 (fALFF) was calculated from the rsfMRI data and a group difference z-map was created. The clusters identified from this map were used as seeds for group connectivity difference z-maps (figure 1). Finally, the DTI data was used to create group difference z-maps based on radial and axial diffusivity. All difference maps were corrected using 3dClustSim (AFNI).

Results

Over 400 morphological FreeSurfer-based features were examined for group differences between ACLR and ACLU subjects. After False Discovery Rate (FDR) correction, several areas previously implicated in the processing of knee pain were identified2, including cortical thickness differences in the orbitofrontal, temporal, and parietal areas as well as grey matter volume differences in the superior cingulate and parahippocampal gyrii. fALFF was used to find areas of changes in local BOLD fluctuations between the ACLR and ACLU groups. Two nodes were identified: the left anterior insula and right anterior superior cingulate, both of which are part of the salience network and have been shown to be involved in pain perception and processing. Overall fALFF-based measures were higher for ACLR subjects in the anterior insula and lower in the anterior cingulate. These nodes were then used as seeds for whole brain connectivity maps. Significant clusters of connectivity differences to the two seeds are shown in figure 1, including the left somatosensory cortex, the left dentate/fastigial nuclei, and the left superior parietal lobule. ACLR subjects also showed stronger connectivity between the left anterior insula and right anterior cingulate. Figure 2 shows lower radial diffusivity (RD) in the left internal capsule and lower axial diffusivity (AD) in the left external capsule for ACLR subjects compared to ACLU subjects.

Conclusion

We present an initial exploration of brain changes based on ultrahigh field MRI. We compared subjects 10+ years after ACLR with subjects after recent ACL tear prior to surgery to investigate changes associated with pain related to ACLR, post-operative rehabilitation, and potential early PTOA. This study showed significant increases in connectivity in several nodes of the pain matrix, including anterior insula, anterior cingulate, and somatosensory cortex in subjects 10+ years after ACLR. These areas include nodes involved with integrating pain signals as well as areas involved with handling pain-associated emotions. Diffusion imaging revealed differences involving the internal capsule, a central white matter tract carrying sensory information between several of these pain-involved nodes. Interestingly, higher RD in tissue is typically suggestive of microtissue damage, indicating that perhaps ACLU subjects have more pain-related activity in this pathway than ACLR. The interpretation of differences in AD is less clear and could indicate either more or less damage to this tissue. Normative data may help with this interpretation.

In this ongoing study, we plan to compare the two patient groups with healthy controls and follow them to better understand pain trajectories and adaptive brain changes related to PTOA. Further examination of these areas may lead to better understanding of pain processing and reveal potential targets for treatment of symptomatic PTOA.
