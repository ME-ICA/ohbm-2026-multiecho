Introduction

Oscillatory activity captured by electroencephalography (EEG) reflects coordinated neuronal dynamics across large-scale cortical circuits1,2. Certain band powers are robust traits linked to functional brain state, cognitive performance, and neuropsychiatric vulnerability3,4. Twin studies indicate moderate to high heritability for EEG oscillations, yet their broader biological relevance across the human phenome remains under explored5,6. While genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have begun to identify loci associated with EEG traits, the downstream consequences of genetic variation influencing brain rhythms—spanning brain structure, systemic physiology, cognition, and behavior—remain largely unknown. Here we present a new and extended analyses of the ENIGMA-EEG consortium. A phenome-wide association approach can provide an integrated picture of the multidimensional traits that share genetic architecture with EEG oscillatory power. 

Objectives 

The present study aimed to: 

  1. Quantify the genetic architecture of resting-state EEG oscillatory power using large-scale meta-analysis of GWAS. 
  1. Conduct a phenome-wide genetic correlation scan (PheWAS) across thousands of UK Biobank phenotypes to identify domains that share genetic liability with EEG oscillations. 
  1. Characterize the specificity of cross-trait associations and evaluate whether distinct EEG frequency bands show divergent phenomic profiles. 

Methods

Resting-state EEG (eyes-closed, Cz and occipital channels) was collected from nine independent cohorts (N ≈ 14,000). Standardized preprocessing and trait extraction yielded a series of power measures. GWAS were performed within cohort and meta-analyzed using inverse-variance weighting. 

Polygenic indexes (PGI) for each EEG trait were computed in UK Biobank participants of European ancestry. Phenome-wide association analyses were performed using the PHESANT pipeline7, which systematically tests an exposure variable against ≥2,000 curated phenotypes spanning imaging-derived measures, physical and metabolic traits, cognitive and psychiatric variables, and lifestyle factors. PHESANT’s regression framework automatically selects the appropriate model type (continuous, ordered, logistic, or multinomial), and we adjusted for age, sex, assessment center, genotyping array, and 20 ancestry principal components. Bonferroni and FDR procedures were applied to correct for multiple testing. 

Results

The PheWAS revealed distinct and interpretable genetic association patterns across the EEG frequency bands. Broadband power showed its strongest associations within brain imaging domains, including cortical surface area in occipital and parietal regions. Further strong associations were observed for global white matter volumes globally and FA in corpus collosum, internal capsule, and corona radiata. Most imaging correlations remained significant after correcting for total ICV. Cortical thickness showed much less strong associations. See the figure for the broadband power PheWAS results. Across all EEG traits, significant phenome-wide FDR discoveries clustered strongly within the Imaging domain, whereas Lifestyle, Mental Health, and Physical Measure traits produced fewer associations after multiple testing correction.  

Notable correlations with behavioral and mental health traits were “Worry too long after embarrassment”, “Mood swings”, “Neuroticism score”, education variables, and risk-taking variables. 

Conclusion

This phenome-wide genetic correlation analysis reveals that EEG oscillatory power—particularly broadband power—maps onto distinct, biologically coherent patterns across the human phenome. The strong enrichment of associations in imaging-derived cortical and subcortical measures validates EEG rhythms as genetically informed indicators of underlying brain architecture and function, proving that our GWAS of EEG parameters holds enough signal despite the relatively low number of subjects (~14.5k).  Both white matter and grey matter parameters are associated with polygenic indices for EEG. 

Our results also showed highly significant associations with behavioral and mental health traits, most notably those related to neuroticism and worry. Higher polygenic scores on theta and alpha power were associated with lower neuroticism scores.  At the same time, no significant associations were observed for medication use.  

These findings underscore the potential of EEG oscillations as scalable markers of neurobiological organizations that bridge electrophysiology, brain morphology, and cognitive function. By clarifying the phenomic landscape associated with EEG rhythms, this work lays a foundation for integrating EEG-based genetic biomarkers into multimodal imaging genetics frameworks and for probing mechanistic links between neural dynamics and behavior. The size of the available genetic biobanks allows for an unprecedented scale for discovery of novel EEG parameters linked to mental health and behavioral outcomes while controlling (or investigating) for large numbers of covariates, including ancestries, sex, age, medication intake, and other confounding variables.
