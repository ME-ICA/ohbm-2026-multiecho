Introduction

Millions of children worldwide grow up as left-behind children (LBC) due to parental labor migration, and meta-analytic work indicates elevated anxiety and depression symptoms in this group.^1 Yet symptom burden alone cannot reveal whether the core capacity for positive adaptation—resilient functioning—is itself compromised under chronic caregiving disruption. Emotion regulation (ER) is a key scaffold of resilient functioning and is strongly shaped by early caregiving environment: supportive caregiving fosters flexible use of cognitive reappraisal, whereas unfavorable caregiving promotes reliance on expressive suppression and reduced regulatory efficacy^2. At the neural level, reappraisal depends on prefrontal-amygdala circuits,^3 which undergo prolonged development and are sensitive to early environment exposure. However, it is unknown whether parental labor migration alters these multi-level ER processes in LBC, and whether such alterations help explain why some LBC show heightened emotional distress and reduced resilient functioning. Here we combine population-scale behavioral modelling and prediction model with task-based fMRI and dynamic causal modelling to test whether ER tendencies, prefrontal recruitment during reappraisal, and prefrontal-amygdala effective connectivity transmit and recalibrate the impact of parental labor migration on resilient functioning in childhood.

Methods

Participants

We used a two-sample design combining a large behavioral cohort and an independent neuroimaging cohort of Chinese children. The behavioral sample included 1,614 participants (age (M±sd) = 11.09±0.91 years, 812 girls; 925 LBC, 689 non-left-behind controls, nLBC) from three primary schools. The neuroimaging sample comprised 75 children (age (M±sd) = 11.87±0.81 years, 40 girls; 30 LBC, 45 nLBC) recruited from a fourth school and assessed during a 3-day university camp. Exclusion criteria were history of neurological or psychiatric disorder, head trauma, and MRI contraindications (imaging sample). All procedures had ethical approval and written parental consent and child assent.

Behavioral measures

Emotional distress was assessed with anxiety and depression scales (SCAS-S plus CES-DC in the behavioral sample; GAD-7 plus CES-DC in the imaging sample). A one-factor confirmatory factor analysis estimated latent distress scores, which were standardized. Stress exposure was indexed by 26 recent life events (ASLEC). ER tendencies were measured via the Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (reappraisal and suppression subscales). In the imaging sample, trial-level unpleasantness ratings during the emotion-regulation task indexed reappraisal efficacy.

Analysis

In the behavioral sample, we trained a random-forest regressor to predict distress from the 26 life-event indicators using nested cross-validation (10-fold outer loop; 5-fold inner loop for hyperparameter tuning). Individual resilient functioning was defined as the z-standardized difference between predicted and observed distress (predicted minus observed). The final model was then locked and applied to the neuroimaging sample to obtain out-of-sample distress predictions and a parallel resilient-functioning index. Group differences in distress, resilient functioning, ER tendencies, and reappraisal efficacy were tested using linear mixed-effects or fixed-effect models with age and gender as covariates (plus mean framewise displacement for imaging analyses). Bootstrap mediation models (2,000 resamples) tested whether ER tendencies, task-evoked activation, or effective connectivity transmitted the association between left-behind status and resilient functioning.

Task fMRI acquisition, preprocessing and analysis

Neuroimaging data were acquired on a 3T Siemens TrioTim scanner. During fMRI, children performed an instruction-based reappraisal task (Fig. 1d) with three conditions: LOOK-neutral, LOOK-aversive, and LESS-aversive (reappraisal). Two runs of 30 trials (20 per condition overall) were presented. Preprocessing used fMRIPrep (motion correction, normalization to MNI152NLin6Asym, ICA-AROMA denoising, 6-mm smoothing). First-level GLMs in SPM12 modelled the three conditions; the reappraisal contrast (LESS-aversive > LOOK-aversive) was entered into group-level models with covariates (age, gender, mean FD). We identified bilateral dmPFC and vlPFC clusters (FDR-corrected p<0.05) and extracted subject-level contrast estimates. For effective connectivity, we specified a six-node DCM (Fig. 2a) including bilateral dmPFC, bilateral vlPFC, and bilateral amygdala. All nodes were reciprocally connected; trial onsets served as common driving input; the LESS condition modulated all connections and self-connections, and the LOOK-aversive condition modulated amygdala self- and inter-connections only. Volumes of interest were defined from group-level peaks for amygdala (LOOK-aversive > LOOK-neutral) and subject-specific peaks near group maxima for prefrontal ROIs (LESS-aversive > LOOK-aversive). At the group level, Parametric Empirical Bayes with automatic Bayesian model reduction and averaging estimated common and group-dependent connectivity; parameters with posterior probability >0.99 were considered robust. Significant group-dependent connections were related to resilient functioning using linear models and exploratory mediation analyses controlling for age, gender, and motion.

Results

Behavioral Resilience Deficits in Left-Behind Children

In the behavioral cohort (N=1,614), LBC showed higher anxiety (SCAS-S: 0.94±0.07 vs 0.66±0.06; t=5.53, p<.001, d=0.46) and depression (CES-DC: 0.81±0.06 vs 0.58±0.06; t=5.62, p<.001, d=0.46) than nLBC (Fig. 1a). Latent emotional distress was similarly elevated (0.27±0.13 vs -0.27±0.13; t=6.86, p<.001, d=0.56). Adverse life-event counts correlated with distress in both groups (r≈0.58, p<.001). A random-forest model predicting distress from the 26 life-event items achieved robust performance (overall R²=0.38, RMSE=0.79; r=0.60 in LBC, r=0.62 in nLBC, Fig. 1b). Resilient functioning (predicted minus observed distress, both z-scored) was significantly lower in LBC than nLBC (-0.13±0.07 vs 0.15±0.06; t=-3.90, p<.001, d=-0.33; Fig. 1c).

Neuroimaging Cohort: Generalized Resilience Model and Reappraisal Efficacy

LBC reported less habitual cognitive reappraisal (t=-4.48, p<.001, d=-0.36) and greater expressive suppression (t=2.86, p=.005, d=0.24). Reappraisal correlated positively with resilient functioning (r=0.12, p<.001), whereas suppression correlated negatively (r=-0.19, p<.001). Bootstrap mediation confirmed that both reduced reappraisal and heightened suppression partly transmitted the association between left-behind status and lower resilient functioning (indirect effects -0.02 and -0.03, both 95% CIs excluding zero), while direct effects remained significant.

Prefrontal-Amygdala Circuits Recalibrate Resilience 

In the neuroimaging cohort (N=75), the behavioral random-forest model generalized well: distress predictions correlated with observed scores in both LBC (r=0.65, p<.001) and nLBC (r=0.62, p<.001). Resilient functioning was again lower in LBC (-0.38±0.16) than nLBC (0.24±0.13; t=2.89, p=.004, d=-0.73). Mixed-effects analysis of trial-wise ratings revealed a strong condition effect (p<0.001) and a condition×group interaction (p<.001, Fig. 1e): both groups benefited from reappraisal, but nLBC showed a larger reduction in unpleasantness (Δ=0.60±0.04, z=15.33, p<.001) than LBC (Δ=0.34±0.05, z=7.16, p<.001). Proportional reappraisal efficacy was lower in LBC (0.08±0.04 vs 0.20±0.03; t=-2.22, p=.03, d=-0.57) but did not significantly predict resilient functioning when controlling for group, nor did it mediate the resilience gap (p=.31).

Whole-brain analysis of the reappraisal contrast (LESS-aversive > LOOK-aversive) identified four prefrontal clusters in bilateral dmPFC and vlPFC (Fig. 1f). Across all clusters, LBC showed attenuated activation relative to nLBC (all p<.05, Fig. 1g). Right dmPFC activation was most strongly linked to resilient functioning (r=0.44, p<.001). Mediation analysis revealed that reduced right dmPFC activation significantly transmitted the association between left-behind status and lower resilient functioning (indirect effect =-0.13, 95% CI [-0.26, -0.03]), whereas other clusters did not show significant mediation.

Dynamic causal modelling of a six-node dmPFC-vlPFC-amygdala circuit showed reliable group differences in both inhibitory and excitatory couplings (posterior probability>0.99). Relative to nLBC, LBC exhibited weaker top-down inhibition from right dmPFC to right amygdala, weaker inhibitory coupling from left to right amygdala, reduced excitatory feedback from right amygdala to bilateral dmPFC, and reduced self-inhibition in right dmPFC (Fig. 2b). These altered couplings did not mediate the main effect of left-behind status on resilient functioning, but exhibited group-dependent associations (Fig. 2c). Stronger right dmPFC→right amygdala inhibition predicted better resilient functioning in nLBC (β=-0.48, p<.001) but not in LBC, whereas stronger right amygdala→right dmPFC excitation predicted better resilient functioning in LBC (β=0.56, p=.003) but not in nLBC. Thus, resilient functioning in nLBC was supported by canonical prefrontal inhibition, whereas a compensatory bottom-up excitatory pathway appeared selectively beneficial in LBC.

Conclusion

Across complementary behavioral and neuroimaging cohorts, parental labor migration was associated with both greater emotional symptoms and a reproducible deficit in resilient functioning among left-behind children (LBC). By combining machine-learning estimates of expected distress with multi-level measures of emotion regulation, we show that this resilience gap is not merely a by-product of increased stress exposure but is partly transmitted through hierarchical ER difficulties. LBC reported underuse of cognitive reappraisal and overuse of expressive suppression, achieved smaller affective relief during instructed reappraisal, and recruited the prefrontal reappraisal network—especially right dmPFC—less strongly than their non-left-behind peers. Reduced right dmPFC activation during reappraisal, in turn, emerged as a key neural mediator linking left-behind status to diminished resilient functioning.

Effective-connectivity analyses further revealed that parental labor migration is associated with a qualitative recalibration of prefrontal-amygdala signaling. In nLBC, resilient functioning was supported by canonical top-down inhibition from dmPFC to amygdala, consistent with typical maturation of regulatory control circuits. In LBC, however, resilient functioning was preferentially associated with a compensatory bottom-up pathway in which stronger right amygdala→dmPFC excitation predicted better outcomes, despite overall blunted prefrontal engagement. These dual pathways highlight that children growing up with disrupted caregiving may rely on alternative circuit configurations to support adaptation.

Conceptually, our findings extend resilience theory by identifying prefrontal emotion-regulation circuits as a sensitive neural substrate through which structural adversity reshapes both emotional distress and resilient functioning. Practically, they suggest that interventions for LBC should target both behavioral ER habits (especially strengthening reappraisal) and dmPFC-centred regulatory circuits, while acknowledging individual differences in whether resilience is rooted in preserved top-down control or compensatory bottom-up drive. More broadly, this framework illustrates how integrating population-level behavioral modelling with circuit-level effective connectivity can reveal hidden neuroadaptive pathways through which early adversity recalibrates the developing brain. Finally, the current findings help to understand how macro-level labour migration policies and rural-urban arrangements are instantiated in children’s neurocognitive systems, highlighting childhood as promising windows for interventions that strengthen cognitive reappraisal and prefrontal regulatory capacity in LBC.
