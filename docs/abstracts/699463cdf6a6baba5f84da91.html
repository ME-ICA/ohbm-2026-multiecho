Introduction

Incomplete hippocampal inversion (IHI) is proposed as a developmental biomarker for mesial temporal lobe epilepsy. To use IHI as a reliable epidemiologic or AI/ML feature, harmonization must address scanner, sequence, and rater variability. Uncalibrated Cury criteria (C1–C5) can distort prevalence estimates and undermine diagnostic preservation. Just as scanners and acquisition protocols require harmonization, rater performance is itself a factor that must be harmonized. Consensus labeling strategies, defined as the aggregation of multiple raters’ annotations into a group label, can stabilize annotations in Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) pipelines. We compared two approaches: majority vote, which assigns the most common label across raters, and the Dawid–Skene Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm, which probabilistically estimates the most likely true label. Studying first- and second-year medical student raters provides insight into how consensus can transform variable judgments into reliable group-level labels, advancing goals in medical education, residency preparation, diagnostic preservation, and scalable epidemiology.

Methods

Eighteen subjects from the Queensland Twin Adolescent Brain Imaging (QTAB) dataset (excluding IDs 9 and 16) were analyzed. Eight novice raters graded bilateral hippocampi using the Cury criteria (C1–C5, Total) in ITK-SNAP (v4.4.0). First, to identify prevalence and criterion-level drivers of variability, prevalence of IHI was defined as the presence of any positive grade, with Wilson 95% confidence intervals (CI). To identify criteria influencing IHI designation, the proportion of IHI-positive cases per criterion was calculated with Wilson 95% CIs. Second, to assess harmonization, consensus labels were generated by majority vote and by Dawid–Skene Expectation–Maximization. Reliability was assessed using Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) (2,1) and ICC(2,k), weighted kappa, and percent agreement. Analyses were conducted in Python (v3.11) using pandas, NumPy, and scikit-learn.

Results

IHI prevalence was consistent across hemispheres. In the right hippocampus, prevalence was 58.2% (95% CI: 38.6–75.5%) and in the left 59.1% (95% CI: 39.4–76.3%). Criterion contributions showed that in the right hippocampus, C5 (57.1%, 95% CI: 40.9–72.0) and C3 (48.6%, 95% CI: 33.0–64.4) were the dominant drivers of IHI designation. In the left hippocampus, C3 (48.6%, 95% CI: 33.0–64.4) and C5 (51.4%, 95% CI: 35.6–67.0) again contributed most strongly.

Consensus labeling improved reproducibility across nearly all criteria. Under majority vote, ICC(2,k) values increased from 0.72 to 0.88 for Right C1, from 0.66 to 0.83 for Right C2, from 0.30 to 0.66 for Right C3, and from 0.49 to 0.77 for Right Total. Dawid–Skene EM consensus produced even stronger improvements. For the Right

hippocampus, ICC(2,k) increased from 0.30 to 0.71 for C3 and from 0.75 to 0.90 for C5, while Left ICC(2,k) rose from 0.56 to 0.82 for C3 and from 0.43 to 0.75 for C5. Importantly, DS consensus rescued Left C4, where reliability improved from –0.27 to 0.67. Consensus narrowed interval widths, indicating improved precision.

Conclusion

Incomplete hippocampal inversion was present in over half of subjects in this QTAB pilot analysis. Classification relied most on C3 and C5, with additional influence from C2. These findings identify where novice interpretation is vulnerable. Consensus labeling significantly increased ICC values and reduced uncertainty, with gains most pronounced for these subjective features (C3 and C5). Dawid–Skene EM was able to stabilize fragile criteria such as C4 by modeling rater error directly. For medical education and residency preparation, consensus serves as a calibration benchmark. For scalable epidemiology, it demonstrates that consensus labeling is a powerful rater harmonization strategy, complementing scanner and protocol harmonization to ensure that IHI prevalence and asymmetry estimates are reproducible and suitable for AI/ML pipelines.
