Introduction

Whole Slide Imaging (WSI) has become a crucial modality in modern histopathology, enabling the digital conversion of tissue on glass slides into high-resolution virtual slides. Beyond facilitating precise, large-scale visualization, WSI supports the reconstruction of three-dimensional (3D) volumes from serial histological sections, providing new opportunities to study the microstructural organization of complex tissues. However, 3D reconstruction from serial WSI is challenged by structural artifacts introduced during tissue preparation and imaging. Global 3D deformations arise from tissue extraction and processing, including shrinkage due to dehydration and fixation, or distortions from mechanical stress and embedding.1 Sectioning and mounting can introduce slice-specific 2D deformations such as tearing, folding, shearing, and local warping.1

In 3D histological reconstruction, these inconsistencies can accumulate across sections, degrading volumetric integrity. The level-set method (LSM) is a numerical technique that embeds surface information into a signed distance function, representing surfaces implicitly as level-sets to capture complex geometric and topological changes. This study presents an enhanced framework for histological image reconstruction, guided by a non-distorted and lower resolution MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) volume. The workflow mitigates residual distortions by integrating LSM into the non-linear stage of reconstruction in The Possum Framework pipeline.2 These constraints restore native morphology and improve robustness against common histological artifacts. Additionally, large-scale projects, region-specific studies of structures, and non-human primate research have often relied on data-set specific approaches or require significant manual intervention for reconstruction.

Methods

The animal tissue used for this study was obtained in accordance with the guidelines of the Canadian Council for Animal Care and approved by the McGill University Animal Care Committee.

This study utilized in-vivo MRI and histological data from a single adult female marmoset brain.3 A T1-weighted anatomical scan was acquired at 300 μm isotopic resolution. The brain was perfusion-fixed, extracted, and placed in 4% paraformaldehyde. The brain was cryosectioned in the coronal plane at 20 μm thickness. To match the resolution of the MRI scan, 1 in every 15 sections was retained, resulting in 300 μm spacing between sections. During sectioning, blockface images were captured to facilitate accurate reconstruction and alignment. Sections were co-stained using a Hoechst 33342 and a Myelin Basic Protein antibody. Sections were imaged using ImageXpress High-Content Imaging System (Molecular Devices, USA) with a 10x objective to acquire image tiles for each section (0.67 μm resolution). Each section contained up to a maximum of 256 tiles.  

All image registration in this work utilized Advanced Normalization Tools (ANTs).4 The T1-weighted MRI was upsampled and underwent a linear rigid then affine 3D registration to the blockface volume to match the cutting plane. The 2D blockface images were aligned, referencing the registered T1-weighted MRI, following a four-step framework.2 First, a coarse-scale step registers each moving slice affinely to its corresponding slice in the reference volume (pairwise). Next, moving slices were registered to neighbouring slices to improve fine scale alignment (sequential). In the last step, the sequential transformations are high-pass filtered and added to the pairwise transformations, forming a coarse-to-fine step. This step preserves the global shape of the brain while enhancing local slice-slice alignment (coarse-to-fine). 

Histological slices were reconstructed using the resulting blockface volume as the reference, as displayed in Figure 1. The same framework was used, however, a fourth step using a deformable non-linear registration is introduced to correct for localized distortions. In this step, key structures (white matter and gray matter) were segmented individually in both the moving and fixed images relevant to the target anatomy. This set of binary masks were used to generate level-set maps based on a signed distance function. The surface of each segmentation was defined as the zero level, values inside were negative, and the values outside are positive. The level-set maps are integrated into an ANTs registration call that combines both Mutual Information (MI) between the reference and moving images and Cross-Correlation (CC) for their corresponding level-set representations. By doing so, the registration algorithm gains access to geometric boundary information that improves alignment precision during non-linear corrections.  

The evaluation of inter-modality registrations is calculated through normalized mutual information (NMI) scores. NMI quantifies the statistical dependence between the intensity distributions of two images, providing a measure of how much information one image conveys about the other. A higher NMI value reflects greater shared information content, indicating improved alignment between the registered images. NMI was calculated in 2D, where the value is the average of each slices’ NMI. Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) quantifies the similarity between two images based on patterns of luminance, contrast, and structure. SSIM values range from –1 to 1, where values closer to 1 indicate higher structural similarity and better alignment between images.

Results

In Figure 2, four representative coronal slices are presented. The blockface reference image is displayed as well as the resulting image following the coarse-to-fine stage, and following the deformable registrations, both without and with integration of LSM. The green outline highlights the structure of the whole reference slice, and the blue outline highlights the structure of white matter in the reference slice. Visually, local regions within the coarse-to-fine volume are misaligned to the corresponding local regions within the reference slice. The deformable registration without the integration of LSM results in an unstable warping with structural unalignment to the reference such as slice 48 in Figure 2. The integration of LSM into the deformable stage demonstrates structural alignment in both the whole slice and white matter regions. Quantitatively, the coarse-to-fine reconstruction has a 2D NMI of 0.4470 and SSIM 0.8022. The proposed method has an NMI of 0.4946 and SSIM of 0.8297 while The Possum Framework has an NMI of 0.4617 and SSIM of 0.8030. The proposed method indicates an improvement of 10.7% and 3.4%, respectively, while The Possum Framework has an improvement score of 3.3% and 0.1%. Conclusion

This novel framework addresses a critical bottleneck in the reconstruction of 2D serial sections into accurate 3D volumes. The ability of integrating the LSM in regions of interests should allow the framework to extend beyond the brain to other organ systems. The proposed method addresses the loss of 3D spatial information introduced during histological processing by offering a flexible, structurally guided approach that can adapt to such tissue-specific challenges. Ultimately, this pipeline enhances the quality and reliability of 2D-to-3D reconstructions, offering a unified solution to a longstanding limitation in histological image analysis.
