Introduction

Reading relies on the integrity of white matter tracts organized into two primary pathways: the dorsal stream (including AF and SLF) supporting phonological processing, and the ventral stream (including IFOF, ILF, and UF) facilitating semantic mapping. While Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) has linked microstructural metrics like fractional anisotropy (FA) to reading skills, findings in alphabetic languages remain inconsistent, reporting conflicting positive and negative correlations across different cohorts.

Two major gaps persist in the literature. First, Chinese reading differs significantly from alphabetic systems, relying more heavily on visuospatial and semantic processing; thus, models derived from alphabetic languages may not generalize to Chinese readers. Second, while white matter follows a non-linear trajectory across the lifespan, most research is restricted to narrow age groups (e.g., children or adolescents), neglecting the dynamic changes from childhood through aging.

This study utilizes cross-sectional data to investigate the relationship between white matter development and reading performance in Chinese readers across a broad age range (7-77 years). Analyzing seven key fiber tracts (AF, SLF I-III, ILF, IFOF, UF), we aimed to: (1) elucidate the developmental trajectories of white matter metrics; (2) identify tract-specific associations with reading performance; and (3) explore how age modulates the relationship between white matter structure and reading ability.

Methods

Participants

The participants were selected from a larger cohort (Liu et al., 2024). Ultimately, 129 participants remained in the final analysis after quality control, including 48 children (group CH; 27 females, 10.96 ± 1.92 years), 33 young adults (group YA; 20 females, 21.24 ± 1.85 years), and 48 middle-aged and older adults (group MOA; 32 females, 53.09 ± 14.01 years). Exclusion criteria included Raven reasoning scores lower than the 25th percentile, reading scores 2 SDs below the age-specific mean, excessive head motion (> 0.5 mm), or incomplete imaging data. All participants were right-handed native Mandarin speakers with normal or corrected-to-normal vision/hearing and no history of neurological diseases. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their guardians. This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee at the Brain Imaging Center of Beijing Normal University.

Behavioral measures

Intelligence was screened using Raven’s Standard Progressive Matrices, ensuring all participants scored above the 25th percentile. Reading performance was evaluated by two tests: the Word Reading Efficiency (WRE) test and the Silent Sentence Reading (SSR) test. The WRE measured the number of high-frequency two-character words read correctly per minute. The SSR assessed sentence comprehension and fluency, requiring participants to judge the semantic correctness of sentences within 3 minutes; the score was calculated as the net number of correct characters processed per minute. A character recognition test was administered solely for screening purposes.

Image Acquisition

Neuroimaging data were acquired on a SIEMENS PRISMA 3-T scanner. T1-weighted images were acquired with the following parameters: TR/TE = 2530/2.27 ms, flip angle = 7°, 208 slices, voxel size = 1 × 1 × 1 mm³. Diffusion-weighted images (DTI) were acquired using a single-shot EPI sequence with 64 non-collinear directions (b = 1000 s/mm²) and 10 b0 images. Parameters were: TR/TE = 4000/74 ms, 68 slices, voxel size = 2 × 2 × 2 mm³, with anterior-posterior phase encoding.

Image processing

Preprocessing was performed using QSIPrep 0.22.1. Anatomical images were reoriented, skull-stripped using SynthStrip, and segmented using SynthSeg. Diffusion data underwent MP-PCA denoising, Gibbs unringing, and B1 field inhomogeneity correction. FSL’s eddy was used for motion and eddy current correction. Fiber bundles were reconstructed using TractSeg v2.7, a deep-learning-based segmentation method. Tensor metrics (FA, MD, AD, RD) were calculated via MRtrix3 using an iterative weighted least-squares algorithm. We extracted mean metrics from seven pairs of bilateral reading-related tracts: AF, SLF (I, II, and III), ILF, IFOF, and UF. The corticospinal tract (CST) and optic radiation (OR) were segmented as controls.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R. To assess developmental trajectories, multiple linear regression models were fitted for each tract metric (FA, MD, AD, RD) against age, including quadratic age terms to detect potential non-linear patterns, while controlling for gender and IQ. To examine brain-behavior associations, regression models were employed with reading scores (WRE or SSR) as independent variables and tract metrics as dependent variables, covarying for age, gender, and IQ. Furthermore, to test age-dependent interactions, correlations between reading scores and white matter metrics were analyzed separately within each age group (CH, YA, MOA) and compared using Fisher’s z-tests. Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction was applied for multiple comparisons.

Results

Demographics and Behavioral Results

Behavioral analyses indicated that reading performance (WRE and SSR) followed a significant non-linear trajectory across the lifespan, characterized by an initial increase during development that peaked in young adulthood, followed by a decline in aging. Significant group differences were observed in IQ and reading scores among the three age cohorts, consistent with this inverted U-shaped developmental pattern.

Age Effects on White Matter Tracts

Controlling for gender and IQ, we identified distinct developmental trajectories for white matter microstructure. In the left hemisphere, most tracts (AF, SLF I, SLF III, IFOF, and UF) exhibited quadratic relationships with age, characterized by an inverted U-shaped trajectory for FA (initial increase followed by decline). A notable exception was the left ILF, which showed a linear positive correlation with age. In the right hemisphere, similar inverted U-shaped FA trajectories were observed for SLF I, SLF III, and IFOF. However, the right AF exhibited a linear increase with age, while the right UF showed no significant age-related association. Across both hemispheres, non-FA metrics (MD, AD, and RD) consistently demonstrated U-shaped quadratic relationships with age in all seven tracts, indicating a decrease in diffusivity during maturation followed by an increase in aging.

Associations Between White Matter Tracts and Reading Abilities

Partial correlation analyses (controlling for age, gender, and IQ) revealed significant associations between white matter integrity and reading performance. In the left hemisphere, both WRE and SSR scores were significantly positively correlated with the average FA of the AF, SLF III, and IFOF. Complementary analyses generally showed negative correlations between reading scores and diffusivity metrics (MD, AD, RD) in these tracts. A similar pattern was observed in the right hemisphere: reading scores positively correlated with FA in the SLF I, SLF III, and IFOF, with SSR also correlating with the right AF. To validate specificity, identical analyses were performed on control tracts (CST and OR); no significant correlations were found between reading scores and these control tracts, confirming that the observed associations are specific to reading-related pathways.

Age-Stratified Correlations Between Reading and Diffusion Metrics

To explore age-dependent interactions, we analyzed correlations separately within groups (CH, YA, MOA). Significant group differences emerged in the left SLF I and SLF II. Specifically, the CH group exhibited a significant negative correlation between average FA and reading performance (both WRE and SSR) in these dorsal tracts. Correspondingly, children showed significant positive correlations between reading scores and RD/MD. In contrast, the YA and MOA groups showed no significant correlations or trends toward positive associations. Fisher’s z-tests confirmed that the correlation coefficients in the CH group were significantly different from those in the YA and MOA groups. This finding suggests a developmental shift in the structure-function relationship of the dorsal pathway, where the typical "higher FA = better reading" pattern does not apply to Chinese children in these specific tracts. No significant age-dependent interactions were observed in the right hemisphere.

Conclusion

This study provides a comprehensive examination of the microstructural development of reading-related white matter tracts and their association with reading proficiency in Chinese readers across the lifespan.

Lifespan Trajectories of White Matter:Our findings largely confirmed a nonlinear, inverted U-shaped developmental trajectory for FA in most reading-related tracts (e.g., AF, SLF I, SLF III, IFOF, UF), characterized by maturation during childhood and adolescence followed by decline in aging. This mirrors the trajectory of reading ability itself. Notably, the left ILF and right AF deviated from this pattern, exhibiting a sustained linear increase in FA well into adulthood. Given the role of the left ILF in orthographic processing and the right AF in visuospatial processing (Gao et al., 2022; Su et al., 2018), this linear growth suggests a prolonged maturation window and sustained reliance on these tracts for Chinese reading. The cognitive demands of logographic processing—requiring intensive visual-semantic integration—may delay the aging of these specific pathways, providing a neural basis for cognitive resilience in older Chinese readers.

Core, Age-Independent Reading Network:We identified a robust, age-independent association between reading fluency and the microstructure of the bilateral AF, SLF III, and IFOF. Higher FA in these tracts consistently predicted better performance across all age groups. The bilateral involvement of these dorsal (AF, SLF III) and ventral (IFOF) tracts supports the view that Chinese reading recruits a widespread bilateral network, likely due to the spatial-holistic nature of character processing (Tan et al., 2005). These tracts appear to form the stable structural backbone of the reading network, facilitating phonological decoding and semantic access regardless of developmental stage.

Age-Dependent Mechanisms in the Dorsal Stream:Crucially, we observed a developmental shift in the left SLF I and SLF II. In children (CH group), lower FA (and higher RD) in these tracts was associated with better reading performance—a negative correlation that vanished in adults. This counterintuitive finding may reflect distinct neural strategies. The SLF I and II support domain-general functions like spatial attention and sensory integration. Less-skilled child readers might rely heavily on these dorsal tracts as a compensatory scaffolding mechanism. Conversely, skilled children may have shifted towards more specialized, automated processing, reducing reliance on these domain-general pathways. Further analysis indicated this effect was driven by Radial Diffusivity (RD), suggesting that the timing of myelination is critical. An atypical or "premature" myelination trajectory in these dorsal tracts might be suboptimal for the flexible plasticity required during early Chinese reading acquisition. The dissociation between SLF branches underscores the importance of anatomically distinct analyses in future developmental research.
