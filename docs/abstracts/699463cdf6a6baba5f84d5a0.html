Introduction

Magnetic resonance-guided focused ultrasound (MRgFUS) thalamotomy has emerged as a promising treatment for medication-refractory essential tremor (ET) 1. Although disrupted topological properties have been reported in ET 2-4, the impact of MRgFUS on functional brain network organization remains unclear. Therefore, we performed graph theory analysis to investigate changes in small-world properties and modular architecture in ET patients six months after unilateral MRgFUS of the thalamic ventral intermediate (VIM) nucleus. 

Methods

We enrolled 37 ET (mean age 69.71 ± 10.81 years) patients who underwent unilateral VIM-MRgFUS and clinical/MRI assessments at baseline and 6 months. fMRI data were preprocessed using CONN 22a (http://www.nitrc.org/projects/conn), including realignment, slice-timing correction, co-registration, segmentation, normalization, smoothing (6 mm FWHM), and denoising with CompCor, motion regressors, and a 0.01 Hz high-pass filter. Functional connectivity matrices were constructed using the Human Brainnetome Atlas (https://atlas.brainnetome.org/). Graph-theoretical metrics were calculated across sparsity levels (0.05–0.50), with AUC used for threshold-independent estimates. Community detection employed a modified greedy algorithm 5; inter-/intra-modular connectivity and nodal properties were assessed. Statistical analyses were performed in SPSS 27 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Illinois), with normality assessed via the Shapiro–Wilk test. Baseline–follow-up differences were evaluated using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon tests as appropriate, and associations with FTM scores were examined using Spearman correlations. Based on a priori hypotheses, one-tailed tests were applied to global metrics, while false discovery rate (FDR) correction was used for local and modular measures.

Results

Tremor severity and tremor-related disability showed robust clinical improvement after MRgFUS (p < 0.001), with treated-side tremor scores improving by 65.39% and FTM C scores by 66.97%. Small-worldness and normalized clustering coefficient significantly increased after MRgFUS (p < 0.05), while clustering coefficient, characteristic path length, normalized characteristic path length, global efficiency, local efficiency and modularity remained unchanged (Fig. 1a). Community detection analysis revealed a modular organization consisting of six functional modules (Q = 0.430) (Fig. 1b). Trend-level enhancements in inter-modular connectivity were observed between frontoparietal and subcortical modules, as well as between frontoparietal and frontotemporal-parietal modules (Fig. 1c). Within these modules, betweenness centrality increased in specific cortical hubs, including the left superior frontal gyrus (p = 0.036, FDR-corrected), the right superior parietal lobule (p = 0.036, FDR-corrected), and the left postcentral gyrus (p = 0.045, FDR-corrected). 

Conclusion

The findings suggest that unilateral VIM MRgFUS induces a reorganization of functional networks, enhancing the integration of cortico-subcortical and large-scale cortical networks, which has been reported to be impaired in ET.
