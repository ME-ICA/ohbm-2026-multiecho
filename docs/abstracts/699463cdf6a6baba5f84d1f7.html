Introduction

The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) is critical for emotional regulation, decision-making, and social cognition.1 Adult neuroimaging reveals medial-lateral functional specialization: medial OFC engages limbic-default networks for subjective valuation and self-referential processing, while lateral OFC recruits frontoparietal circuits for cognitive control and complex socioemotional behaviors.2 This specialization enables OFC to integrate multimodal information and coordinate adaptive behavior.

Structural studies show protracted myelination extending through adolescence, with OFC among the last cortical regions to mature. Yet neurodevelopmental disorders implicating OFC dysfunction—including autism and conduct problems—emerge during early childhood, indicating critical organizational windows in infancy.3 Traditional developmental studies using cross-sectional designs and adult-derived parcellations cannot resolve when functional specialization emerges or capture age-specific organizational dynamics.

We systematically map OFC functional architecture across the first three years using spatiotemporal parcellation applied to longitudinal resting-state fMRI. This framework uniquely integrates connectivity patterns with developmental trajectories. Our data-driven approach characterizes functional subdivisions with heterogeneous maturation timelines and quantifies dynamic network integration through meta-analytic decoding. Cross-cultural validation confirms these developmental principles while identifying population-relevant organizational patterns.

Methods

Participants and Image Acquisition

The BCP dataset comprised 475 longitudinal scans from 230 healthy term-born infants (105 males, 125 females; ages 0-36 months) acquired on 3T Siemens Prisma scanners. The CBCP included 193 scans from 178 infants on 3T United Imaging scanners across three Chinese sites. All procedures were IRB-approved with parental consent. Data underwent infant-specific fMRI preprocessing with comprehensive quality control.4

Spatiotemporal Functional Parcellation

We developed a spatiotemporal integrated parcellation approach using OFC regions from the AAL3 atlas.5 Voxel-wise functional connectivity was calculated as Pearson correlations between each OFC voxel's BOLD time series and rest of brain regions, with Fisher's r-to-z transformation. To characterize developmental trajectories, we applied generalized additive mixed models (GAMMs) to each voxel-region connection, incorporating age-related changes (cubic spline basis, k=3), sex, scanning site, mean framewise displacement as covariates, and subject ID as random effects. Fitted connectivity values were generated at monthly intervals (0-36 months). Spatiotemporal feature vectors (3046 voxels × 5624 features) concatenated connectivity profiles across regions and timepoints (Figure 1a). Hierarchical clustering with average linkage determined OFC subregions (Figure 1b-c), with optimal cluster number validated by elbow method, silhouette coefficients, and cross-validation stability (Adjusted Rand Index).

Developmental Characterization

Region-level connectivity trajectories were modeled using GAMMs and classified into seven patterns: linear increase/decrease, S-shaped, U-shaped/inverted U-shaped, and flat (Figure 2a). Voxel-wise analyses examined whole-brain connectivity for each OFC subregion with Gaussian Random Field correction (voxel P<0.00001, cluster P<0.01). Functional network integration was assessed via spatial correlations between OFC connectivity maps and 10 canonical networks. Functional decoding used Neurosynth meta-analysis for top-20 cognitive terms per subregion. To examine the generalizability and robustness of our OFC parcellation findings, spatial correspondence between BCP and CBCP parcellations was evaluated using Brain Surrogate Maps with Autocorrelated Spatial Heterogeneity (BrainSMASH) with 1000 spatially-autocorrelated surrogate maps. Developmental trajectory consistency was assessed via Pearson correlations, validated with 1000 spatially-constrained permutations.

Results

Spatiotemporal Parcellation Identifies Six OFC Subregions

Following a posterior-to-anterior, medial-to-lateral, and superior-to-inferior organization, the infant OFC was parcellated into six distinct subregions (adjusted rand index=0.54, symmetry index=0.9, Figure 1c): posterior-ventromedial OFC (subregion 1), central medial OFC (subregion 2), posterolateral OFC (subregion 3), anterolateral OFC (subregion 4), rectus gyrus (subregion 5), and inferior medial OFC (subregion 6).

Developmental Trajectory Patterns

Log-linear modeling revealed significant three-way interactions among subregions, brain regions, and developmental trajectories (Figure 2a, P<0.0001). Subregions showed distinct developmental progressions (Figure 2b): subregion 1 transitioned from widespread to focused anterior cingulate connectivity (emotion regulation); subregion 2 established default network connections by 6 months (self-referential); subregion 3 formed orbitofrontal-temporal circuits (auditory-emotional integration); subregion 4 expanded toward angular/temporal regions (cognitive control to semantic processing); subregions 5-6 developed default network connectivity (social processing to theory of mind/decision-making). Inter-subregional connectivity showed progressive differentiation, with 8 of 15 pairs exhibiting significant decreases, most pronounced for higher-order cognitive subregions (3-5), while connections with foundational regions (1-2) remained stable, indicating hierarchical refinement. Network corrlation analysis revealed developmental progression: subregion 1 strengthened default mode coupling post-12 months; subregion 2 shifted from executive to default mode; subregion 3 increased auditory coupling; subregion 4 integrated with frontoparietal networks; subregions 5-6 showed predominant default mode connectivity (Figure 2c). CBCP dataset validation demonstrated significant spatial correspondence (adjusted mutual information=0.532, Pperm<0.001) and high developmental trajectory consistency (r=0.63-0.94, all Pperm<0.0001). CBCP dataset exhibited population-specific functional organization: emotion regulation and self-cognition systems showed integrated patterns, whereas executive control and language processing systems exhibited differentiated profiles.

Conclusion

This study revealed the subregional organization and developmental dynamics of the infant OFC through spatiotemporal integrated analysis, demonstrating progressive functional specialization from domain-general processing toward region-specific cognitive-social functions. The six anatomically and functionally distinct subregions exhibited heterogeneous developmental trajectories and hierarchical functional differentiation, emphasizing the core role of the OFC in early emotional, cognitive, and social development. Cross-cultural validation demonstrated both universal developmental principles and population-specific organizational patterns, with CBCP infants exhibiting integrated emotion-self systems and specialized executive-language differentiation. These findings establish a comprehensive framework for understanding OFC functional organization during early childhood, revealing progressive specialization and cross-population variations that align with the emergence of emotion, self-awareness, and social cognition.
