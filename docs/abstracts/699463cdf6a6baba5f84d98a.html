Introduction

Cognitive ability undergoes substantial development during adolescence, accompanied by pronounced interindividual variability. Accumulating evidence1 suggests that this variability reflects the combined influence of multifactorial exposome and genetic factors. Critically, the impact of these factors on cognitive development is thought to operate through ongoing brain maturation processes, which are highly dynamic during adolescence1. However, prior studies have largely examined isolated factors or cross-sectional associations, limiting insight into multivariate pathways and longitudinal mechanisms. Here, we systematically investigate mediation patterns linking comprehensive environmental, genetic, and pubertal influences with brain maturation and cognitive development, with the aim of identifying key factor domains and brain attributes that statistically mediate adolescent cognitive trajectories.

Methods

We analyzed data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study, including 1,112 adolescents recruited from 22 sites. A comprehensive set of exposome and genetic factors was constructed from 166 variables across nine domains, spanning early development, life events, family, neighborhood and school environments, puberty, substance use, temperament, and genetic factors. Brain maturation was characterized using 4,882 multimodal MRI–derived structural and functional measures. For each participant, we calculated the general factor of cognitive ability based on 6 cognitive test scores2. Based on these multidimensional phenotypes, we adopted longitudinal machine learning approaches3 to investigate brain association patterns and to characterize mediation pathways linking multifactorial factors, brain maturation, and cognitive development.

Results

Based on the similarity of brain association patterns, genetic factors exhibited the strongest shared brain-related associations with cognition. Family income, neighborhood population density and DHEA level showed the next strongest associations, exceeding those observed for other exposome domains.

Identified longitudinal mediation patterns highlighted two brain regions showing the most consistent and robust mediation effects across multiple factor domains. The left inferior part of the precentral sulcus is related to four factor domains: family, life events, school and substance use. Whereas the right fronto-marginal gyrus and sulcus is related to three factor domains: family, life events and neighborhood.

Conclusion

Genetic factors demonstrated substantially stronger overall brain-related associations with cognition compared with individual exposome components, underscoring the prominent role of genetic influences on interindividual variability in cognitive ability.

However, our further longitudinal analyses showed that domains related to family and life events exhibited the most pronounced associations with brain development and subsequent cognitive outcomes. Variability in cognitive development during adolescence may be particularly shaped by modifiable, experience-dependent environmental factors.
