Introduction

Anorexia nervosa (AN) is a severe disorder with one of the highest psychiatric mortality rates and frequently emerges during late-childhood or adolescence. Chronic undernutrition can lead to widespread metabolic and hormonal alterations, multi-organ complications and is frequently associated with psychiatric comorbidities. Despite growing support for a metabo-psychiatric model integrating brain–body interactions, the pathophysiology of AN remains poorly understood, highlighting the need for biological approaches to elucidate underlying brain mechanisms, especially in early-onset forms occurring during critical periods of brain maturation. At the neurobiological level, structural MRI studies in children with early-onset AN (EOAN; onset of AN under 13 years) have demonstrated widespread reductions in cortical thickness, increased cerebrospinal fluid volume and reduced thalamic volume (Moreau et al. 2025), raising questions about their functional consequences during brain development. Functional connectivity data in children remain scarce and resting state functional MRI (rs-fMRI) studies in late-adolescent and young adults with AN reported mixed findings (Boehm et al. 2024)(Bracké et al. 2025). This heterogeneity highlights the methodological sensitivity of rs-fMRI findings and the need for multiverse analyses to assess preprocessing and denoising effects, particularly during early developmental stages. The ENIGMA consortium has developed a open-source software tool, HALFpipe, designed to streamline functional MRI analyses (Waller et al. 2022). HALFpipe integrates well-established neuroimaging software packages to support preprocessing, single-subject feature extraction, quality control, and group-level analyses providing standardized guidelines for rs-fMRI analyses. We therefore implemented HALFpipe to disentangle preprocessing and disease-related effects in functional brain alterations in children with early-onset anorexia nervosa.

Methods

We analyzed data from 131 participants (58 acutely underweight EOAN, 73 controls; mean age 11.1) at Robert-Debré Hospital. Data were processed using HALFpipe. After quality control, 72 participants (41 AN, 31 controls) were included. Connectomes were derived using a combined atlas (Schaefer, Freesurfer, and Buckner) applying five denoising strategies: 1) aCompCor; 2) motion parameters + scrubbing; 3) motion parameters + scrubbing + global signal regression (GSR); 4) motion parameters; 5) motion parameters + GSR (figure 1.). Case-control connectome-wide associations (CWAS) were performed controlling for age, sex,magnetic field strength,scanner and mean framewise displacement. Network-based CWAS were performed by averaging region-of-interest (ROI) time series within each functional network, yielding a 19 × 19 network-level connectome (Yeo 17-network atlas, cerebellum and subcortical networks). CWAS analyses were then conducted on these network-level connectivity matrices, and results were thresholded at q < 0.05 using false discovery rate (FDR) correction. 

Results

Network-level CWAS revealed that functional connectivity differences between children with early-onset anorexia nervosa and controls were highly dependent on the denoising strategy applied (figure 2.). Pipelines relying solely on motion parameter regression identified widespread and diffuse underconnectivity across most functional networks, whereas more conservative approaches incorporating physiological noise correction and/or global signal regression yielded fewer, more focal effects. Across pipelines, recurrent alterations primarily involved sensorimotor, attentional, salience, and control networks; however, the direction, extent, and magnitude of these effects varied substantially depending on preprocessing choices. Notably, the inclusion of global signal regression markedly altered the pattern of significant associations, reducing the number of detected effects while increasing their effect sizes. Overall, these findings indicate that denoising strategy critically influences the detection and interpretation of functional connectivity alterations in pediatric EOAN.

Conclusion

Our findings highlight the need for multiverse analyses to separate methodological from disease-related effects, enhancing reproducibility, within-disorder consistency, and the identification of biologically meaningful transdiagnostic biomarkers in psychiatric neuroimaging.
