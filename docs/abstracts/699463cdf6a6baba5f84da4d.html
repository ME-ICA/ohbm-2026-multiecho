Introduction

Apraxia is a common but heterogenous higher order motor disorder post-stroke, which is highly disabling yet remains poorly understood. Here we identify its core components that are most predictive of disability. While 30-50% of stroke survivors exhibit apraxia, the relationship between specific apraxic deficits and functional independence is unclear. Traditional subtypes (ideational, ideomotor, constructional) may not capture functionally-relevant dimensions. Previous studies were limited by small samples and severe multicollinearity preventing identification of independent predictors. We used comprehensive behavioural phenotyping with varimax-rotated principle component analyses to dissociate distinct apraxia components and determine their differential impact on instrumental activities of daily living in the UK's largest post-stroke cohort comprehensively tested for apraxia.

Methods

84 stroke patients (mean 60.5±11 years, 42% female, median 3.6 years post-stroke, 61% ischaemic) completed comprehensive apraxia battery: gesture production (transitive/intransitive), single/multi-object use, meaningless/meaningful imitation, object recognition, mental rotation, complex figure copy/recall, biological motion. Eleven composite z-scores underwent principal component analysis with varimax rotation following KNN imputation. Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin measure assessed factorability. Multiple regression determined which components independently predicted Frenchay Activities Index. Further analyses will involve lesion symptom mapping regressing principle components to unravel the neural correlates underlying them.

Results

Data showed excellent factorability (KMO=0.807, Bartlett's p<0.001). Varimax rotation revealed three interpretable factors explaining 62% variance: Factor1-Gesture/Praxis (23.2%: single-object=0.85, gesture-intransitive=0.81); Factor2-Visuospatial/Imitation (21.4%: complex-figure-copy=0.81, meaningless-imitation=0.63); Factor3-Executive/Sequential (17.4%: biological-motion=0.77, multi-object=0.68). All three factors independently predicted iADL (Adj-R²=0.338, p<0.001): Gesture/Praxis β=0.82 (p<0.0001), Visuospatial β=0.70 (p<0.0001), Executive β=0.36 (p=0.038). Gesture/praxis showed strongest impact on outcomes. 

Conclusion

Our findings reveal that apraxia fragments into three dissociable components with differential functional impact, challenging traditional unitary deficit models. Gesture production emerged as the strongest determinant of independence, explaining why patients with similar overall severity show markedly different functional outcomes. Visuospatial/imitation abilities—including meaningless imitation and complex figure copying—contribute independently to daily function. The weaker association with executive/sequential deficits suggests compensatory strategies may successfully bypass planning impairments. These distinct components suggest distinct neural underpinnings (being investigated with lesion symptom mapping analyses) that might argue for targeted rehabilitation of the disorder in future, with resources allocated based on component severity.
