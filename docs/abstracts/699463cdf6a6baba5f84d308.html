Introduction

Stroke is a leading cause of mortality and morbidity worldwide. MRI-visible perivascular spaces (PVS) are an emerging marker of cerebral small vessel disease, cognitive decline, and dementia1, and may have prognostic value in stroke. We investigated longitudinal changes in PVS volume and cluster count following ischaemic stroke. 

Methods

PVS volumes and cluster counts were compared between stroke survivors (n=124; 39 women; median age=70 [IQR: 62, 76] years) and healthy controls (n=39; 15 women; median age=69 [66, 72.5] years). MRI scans were acquired at 3 months, 12 months, and 36 months post-stroke2. PVS were automatically segmented from T1-weighted MRI using a validated deep learning algorithm (nnU-Net)3,4. Generalised linear mixed-effects models were used to assess group differences and longitudinal changes in PVS, adjusting for baseline age, sex, total intracranial volume, and BMI. 

Results

At 3 months post-stroke, stroke participants had significantly higher total PVS volume (β=0.31, 95% CI [0.11, 0.5], p=0.003) and PVS cluster count (β=0.3, 95% CI [0.11, 0.5], p=0.003) than controls, but at 12 months, no significant differences in PVS metrics were observed between groups. By 36 months, we observed a significant reduction in PVS volume (β=-0.08, 95%CI [-0.16, -0.01], p=0.03) and cluster count (β=-0.09, 95%CI [-0.17, -0.002], p=0.04) in the stroke group compared to control (Figure 1). Regionally, by 36 months, stroke patients demonstrated significant PVS reductions relative to controls in the parietal (PVS volume: β=-0.9, 95%CI [-0.18, -0.004], p=0.048; PVS cluster counts: β=-0.14, 95%CI [-0.3, 0.02], p=0.001) and frontal lobes (PVS volume: β=-0.11, 95%CI [-0.22, -0.01], p=0.04; PVS cluster counts: β=-0.1, 95%CI [-0.2, 0.01], p=0.06).

Conclusion

These findings suggest that ischaemic stroke is associated with dynamic and regional changes in PVS volume and counts.
