Introduction

In routine infant MRI, early brain maturation is assessed by visual inspection of myelination on 2D T1- and T2-weighted images. This assessment is largely qualitative and observer-dependent, with limited sensitivity to subtle developmental delay.1 Most quantitative neurodevelopmental models, however, are developed using research-grade 3D MRI, which is often infeasible in infant imaging due to long acquisition times and motion sensitivity. We propose a fully automated feature extraction pipeline for clinical 2D infant MRI and demonstrate its utility for brain age prediction and developmental delay detection. The framework achieves accurate brain age prediction (Mean Absolute Error = 1.16 ± 0.08 months) and distinguishes developmental delay from typical development with 0.96 accuracy (ages 6-24 months), supporting clinical translation of quantitative analysis from routine 2D data.

Methods

A total of 2893 participants aged 0-2-year-old who underwent brain MRI examinations (available 2D T1w and T2w images) at Beijing Children's Hospital, Capital Medical University, were included in this study. These were categorized into typically developing children (TDC, n = 2,204), children with clinically diagnosed developmental delay (DD-C, n = 539), and children with both clinical and MRI-based developmental delay (DD-CM, n = 150). 

Our approach (Figure 1) began with the selection of 25 high-quality 2D T1w images from 24-month-old individuals to construct the clinical 2D T1-weighted average template. Using Advanced Normalization Tools (ANTs), we performed both the template creation and the subsequent generation of the 18-region 2D segmentation map by mapping existing 3D atlases.2 To optimize gray-white matter contrast, we generated sT1w/T2w combined images,3 from which we extracted 46 radiomic features (intensity and texture) per region.4 Feature selection was performed on the TDC training subset (80%) and involved two steps: first, removing redundant features (Pearson similarity >90%); and second, iterative screening using Gaussian Process Regression with Automatic Relevance Determination (GPR-ARD) in a ten times ten-fold cross-validation scheme to identify the most predictive features for brain age based on ARD length scales. The final brain age prediction model was then constructed using the 34 most important features on the TDC training subset (80%) and validated on the remaining TDC test subset (20%) and the two Developmental Delay cohorts (DD-C and DD-CM).

Finally, we modeled the normative developmental trajectory for the most significant feature, Corpus Callosum Median (CC-Median), using the Generalized Additive Models for Location, Scale and Shape (GAMLSS) framework, drawing inspiration from WHO growth chart guidelines.5 This GAMLSS model employed the Box-Cox Cole and Green (BCCG) distribution and was smoothed using cubic splines. Model optimization, based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and the Generalized AIC with penalty k=3 (GAIC3), determined the optimal parameters. We subsequently computed and plotted the corresponding centile values (3rd, 10th, 50th, 90th, and 97th percentiles), with the 3rd percentile then utilized as the diagnostic threshold for binary classification between the TDC and DD-CM cohorts.

Results

Ten-fold cross-validation on the TDC dataset showed high brain age prediction accuracy (MAE = 1.16 ± 0.08 months). On the independent test set (Figure 2A-D), prediction error increased across groups, with MAE rising from TDC (1.19 months) to DD-C (1.60 months) and DD-CM (2.17 months). Consistently, mean predicted age difference (PAD) values showed a progressively more negative shift across the same groups (TDC: 0.06, DD-C: –0.56, DD-CM: –2.09 months). The GAMLSS-derived CC-Median normative trajectory (Figure 2E) showed that many DD-CM cases fell below the 3rd percentile, yielding a classification accuracy of 0.96 (balanced accuracy 0.91) for ages 6-24 months.

Conclusion

Our study establishes a data-driven framework for extracting quantitative developmental information from routine clinical 2D infant MRI. The proposed method enables accurate brain age prediction and identifies CC-Median as a critical, interpretable biomarker for early detection of developmental delay. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of quantitative neurodevelopmental assessment from conventional 2D MRI and highlight its potential to accelerate clinical translation and advance research into neurodevelopmental disorders.
