Introduction

Introduction: Preclinical research indicates that the integrity of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) is an important resilience factor. Stress and maladaptive stress-related behavioural alterations have been associated with low-grade peripheral inflammation and inflammation-associated impairments in BBB function that allow inflammation mediators to enter the brain parenchyma and adversely affect neural processes. Further, there is preclinical evidence suggesting that the mTOR pathway regulates BBB function and that pharmacological inhibition of this pathway may improve stress resilience.

Methods

Methods: Translating these results to the human, we aim to establish a prospective association between BBB integrity and stress resilience in stressor-exposed individuals at risk to develop stress-related mental health problems. Using an observational study design, we primarily exploit assumed natural inter-individual variation in BBB integrity, assessed with neuroimaging, which we attempt to relate to inter-individual variation in stress resilience. To measure BBB integrity, a Multiple-Echo time Arterial Spin Labeling (multi-TE ASL) sequence is used. We hypothesize that better whole-brain BBB integrity predicts better stress resilience. As a further measure to maximize sensitivity, we attempt to enhance inter-individual variation in BBB integrity by administering the indirect mTOR pathway inhibitor metformin to half of the participants, using a randomized parallel-group placebo-controlled double-blind multi-centre experimental design. Participants are scanned before and after a 12-week period of pharmacological manipulation. Over 36 weeks, participants also provide reports about their stressor exposure and their mental health problems at four-week intervals. 

Results

This experimental-interventional approach allows us to ask whether metformin improves whole-brain BBB integrity and whether this in turn improves stress resilience, via mediation analysis. Such a finding would suggest a causal role for BBB integrity in stress resilience.

In additional analyses, blood-based biomarkers and regional neuroimaging metrics are used as measures of BBB integrity, and the influence of socio-demographic, anamnestic, psycho-social, immunological, metabolic, and other neuroimaging variables on BBB function and stress resilience is also considered. 

Conclusion

Discussion: The study may, for the first time, delineate a relationship between BBB function and stress resilience in humans and potentially implicate BBB integrity as a causal factor in resilience. Findings may inform the development of novel pharmacological strategies to improve BBB function and stress resilience, to be investigated in follow-up studies.

Trial Registration: Registered under National Library of medicine under the ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06965868.
