Introduction

Patients with obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) show deficits across both primary sensorimotor and higher-order cognitive domains. 1 Recent brain connectome studies have revealed a principal sensorimotor-to-association gradient that captures the macroscale hierarchy of functional brain organization, supporting the spectrum from unimodal to transmodal processing systems. 2 Disruptions in this gradient may reflect aberrant hierarchical information integration, which could contribute to the sensorimotor and cognitive deficits observed in OCD. Nevertheless, such gradient-level alterations in OCD remain largely unexplored. Moreover, sexual dimorphism in OCD has been well documented, including differences in symptom profiles and brain structure 3, highlighting sex as a critical factor in OCD pathophysiology that may also modulate the functional connectome disruptions. In this study, we investigated alterations in the principal functional connectome gradient in a well-characterized cohort of medication-free, non-comorbid OCD patients. To elucidate their functional and molecular relevance, we conducted meta-analytic functional decoding and postmortem gene expression analyses. Additionally, we examined sex-specific patterns of functional connectome gradient alterations.

Methods

A total of 92 OCD patients (mean age = 29.4±8.7 years; 57 males, 35 females) and 90 healthy controls (mean age = 28.3±10.9 years; 55 males, 35 females) were included. For each subject, voxel-wise resting-state functional connectivity matrices were constructed and used to compute individual functional connectome gradients via diffusion map embedding. We focused on the principal sensorimotor-association gradient, which captures the greatest variance in connectome organization. Group differences in global, voxel-wise, and network-level gradients were assessed using general linear models (GLM), controlling for age, sex, and mean framewise displacement. To functionally characterize regions showing significant group differences, we used the Neurosynth database to compute spatial correlations between thresholded group-difference maps and meta-analytic activations maps associated with each feature term. To further elucidate the molecular basis, microarray-based gene expression data from the Allen Human Brain Atlas were preprocessed using a standard pipeline, including probe-to-gene verification, filtering, mapping to brain regions, and normalization. Partial least squares (PLS) regression was then applied to relate transcriptional profiles (z-score normalized gene-expression matrix: 347 regions × 15631 genes) to between-group gradient differences (z-score vector of case-control difference: 347 regions × 1), with significance estimated using spatial autocorrelation-corrected permutation tests. Functional enrichment analyses of Gene Ontology (GO) biological processes and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways were subsequently conducted. Finally, diagnosis-by-sex interactions were examined using additional GLM, followed by post hoc comparisons between OCD and control groups separately in males and females.

Results

The spatial patterns of the group-averaged principal gradient maps were highly similar between OCD and controls, organized along a gradual axis from the sensorimotor and visual networks toward the default mode network (Fig. 1a). No group difference was observed in global gradient metrics. However, focal alterations were detected, with higher gradient values in the precentral gyrus, supplementary motor area, lingual gyrus, and cuneus, and lower values in the inferior parietal lobule. At the network level, increased gradients were observed in the sensorimotor network and decreased gradients in the frontoparietal network (Fig. 1b). Within the OCD group, higher gradient values in the right precentral gyrus were positively correlated with anxiety severity (Fig. 1c). Neurosynth decoding revealed that regions with higher gradients in OCD were primarily associated with motor functions, whereas those with lower gradients were linked to tasks requiring top-down attentional control such as working memory (Fig. 1d). Transcriptional association analysis revealed a significant spatial correspondence between gradient alterations and gene expression patterns (r=0.437, p<0.0001), characterized by high expression in the visual and sensorimotor cortices and low expression in the frontal and temporal regions (Fig. 1e). Gene enrichment analysis indicated that these genes were mainly associated with synaptic processes, including synaptic signaling and transmission, as well as presynaptic and postsynaptic organization, and neurodevelopmental pathways such as neuronal projection development (Fig. 1f).

As shown in Fig. 2a, the overall distributions of principal gradient values were comparable between the OCD and control groups. However, the distribution range was expanded in male patients compared with male controls and compressed in female patients compared with female controls. Global gradient metrics revealed that male patients exhibited a wider gradient range and greater variance than male controls, whereas female patients showed a narrower range and less variance relative to female controls. Regionally, female patients displayed decreased gradient values in the medial prefrontal cortex, whereas no significant difference was observed in males. At the network level, a similar sex-specific pattern emerged as in the regional analysis, with differences observed in females but not in males, in the sensorimotor, limbic, and default mode networks. Specifically, compared with female controls, female patients showed significantly increased gradient values in the sensorimotor network and decreased values in the limbic and default mode network, whereas no significant differences were found between male patients and male controls (Fig. 2b).

Conclusion

The sensorimotor-association gradient in the functional connectome was preserved to some extent in OCD, as the overall hierarchical organization remained relatively intact. However, patients showed a subtle tendency toward a compressed gradient structure, with higher gradient values in the sensorimotor regions and lower values in the higher-order associative cortices. These focal alterations suggest diminished functional differentiation across cortical hierarchies, which may underlie impairments in both sensorimotor and higher-order cognitive functions in OCD. The gradient alterations were further associated with anxiety severity and transcriptional profiles enriched for synaptic and neurodevelopmental processes, indicating a molecular basis for disrupted large-scale functional connectivity. Moreover, sex-specific analyses revealed divergent patterns, where male patients exhibited an overall expansion of gradient range and variability, whereas female patients showed a global compression with focal alterations in the medial prefrontal cortex, sensorimotor, default mode, and limbic networks. Together, these findings highlight sex-dependent disruptions of the cortical functional hierarchy in OCD, linking macroscale connectome alterations to molecular architecture and clinical manifestations.
