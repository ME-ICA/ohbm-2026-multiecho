Introduction

Depressive disorder is a leading cause of global disability[1], yet many patients show inadequate response to first-line treatments. Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) is an effective neuromodulation therapy for depression, with the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (lDLPFC) as its most commonly used target. This approach aims to modulate emotion-regulating nodes such as the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex (sgACC) via a circuit model. Although static functional connectivity-based "anti-subgenual" targeting has been proposed, its failure to predict efficacy in large-scale prospective studies reflecting real-world clinical diversity highlights the fundamental limitations of static connectivity models[2].

More importantly, while rTMS treatment courses are short (several weeks), antidepressant effects can persist for months[3]. This "short-stimulation–long-effect" dissociation suggests that therapeutic efficacy does not arise from sustained modulation of static circuits but may depend on intervention-induced neuroplasticity that unfolds over time. Therefore, a treatment-responsive dynamic marker capable of tracking such plasticity is needed.

We hypothesize that the efficacy of rTMS stems from its ability to initiate self-sustaining neuroplasticity rather than from sustained modulation of static circuits. To test this, a macroscopic plasticity indicator is required. Structure–function coupling (SFC) quantifies the alignment between a region's functional connectivity and its anatomical wiring[4], reflecting a dynamic balance between neural stability and functional flexibility. In depression, the intrinsically low SFC in transmodal hubs such as the sgACC may confer greater potential for intervention-driven plasticity, and depression-specific alterations in SFC are associated with neurotransmitter systems. Thus, SFC may serve as a network-level marker of pathophysiology and potential plasticity.

Methods

1 Study Design and Participants

This randomized controlled trial was conducted at Peking University Sixth Hospital (Feb 2023–Mar 2025). Outpatients/inpatients (aged 18–65) with moderate-to-severe major depressive or bipolar II disorder (HAMD-17≥18, ≥1 prior adequate antidepressant treatment failure) were enrolled. Key exclusions included major psychiatric comorbidities, MRI/TMS contraindications, and neuromodulation treatments within the past year. The study was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300068413) and approved by the local Ethics Committee.

2 Treatment Protocol

All participants received 20 sessions of intermittent theta-burst stimulation (iTBS) targeting the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (lDLPFC) using neuronavigation. Treatment was delivered twice daily (2-hour interval) over 2 weeks (total 36,000 pulses). Stimulation intensity was set at 90% of resting motor threshold, adjusted for scalp-cortex distance. Concomitant psychotropic medications were stabilized.

3 Clinical and Neuroimaging Assessments

Depressive symptoms were rated using the HAMD-17 at baseline (V1), week 1 (V2), and week 2 (V3). Response was defined as ≥50% reduction in the HAMD-17 score; remission as HAMD-17<8.MRI scans (T1, resting-state fMRI, diffusion MRI) were acquired at the same three timepoints on a Siemens 3T scanner.

4 Image Processing

T1-weighted images were processed with fMRIPrep to perform skull stripping, tissue segmentation, and spatial normalization, generating a five-tissue-type segmentation for downstream analysis. Functional MRI data were preprocessed using fMRIPrep (with --fs-no-reconall), including motion correction, slice-timing correction, susceptibility distortion correction, coregistration to T1, and spatial normalization. Subsequent denoising was performed with xcp_d, applying a '36P' nuisance regression strategy to mitigate physiological and motion artifacts. Diffusion-weighted data were preprocessed using MRtrix3: denoising (dwidenoise), susceptibility and eddy-current distortion correction with FSL's topup and eddy, coregistration to T1 space, and bias-field correction with ANTs.

5 Connectivity and SFC Computation

Functional connectivity (FC) matrices (246 Brainnetome[5] regions) were derived from Pearson correlations of regional time series. Structural connectivity (SC) matrices were constructed using streamline counts from tractography.

Structure–function coupling (SFC) for each region was calculated as the Spearman correlation between its SC and FC profiles. Primary analysis focused on SFC within the sgACC (A32sg). Early (V2–V1) and 2-week (V3–V1) ΔSFC were computed.

6 Statistical and Predictive Modeling

sgACC SFC trajectories were compared between remitters and non-remitters using t-tests. A logistic regression model (L2 penalty) with 5-fold cross-validation was built to predict 2-week HAMD remission using early ΔSFC. Mediation analysis (5000 bootstraps) tested whether early ΔSFC mediated the relationship between baseline lDLPFC-sgACC FC and HAMD reduction rate. Analyses controlled for polypharmacy using six-dimensional medication vectors.

7 Identifying subgroups through k-means clustering

Neurophysiological subgroups were identified by applying k-means clustering to the longitudinal sgACC SFC trajectories of each participant, defined by their SFC values at V1,V2, and V3. The optimal number of clusters was determined by evaluating candidate solutions (k=2 to 6) using two internal validation metrics: the silhouette score, which assesses cluster cohesion and separation, and the Calinski-Harabasz index, which quantifies the ratio of between-cluster to within-cluster dispersion. 

Results

Results

1 Participant Characteristics

The study included 105 participants with complete neuroimaging and clinical data. Based on the 2-week outcome measurements, 37 (35.2%) were classified as remitters (HAMD-17<8) and 68 (64.8%) as non-remitters.

2 Early sgACC SFC Dynamics Predict Treatment Response

The change in sgACC SFC during the first treatment week (early ΔSFC) robustly differentiated future remitters from non-remitters (p < 0.01), with remitters showing a greater increase. Early ΔSFC magnitude correlated with week-2 HAMD reduction rate (r = 0.258, p < 0.05). In predictive modeling, a logistic regression model using early ΔSFC as the sole predictor showed the best performance (AUC = 0.68, p < 0.01), confirming its utility as a prognostic biomarker.

3 SFC Plasticity Mediates Static Targeting Effects

Mediation analyses revealed that the early change in sgACC SFC fully explains how baseline static circuit targeting leads to HAMD reduction rate. 

Statistically, at the 2-week endpoint, the indirect effect was significant ( β = −0.080, 95% CI [−0.176, −0.024]), supported by a strong path a (β = −0.300, p = 0.0019) and a significant path b (β = 0.225, p = 0.0210). The direct effect was non significant (c′: β = 0.143, p = 0.1562), indicating full mediation. At 6 weeks, the indirect effect remained significant (β = −0.050, 95% CI [−0.131, −0.005]), again driven by the same robust path a (β = −0.300, p = 0.0019), while path b showed a positive though marginal effect (β = 0.160, p = 0.1021). 

Together, these results establish that early sgACC plasticity is the key pathway through which baseline circuit connectivity translates into acute and early sustained clinical benefit, with a stronger anti-subgenual target promoting greater plasticity, which in turn drives greater symptom improvement.

4 Neurophysiological Subgroups via SFC Trajectories

To directly investigate this possibility and move beyond a priori remission-based classification, we employed an unsupervised k-means clustering analysis on longitudinal sgACC SFC measures to identify data-driven neurophysiological subgroups. Evaluation of cluster solutions (k=2-6) using silhouette and Calinski-Harabasz indices consistently identified a four-cluster model as optimal, revealing distinct trajectories of neuroplastic response to TMS treatment. 

This analysis revealed four distinct subgroups (Subgroup 1-4) with characteristic SFC trajectories. The four subgroups did not differ significantly in demographic and clinical characteristics, including age, age at onset, baseline severity, proportion of females, number of inpatient admissions, and treatment history. A linear mixed-effects model, controlling for baseline severity, revealed a significant main effect of subgroup on overall depression severity (HAMD) across the 12-week follow-up (F(3,99)=12.41, P<.0001). While all groups improved, a consistent clinical hierarchy emerged: Subgroup 4 demonstrated the most rapid and robust symptom reduction, while Subgroup 1 exhibited the poorest response. Post-hoc comparisons at week 2, for instance, showed that Subgroup 1 had a HAMD score 2.68 points higher than Subgroup 4 (95%CI [0.65–4.70], P=0.00999). This differential response was specific to core depressive and retardation symptoms, with no significant differences in anxiety or sleep factors. The rate of early improvement further distinguished the subgroups, with Subgroup 4 showing a significantly greater HAMA reduction rate at week 1 and 2 compared to Subgroup 1(at week 2: -0.159, P=0.0002). These findings demonstrate that SFC-sgACC-based subgroups map onto clinically meaningful subgroups with distinct prognostic profiles.

Conclusion

In this mechanistic neuroimaging analysis nested within a randomized rTMS clinical trial, we identify sgACC SFC as a dynamic marker that tracks treatment-related neuroplasticity over the course of treatment. Early increases in sgACC SFC differentiated subsequent remitters from non-remitters and correlated with later symptom improvement, indicating that clinically meaningful change is detectable within the first treatment week. Moreover, mediation analyses suggest that early sgACC SFC change statistically links baseline lDLPFC–sgACC circuit connectivity to clinical benefit, supporting a model in which dynamic plasticity—rather than static circuit strength alone—captures a key pathway to response. Beyond outcome-based labels, unsupervised clustering of individual sgACC SFC trajectories revealed four neurophysiological subgroups with distinct plasticity patterns that mapped onto differential depressive symptom trajectories during follow-up despite broadly comparable baseline characteristics.

Together, these findings position longitudinal sgACC SFC dynamics as a parsimonious candidate biomarker for probing mechanism, early prognosis, and patient stratification. This dynamic framework complements the comparative evaluation of efficacy across localization strategies in the parent clinical trial, by providing a quantitative readout of the plasticity processes through which effective stimulation may translate into sustained antidepressant outcomes.
