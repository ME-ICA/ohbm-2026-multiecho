Introduction

Neuroprotective Erythropoietin (NeuroEPO) is a neuro-pharmacological agent with emerging potential to modulate brain activity in neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson’s disease (PD). EEG provides a direct window into neural dynamics, enabling characterization of both oscillatory alpha (α) components (Aα, Bα, Eα, Fα) and aperiodic Xi (ξ) activity, which are disrupted in PD and closely linked to cognitive function. The Xi-AlphaNET have bene used effectively to describe the lifespan trajectories of the sources 1. This framework emphasizes the importance of explicitly modeling spatial covariance and network structure, motivating whole-brain, multivariate approaches.

Despite these advances, many pharmacological EEG studies rely on univariate or region-isolated analyses, limiting sensitivity to spatial dependencies and inter-individual variability across the cortex. Recent work demonstrates that separating oscillatory (alpha) and aperiodic EEG components provides complementary information about large-scale cortical dynamics, with the aperiodic component capturing broadband, network-level changes not explained by oscillatory power alone. This supports whole-brain, network-based analyses of pharmacological modulation of EEG spectra, as implemented in the present NeuroEPO study 2. Here, we address these limitations by investigating dose-dependent effects of NeuroEPO on EEG cortical spectral parameters, decomposing EEG source activity into oscillatory alpha (α) and aperiodic Xi (ξ) components using the Xi–AlphaNET framework, which models connectome-constrained cortical dynamics. Effects were examined across regions of interest defined by the Yeo-7 parcellation, providing an interpretable representation of large-scale functional networks 3. We applied permutation-based whole-brain inference to assess dose-dependent NeuroEPO effects on alpha (α) and Xi (ξ) EEG components derived using Xi–AlphaNET across Yeo-7 cortical networks in Parkinson’s disease.

Methods

Participants and Data Collection

EEG data were collected from 67 subjects receiving varying cumulative doses of NeuroEPO, in PD patients and in placebo, at baseline and after a 9-month post-treatment follow-up.

Spectral Analysis and Standardization: Spectral components were estimated at the cortical source level using the Xi-AlphaNET inverse solution and subsequently projected onto regions of interest (ROI) defined by the Yeo-7 parcellation. For each subject and ROI, the primary outcome was the standardized change in spectral parameters from baseline to post-intervention, computed as the difference between post- and pre-treatment values normalized by the baseline variability across subjects. To stabilize variance and reduce deviations from Gaussianity, outcomes were Box–Cox transformed and z-scored. Extreme values were excluded using an interquartile range–based criterion (|Z| > 2.5).

Robust Linear Regression and Permutation Testing: Dose effects were estimated at each ROI using robust linear regression with Huber loss, accounting for subject-specific random intercepts to model inter-individual variability. To test the null hypothesis of no dose effect on spectral change across the brain, permutation-based inference was performed using 5,000 permutations, in which dose labels were randomly permuted across subjects while preserving the observed spectral data. The resulting empirical null distribution was used to evaluate whether the observed whole-brain effects exceeded those expected by chance.

Results

The analysis revealed significant dose-dependent changes in EEG spectral parameters across the whole brain, highlighting the impact of NeuroEPO on various cortices. The absolute value of standardized effects for spectral metrics, including α and ξ components, showed notable variation across brain regions. 

The whole-brain standardized effects plot illustrates dose-dependent modulation across spectral parameters at the whole-brain level as shown in Figure 1 (a). The network-level heatmap shows stronger standardized effects within the Dorsal Attention (DAN), Ventral Attention (VAN), and Default Mode (DMN) networks, revealing a gradient in alpha-related effects from sensory to frontal cortical regions as shown in Figure 1 (b). These networks exhibit larger standardized effect sizes, indicating more consistent modulation by NeuroEPO. Asterisks denote statistically significant standardized effects across Yeo-7 networks, with prominent effects observed in the Fronto-Parietal Network (FPN), particularly for parameters related to delay.

The cortical distribution of standardized effects maps the spatial patterns of alpha (α) and aperiodic (ξ) modulation across the cortex as shown in Figure 2. Widespread effects are observed in the visual network (VIS), especially within occipital and temporal cortices, whereas the FPN shows stronger effects in frontal and parietal regions. Warmer colors indicate larger standardized effect sizes, highlighting network-specific sensitivity to NeuroEPO.

The global multivariate test, incorporating permutation procedures, confirmed that the observed dose effects were robust to non-Gaussianity and spatial dependencies. The Yeo-7 parcellation analysis revealed distinct spectral changes across networks, with the DMN showing particularly high standardized effect values, indicating greater involvement or modulation by the intervention. The permutation results reinforced the consistency of these effects across spectral metrics, emphasizing the robustness of the observed dose effects across ROIs.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates dose-dependent modulation of EEG alpha (α) and aperiodic (ξ) components following NeuroEPO administration in Parkinson’s disease, with spatially structured effects across large-scale cortical networks. Strongest standardized effects followed a sensory-to-frontal gradient, prominently involving the Default Mode, Attention, and Fronto-Parietal Networks. These findings highlight the value of whole-brain, permutation-based frameworks for detecting network-level pharmacological effects in neurodegenerative disease.
