Introduction

Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a hormone-related mood disorder characterized by marked affective symptoms and impaired emotional regulation during the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle. Although functional neuroimaging studies implicate altered corticolimbic responsivity, the structural network organization that constrains these functional dynamics remains unclear 1. White matter topology plays a central role in shaping efficient information transfer across large-scale neural systems, and disturbances in structural connectivity may underlie the recurrent emotional instability observed in PMDD 2. To date, however, no study has examined topology-level organization of diffusion-derived brain networks in PMDD. The present study therefore investigated, in a well-characterized sample, whether women with PMDD exhibit alterations in global or regional structural network architecture as well as structural connectivity strength across multiple spatial scales, and whether such alterations relate to symptom severity.

Methods

Sixty-five women with PMDD and forty healthy controls underwent diffusion tensor imaging during the symptomatic (late luteal) phase of the menstrual cycle, confirmed through daily symptom ratings and cycle tracking. All participants were naturally cycling, right-handed, free of psychotropic medications, and had no history of neurological illness. Imaging was performed on a 3T scanner using a standardized diffusion-weighted protocol optimized for whole-brain tractography.  

Preprocessing was conducted in FSL and included brain extraction, correction for eddy-current distortions and head motion, and rotation of b-vectors to maintain accurate gradient alignment. Diffusion tensors and principal diffusion directions were estimated using dtifit. To model complex fiber configurations, we applied FSL’s bedpostx for multi-fiber orientation estimation, after which probabilistic tractography was carried out using probtrackx2. Connectivity matrices were constructed by quantifying the number of streamlines between node pairs and normalizing these values by seed mask volume, resulting in weighted, undirected adjacency matrices for each participant. Two network parcellations were examined to capture both whole-brain and disorder-relevant architecture: (1) a 246-node whole-brain connectome based on the Human Brainnetome (HB) Atlas, and (2) a PMDD-informed network of 192 nodes curated from regions consistently implicated in PMDD and menstrual cycle-related effects 1, 2.  

Graph-theoretical analyses were performed using BRAPH 2.2 3, employing weighted, undirected graph representations. For each participant and network, we computed global metrics including clustering coefficient, global and local efficiency, average degree, modularity, and rich-club organization to characterize integration, segregation, and hub structure. Nodal metrics were also derived to identify region-specific alterations. Group differences in global and nodal measures were assessed using non-parametric permutation testing with false discovery rate correction applied across metrics and nodes to control for multiple comparisons. To evaluate whether PMDD was associated with differences in the strength of specific white matter pathways, we performed Network-Based Statistics (NBS) using NBS version 1.2 4 on the weighted connectivity matrices. Finally, within the PMDD group, we examined associations between structural network properties and symptom severity using partial correlations adjusted for age.

Results

Global network organization. Graph-theoretical analysis revealed consistent disruptions in network architecture across both network scales examined (whole-brain and PMDD-informed). In the whole-brain network, PMDD was associated with markedly reduced clustering coefficient, average degree, rich-club coefficient, and global efficiency (all pFDR < 0.05), reflecting diminished local interconnectedness, reduced overall wiring, and lower capacity for parallel information transfer. Conversely, modularity was significantly increased in PMDD, indicating a more segregated and compartmentalized network structure. These patterns were reproduced in the PMDD-informed network, where PMDD again showed lower clustering, degree, and rich-club organization, together with elevated modularity. Collectively, these findings suggest that women with PMDD exhibit a less integrated, less hub-dominated architecture, with decreased communication efficiency across large-scale networks.  

Nodal network alterations. Widespread regional disturbances were observed across cortical and subcortical structures. Nodes showing reduced degree and rich-club degree in PMDD included the orbitofrontal cortex, middle frontal gyrus, anterior cingulate cortex, insula, putamen, and thalamus: regions critical for emotional evaluation, salience processing, and cognitive control. Reduced efficiency was observed in the anterior cingulate cortex, amygdala, and parahippocampal gyrus, pointing to diminished capacity of these regions to facilitate communication across the broader network. Additionally, reduced participation coefficient in regions such as the precentral gyrus, inferior frontal gyrus, superior temporal gyrus, and amygdala indicated weaker cross-module integration. These nodal disruptions implicate distributed corticolimbic and fronto-striatal systems known to support emotion regulation and sensorimotor-affective coupling.  

Connectivity strength. Network-Based Statistics identified no significant group differences in edge-level connectivity strength across any threshold tested. This suggests that PMDD-related alterations arise primarily from reconfiguration of network topology (how nodes are organized and integrated), rather than from loss or gain of specific structural connections.  

Associations with symptom severity. Within the PMDD group, no significant correlations were observed between global or nodal graph metrics and total DRSP score or any DSM-5 affective symptom dimensions after multiple testing correction. These results indicate that network alterations in PMDD may reflect stable, trait-like neurobiological features rather than direct correlates of symptom severity during the scanned cycle.

Conclusion

This study provides the first evidence that PMDD is characterized by widespread alterations in the topological organization of structural brain networks, rather than focal disruptions of individual pathways. Reduced global integration and diminished hub-like properties within corticolimbic and prefrontal circuits suggest that inefficiencies in anatomical communication may contribute to vulnerability for cyclical affective dysregulation. The findings extend current neurobiological models of PMDD by highlighting large-scale network architecture as a potential structural substrate of emotional lability and underscore the value of multimodal network approaches for understanding hormone-related mood disorders.
