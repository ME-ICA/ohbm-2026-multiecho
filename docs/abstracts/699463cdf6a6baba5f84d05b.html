Introduction

The aging population in Europe is associated with a projected rise in neurodegenerative disorders, creating a strong need for sensitive, standardized, and scalable tools for detecting early neurocognitive alterations. Despite advances in cognitive neuroscience, clinicians still lack a comprehensive, modular system for evaluating visuo-cognitive functions and associated autonomic markers relevant to prodromal stages of these conditions. BegiBrainTool aims to fill this gap by providing a computerized, multimodal battery of visual and cognitive tasks designed to assess spatial and dynamic visual processing, visuo-attentional performance, and autonomic reactivity, thereby supporting both clinical evaluation and research in translational neuroscience.

Methods

BegiBrainTool comprises three interactive modules, all implemented in PsychoPy[1]. Each task is preceded by clear on-screen instructions, and more demanding paradigms include step-by-step training tutorials to ensure task comprehension and reduce cognitive load variability.

The spatial vision module evaluates elementary and semantic visual processing through stimuli parametrically varying in spatial frequency, contrast/luminance thresholds, color sensitivity, and low-level perceptual features associated with early visuo-cognitive encoding.

The dynamic vision and oculomotor module assesses moving-stimuli discrimination, visual search performance, and flicker fusion thresholds, incorporating integrated eye-tracking to capture high-resolution metrics of gaze behavior, oculomotor control, and pupillometric indices linked to attentional and autonomic regulation.

The autonomic response module measures implicit reactivity to elemental and affective visual stimuli, combining pupil dynamics, heart rate variability, and galvanic skin response to index central–autonomic coupling, a domain increasingly recognized as relevant for early neurocognitive decline.

All modules are designed with a unified, user-friendly interface and configurable parameters, enabling adaptation to heterogeneous clinical and experimental contexts. Ongoing validation efforts include testing in healthy controls, using a structured one-hour protocol split into two balanced sessions to minimize fatigue while ensuring comprehensive assessment.

Results

This project is currently ongoing. Initial work focuses on validating the psychophysical parameters, biometrics acquisition pipeline, and cognitive task structure, as well as establishing reproducible protocols for data collection across sites. Expected outcomes include a fully validated, open-source toolbox suited for multimodal assessment of visuo-cognitive and autonomic function.

Conclusion

BegiBrainTool provides a novel, interdisciplinary platform for examining visual and visuo-cognitive processing in the context of neurodegenerative disorders. By integrating modular tasks with advanced biometrics and cognitive neuroscience principles, it offers a powerful framework for early detection, longitudinal monitoring, and mechanistic investigation of neurocognitive decline. Its open and customizable architecture positions BegiBrainTool as a valuable resource for clinicians and neuroscientists addressing the growing burden of neurodegenerative conditions.
