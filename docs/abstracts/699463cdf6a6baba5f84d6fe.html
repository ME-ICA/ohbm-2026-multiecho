Introduction

Artificial intelligence, particularly deep neural networks (DNNs), has become vital to neuroscientific research, including auditory processing.1 Here, DNNs are increasingly used to model auditory perception from environmental sounds over music to speech and language2 by evaluating the predictive performance of activations from different layers of the DNN models in response to auditory stimuli and comparing them to brain and behavioral responses to the same stimuli.2 However, the growing number of DNNs and their scattered implementations across platforms and software packages with limited meta-data create prominent barriers for researchers. The application of and extraction of activation from these DNNs are particularly error-prone and cumbersome. To address these problems, we developed CAFENAP (Collection/Application of & Feature Extraction from deep neural Networks for Auditory Processing), a free and open-source Python package.

Methods

CAFENAP streamlines and standardizes the application of and activation extraction from DNNs focused on auditory processing by providing a common interface to a wide range of models, including widely used large-scale models such as SoundNet and Wav2Vec, alongside smaller, task-specific models developed and published by individual research groups. It facilitates the following key tasks: CAFENAP simplifies accessing, training and applying existing DNNs to auditory data via organizing and utilizing them in a standardized API, including model setup and input validation. This allows users to either use DNNs in a pre-trained state (by downloading respective weights), train them on a new task or stimulus set or utilize them in a randomly-initialized state. After DNNs are trained and/or applied, activations in response to auditory stimuli can be automatically extracted from all DNN layers. Layer activations can be prepared for comparison with brain and/or behavioral data by either time-averaging or summing activations with a temporal dimension or computing representational dissimilarity matrices3 per layer. The respective outcomes can then be submitted to different analytical approaches (e.g., regression or representational similarity analysis (RSA)3) in commonly used neuroimaging software packages such as nilearn or mne, or in general-purpose packages such as scikit-learn. To increase reproducibility and reusability, obtained outputs are accompanied by meta-data files including DNN and layer information and follow a BIDS-like organization.4

Results

Using several open datasets, DNN layer activations were extracted from 10 pre-trained models and compared to brain and behavioral representations via ridge regression5 and RSA3 to demonstrate the utility of CAFENAP. This replicated the results of prior studies, suggesting that DNNs trained on tasks related to auditory processing exhibit a correspondence with auditory processing in the human brain; that is, earlier and middle-layer activations best predicted primary auditory cortex activations, and later-layer activations predicted non-primary auditory cortex activations.2 Furthermore, CAFENAP enabled a straightforward comparison of DNNs trained on different tasks and against randomly initialized DNNs, which are commonly considered baseline models,2 confirming that learning-driven representations outperform random architectures in predicting neural responses to varying degrees.

Conclusion

CAFENAP enables users to utilize DNNs focused on auditory processing in neuroscientific investigations by streamlining and standardizing DNN applications and respective layer activation extraction in a user-friendly and reproducible manner, as well as preparing layer activations for comparison with brain and behavioral responses. The toolbox is available on PyPI and developed and maintained on GitHub, with comprehensive documentation and supports community contributions. Ongoing development plans aim at expanding the model collection and analytical capabilities, including multiple-task networks and lesioning.
