Introduction

Alzheimer's disease (AD), involves progressive neurodegeneration with amyloid-beta plaques and hyperphosphorylated tau. Neurodegeneration begins over a decade before clinical diagnosis, limiting treatment efficacy due to irreversible neuronal loss. While CSF Aβ42 and amyloid PET are standard biomarkers, with the advent of disease-modifying treatments, optimised strategies are needed to best identify an AD process in the context of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and dementia. Recent evidence suggests that volumetric decline in thalamic nuclei occurs in AD1, although this has not been examined from the perspective of AD biomarkers. In this study, we examined the value of incorporating the integrity of individual thalamic nuclei, easily derived from a T1-Weighted MRI, relative to the gold standard of a positive PET-amyloid scan2.

Methods

We used a PET-amyloid centiloid cut-off>24 for diagnostic confirmation of a positive scan3,4 from the ADNI 2 and ADNI 3 datasets. Combined with clinical data (CDR, MMSE), this established groups of 274 MCI and 143 AD subjects; 592 controls were included. We segmented 11 thalamic nuclei and the mamillothalamic tract using HIPS-THOMAS5, which synthesizes white-matter-nulled-like contrast from T1w MRI regardless of field strength/scanner. Volumes were ICV-corrected and harmonized across sites using NeuroCombat. Analysis proceeded in four stages: (1) Group differences in thalamic volumes were assessed; (2) The association between nuclei volumes and centiloid, controlling for age, gender, and education; (3) Random Forest classifiers on controls, MCI, AD patients, with permutation feature importance testing. A two-stage statistical approach first identified the most predictive thalamic nuclei through permutation importance rankings, then sequentially tested their additive value using statistically-paired cross-validation with FDR correction for multiple comparisons; ROC analysis further highlighted differences between models by group with permutation distributions comparisons (4) Subgroup analysis on groups with unusual amyloid load: we compared controls with high amyloid status (centiloid >24; N=156) and amyloid negative dementia patients defined as AD subjects by the ADNI based on MMSE and CDR scores (centiloid <24; N=31); relative to a reference group of controls (centiloid <24 ; N=435).

Results

We found that (1) the volume of all thalamic nuclei differed among all groups except the left ventrolateral anterior (VLa) nucleus. Bilateral anteroventral (AV), Mediodorsal Parafascicular (MD-Pf), and pulvinar (Pul), showed largest effects (effect size  >0.8 for AD vs controls, >0.5 for MCI vs controls). (2) All nuclei except ventral anterior (VA), VLa, and habenular (Hb) correlated with amyloid burden, with AV, MD-Pf, and Pul showing the strongest negative correlations (r[0.22-0.27] ; p-FDR <0.001). Correlation did not differ across groups. (3) Permutation importance identified left AV, left medial geniculate nuclei (MGN), and left Pul as top predictors. Adding these three nuclei to demographics and centiloid prediction models increased diagnostic accuracy by 5.9% (paired t-test p=0.038, Fig. 1A), with a significant AUC improvement of 3.3% for MCI (permutations-p=0.003), 5.1% for AD (permutation-p <0.001) but not for controls (0.8%). (4) Subgroup analysis revealed that: controls with high amyloid significantly differed from controls with low amyloid for most nuclei with small effect size ([0.2- up to 0.57 for the Pul]); amyloid negative dementia patients showed significant atrophy in all nuclei except VA/VLa, with very large effect sizes in bilateral AV, MD-Pf and Pul (Fig. 1B).

Conclusion

These findings suggest that the volumes of the AV, MD-Pf, and Pul are particularly useful additional biomarkers for people with cognitive impairment, including those with an MCI status or dementia. These nuclei show: (1) Progressive atrophy across the AD continuum; (2) Strong correlations with global amyloid burden; (3) Increased diagnostic accuracy beyond that provided by an amyloid-positive cut-off in cognitively impaired individuals; (4) Thalamic nuclei volume revealed small atrophy in amyloid-positive controls but severe atrophy in amyloid-negative dementia patients. These findings suggest a detectable atrophy in controls with high amyloid burden, despite preserved clinical status, reflecting potential early anatomical vulnerability preceding symptom onset. It also underscores that thalamic nuclei atrophy can enhance diagnostic accuracy and improve patient stratification but could also be used as an outcome measure in clinical trials, potentially guiding more targeted interventions.
