Introduction

Spatiotemporal (4D) infant cortical surface atlases with densely sampled ages are important tools for characterizing the dynamics of early brain development [1]. However, most existing infant cortical surface atlases have been built from cohorts outside China, and, to our knowledge, 4D cortical surface atlases specifically derived from Chinese infants are not yet available. Here, we construct the first 4D cortical surface atlases for Chinese infants at seven age time points (1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 16, and 24 months) using T1-weighted MRI data from 81 healthy infants.

Methods

T1-weighted MRI scans were acquired from 81 infants in the Chinese Baby Connectome Project (CBCP) [2] at above seven age periods. Cortical surfaces were reconstructed using FreeSurfer [3]. We then aligned all cortical surfaces to a common spherical space using group-wise spherical demons registration [4] driven by cortical folding attributes. After registration, all spherical surfaces were resampled to the same mesh tessellation (163,842 vertices) to establish vertex-wise cortical correspondences.

To construct 4D cortical surface atlases, for each cortical vertex, we computed the Wasserstein barycenter of cortical folding attributes (e.g., curvature, convexity) within corresponding spherical patches. Each patch was defined as the 4-ring neighbors on the spherical surface mesh. Due to patch overlapping, each vertex was covered by multiple spherical patches. Therefore, the final vertex-wise values of curvature and convexity were obtained by averaging their patch-wise estimates across all patches covering the vertex. Using this procedure, we constructed month-specific cortical surface atlases for ages 1–24 months by integrating multiple cortical attributes (e.g., average convexity and mean curvature).

Results

Fig. 1 shows the constructed 4D cortical surface atlases with the color-coded average convexity and mean curvature at each month, visualized on (a) spherical surfaces and (b) the average inner cortical surfaces. As can be seen, the major cortical folding patterns, as reflected by average convexity and mean curvature, were established by term birth and remained broadly consistent across the first 24 postnatal months. Over this period, the magnitude of average convexity increased, whereas the magnitude of mean curvature showed a gradual decrease. 

Fig. 2 shows the correlation coefficient (mean ± SD) after registering the testing subjects to the corresponding month-specific atlas (50% for atlas construction and 50% for testing). Larger correlation coefficients indicate better alignment of cortical surfaces across different subjects. Across all ages, the mean correlation coefficient was above 0.76, suggesting that our atlases exhibit sharper and more consistent cortical folding patterns under this evaluation.

Conclusion

We constructed the first 4D cortical surface atlases of Chinese infants from 1 to 24 months. These atlases preserve sharp cortical folding patterns and are associated with improved registration performance when aligning independent subjects to the corresponding atlas.
