Introduction

Macroscale cortical gradients derived from resting-state or task-based functional connectivity provide a powerful framework for describing unimodal–transmodal organization and its development.^1,2 However, most work relies on within-subject connectivity matrices and long, high-quality scans, which are difficult to obtain in pediatric cohorts. At the same time, naturalistic movie-viewing paradigms have become a key tool in developmental neuroimaging, where intersubject synchrony offers a robust handle on shared, stimulus-locked population responses.^3

Yet it remains unclear how stimulus-driven population synchrony is organized along cortical gradients in childhood, how these organizational axes reconfigure from childhood to adolescence, and whether neurodevelopmental conditions such as ASD show altered timing of this macroscale reorganization. Moreover, current gradient approaches rarely quantify how individual children deviate from age-appropriate gradient structure in ways that relate to symptom burden.

Here we propose a population synchrony gradient framework that extends cortical gradient mapping from within-subject measures to across-subject synchrony geometry by embedding ROI-wise intersubject correlation patterns within age-stratified cohorts to derive organizational axes of macroscale synchrony at the population level. Applied to naturalistic movie fMRI in typically developing (TD) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD) children aged 5–17 years, this framework allows us to (1) assess how stimulus-driven population synchrony relates to canonical functional hierarchies, (2) characterize developmental reconfiguration of population synchrony axes from childhood to adolescence, and (3) identify ASD-related shifts and symptom-linked deviations from TD population synchrony structure.

Methods

We analyzed the Healthy Brain Network (HBN) naturalistic movie-viewing fMRI data ("Despicable Me"; ages 5‒17) and stratified participants into five age bins: 5–7, 7–9, 9–11, 11–13, and 13–17 years.^4 TD sample sizes were 41, 33, 20, 23, and 22 (total N = 139); ASD sample sizes were 16, 26, 20, 17, and 19 (total N = 98). Cortical time series were extracted from the Schaefer 1000-parcel parcellation with 7-network labels.

Within each age-bin cohort and diagnostic group, we first characterized population-level synchrony structure at the ROI level. For each ROI, we computed a participant-by-participant intersubject correlation (ISC) matrix by correlating the ROI time series across all participant pairs within the cohort. We then vectorized the upper-triangular ISC entries to obtain an ROI-specific synchrony fingerprint. ROI-to-ROI synchrony similarity was quantified by correlating synchrony fingerprints across ROIs, yielding an ROI×ROI synchrony-similarity matrix. Because this matrix is high-dimensional and difficult to interpret directly, we used gradient embedding to extract continuous organizational axes of macroscale synchrony that capture its principal structure. Specifically, we constructed an affinity matrix from the ROI×ROI synchrony-similarity matrix using a cosine-similarity kernel and applied diffusion map embedding to obtain a low-dimensional representation (analyses focused on the first two eigenvalue-ranked gradients, G1–G2). No sparsification or thresholding was applied prior to embedding.

To probe clinical relevance in ASD, we next quantified ASD-specific individual deviations relative to age-matched TD reference structure. For each ASD participant, we recomputed the synchrony-similarity matrix within the corresponding age bin after adding that individual to the TD cohort, and defined an individual deviation matrix as the difference between this TD+ASD matrix and the TD-only matrix. Deviation matrices were summarized at the 7-network level to yield participant-specific deviation features. These features were related to symptom profiles (e.g., Social Responsiveness Scale [SRS], Autism Spectrum Screening Questionnaire [ASSQ], and Child Behavior Checklist [CBCL]) using partial least squares (PLS) analysis.

Unlike conventional gradients derived from within-subject functional connectivity, this approach embeds ROIs based on across-subject synchrony geometry within each age-bin cohort, enabling population-level characterization of developmental reconfiguration and ASD-related deviations.

Results

Across TD and ASD cohorts, diffusion embedding consistently revealed two prominent organizational axes of macroscale synchrony that were topographically conserved across age bins and qualitatively aligned with canonical functional gradients. Network-wise ridge plots supported interpretation of these axes: one reflected a unimodal–transmodal differentiation, anchored at the transmodal end by the Default Mode and Frontoparietal Control networks, whereas the other reflected a Visual–Sensorimotor (SomMot) differentiation axis. While these spatial organizations of the two axes were conserved across age bins, their rank order (i.e., eigenvalue-based ordering as G1 vs G2) changed with development.

In TD, the Visual–Sensorimotor axis was predominantly expressed as the first gradient during childhood (ages 5-13). Across the transition into adolescence, the unimodal–transmodal axis became the first gradient and the Visual–Sensorimotor axis shifted to the second gradient, indicating a G1↔G2 rank swap (gradient "shift").^5

In ASD, the G1↔G2 gradient shift emerged earlier, with the unimodal–transmodal axis becoming the first gradient already from 9–11 to 11–13 years, preceding the TD transition window. Motivated by this earlier reconfiguration, we quantified ASD-specific individual deviations relative to age-matched TD reference structure and related deviation patterns to symptom profiles using PLS analysis. The primary brain–behavior dimension was dominated by social impairment measures (e.g., SRS) and broader behavioral difficulties (e.g., ASSQ/CBCL), with an opposing contribution from prosocial behavior; brain loadings emphasized transmodal systems, prominently involving the Default Mode and Frontoparietal Control networks.

Conclusion

We introduce a population synchrony gradient framework that extends cortical gradient mapping from within-subject measures to across-subject synchrony geometry. By embedding ROI-wise intersubject correlation patterns within age-stratified cohorts, this framework exploits shared stimulus-locked responses across children to derive robust population-level functional hierarchical organization despite short, noisy, and developmentally heterogeneous single-subject data. It reframes developmental analysis around the reconfiguration of population synchrony hierarchies, offering a systems-level view of how large-scale cortical organization develops from childhood to adolescence and diverges between TD and ASD.

Together, our findings demonstrate that population synchrony gradients (1) cover two canonical functional organizational axes—a unimodal–transmodal axis and a visual–sensorimotor axis—in both TD and ASD, (2) reveal a childhood-to-adolescence gradient rank swap in TD, with the dominant axis shifting from visual–sensorimotor to unimodal–transmodal organization, and (3) show an earlier gradient shift in ASD, whose individual deviation patterns from TD covary with social impairment and broader behavioral difficulties.
