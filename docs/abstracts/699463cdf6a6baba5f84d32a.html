Introduction

Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is associated with impairments in contextual memory, emotion regulation, and sensory–interoceptive integration (Pitman et al., 2012). Despite substantial evidence of hippocampal–prefrontal and limbic network abnormalities, comprehensive analyses integrating structural and functional markers within a single PTSD cohort remain limited. This study aimed to characterize baseline neurobiological signatures of PTSD by examining gray-matter morphology and intrinsic functional connectivity in a well-defined sample, and by evaluating their associations with clinical symptom severity.

Methods

Seventy-one individuals with PTSD and thirty-seven healthy controls (HCs) underwent T1-weighted structural MRI and resting-state fMRI. After preprocessing and quality control, voxel-based morphometry (VBM) was performed on segmented gray-matter maps normalized to MNI space. Age and total intracranial volume (TIV) were included as covariates.

Resting-state functional connectivity was evaluated using seed-to-voxel analyses with PCA decomposition. Three a priori regions implicated in PTSD—medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), thalamus, and amygdala—served as seed regions. Partial correlations examined associations between neural alterations and CAPS symptom scores.

Results

Compared with HCs, PTSD patients showed widespread gray-matter volume reductions (FWE p < 0.05, k > 100) in the parahippocampal gyrus, hippocampus, gyrus rectus and orbital frontal cortices, anterior and middle cingulate cortices, insula, Rolandic operculum, superior and middle temporal gyri, postcentral gyrus, inferior frontal gyrus, and cerebellar Crus I/II and lobules VII–VIII.

Within the PTSD group, gray-matter volume in the left postcentral gyrus and left middle frontal gyrus showed significant negative correlations with CAPS severity.

Functionally, PTSD patients exhibited decreased connectivity in the mPFC–precuneus and right thalamus–left frontal pole circuits compared with HCs, reflecting weakened default mode network integration and impaired sensory–cognitive relay. In contrast, left amygdala–posterior cingulate cortex connectivity showed a positive association with CAPS scores, indicating enhanced coupling between threat reactivity and self-referential processing.

Conclusion

The present findings identify a convergent pattern of structural atrophy and functional dysconnectivity that characterizes the neurobiological profile of PTSD. Gray-matter reductions across hippocampal, prefrontal, cingulate, insular, somatosensory, and cerebellar regions highlight widespread disruptions in circuits supporting contextual memory, emotion regulation, bodily representation, and executive control (Bromis et al., 2018). Complementary alterations in resting-state connectivity—specifically weakened mPFC–precuneus and thalamus–frontal pole coupling, alongside heightened amygdala–PCC connectivity—further demonstrate impairments in self-referential processing, sensory–cognitive integration, and threat-related emotional reactivity (Sripada etl al., 2012; Bluham et al., 2009). Importantly, several of these structural and functional abnormalities were directly associated with clinical symptom severity, underscoring their relevance as potential biomarkers (Homes et al., 2018). Together, these results provide a coherent neurobiological framework for understanding PTSD and offer promising targets for future interventions aimed at modulating maladaptive memory and emotion-regulation networks.
