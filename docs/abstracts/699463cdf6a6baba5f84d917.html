Introduction

Functional connectivity gradients embed the cortex in a continuous, low-dimensional coordinate system that captures large-scale hierarchical organization.¹ Rather than treating regions as discrete network members, gradients position each cortical location along smooth axes reflecting its functional relationships to the rest of the brain, providing a geometrically interpretable framework linking spatial topography to modes of information processing. Meanwhile, a growing literature demonstrates that functional connectivity is not static but fluctuates over seconds to minutes, with transient reconfigurations that relate to behavior and clinical variation.²,³ These dynamics also show lifespan structure, with network-level dynamic connectivity profiles evolving from childhood through late adulthood.⁴ Yet despite the interpretive power of both frameworks, they have largely developed in parallel: dynamic FC studies often rely on discrete-state or edge-level descriptions that lack a global hierarchical reference frame, whereas gradient analyses have predominantly examined time-averaged connectivity. Here, we unite these approaches by tracking how cortical hierarchy dynamically reconfigures in 3,975 participants spanning infancy to late adulthood. Computing diffusion-map gradients from sliding-window connectivity matrices, we quantify moment-to-moment fluctuations in hierarchical position and subspace geometry—metrics that capture complementary modes of dynamic reorganization. This framework provides a geometrically grounded characterization of cortical flexibility that connects time-varying functional connectivity to macroscale organizational principles that mature across the human lifespan.⁵

Methods

We analyzed resting-state fMRI from 3,975 participants (13,351 acquisitions) spanning infancy through late adulthood, pooled across BCP, HCP-D, HCP-YA, HCP-A, and HBN. Acquisitions varied in TR (0.72–0.80 s); window duration was fixed at 60 s to ensure consistent temporal resolution across cohorts. For each acquisition, we estimated time-resolved functional connectivity using sliding windows (60 s duration, 5 TR step). Within each window, we computed pairwise Pearson correlations among cortical vertices (~5k per hemisphere), applied Fisher z-transformation, retained positive values, and sparsified rowwise to the top 10% of connections before symmetrizing. Diffusion-map embedding (K = 3 components) was applied to the resulting connectivity-derived similarity matrix to obtain a low-dimensional gradient representation for each window. Window-wise embeddings were aligned sequentially within each acquisition using orthogonal Procrustes (rotation + translation; no scaling) to enable direct comparison of gradient coordinates across time.

Dynamic metrics were designed to capture distinct modes of time-varying FC reconfiguration in gradient space. Gradient coordinate variance quantified temporal variability of each vertex’s position in the low-dimensional manifold, summarized across vertices and components. To dissociate reorientation within a stable subspace from restructuring of the subspace itself, we computed two complementary metrics. Subspace change was quantified as the Grassmann geodesic distance between consecutive K-dimensional gradient subspaces, computed as the L2 norm of principal angles; higher values indicate reconfiguration of the low-dimensional representation itself. The rotation-explained fraction indexed the proportion of stepwise embedding change captured by the optimal Procrustes rotation, computed as 1 − (post-alignment residual change / pre-alignment change); higher values indicate dynamics dominated by within-subspace reorientation rather than subspace restructuring.

Lifespan trajectories were modeled using generalized additive mixed models (gamm4 in R) with a penalized smooth term for √age, covariate adjustment for head motion (mean framewise displacement), and crossed random intercepts for cohort and subject. Vertex-wise coordinate variance maps at representative ages (Fig. 1E) display acquisition-level averages within 1-year age bins centered on the indicated age. Associations between rotation-explained fraction and Grassmann subspace change were evaluated using Pearson correlations of conditional mixed-model residuals after adjusting for √age, motion, cohort, and subject.

Results

Dynamic gradient metrics exhibited distinct nonlinear trajectories across the lifespan, with all three summary measures showing significant age effects after controlling for head motion (GAMM smooth terms, all p < 2×10⁻¹⁶). Gradient coordinate variance—quantifying how much each cortical location’s position in the low-dimensional FC manifold fluctuates across time—increased sharply from infancy through late childhood, peaking at approximately ~10 years, before declining steadily across adolescence and adulthood (Fig. 1A). This trajectory suggests that development is characterized by progressively larger moment-to-moment repositioning of regions within the functional hierarchy, reaching maximal within-manifold “flexibility” around the transition to adolescence, followed by gradual attenuation thereafter.

In contrast, subspace change, measured as the Grassmann geodesic distance between consecutive gradient subspaces, followed a distinct U-shaped trajectory (Fig. 1B). Infants showed the highest subspace volatility, indicating that the dominant low-dimensional axes used to describe FC organization are themselves comparatively unstable early in life. Subspace change decreased through childhood, reaching a minimum near ~14 years, consistent with consolidation of a stable gradient architecture during maturation. Subspace change then gradually increased across adulthood and into late life, a pattern that may reflect reduced constraint on the macroscale hierarchy or age-related dedifferentiation of large-scale functional organization.

The rotation-explained fraction—the proportion of stepwise embedding change captured by optimal within-subspace rotation (as opposed to residual change not explainable by rotation)—showed a developmental trajectory paralleling coordinate variance (Fig. 1C), increasing from infancy to a peak near ~10 years and declining thereafter. Higher values indicate that short-timescale FC reconfiguration is expressed primarily as reorientation within a stable low-dimensional representation rather than restructuring of that representation. The developmental rise therefore suggests a shift in the mode of dynamics: as the gradient subspace stabilizes through childhood, dynamic FC increasingly manifests as movement within a consolidated hierarchical reference frame.

Consistent with a dissociation between these modes, rotation-explained fraction was negatively associated with Grassmann subspace change after residualizing for age, motion, cohort, and subject (r = −0.237, p = 7.2×10⁻¹⁶⁹; Fig. 1D). This relationship supports an interpretable coupling: when the gradient subspace itself is changing rapidly from window to window, a smaller fraction of stepwise change is captured by within-subspace rotation, implying relatively greater “out-of-subspace” reconfiguration of the low-dimensional FC geometry.

Finally, vertex-wise coordinate variance maps revealed a marked spatial redistribution of dynamic flexibility across development (Fig. 1E). In early childhood (2 years), the highest coordinate variance localized to unimodal sensorimotor and visual cortices, with transmodal regions showing relatively lower temporal variability. By 5–10 years, frontal and parietal association areas exhibited increased involvement while unimodal peaks remained prominent. In young adulthood (20 years) and midlife (50 years), the spatial pattern shifted further, with the highest coordinate variance concentrated in transmodal association cortex (including lateral frontal and lateral temporoparietal regions), while unimodal areas showed comparatively attenuated dynamics. Together, these results indicate that lifespan changes in dynamic gradients are not uniform across cortex, but reflect both changes in magnitude (an inverted-U coordinate-variance trajectory) and changes in mode (a U-shaped subspace-change trajectory), alongside a developmental shift from unimodal-dominant to association-dominant dynamic reconfiguration.

Conclusion

By applying the powerful functional connectivity gradient mapping framework to the dynamic FC regime, we provide a unified, geometrically interpretable account of how macroscale cortical hierarchy dynamically reconfigures from infancy through late adulthood. Across 3,975 participants, dynamic gradient behavior was not explained by a single “flexibility” construct: coordinate variance and rotation-explained fraction followed an inverted-U trajectory peaking near late childhood, whereas Grassmann subspace change showed a U-shaped pattern with elevated subspace volatility in infancy and increasing subspace reorganization in later life. The negative coupling between rotation-explained fraction and subspace change further indicates a tradeoff between within-subspace reorientation and out-of-subspace restructuring as distinct modes of time-varying FC dynamics. Spatially, flexibility redistributed from unimodal cortex early in development toward transmodal association cortex in adulthood, suggesting that maturation involves both consolidation of a stable hierarchical reference frame and a progressive shift in where dynamic reconfiguration is expressed. Together, these findings establish dynamic gradients as a principled bridge between time-resolved connectivity and cortical hierarchy, offering complementary markers of developmental stabilization and age-related reorganization that can be applied to normative lifespan mapping and neurodevelopmental/aging-related deviations.
