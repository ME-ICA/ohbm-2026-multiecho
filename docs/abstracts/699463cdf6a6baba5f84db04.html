Introduction

Substance use disorders (SUDs) are the most prevalent and deadly forms of psychopathology with a heavy societal burden[1]. More than half of substance use begins in adolescence, with a considerable proportion of users transitioning to higher levels of use or developing dependencies[2]. 

Thus, early identification of individuals at risk for escalating substance use could be crucial for preventative interventions. Studies have found imaging evidence related to the initiation and abuse of drugs[3]. However, little research focuses on the transition between the key stages of SUDs, for example, the escalation to problem use. Using longitudinal neuroimaging data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) study, this investigation aims to identify neuroimaging biomarkers that could potentially predict substance use trajectories following initial exposure at a young age.

Methods

We established a cohort of adolescents from the ABCD study who reported no substance use at baseline (age 9–10 years) and tracked substance involvement over four years. Based on longitudinal consumption patterns across alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, and other substances, participants were classified into three groups: low risk, mild escalation, and severe escalation.

Multimodal neuroimaging measures were derived from T1-weighted MRI, resting-state fMRI, and diffusion MRI, and brain-network topology metrics were additionally computed to characterise global and nodal properties. All imaging features were harmonised across sites using ComBat. Group differences were tested using covariate-adjusted ANCOVA with Tukey post-hoc analysis. Multiple testing across features was controlled using Benjamini–Hochberg FDR within each metric family. Demographic variables were compared at baseline and included along with imaging-related covariates (e.g., intracranial volume, motion, tSNR) consistently across models.

To explore multivariate robustness, we then fit LASSO logistic regression for low risk and any escalation using repeated 10-fold cross-validation; predictors were standardised within folds and covariates were forced in (unpenalized). Features were ranked by selection frequency. A final unpenalized logistic regression refit on the full sample using stable features was reported descriptively.

Results

The cohort included 314 subjects (126 low-risk, 96 mild escalation, 93 severe escalation), with no baseline demographic differences found among the groups. After FDR correction, comparative neuroimaging analyses identified a dozen features related to substance use trajectories (q<0.05), dominated by diffusion-derived microstructure. These effects were spatially widespread and repeatedly involved key brain regions such as marginal branch of the cingulate sulcus, subparietal sulcus, postcentral sulcus and precuneus. Additionally, non-linear post-hoc patterns were frequently indicated by the most diffusion findings, as the mild escalation often showed the largest deviation, rather than a monotonic severity gradient. 

Among those diffusion affected regions, right fronto-marginal gyrus and sulcus also demonstrated a similar pattern of structural differences in T1 intensity among groups (severe_mild: estimate=-0.62, p_adj=0.002; mild_low-risk: estimate=0.35, p_adj=0.04),   suggesting that microstructural alterations may co-occur with subtler macrostructural changes. However, other structural changes such as cortical area and cortical volume in left anterior occipital sulcus and preoccipital notch showed a different pattern with a general increase compared with low risk.

Resting-state fMRI effects indicated group differences in the large-scale network. Several AUC-based topology metrics, such as clustering, local efficiency and small-worldness, differed between groups, typically showing higher values in both escalation groups compared to low risk, whereas one of the nodal properties, betweenness of the auditory network, showed the opposite direction(mild_low-risk: estimate=-0.02, p_adj=0.005; mild_low-risk: estimate=-0.02, p_adj=0.003).

In the collapsed contrast of low risk vs any escalation (126 vs 188), repeated 10-fold CV-LASSO identified 6 stable features selected in ≥90% folds: sMRI sulcal depth in left orbital sulci,  Mean Diffusity contrast of GM and WM in right postcentral sulcus, restricted normalised isotropic diffusion in left pericallosal sulcus, and three rs-fMRI connectivity features: between auditory network and cingulo-parietal network, between dorsal attention network and subcortical region left pallidum and between visual network and subcortical region right putamen. In the final refit (Firth penalised LR), the structural/diffusion features showed positive associations with escalation (Std_OR: 1.59, 1.56, 1.41), whereas the three connectivity features were higher in low risk (Std_OR= 0.66, 0.67, 0.65), suggesting reduced cortico–subcortical functional connectivity.

Conclusion

Using baseline neuroimaging at age 9–10, we identified prospective brain differences that distinguish adolescents who later show low-risk vs escalating substance-use trajectories over four years. FDR-controlled ANCOVA indicated that effects were dominated by diffusion/RSI microstructural features, with recurring involvement of cingulate–parietal–sensorimotor sulcal regions such as marginal cingulate branch and postcentral sulci. Post-hoc contrasts frequently suggested non-linear severity patterns, with mild escalation often deviating most from low risk. Structural MRI and rs-fMRI effects were fewer but broadly convergent. Complementary CV-LASSO stability analysis identified a small cross-modal baseline signature separating low risk from any escalation, linking escalation to microstructural/sulcal-depth markers and low risk to stronger cortico–subcortical connectivity. These findings nominate baseline microstructure and network integration as candidate markers of vulnerability to subsequent escalation, warranting longitudinal replication.
