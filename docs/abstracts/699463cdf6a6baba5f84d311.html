Introduction

In humans, affective disorders such as major depressive disorder and generalized anxiety disorder are associated with widespread alterations in large-scale functional networks involving the hippocampus.^1,2 Preclinical studies implicate cornu ammonis 1(CA1)-specific hippocampal projections in stress-related pathology, raising the possibility that CA1 contributes uniquely to the large-scale network disruptions observed in human mood and anxiety disorders. However, the functional contributions of individual hippocampal subfields to functional networks remain poorly understood. Leveraging ultra-high-field 7T fMRI, which provides the spatial resolution needed to isolate subfield-level signals, the present study tests whether individuals with mood and anxiety disorders show selective alterations in CA1 network connectivity, providing a potential mechanistic link between subfield-specific hippocampal dysfunction and affective pathology. 

Methods

Data were acquired on a Siemens Magnetom 7T scanner for 24 healthy controls (HC) and 28 patients with mood and anxiety disorders (MA). High resolution structural images included T1-weighted (0.7mm isotropic) and T2-weighted (0.4x0.4x2.0mm, coronal-oblique) scans. Resting-state fMRI was collected at 1.1mm isotropic (TR=2s, A-P phase encoding) with short scan of opposite phase-encoding direction for distortion correction. T1 and T2 images underwent gradient nonlinearity correction using Human Connectome Project gradunwarp tools to reduce spatial distortions. Cortical reconstruction and volumetric segmentation were performed in FreeSurfer v7.4.1 using the Desikan-Killiany atlas (n ROIs=82)^3. Hippocampal subfields (CA1, CA3, CA4/dentate gyrus, subiculum, presubiculum, and parasubiculum) were segmented from the T2-weighted images in FreeSurfer v7.4.1 and co-registered with the Desikan-Killiany parcellation into native fMRI space. 

Resting state fMRI preprocessing was first performed in fMRIprep 23.1.47 including slice-timing correction, susceptibility distortion correction, and motion correction. Additional preprocessing in AFNI included spatial smoothing to 1.25mm, bandpass filtering, detrending and confound regression in AFNI. Confound regression included Friston 24 motion parameters and the first five aCompCor components.

Subject-level functional connectivity (FC) matrices (i.e. fully connected graphs) were generated in AFNI containing Fisher-z transformed correlations between all FreeSurfer ROIs including the hippocampal subfields (matrix size: 94 x 94). In this study, we focused on the involvement of hippocampal subfields in whole brain connectivity using graph theoretical measures. For each subfield, intrinsic node strength (sum of within-hippocampal edges) and extrinsic node strength (sum of non-hippocampal edges) were separately calculated for positive (FC > 0) weights. Group differences in subfield node strength were tested using linear models controlling for age, gender, and standardized hippocampal subfield volume. Significance was defined using false discovery rate (FDR) correction with a threshold of pfdr < 0.05, controlling for the number of hippocampal subfields (n=12) 

Subfields showing significant group effects were further explored for network-specific and edge-specific effects. Freesurfer ROIs were mapped to the Yeo 7 canonical networks (default mode, limbic, frontoparietal, dorsal attention, ventral attention, somatomotor, visual).^4 Since our parcellations included additional subcortical ROIs not represented in the Yeo 7 atlas, we defined ‘Subcortical’ as an eighth network. For each subfield, network-specific node strength was calculated by summing positive weights within each network. Group differences in this network strength were tested using linear models with same covariates (age, gender, and standardized subfield volume). Statistical significance was determined using FDR correction with a threshold of pfdr < 0.05, controlling for the number of networks (n=8). To complement the network-level analyses, individual edges from each significant subfield were also examined using the same linear modeling framework.

Results

MA participants included individuals with major depressive disorder (n=13), generalized anxiety disorder (n=7) and both (n=8). HC and MA groups did not differ in age, gender, hippocampal subfield volumes, framewise displacement (mean FD = 0.175mm), or Montreal Cognitive Assessment scores (all p’s > 0.05). 
            Among all hippocampal subfields, only left CA1 showed altered node strength, with MA participants exhibiting significantly increased extrinsic node strength (t(50)=3.06, puncor = 0.0037, pfdr= 0.03, Cohen’s d=0.85) (Figure 1A). No group differences were found for any intrinsic connections.

Network-level analyses of left CA1 revealed that increased left CA1 extrinsic node strength in MA participants was distributed across multiple networks. Significant increases were observed for limbic (t(50)=3.44, puncor= 0.001, pfdr= 0.006, Cohen’s d=0.96), dorsal attention (t(50)=3.37, puncor = 0.001, pfdr= 0.006, Cohen’s d=0.94), subcortical (t(50)=3.08, puncor = 0.003, pfdr= 0.009, Cohen’s d=0.86), frontoparietal (t(50)=2.94, puncor = 0.005, pfdr= 0.009, Cohen’s d=0.82), default mode (t(50)=2.91, puncor = 0.005, pfdr= 0.009, Cohen’s d=0.81), and somatomotor (t(50)=2.27, puncor = 0.028, pfdr= 0.037, Cohen’s d=0.63) networks (Figure 1B). 

No individual edges survived multiple-comparison correction. Nonetheless, exploratory edgewise analyses suggested that 33 left CA1 extrinsic edges exhibited uncorrected increases in functional connectivity in MA volunteers relative to HC (Figure 1C). 

Conclusion

Using 7T fMRI and native-space, subfield-resolved functional connectivity analyses, we identify selective hyperconnectivity of the left CA1 subfield across multiple functional networks in mood and anxiety participants. The pattern of increased extrinsic, but not intrinsic, CA1 connectivity alterations aligns with preclinical evidence of CA1-specific network involvement in affective pathology. Although no individual edges survived correction, robust network and whole-brain level increases in left CA1 connectivity in mood and anxiety disorders suggest a broad alteration in how CA1 engages with distributed affective and attentional networks. These findings reveal CA1-specific network alterations in mood and anxiety disorders that have not been previously demonstrated in human neuroimaging using 7T fMRI, highlighting CA1 may be a locus of disease-related connectivity alterations and motivating future work examining how CA1-specific connectivity relates to symptoms, disease progression, and treatment response. More broadly, our findings highlight the value of 7T fMRI for localizing disease-related effects to specific hippocampal subfields, providing mechanistic specificity that strengthens translational links to preclinical models of CA1 vulnerability
