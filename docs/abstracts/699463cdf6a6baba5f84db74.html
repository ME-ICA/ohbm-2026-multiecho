Introduction

Neuronal fiber tracts underpin communication between distinct brain regions. These tracts are established in utero and are largely formed by term birth, undergoing rapid maturation thereafter to support cognitive and behavioral development. Tract properties, such as fractional anisotropy (FA), an index of tract microstructure, can be quantified using diffusion MRI. Very preterm (VPT) birth has been associated with alterations in tract properties that are linked to later cognitive impairments and increased risk of psychopathology [1, 2]. However, it remains unclear whether neonatal tract properties are associated with longer-term cognitive and mental health outcomes. This longitudinal study investigates associations between neonatal FA across 42 white-matter tracts in VPT infants and developmental outcomes at 18–24 months, as well as cognition and psychopathology in childhood.

Methods

This study included 396 very preterm infants (<33 weeks’ gestation) enrolled in the Evaluation of Preterm Imaging Study (See Figure 1A for study design). Mean gestational age at birth was 29.82 weeks, and 50.5% infants were female. Perinatal clinical data were summarized into a neonatal sickness index [3]. All infants underwent multimodal MRI at term-equivalent age (37–45 weeks) [4]. Diffusion MRI was preprocessed using FSL, and whole-brain tractography was performed with the baby-XTRACT pipeline [5] to reconstruct 42 major white matter tracts (Figure 1B). Mean fractional anisotropy (FA) was extracted for each tract. Developmental outcomes were assessed using the Bayley Scales of Infant and Toddler Development–III (BSID-III) at 18–24 months, and the Wechsler Preschool & Primary Scale of Intelligence (WPPSI-IV) and the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) at 4–7 years. Linear regression examined associations between neonatal FA and later outcomes, adjusting for sex, socio-economic status, neonatal sickness index, and age at assessment (for SDQ only). Multiple comparisons were corrected using false discovery rate (FDR).

Results

Greater FA in the corticospinal tract and fornix was associated with better cognitive, motor, and language scores at 18–24 months. Several additional tracts, including the forceps major, optic radiation, superior thalamic radiation, middle longitudinal fasciculus, and arcuate fasciculus, showed positive associations with cognitive and motor development. Moreover, motor development was positively associated with FA in the acoustic radiation, cingulum (dorsal and peri-genual), inferior fronto-occipital fasciculus, and superior longitudinal fasciculus I and III. All findings survived FDR correction. In childhood, higher fornix FA remained associated with higher WPPSI-IV cognitive scores and fewer SDQ difficulties.

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that higher neonatal white matter FA is associated with better cognitive, language, and motor abilities during infancy, as well as enhanced cognitive function and more favorable mental health in childhood. These results highlight the importance of neonatal white-matter tract integrity for long-term neurodevelopment. Although the fornix showed consistent associations with both early and later developmental measure, caution should be warranted when interpreting neonatal fornix findings due to potential partial volume effects and its proximity to the ventricles, which may bias FA estimates. Higher-resolution imaging and advanced diffusion models are needed to validate these observations.
