Introduction

With online gaming becoming globally widespread, concerns have risen regarding maladaptive use and early vulnerability to Internet Gaming Disorder (IGD).1 Individuals with excessive online gaming (EOG) often exhibit impaired control and social dysfunction, representing a subclinical high-risk group preceding IGD.2 IGD and related behaviors have been associated with structural and functional alterations within cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuits and neurotransmitter systems implicated in reward learning and cognitive control.1,2 Source-based morphometry (SBM) provides a data-driven approach to detecting distributed gray-matter covariance networks relevant to these systems,3 while receptor-distribution mapping allows spatial interpretation of structural alterations relative to serotonin- and dopamine-related neurochemical gradients.4,5 Integrating these methodologies enables the identification of early structural markers linked to neurochemical vulnerability in EOG

Methods

Fifty-nine right-handed young adults (30 EOG, 29 non-EOG) completed T1-weighted MRI (3T Siemens scanner; MPRAGE; voxel size 1 mm³). Behavioral assessments included the IGDS9-SF and Liebowitz Social Anxiety Scale. T1 images were preprocessed in CAT12/SPM12 (bias correction, segmentation, DARTEL normalization, modulation, and smoothing at 8 mm FWHM). Source-based morphometry (GIFT/SBM) was applied to identify independent gray-matter covariance networks. Component loading values were compared between groups using two-sample t-tests. Associations with symptom severity were assessed using partial correlations (age, sex, TIV controlled). Behavioral characterization was conducted using the Brain Annotation Toolbox, and receptor-level correspondence was quantified using JuSpace by correlating component spatial maps with PET-derived density maps of serotonin (5-HT1A, 5-HT2A, transporters) and dopamine (D1, D2, DAT) systems.

Results

Groups did not differ in age, sex distribution, or LSAS scores (p > 0.05). EOG participants showed higher IGDS9-SF scores (p < 0.05). SBM yielded six gray-matter networks. A single network (Component 6) demonstrated significantly reduced loading in EOG relative to non-EOG (t = 2.38, p = 0.02). Component 6 loadings were inversely associated with IGDS9-SF scores (r < 0, p < 0.05). Peak contributions localized to the right thalamus and right precentral gyrus, encompassing sensory-motor integration, action control, and perceptual gating pathways. Regional gray-matter variations exhibited distinct neurochemical profiles: thalamic alterations correlated positively with serotonin and dopamine receptor/transporter density maps, whereas precentral gyrus alterations showed negative associations with serotonergic markers and positive associations with dopaminergic markers. Behavioral annotation linked this network to perceptual processing, social–communicative functioning, motor coordination, and executive control.

Conclusion

EOG is characterized by reduced integrity of a distributed sensory–motor structural network anchored in the right thalamus and precentral gyrus. Network reductions scale with gaming-related symptom severity and align with spatial distributions of serotonergic and dopaminergic systems. These findings highlight a multilevel vulnerability architecture in which early structural deviations, linked to neurochemical gradients underlying reward processing and motor control, may precede the emergence of clinically impairing IGD. The combined SBM–receptor mapping framework offers a sensitive approach for detecting preclinical markers of gaming-related addictive behavior.
