Introduction

Suicidality is a major public health concern 1. Identifying biomarkers associated with suicidal behaviors (SBs, including suicide attempt and death by suicide) holds potential for improving risk assessment and informing novel therapeutic strategies 2.

Methods

In this study, we analyzed 2,920 plasma proteins in over 50,000 UK Biobank adults, with follow-up to 15 years. Using logistic regression and Cox models, we identified individual proteins and co-regulated protein networks associated with past and future SBs, respectively. Subsequent functional annotation was conducted to elucidate the biological processes involved. Furthermore, we examined their correlations with brain structural measures. Additionally, Mendelian randomization (MR) analyses were applied to evaluate potential causal relationships between identified proteins, co-regulated networks, and SBs. Finally, we developed machine-learning models to determine the predictive accuracy of plasma proteomic profiles in classifying past SBs and estimating future risk.

Results

We identified 421 proteins significantly associated with past SBs, of which 15 predicted future risk. These proteins were predominantly enriched in inflammatory pathways. Co-regulated network analysis revealed three distinct protein modules linked to SBs, enriched for inflammation and cell-cell adhesion. These proteomic signatures showed significant correlations with grey matter volume in the medial/lateral orbitofrontal cortex, insula, middle temporal cortex, and superior frontal cortex. MR analysis implicated GGH as a potential causal factor for SBs and a mediator of the body mass index effect on SBs. A machine-learning model incorporating proteomic and demographic data achieved an AUC of 0.79 in identifying past SBs.

Conclusion

This study provides valuable insights into the biological mechanisms underlying SBs and offers potential therapeutic targets.
