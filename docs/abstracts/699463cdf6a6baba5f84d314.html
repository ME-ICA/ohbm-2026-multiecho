Introduction

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is characterized by the accumulation of amyloid-beta (Aβ) and hyperphosphorylated tau proteins, which drive neurodegeneration and cognitive decline. However, a notable number of individuals maintain cognitive function despite substantial AD pathology, known as cognitive reserve (CR) ^1. While environmental and genetic factors are known to influence CR ^2, the pathophysiological mechanisms through which genes or genetic variants confer CR remain poorly understood. On the other hand, the interaction between Aβ and Tau is critical for cognition, as regional Aβ promotes the propagation of tau, which is closely linked to cognitive decline^3. A significant discrepancy between these biomarkers can be associated with preserved cognition. To move beyond prior dichotomous classifications that can oversimplify this relationship, we newly introduce the concept of Tau resilience, a continuous measure defined as the degree to which observed Tau burden is relatively lower than the level predicted by Aβ deposition.

 In this study, we aim to elucidate the biological mechanisms linking genetic variants, Tau resilience, and CR. We examined the association between regional Tau resilience and CR and identified single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with tau resilience. We hypothesized that specific SNPs would be associated with greater Tau resilience in particular brain regions, thereby contributing to enhanced CR.

Methods

This study utilized multimodal data of 586 subjects in the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) database. To quantify CR and Tau resilience, we used a residual-based approach^4. CR marker was defined as the residual variance in cognitive performance that could not be explained by demographic and pathological variables. Similarly, the Tau resilience marker was calculated as the regional residual of tau burden after adjusting for Aβ and other risk factors. Genome-wide interaction analysis was conducted to identify genetic variants that moderate the effect of tau resilience on CR. Functional annotation of significant loci was carried out using the FUMA platform^5. To elucidate the biological mechanisms, we performed cis-expression quantitative trait loci (cis-eQTL) mapping using the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx v8) database, specifically focusing on gene expression data from the frontal cortex. 

Results

The residual-based CR marker was robustly validated through its association with established proxies and its protective effect against pathology. We observed significant positive correlations between CR and Tau resilience in the frontal regions, including the prefrontal cortex (R = 0.1262, p = 0.0022), orbitofrontal cortex (R = 0.1403, p = 0.0007), and anterior cingulate cortex (R = 0.0992, p = 0.0163). Genome-wide interaction analysis identified five SNPs significantly associated with the Tau Resilience in the frontal cortex. Notably, rs7797819 was identified as a key functional variant. Cis-eQTL analysis in the frontal cortex revealed that the risk allele of rs7797819 is significantly associated with increased expression of the long non-coding RNA WASL-DT (β = 0.4569, p < 0.0001). In the frontal cortex, WASL-DT expression exhibited strong positive correlations with AD-protective genes, including PPM1B (R = 0.8286, p < 0.0001), POT1 (R = 0.8325, p < 0.0001), ZMPSTE24 (R = 0.5832, p < 0.0001), and GALC (R = 0.8304, p < 0.0001).

Conclusion

These findings suggest that high CR strategically prioritizes Tau resilience in the frontal cognitive control network to preserve executive function. We propose that this region-specific resilience is mechanistically supported by the rs7797819-driven upregulation of WASL-DT, which orchestrates a neuroprotective gene network involving PPM1B and POT1. Collectively, our study identifies a novel genetic pathway linking CR to molecular defense mechanisms, offering potential targets for precision medicine in AD.
