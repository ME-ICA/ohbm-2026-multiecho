Introduction

Dense individual fMRI datasets are emerging as critical resources to advance NeuroAI and to model how the human brain integrates complex sensory and cognitive information. Most prior large-scale individual datasets have focused on single tasks, such as visual object recognition (NSD1) or movie viewing2. Yet, a hallmark of human cognition is the ability to navigate flexibly across processes and domains, such as perception, memory, language, and emotional understanding. To capture this integrative capacity, the Courtois Project on Neuronal Modelling (CNeuroMod) has completed a 5-year data collection effort, and acquired an unprecedented amount of neuroimaging data from six human participants.
CNeuroMod is currently the largest and most comprehensive dense individual fMRI dataset, including ~200 hours of fMRI per participant, combined with structural MRI, behavioral recordings, physiological and eye-tracking signals. The CNeuroMod tasks include naturalistic (e.g., movies, TV series, videogames, narratives) and controlled paradigms probing broad cognitive functions (e.g., memory, semantics, functional localizers) that can be used model complex subject-specific brain processes. Because many CNeuroMod tasks overlap with other openly available datasets (e.g., Narratives; THINGS; Le Petit Prince), the resulting dataset is also uniquely positioned to support research on generalization, transfer learning, and subject-specific encoding of complex brain processes.
This  open large-scale dataset provides the international community with a powerful foundation to build, evaluate, and benchmark NeuroAI models, and to explore how brain representations evolve through a rich cognitive environments.

Methods

Participants aged 31 to 47 at the time of recruitment in 2018; 3 women and 3 men; 3 native French & 1 native English speakers, 2 bilingual; all right-handed) consented to participate for at least 5 years of data collection. All participants had good general health and normal hearing for their age.  One subject, sub-04, withdrew from the study during year 2, and thus completed only a small portion of the data collection. FMRI data were acquired with a 3T scanner. Setup included physiological signal recording, headcases to minimize motion, and a custom-built fiber optic controller to play videogames. Data were preprocessed with the fMRIprep pipeline LTS (https://docs.cneuromod.ca;  https://fmriprep.org/en/stable/). 

The CNeuroMod Databank. The databank includes 29 fMRI datasets targeting several cognitive domains and modalities. Per subject, there are 197h of fMRI data, 17h of anatomical MRI data, 29h of data collected outside the scanner, including longitudinal hearing measures and videogame (Shinobi) training. The following is a breakdown of the fMRI datasets by cognitive domains, per subject. Vision. 22 datasets (175h) including movies (12h), 7 seasons of TV-show Friends (70h), & functional localizers. ood  Algonauts (2h/Ss).  An OOD movie watching dataset acquired for the Algonauts 2025 challenge3. Language. 19 datasets (73h), including listening to Le Petit Prince (3h) in 3 languages, reading a chapter of Harry Potter; triplets  is a  semantic association task involving triplets of words (7h),  & functional localizers. Memory. 5 datasets (44h), including a memory task using THINGS (18h), and  a working-memory task called multfs (8h). Emotions. 13 datasets (147h), including gifs (5h) that evoke varying emotional states. Auditory. 18 datasets (146h), including mutemusic, an  auditory imagery task, and narratives, a listening task with verbal recall inside the scanner. Videogames (48h). In-scan playing of videogames  - shinobi, supermario, mariostars, and mario3.

Results

Data access. After processing and quality checks, each dataset is being released and shared  with the community. Raw and preprocessed fMRI data, behavioural responses and physiological recordings are formatted in BIDS3 and made available via DataLad version control (https://www.datalad.org/).  Data for 5 subjects are accessible without any restrictions (CC0 license) through the Canadian Open Neuroscience Platform portal (https://portal.conp.ca/), while data for all 6 subjects are available via registered access at https://www.cneuromod.ca/. 

Example of community-driven applications

Three CNeuroMod datasets (friends, movie10 and ood) were recently featured in the Algonauts 2025 challenge4, for which competitors trained models to predict brain activity elicited by movie watching (see Figure 1). The impact of this initiative on the neuroAI community is two-fold. First, novel approaches to build and train models that predict brain dynamics from multimodal (vision, audio and text) features are documented via pre-prints and code sharing. Second, the competition’s benchmark remains available on Codabench for modellers to compare the performance of their brain encoding models against top-ranking entries (https://www.codabench.org/competitions/4313), including META’s winning TRIBE model5. We note that the Algonauts benchmark reflects a small portion of the CNeuroMod dataset, and that substantive gains in brain encoding generalization can be achieved by expanding training regimes to include datasets that capture a broader range of cognitive states. 

Conclusion

The CNeuroMod project has assembled an unprecedented resource to model individual brain function using a wide range of controlled and naturalistic tasks. These different datasets can be combined across subjects and datasets, within and outside the CNeuroMod ecosystem, and expand our capacity to model complex brain dynamics. The wealth of additional data that will be released in the coming years will fuel innovation and support the development of new generations of neuroAI models.
