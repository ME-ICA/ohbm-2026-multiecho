Introduction

The exponential growth of ‘big data’ neuroscience has created an urgent need for integrated platforms that facilitate data sharing and collaborative research. For neuroscience to benefit globally underserved populations, translational applications from low-cost non-invasive and globally accessible brain imaging technology such as electroencephalography (EEG) must demonstrate general applicability for investigating brain disorders (Mushtaq 2025).

As articulated in a recent survey commemorating 100 years of EEG (Mushtaq 2024), these gaps in accessibility and standards remain critical priorities for the broader neuroelectrophysiology research community. Fundamental to these outcomes is a common basis of data sharing, interpretation and analytics, supported by community-driven standards such as EEG-BIDS and HED tags (Pernet 2019).

In view of these community priorities, EEGNet.org launched in 2023 an online open platform (EEGNet.loris.ca) for open collaboration in building gold-standard annotated datasets for exploitation in machine learning and early biomarker detection.  

This integrative data and analytics hub is designed to drive and embody community consensus around standards, data and tools for reproducible neuroscience. This platform continues to grow with the recent addition of data sets from global consortia such as the #EEGManyLabs initiative (Pavlov 2021).

For these reasons, EEGNet delivered an open data and analytics hub serving to:

• Implement, adapt and create standardized BIDS-compliant resources and workflows

• Release open datasets on the EEGNet platform

• Deploy and support open-source analysis tools via EEGNet (specifically CBRAIN).

Since its pilot release, EEGNet has grown to over 19000 minutes of recordings for research community members to log on, test, annotate, download, query and provide input on workflows.

Methods

Key outcomes of EEGNet’s initial implementation are:                

 • Accelerated analysis of EEG data across >90 research groups from 33 countries
 • Provided accessible standardized open data for analysis for >300 new users.
 • Open data release for existing consortia, e.g. EEGManyLabs
 • Enabled interactive signal annotation review with clinical standards (HED-SCORE)

EEGNet combines data and analytics in a unified platform via the LORIS and CBRAIN platforms (FIGURE 1). LORIS is an open-source data and project management tool for storing multimodal data, specialised for managing large datasets acquired in different locations. CBRAIN is a portal to high-performance computing sites and public/private clouds (e.g., Amazon EC2, Azure), accelerating the time to produce scientific outputs from days to hours. CBRAIN supports >2,000 users in 59 countries and 150+ advanced analysis workflows, including quantitative EEG (qEEG) analysis pipelines (Li 2022).

A stakeholder consultation was conducted in 2021-2 through a Scientific/Clinical needs assessment and Technical Landscape community survey, enabling prioritization of initial deliverables and services. The community response identified primary barriers in data sharing, open analytics tools access, and the critical impact of standardization in open science and the electrophysiological research community.

As a result, the EEGNet development team added granular controls for collaborative data annotation and validation, advanced metadata querying, and standardized annotation capacities. Additional visualization tools were added and standardized filter templates developed.  The BIDS standardization metadata and conversion workflows were also streamlined, automated publication on the Canadian Open Neuroscience platform was piloted.

An initial cohort of EEGNet analytics tools mounted on CBRAIN, included:
• HarMNqEEG: harmonized global norms for quantitative EEG
• PyLossless: Python-based independent component analysis and QC.
• BrainStorm: Deployed for automated MEG/EEG analysis (PI: Albouy)
     includes BEst pipeline (Grova, Concordia) – Brain Entropy in Space and time     
• qEEGt: Quantitative EEG Toolbox

EEGNet continues to drive data sharing and open tool deployment as well as implementation of the emerging BIDS derivative standard through community engagement and active participation in global working groups, and contributes to open EEG data preparation tools.

Through these efforts and in partnership with the Global Brain Consortium (globalbrainconsortium.org) and the Canadian Open Neuroscience Platform (CONP.ca) EEGNet’s combined data and analytics hub removes key barriers to open EEG research collaboration.

Results

All members of the clinical and scientific research community are welcome to log into EEGNet.loris.ca - begin by requesting an account to access, navigate, visualize, query, and download or export all data on the platform.  At the pilot launch EEGNet data and analytics hub included 7 open EEG-BIDS datasets, 200+ recordings, and 4 open analytics tools on CBRAIN. A portal listing all EEGNet community-contributed datasets and tools is also published at EEGNet.org

Spanning over 35 Canadian researchers across 10 sites, EEGNet’s scientific and clinical partners will continue to provide guidance to optimize platform utility for the research, informatics and clinical EEG community.

Since 2023 EEGNet.Loris.ca has released:
• Over 1100 recordings from Canada and 6 other countries
• Over 19,000 minutes of electrophysiology recordings
• World-leading online signal visualization and interactive data discovery workflows 
• Open analytics tools deployed on the CBRAIN high-performance computing portal 
• Open standards support for EEG-BIDS format and HED-SCORE clinical ontology 

Additional enhancement of workflows has included granular controls for collaborative data annotation and validation, advanced metadata querying, and enhanced visualization utilities – such that EEGNet now provides the most advanced web-accessible open EEG visualization tools in the field (FIGURE 2). 

EEGNet’s BIDS-compliant hub combines open-source infrastructure (LORIS, CBRAIN) harnessing the national Compute Canada cluster computing network. EEGNet also leverages groundwork by the Canadian Open Neuroscience Platform (CONP.ca) in interoperability, ethics and data governance.

All datasets are available under registered access and subject to Terms of Use established in concordance with the CONP Ethics advisory group. Users requesting access to the EEGNet platform must accept to respect these terms, including ethics governing data re- use, licensing and citation information provided by contributors.  Additional data formats are added with a growing collection of datasets.


EEGNet has partnered with the EEGManyLabs consortium since 2024 to serve as distribution platform for their open resting state datasets. Open-source EEGNet features are released open-source as well as imported into the technical stack of external informatics partners to support large-scale EEG research data. 

The EEGNet community portal of datasets and tools published on EEGNet.org to provide community resources, direct links to the datasets, and broader reach for research outputs. 

Open EEGNet notebooks and toolkits (https://github.com/Andesha/EEGStudyFlow) are used in training graduate students across multiple Canadian institutions and adopted by computing networks such as the Digital Research Alliance of Canada and SHARCNET in their training and education workshop program. 

All datasets are additionally linked via the Canadian Open Neuroscience Platform (portal.conp.ca) with data author ORCID affiliation and links to the EEGNet platform data packages.

These contributions collectively represent a significant advance in how EEG-mediated neuroscience research can be conducted, shared, and applied globally, with potential far-reaching implications for understanding brain health and developing interventions.

Conclusion

The EEGNet platform integrates standardized datasets, workflows, and computational infrastructure for collaborative annotation and analysis across high-performance computing clusters, using the BIDS format in accordance with FAIR principles. By reducing data sharing barriers and leveraging the EEG-BIDS standard, EEGNet employs independent component analysis pipelines and qEEG toolboxes to facilitate machine learning (ML), biomarker identification and global health applications.

Through these technical and scientific milestones, the EEGNet platform has established new benchmarks for open EEG data sharing and dynamic visualization of data, combined with high-performance computing analysis tools at a global level. The integration of these outputs delivers a powerful open infrastructure platform for democratizing and accelerating research across the global research landscape in brain imaging. 

The evolution of EEGNet toward a comprehensive multi-modal infrastructure for electrophysiology marks a substantial advance in neurophysiological investigation. In contrast to existing open platforms such as OpenNeuro and NEMAR, EEGNet provides interactive online EEG visualisation and discovery tools to facilitate annotation and collaborative knowledge-building directly on the web. 

Through these collaborative open workflows, EEGNet democratizes access to neurophysiological data through strategic alliances with international research networks, such as the EEGManyLabs as well as the EEGManySteps consortia, while adding advanced recording methodologies within open-science frameworks. The implementation of innovative analytical approaches to neural mass models will establish novel pathways for biomarker discovery and mechanistic understanding of human brain dynamics, with implications for clinical diagnostic protocols, therapeutic development, and preventive medicine in neurological disorders.

The ultimate convergence of reliable and reproducible research findings accelerated by recent ML advances will be openly accessible to the research community for integration into clinical care pathways to facilitate early identification and enhance precision medicine. EEGNet’s data and analytics resources provide the infrastructure to systematically evaluate and benchmark artifact removal approaches across heterogeneous datasets, setting new standards for best practice in signal cleaning, removing a long-standing obstacle to EEG adoption and transforming everyday research practices worldwide.
