Introduction

Pathological changes in Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and related dementias (ADRD) can begin decades before clinical symptoms emerge, making early detection a significant challenge. Genetic variants provide a critical tool for investigating this preclinical phase (1-3). Given the high heritability of AD risk, the role of neuro-glial genetics in individual differences in working memory function, particularly in the context of ADRD progression, has been understudied. 

Here we investigated whether single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in key astrocyte-related and glutamate signaling genes are associated with subtle differences in working memory performance and task-evoked neurovascular responses in cognitively intact older adults. Here we tested the hypothesis that genetic variation in these pathways is associated with differences in functional MRI Blood-Oxygen-Level-Dependent (BOLD) signals and performance efficiency during working memory retrieval.

Methods

We investigated (SNPs) in astrocyte-related genes and a NMDA receptor subunit gene for associations with working memory  performance and fMRI BOLD responses.  We analyzed genetic, behavioral, and functional MRI (fMRI) data (4) from 21 cognitively intact older adults (mean age = 76.24) from the University of Kentucky Alzheimer’s Disease Research Center (UK-ADRC). 

We examined 1,889 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) across five astrocyte-related genes (SLC1A2, NFATC3, GFAP, APOE, S100B) and the NMDA receptor subunit gene GRIN2B. Multiple linear regressions tested associations among SNPs, working memory efficiency (reaction time, accuracy), and working memory-evoked BOLD signals, controlling for age, sex, and education. Significance was assessed using Bonferroni correction for behavior and adjusted R² for fMRI models (Figure 1).

Results

GRIN2B polymorphisms were associated with differences in reaction time, supporting the gene’s role in processing speed, which is consistent with our previous reports (5). After severe Bonferroni correction (p < 0.05/1889), two GRIN2B SNPs remained significantly associated with task accuracy (R² = 0.91), hit rate (R² = 0.83), and miss rate (R² = 0.89).

The fMRI - SNPs analyses revealed significant hemispheric asymmetries in the genetic associations with BOLD signals.  For instance, SLC1A2 is strongly associated with task-evoked BOLD signals at the Left DLPFC, left ITLO, and Right Insula (Figure 2). The strongest association was for SNP rs75627662 (located downstream of APOE) in the left fusiform gyrus, a region involved in high-level visual processing and linked to Alzheimer’s disease (AD)-related atrophy. This SNP showed the highest effect size in our individual analyses (adjusted R² = 0.57, β = 3.38, SE = 0.72, p = 0.0003). Notably, rs75627662 has previously been implicated in structural brain changes, including entorhinal cortical thickness, hippocampal volume, and amygdala volume (5).

Conclusion

Our findings demonstrate that genetic variation related to astrocytic and glutamatergic signaling shapes individual differences in task-evoked neurovascular coupling and working memory efficiency. This provides novel evidence for a neuro-glial genetic basis of preclinical brain function and suggests that astrocyte-mediated processes likely contribute to the earliest stages of Alzheimer’s disease and related dementia (AD/ADRD) pathology.
