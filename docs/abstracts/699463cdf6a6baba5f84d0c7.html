Introduction

The fetal cortical plate (CP), subplate (SP), and intermediate zone (IZ) undergo rapid and tissue-specific microstructural changes during mid-to-late gestation, driven by evolving cytoarchitecture, cellular migration, axonal fiber ingrowth, and extracellular water dynamics1. In vivo diffusion MRI (dMRI) can indirectly capture these features by separating signal components, providing a non-invasive window into tissue organization during early brain development. Here, we use multi-shell fetal dMRI to quantify anisotropic and isotropic intracellular as well as extracellular fractions across the CP, SP, and IZ from 22–32 weeks gestational age (wGA) to characterize and directly compare their developmental microstructural trajectories.

Methods

108 typically developing fetuses (54 male; mean 27.6wGA, range 22.1-32.0) from the dHCP were scanned with SAFE dMRI (2 mm isotropic; b = 0, 400, 1000 s/mm²; 15/46/80 repeats) and preprocessed using a SHARD pipeline2. Fractional anisotropy (FA) was estimated from the 0,1000 shells3. Anisotropic fraction (Aniso), and isotropic intra- (IsoInt) and extra-cellular (IsoExt) fractions were derived using multi-shell, multi-tissue (MSMT) CSD4 with dhollander ‘tissue’ response functions (average of 50 oldest subjects) constrained to manual masks (corpus callosum, lateral ventricles, and cortical plate skeleton based on prior conventions). Fractions were intensity-normalized and scaled to sum to 1 per voxel (Fig. 1A). The SP was segmented from 0.5 mm3 T2-weighted reconstructions using a 2D U-Net and manually corrected5. CP and IZ masks were refined with dilation/erosion procedures to reduce CP-CSF boundary artefacts, prevent inclusion of non-IZ tissues, and limit partial-volume leakage across tissue boundaries. Changes in median metrics with GA were evaluated using RANSAC regression (linear or quadratic fits selected by AIC). Also, mixed-effects models were fit for each measure with tissue, GA, and their interaction as fixed effects (accounting for repeated measures across subjects). Tissue slopes were extracted and significance assessed with Wald z-tests (FDR corrected).

Results

All diffusion metrics showed marked developmental changes across 22–32 wGA (Fig. 1B). FA declined in all compartments (CP −0.005/week; SP −0.004/week; IZ −0.003/week; all p < 0.001). Aniso fractions followed similar trends, decreasing most strongly in the IZ (−0.016/week), moderately in the SP (−0.010/week), and least in the CP (−0.005/week) (Fig. 2A). RANSAC fits additionally suggested a subtle late-gestation increase in FA and Aniso fraction in the IZ. Corresponding increases in isotropic components were observed. The CP showed a modest rise in IsoInt fraction (+0.008/week) alongside a decrease in IsoExt fraction (−0.004/week). Both IZ and SP exhibited significant increases in IsoInt fraction (+0.013/week and +0.009/week, respectively), with minimal changes in extracellular IsoExt (non-significant in SP).

Conclusion

Our work characterized developmental changes in anisotropic, and isotropic extra- and intra-cellular signal across 22–32 wGA within the CP, SP, and IZ, providing a novel direct comparison of their microstructural trajectories using a unified repeated-measures framework. In the CP, decreasing anisotropy and external water fraction alongside increasing intracellular isotropic signal align with expected reductions in radial organization and rising cell density1. The SP shares features of both CP and IZ, suggesting a mixed microstructural profile. Differences between SP and IZ in the magnitude and balance of isotropic signal fractions warrant further study. A key limitation of MSMT-CSD is that it models only one anisotropic compartment. This is particularly relevant for fetal tractography efforts, as radial glial anisotropy in the CP cannot be distinguished from axonal terminations in the SP and should be further investigated for future studies of SP-to-CP axon relocation. Several relationships were also non-linear, indicating that linear fits may not capture all developmental patterns. Despite these limitations, evaluated diffusion metrics provide complementary in vivo markers of developmental change in tissue composition and may support future work linking microstructural developmental trajectories in fetuses to long-term outcomes.
