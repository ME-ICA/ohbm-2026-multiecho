Introduction

Coordinated large-scale analyses of diffusion MRI using tract-based spatial statistics (TBSS) in mental disorders (MD) consistently show subtle yet widespread white-matter (WM) alterations, including lower fractional anisotropy (FA) and higher radial diffusivity across association, commissural, and projection pathways. 1 However, the developmental origins of these WM changes remain unclear.  Myelination follows a protracted spatiotemporal trajectory, and adverse early-life experiences, such as institutionalization or childhood abuse, can disrupt normative prefrontal WM maturation. 2 We therefore hypothesize that diffusion and FA alterations index structural subgroups defined by early-life stress severity and timing.

Methods

We analyzed diffusion MRI data from 389 adults, consisting of individuals with a broad spectrum of affective, anxiety and stress-related mental disorders (=A, 218 individuals, therein 141 females, 77 males, mean age= 35.7 years) as well as induviduals unaffected by mental disorders (=UA, 171 individuals, therein 118 females, 53 males, mean age= 32.5 years) from the BeCome Study (Max Planck Institute of Psychiatry, Munich) 3. Early-life stress (ELS) was assessed with the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ). MRI data was acquired on a 3T scanner (Discovery MR750, General Electric, Milwaukee, U.S.A.) using a 32-channel coil, and included structural MRI (Sagittal FSPGR 3D BRAVO, TE 2.3 ms, TR 6.2 ms, TI 450 ms, FA 12°, FOV 25.6 × 25.6 ×20.0 cm3 , matrix 256 × 256 × 200, FDir S/I (7.2 min)) and diffusion tensor imaging (2D Spin Echo DTI, TE 60.9 ms, TR 8 s, interleaved/ bottom-up, 66 diffusion directions, 5 initial B0 images, FOV 25.6 × 25.6 cm2 , ST 2.0 mm, SP 0 mm, 60 slices, matrix 128 × 128, Fat Saturation B0 images repeated using an inverted phase sampling for later unwarping of geometric distortions (9.6 min)). Preprocessing followed standard FSL procedures, including denoising, motion and eddy-current correction, susceptibility distortion correction, and tensor fitting. To obtain detailed tract-level profiles, we applied a bundle analytics pipeline. In contrast to whole-tract TBSS, bundle analytics uses individualized tract reconstruction based on probabilistic tractography and atlas-supported bundle extraction (XTRACT)4 to extract tract-scpecific FA-values.
Statistical analyses: Bilateral tract-specific FA values were averaged, yielding 23 unique FA measures (19 bilateral tracts). Multivariate analysis of covariance (MANCOVA) was performed using a CTQ-based binary trauma-factor (denoting trauma presence above threshold 1 vs. no trauma across CTQ subscales; 168/74 participants, respectively), after exclusion of cases with any missing FA values. Age and sex were included as covariates. Significant multivariate effects were followed by univariate post-hoc tests, including exploratory sex-by-trauma interaction analyses. To account for multiple comparisons, a Bonferroni-corrected significance threshold of p < 0.0022 (0.05/23) was applied.

Results

Across the majority of examined pathways, tract-specific fractional anisotropy (FA) showed plausible age-related declines, with negative correlations observed in 17 of 23 tracts (r < 0, p < 0.05), consistent with known aging-related white-matter changes. Multivariate analysis demonstrated a significant overall effect of the CTQ-based trauma factor on the pattern of tract-specific FA values (Wilks’ Λ = 0.87, p = 0.012), in addition to significant multivariate contributions of age and sex.

Follow-up univariate analyses indicated that trauma-related FA differences were not uniformly distributed across tracts. The largest and most robust effect emerged in the bilateral fornix (F = 10.28, p = 0.001), where individuals reporting childhood trauma showed an approximately 5% lower FA compared with those without trauma exposure, corresponding to a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d = −0.38). No significant trauma-by-sex interaction effects were detected, suggesting that trauma-associated FA differences were comparable across sexes.

Conclusion

Using tract-specific white-matter analyses in a large cohort, we provide preliminary evidence that early-life stress may be associated with focal alterations in white-matter microstructure. The presence of trauma in any CTQ category was linked to lower FA at the multivariate level, with the most pronounced effect observed in the fornix. Given the fornix’s role as a major limbic pathway connecting the hippocampus with medial prefrontal and subcortical regions, this finding may point to heightened sensitivity of hippocampal–limbic circuitry to early adverse experiences. These effects appeared tract-specific rather than globally distributed, suggesting increased vulnerability of late-myelinating frontal and limbic white matter to early-life stress.5 Together, the findings indicate that diffusion alterations may reflect stress-related, developmentally shaped white-matter profiles and highlight the value of tract-specific approaches beyond voxel-wise analyses. Further in-depth analyses will apply segment-wise tract analysis and stratify stressful life events according to age-defined developmental periods.
