Introduction

Neurofibrillary tangles, a hallmark of Alzheimer’s disease (AD), can be quantified in vivo via positron emission tomography (PET). Over the years, multiple tau-PET tracers have been developed, but tracer-specific differences complicate cross-study harmonization and interpretation and direct comparisons in the same individuals remain rare. To advance reproducible brain mapping and improve mechanistic understanding of tau distribution, we conducted a head-to-head study (HEAD), in which individuals along the aging and AD spectrum were scanned with both [18F]FTP and [18F]MK6240. The objectives of our study are to: 1) assess associations of on-target and off-target regions separately between [18F]FTP and [18F]MK6240, 2) evaluate cross-regional associations between the tracers.

Methods

We collected data from 681 individuals from the HEAD dataset. All underwent cross-sectional assessments of magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), [18F]FTP and [18F]MK6240 tau-PETs, and an Aβ-PET scan. Aβ status was given based on visual reads done by two independent raters. Individuals were categorized as cognitively unimpaired (CU) young (CUY) when they were ≤ 25 years of age, CU old (CUO) when they had no objective cognitive impairment 1 and ≥ 50 years of age. Diagnosis of mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and Alzheimer’s disease (AD) were given by each site based on their neuropsychological evaluation. Individuals showing cognitive impairment but a negative Aβ status were combined as a CI Aβ- group. [18F]FTP was acquired 80-100 and [18F]MK6240 90-110 minutes post-injection 2.  Inferior cerebellar grey was used as the reference region following the Berkeley PET imaging pipeline (BPIP) 3. Images were smoothed to achieve a final resolution of 6mm3. On-target regions were: Braak regions I to VI, whole cortex and meta-temporal 4,5. Age-related and off-target regions selected were: pallidum, putamen, caudate, thalamus, choroid plexus, meninges, eroded white matter as well as the extracortical hotspot medial to the entorhinal cortex (EntoEch). 

To visualize group-level patterns, we used MATLAB (R2023a) to generate mean and standard deviation PET images stratified by both diagnostic group and Aβ status. All statistical analyses were conducted in R (version 2023.09). Cut-off for meta-temporal ROI was calculated separately for each tracer as mean from CUY + 2.5 standard deviations (SD). To assess associations between tracers, we implemented linear regression models between [¹⁸F]FTP and [¹⁸F]MK6240 regional values, stratified by Aβ status. These analyses were performed separately in defined on-target regions and off-target regions.

Results

Mean images showed clear differences between [¹⁸F]FTP and [¹⁸F]MK6240 (Figure 1A). In CUY, [¹⁸F]FTP values were higher across the brain than [¹⁸F]MK6240, and both tracers showed EntoEch uptake. In CUO Aβ-, [¹⁸F]FTP remained higher in white matter and subcortical regions, though EntoEch was less pronounced than with [¹⁸F]MK6240. CUO Aβ+ individuals began to show higher [¹⁸F]MK6240 signal in temporal regions, while low-binding individuals overall showed higher apparent [¹⁸F]FTP uptake. At higher binding levels, however, [¹⁸F]MK6240 exceeded [¹⁸F]FTP. This suggests that [¹⁸F]FTP may produce more widespread low-level signal in regions unlikely to accumulate tau that early in the disease process, though it remains unclear whether this reflects early tau changes or off-target binding. For MCI Aβ+ and AD Aβ+, both tracers showed similar spatial patterns, with higher SUVRs for [¹⁸F]MK6240. In CI Aβ-, [¹⁸F]FTP remained higher throughout the brain, especially in white matter. Although [¹⁸F]MK6240 showed meningeal spill-in in CUY, CUO Aβ- and CI Aβ-, uptake in on-target regions for MCI Aβ+ and AD Aβ+ rose above spill-in levels.

The threshold for tau positivity in the meta-temporal ROI was 1.31 for [¹⁸F]FTP and 1.25 for [¹⁸F]MK6240. Tau status did not always align (Figure 1B). In CUO Aβ-, most were tau negative (96% [18F]FTP; 98% [¹⁸F]MK6240). In CUO Aβ+, 74% were tau negative with [18F]FTP and 71% with [¹⁸F]MK6240. In MCI Aβ+, 63% were tau+. In AD Aβ+, tau positivity reached 84% ([18F]FTP) and 88% ([¹⁸F]MK6240). CI Aβ- were largely tau negative (96% both). Discordance ranged from 3–7% across groups except CUO Aβ+, which showed 17.5%.

We assessed associations between the tracers in Braak regions, the meta-temporal ROI, and whole cortex, stratified by Aβ status. All models were significant. For Aβ-, R values ranged from 0.59 to 0.71 (Braak I: 0.71; II: 0.68; III: 0.62; IV: 0.61; V: 0.59; VI: 0.63; meta-temporal: 0.64; whole cortex: 0.59). For Aβ+, correlations were stronger (Braak I: 0.89; II: 0.83; III: 0.94; IV: 0.95; V: 0.95; VI: 0.91; meta-temporal: 0.95; whole cortex: 0.94) (Figure 1C). CUY consistently showed lower values in early on-target regions, while Aβ+ and cognitively impaired groups showed higher SUVRs. CI Aβ- resembled CUO Aβ-. SUVRs increased with Aβ status and diagnosis, except in Braak II for [¹⁸F]FTP, where CUO Aβ- and Aβ+ were similar. [¹⁸F]MK6240 displayed a broader dynamic range in individuals with high Aβ load and cognitive impairment (Figure 1D).

We performed the same analyses in age-related and off-target regions. R values were generally higher for Aβ- than Aβ+ (Figure 1E). For Aβ-, correlations ranged 0.57–0.73 (pallidum: 0.73; putamen: 0.73; caudate: 0.71; thalamus: 0.65; choroid plexus: 0.61; EntoEch: 0.57; meninges: 0.65; white matter: 0.61). For Aβ+, values ranged 0.55–0.80 (pallidum: 0.61; putamen: 0.64; caudate: 0.60; thalamus: 0.58; choroid plexus: 0.55; EntoEch: 0.60; meninges: 0.66; white matter: 0.80). Pallidum, putamen, caudate, thalamus, choroid plexus, and white matter were almost always higher for [¹⁸F]FTP; EntoEch and meninges were consistently higher for [¹⁸F]MK6240. Across groups, CUY showed lower SUVRs in pallidum, putamen, caudate, and thalamus. In choroid plexus, values were similar across groups but higher for [¹⁸F]FTP. For EntoEch and meninges, CUY had higher [¹⁸F]FTP values, whereas [¹⁸F]MK6240 showed higher overall SUVR but comparable group patterns. In white matter, [¹⁸F]MK6240 showed marked variability, especially in AD Aβ+, while [¹⁸F]FTP remained more consistent, possibly due to partial volume effects (Figure 1F).

Conclusion

In this multicenter head-to-head comparison of [¹⁸F]FTP and [¹⁸F]MK6240, we identified both converging and diverging tracer characteristics. The tracers showed highly similar tau-PET patterns in regions expected to accumulate tau, with strong inter-tracer correlations across Braak stages, the meta-temporal ROI, and the whole cortex. Associations were consistently stronger in Aβ+ individuals, reflecting their greater tau burden and supporting the validity of both tracers to detect disease-related tau pathology. In contrast, variability within the Aβ- group was more prominent and likely driven by tracer-specific off-target signals influencing regional quantification. Braak II (hippocampus) showed the weakest association, consistent with known [¹⁸F]FTP spill-in from the choroid plexus. These findings support the robustness of both tracers for mapping tau spread along established anatomical hierarchies. It is particularly important to assess early tau changes, as this has growing implications in both research and treatment, given that anti-Aβ therapies appear to be more effective in individuals with low tau burden. Improved detection at earliest stages therefore matters not only for biological interpretation but also for therapeutic timing and trial stratification.

Each tracer demonstrated distinct off-target characteristics. [¹⁸F]FTP showed elevated uptake in white matter, choroid plexus, and age-related subcortical regions, whereas [¹⁸F]MK6240 displayed off-target binding in the meninges and an extracortical region near the entorhinal cortex. Both tracers showed similar age-related effects, but of different magnitude. Importantly, off-target signals were often correlated across tracers, with differences primarily reflecting magnitude rather than spatial pattern. Correlations between off-target regions were generally higher in Aβ- than Aβ+ individuals, whereas correlations in on-target regions were stronger in Aβ+, suggesting that pathological tau signal increasingly dominates tracer behavior as disease progresses.

These tracer-specific off-target patterns may contribute to the discordant tau-positivity classifications observed, particularly in CUO Aβ+ individuals (17.5%), although causality cannot be inferred. Differences in binding properties may also play a role, as early tau aggregates are enriched in 4R isoforms, whereas more mature tangles contain 3R tau. In silico modeling suggests that [¹⁸F]FTP may preferentially bind 3R tau, while [¹⁸F]MK6240 binds to both 3R and 4R aggregates, potentially explaining its higher SUVRs in AD-related regions.

Together, these findings underscore that while both tracers robustly capture tau pathology, their off-target profiles and binding sensitivities differ in ways that can influence quantification and interpretation in specific diagnostic contexts. While harmonization can be achievable, it might require independent optimization of processing pipelines for each tracer beforehand. Ultimately, understanding both the overlapping and unique properties of [18F]FTP and [18F]MK6240 allows for more precise interpretation of tau-PET data, better design of studies, more reproducible mapping of pathology across cohorts and sites, and informed decision-making in clinical and research contexts.
