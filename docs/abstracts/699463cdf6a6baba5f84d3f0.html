Introduction

At rest, people engage a slew of spontaneous cognitive, emotional, and perceptual processes. The pursuit to understand the neural mechanisms underlying “resting” thought has compelled researchers to employ multidimensional experience sampling (MDES) to measure and model how different contents and processes of thought change together.1 Integrating electroencephalography (EEG) and functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) with MDES further provides the opportunity for scientists to study mental experience unfolding at the neural level in time and space. Given the dearth of research using this multi-modal method to investigate resting state thinking processes, it is imperative that high-quality, rich datasets are proliferated within the wider scientific community to promote accessibility and collaboration as efforts to elucidate spontaneous mental processes continue. Here we introduce and validate a multi-session dataset that includes simultaneous EEG-fMRI with online measures of continuous behavior and a free-thinking condition with MDES. To achieve high-quality EEG data in the scanner, we recorded signals from carbon wire loop sensors.2 The specific goal of this study was to conduct extensive quality checks to the behavioral, EEG, and fMRI data to facilitate the dataset’s accessibility to the neuroimaging research community.

Methods

Simultaneous EEG-fMRI was collected in 24 healthy adults (ages 18-35 years) recruited from the Philadelphia, Pennsylvania area. Participants attended three sessions, including two simultaneous EEG-fMRI sessions (see Figure 1). The study protocol was approved by the local Institutional Review Board. In a first session, written informed consent was obtained from each participant alongside demographic information and surveys pertaining to different mental health conditions. At sessions two and three, EEG data from 31 scalp locations and 4 carbon wire loop sensors were collected using an MR-compatible system. The MRI data were acquired using a 64-channel head coil on a 3-Tesla Siemens Prisma scanner with a multiband sequence (TR = 2.0 sec, voxel size = 2.0x2.0x2.0 mm). 

For each scanning session, participants underwent 1 run (8-minutes) of the Gradual Onset Continuous Performance Task (GradCPT). The GradCPT was designed to engage sustained attention and track spontaneous attentional fluctuations.3 During this task, participants viewed a stream of grayscale images (cities, mountains) and were instructed to respond with index finger button presses to frequent city scenes while withholding responses to infrequent mountain scenes. In the second task, 3 runs of Resting State Multidimensional Experience Sampling (rs-MDES) were administered in each session. We designed the rs-MDES task to naturalistically study the content and dynamics of ongoing subjective experience. Six trials of a resting state period followed by MDES comprised each run. During each trial, participants viewed a central fixation cross and were instructed to let their minds wander. After a random integer time interval, 13 distinct probes appeared in sequence examining domains of thought content, quality, affect, and participant confidence. Each probe was presented with a Likert scale ranging from 1-9 including anchors describing the extremities. Across all sessions and all participants, 840 trials were collected (~65 minutes of data per participant). 

Following data collection, behavioral, EEG, and fMRI data underwent extensive quality checks. Forty-three sessions of GradCPT data were assessed for accuracy through commission (proportion of trials in which the participant incorrectly proceeded with a button press) and omission (proportion of trials in which the participant incorrectly missed a response) error rates to determine whether participants performed at an expected level. In our rs-MDES paradigm, we analyzed responses to a built-in quality check which asked participants to rate their confidence in their responses to all other probes. Quality of the artifact corrected EEG data (see Figure 2A & 2B). was assessed based on a quality control pipeline developed by Delorme et al..4 The quality control pipeline considers the percent of “good” channels, the percent of “good” data, and the percent of components identified from independent components analysis (ICA) as “brain-based”. Quality of functional MRI data was assessed via head movement (mean framewise displacement) and confirmatory generalized linear models (GLMs) using the GradCPT task data. All runs of fMRI data were further manually inspected for artifacts. Separate GLMs were run for correctly withheld responses to mountain scenes (correct omissions) and commission errors. All models utilized a whole-brain voxel-level approach. For each model, a first-level GLM was computed within each run, and a second-level was computed at the group level using FMRIB’s local analysis of mixed effects (FLAME) 1 + 2 with cluster-based thresholding at Z > 3.1 and family-wise error correction at p < 0.05. 

Results

GradCPT omission error rates within this dataset (M = 0.05, SD = 0.05) were within the typical, 3-6% range found in past studies. Commission error rates for this dataset (M = 0.38, SD = 0.18) were somewhat higher than typical rates across past studies, which average approximately 25-30% in healthy adults. Rs-MDES ratings showed a high mean and small standard deviation (M = 7.81, SD = 1.27) for the confidence probe, providing reassurance that most trials within the rs-MDES task can be considered “valid” as rated by the participants themselves. In total, there were 14 trials (1.7%) with confidence ratings below 5 in the dataset.

EEG quality metrics generated from our study were compared with reference to the quality of 30 EEG datasets (ranging from n = 2 to n = 384) collected outside of an MRI scanner.4 Overall, the percent of good channels fell within the same range observed in the comparison datasets with the majority of runs having 90-100% good channels (see Figure 2C). The percent of good data points (50-80%) in our study was slightly below that in the comparison datasets (60-90%). The percent of independent components attributed to brain activity (50-60%) was higher in our dataset than that in the comparison datasets (10-40%).

The median fMRI FD value was 0.08mm (SD = 0.04mm), which is comparable to, or smaller than that in other MRI datasets of similar size.5 Significant head movement flagged through visual inspection is noted in the open-access dataset within a description file.

Contrasts showed that BOLD responses to mountains (including both correct omissions and commission errors) were significantly increased in regions consistent with the dorsal attention network and ventral attention network and decreased in regions consistent with the default mode network (Z > 3.1, p < 0.05). These results are consistent with prior literature findings.3

Conclusion

These data enable the study of the neural bases of various spontaneous cognitive processes, including attentional fluctuations and mind-wandering, thereby promoting insights into the behavioral relevance of resting state brain activity. The dataset also provides a means to study the reliability of temporal relationships between fMRI and EEG data features across different sessions within the same individuals.
