Introduction

Brain connectivity research has traditionally focused on diffusion MRI, resting-state fMRI, EEG, and MEG to characterise structural and functional connections. Molecular imaging, particularly Positron Emission Tomography (PET), provides access to biological processes beyond those captured by structural or functional imaging, enabling quantification of neurobiological substrates, including neurotransmission by targeting specific receptors and transporters, as well as the aggregation of misfolded proteins central to many neurodegenerative disorders. While conventional PET studies examine regional tracer uptakes, recent molecular connectivity studies with ¹⁸F-FDG PET have demonstrated that inter-regional metabolic covariance provides a complementary perspective on large-scale brain organisation, capturing patterns of coordinated metabolic activity.1

In Alzheimer’s Disease (AD), Amyloid (Aβ)-PET quantifies the earliest pathological process, i.e., Aβ plaque formation.  While PET covariance approaches have been applied at the group level, their extension to single-subject Aβ-PET data remains underexplored. Methodological standards for constructing single-subject PET connectomes from static Aβ-PET data remain limited. Existing approaches estimate network structure from regional tracer uptake derived from static Aβ-PET, yet several analytical decisions, including the Aβ quantification metric  (raw SUVr or z-score), the atlas for regional segmentation (anatomical or functional), and the metric used to quantify inter-regional similarities, may substantially influence the resulting connectomes.

In this study, we evaluated how Aβ-PET normalisation, atlas choice, and similarity metrics2,3 shape single-subject Aβ-PET connectomes and identified the most robust and biologically meaningful configurations based on predictive performance on clinical outcomes. Clarifying the impact of these methodological choices provides a foundation for constructing Aβ-PET connectomes in AD research.

Methods

Participants and Aβ-PET imaging data

Aβ-PET images were obtained from the AMYPAD Prognostic & Natural History Study.4 Inclusion criteria were age > 50  and no dementia, defined by a Clinical Dementia Rating (CDR) < 1. Details of Aβ-PET imaging, processing, and harmonisation have been reported previously.4 Standardised uptake value ratio (SUVr) images were generated using the whole cerebellum as the reference region in native space. Global Aβ burden was quantified by converting SUVr values to Centiloid units (CL).

We included participants with at least one SUVr image in MNI space and excluded those with a global SUVr > 2.5. For each participant, we constructed several PET connectomes by varying: 1) SUVr map normalisation; 2) atlas choice; 3) similarity metric.

SUVr Map Normalisation

Two z-score images (zlow and zhigh)  were generated per subject using two different reference populations. After separating subjects by tracer, the low-uptake group was defined as the participants belonging to the lower-mean component of a 2-Gaussian mixture model fitted to global SUVr values, and the high-uptake group comprised the 100 subjects with the highest global SUVr.

Atlas Choice

Aβ-PET SUVr values were extracted from two cortical parcellations, the anatomically defined Desikan-Killiany atlas and the functionally defined Schaefer-100 atlas.

Constructing Aβ-PET connectomes

For each subject, pairwise inter-regional relationships were estimated using two validated approaches:

1. Kullback-Leibler Divergence Similarity Estimation (KLSE)

Regional voxel distributions were estimated via Gaussian-kernel density estimation, and pairwise regional similarities were quantified by computing the symmetrised Kullback-Leibler divergence between their probability distributions. This measure was then converted into a similarity score between 0 and 1, which constitutes the value of the edge between the two regions in the resulting connectome.2

2. Effect Size Approach

Each region was summarised through a scalar deviation measure, defined as the difference between the subject’s regional uptake and the corresponding regional mean in a low-uptake reference population. Inter-regional relationships were quantified using an effect-size formulation, which measures the normalised difference between the respective regional deviations. Once rescaled between 0 and 1, these values formed a subject-specific weighting matrix used to modulate the group-level correlation matrix derived from the same reference population, resulting in the final single-subject connectome.3

In total, ten connectomes were generated per subject: six KLSE (3 image types across 2 atlases) and four Effect Size connectomes (z-score images only to avoid the added complexity of computing separate group-level correlation matrices for each tracer when using SUVr).

Comparing Predictive Capacity of the Connectomes

To determine the most informative connectome configuration, we assessed the predictive performance of each connectome using Connectome Predictive Modelling (CPM).5 We evaluated three binary outcomes: i) Aβ accumulation defined by an annual rate of change over 3 CL, ii) progression in Aβ status from negative to positive, and iii) progression in CDR global score from 0 to 0.5. For each connectome, edge values across subjects were correlated with the corresponding outcome vector. Edges that remained significant after FDR correction were retained, and positive/negative sets based on the sign of the correlation coefficient were averaged per subject to yield two CPM features. These were used as two distinct covariates in a logistic regression.5 To test whether connectome-derived features capture information beyond global Aβ burden, we compared each outcome’s best-performing connectome with a baseline CL-only model, assessing statistical significance using DeLong’s test.

Predictive performance was evaluated using 10-fold stratified cross validation, and mean ROC-AUC scores were reported.

Results

Participant Characteristics

1315 participants met the inclusion criteria for this study. Mean age was 68.26 (± 8.96), and 755 (57%) were females. Overall, participants were cognitively unimpaired, and only 168 (13%) had a CDR = 0.5 (very mild impairment); the average MMSE was 28.93 (± 1.45) and average years of education were 14.61 (± 3.98). ApoE ε4 carriers were 490 (38%). The mean global Aβ-PET burden was 17.64 (± 29.55). Most participants (n = 1,005; 76%) were Aβ-negative (CL < 24.1), while 310 (24%) were classified as Aβ-positive.

Predictive Performance of Single-Subject Aβ-PET Connectomes

Across all three outcome measures, KLSE connectomes generally outperformed Effect Size connectomes, while SUVR and zhigh-scaled maps generally outperformed zlow-scaled maps; choice of atlas mostly did not impact predictive power. Specifically, detectable Aβ accumulation was best predicted by KLSE connectomes using the Desikan-Killiany atlas with zhigh-scaled maps (AUC=0.837) or KLSE connectomes using the Schaefer atlas with SUVR maps (AUC=0.835). Conversely, progression in Aβ status from negative to positive was best predicted by Effect Size connectomes built from zhigh-scaled maps, using either the Schaefer (AUC=0.842) or Desikan-Killiany atlas (AUC=0.826). Finally, worsening in global CDR was best predicted by KLSE connectomes built from SUVR maps in the Desikan-Killiany atlas (AUC=0.696).

Comparison With a Centiloid-Only Model

For detectable Aβ accumulation, the best-performing connectome achieved a higher ROC-AUC (AUC=0.836) than the Centiloid-only model (AUC=0.755), with DeLong’s test confirming a significant difference (p_val = 3.28×10-5). For progression from Aβ-negative to Aβ-positive, performance was comparable between models (p_val =0.806), with the connectome yielding an AUC of 0.842 and the Centiloid-only model slightly higher at 0.850. For progression in global CDR, the best-performing connectome again achieved a higher AUC (AUC=0.696) relative to the Centiloid-only model (AUC=0.579), with a significant difference supported by DeLong’s test (p_val = 4.13×10-5).

Conclusion

In this study, we address the limited availability of methodological standards for deriving network representations from static Aβ-PET data by providing the first systematic evaluation of the methodological choices involved in constructing single-subject Aβ-PET connectomes and clarifying how Aβ quantification metrics, atlas selection, and similarity metrics shape the resulting networks. 

In this preclinical population, KLSE-based connectomes and SUVR or high-z-scaled maps produced the most informative representations, although the optimal configuration appears to be dependent on the clinical outcome of interest. 

Optimal connectome choices achieved higher predictive accuracy than a Centiloid-only model for detectable amyloid accumulation and progression in global CDR, while showing comparable performance for progression from Aβ-negative to Aβ-positive status, demonstrating that single-subject Aβ-PET connectomes capture biologically meaningful structure beyond regional Aβ load.

These findings establish a methodological foundation for deriving robust Aβ-PET connectomes from static PET data and support their utility as complementary biomarkers for tracking early AD-related progression at the individual level.
