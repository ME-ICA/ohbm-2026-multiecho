Introduction

Preterm birth, particularly extreme prematurity, is an increasing global public health challenge and is strongly associated with long-term motor, cognitive, and behavioral impairments. The cerebellum undergoes rapid growth during late gestation and is highly vulnerable to adverse perinatal conditions, placing preterm-born children at elevated risk for cerebellum structural and functional abnormalities. Beyond motor control, the cerebellum is critically involved in higher-order cognitive functions, including language, attention, executive function, and emotional regulation 4. However, prior studies have largely focused on global cerebellum volumetric or functional abnormalities and reporting reduced cerebellum volume and increased structural asymmetry related to later developmental difficulties. Consequently, fine-grained anatomical features, such as lobule-specific developmental patterns, remain poorly characterized, and the extent to which different degrees of prematurity produce region-specific cerebellum vulnerability remains unclear.

Addressing these gaps requires reliable tools capable of capturing cerebellum lobules development across the lifespan. However, accurate and automated cerebellum lobule parcellation remains challenging due to rapid neurodevelopment and substantial inter-site variability in image intensity and contrast, limiting their generalizability. Existing methods are typically designed for specific age ranges or datasets, resulting in inconsistent parcellation across developmental stages. To address these limitations, we develop an unified, fully automated lifespan cerebellum lobule parcellation framework (uCereParc) and leverage it to systematically compared cerebellum lobule volumes between term-born children and those born across varying degrees of prematurity in the ABCD 1 cohort. 

Methods

To investigate cerebellum lobule-specific volumetric variations, we develop uCereParc, a unified anatomy-guided lifespan cerebellum parcellation framework. Specifically, uCereParc first extracts cerebellum regions from T1w MRI and then derives intensity-invariant anatomical features (Sobel edge maps) to facilitate robust cerebellum lobule parcellation across heterogeneous datasets 5. As illustrated in Figure 1(a), uCereParc comprises two stages: (I) a cerebellum extraction network that localizes cerebellum regions and (II) a cerebellum lobule parcellation network that integrates cerebellum intensity with corresponding Sobel edge maps to guide robust parcellation. Both stages are implemented using the SwinUNetR architecture. By explicitly accounting for inter-site intensity and contrast variability, uCereParc achieves consistent and precise lobule parcellation, supporting reliable volumetric analyses of cerebellum lobule structure across degrees of prematurity.

       

       To assess fine-grained cerebellum lobule structure volumetric differences between preterm and term-born children, we divided participants into three preterm categories, i.e., extremely preterm (< 28 gestational weeks), very preterm (28-32 gestational weeks), and late preterm (32-37 gestational weeks), according to WHO criteria, alongside a term-born group, using the ABCD¹ dataset. Owing to the limited number of extremely preterm participants (N = 107), matched subsets of very preterm, late preterm, and term-born children were randomly selected to ensure balanced group comparisons. The final sample comprised 475 participants, including 107 extremely preterm, 126 very preterm, 121 late preterm, and 121 term-born children (demographic details shown in Figure 1(b)). Cerebellum lobule volumes were automatically segmented using the uCereParc framework. Group differences of cerebellum lobule volumes were assessed using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA), with age, sex, and the total cerebellum volume included as covariates to control for demographic effects and overall cerebellum size. The comparison results are shown in Figure 1(c) and 1(d).

Results

For training and evaluating uCereParc, we use the large-scale lifespan HCP datasets 2, comprising BCP (655 scans) 3, HCPD (652), HCPY (1,094), and HCPA (712), yielding a total of 3,113 scans. Cerebellum lobule annotations were generated by propagating predefined cerebellum lobule atlas (SUIT) to individual subjects using the method in Ya Wang et al. 4. In addition, cerebellum lobules from 14 subjects were manually labeled by an experienced neuroanatomist for independent evaluation. uCereParc achieves a mean Dice score of 82.052.30 on the manually labeled subjects, demonstrating its robust and accurate lobule parcellation performance.

       As shown in Figure 1(c) and 1(d), comparisons of cerebellum volumes across preterm groups and term-born children in the ABCD dataset reveal marked group-dependent differences. In the extremely preterm group, four lobules, i.e., R-I_IV (p=0.036), L-I_IV (p=0.041), L-Crus I (p=0.041) and R-VIIb (p=0.036), exhibited significant volumetric alterations, after correction of multiple comparisons. Notably, L-Crus I shows a significant volume increase, whereas R-I_IV, L-I_IV, and R-VIIb show significant volume decreases. In the very preterm group, three lobules, i.e., R-I_IV (p=0.045), L-I_IV (p=0.004), and R-VIIb (p=0.004), demonstrate significant volume decreases. In contrast, the late preterm group shows no significant lobule volume differences compared with term-born children in any lobule.

Conclusion

In this work, we introduce a novel fully automated, and accurate lifespan cerebellum lobule parcellation framework (uCereParc) and perform a find-grained cerebellum lobule volumetric analysis comparing adolescents born preterm with term-born controls. We identified that adolescents born extremely preterm exhibit the most widespread lobular alterations, affecting both anterior (R-I_IV and L-I_IV) and posterior lobules (L-Curs I and R-VIIb), suggesting that severe prematurity disrupts cerebellar regions supporting multiple functional domains. 

This study provides structural evidence linking prematurity to region-specific cerebellum abnormalities, with implications for early identification and clinical intervention.
