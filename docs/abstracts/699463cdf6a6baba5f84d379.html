Introduction

Accumulating evidence has indicated developmental dyslexia (DD) as a multifactorial neurodevelopmental disease, entailing, among the others, sensorimotor impairments1. Behavioral studies suggest that internal action representations, central to motor control and embodied cognition, are altered in DD2, yet the associated neural bases and relative mechanisms remain unclear, with impairments potentially intrinsic to DD or secondary to comorbidity with developmental coordination disorder (DCD). High-field (7T) multimodal MRI enables fine-grained mapping of the neural systems supporting motor imagery and their potential disruption in neurodevelopmental conditions. Here, we investigate the neural bases of locomotor and oromotor internal action representations, both previously reported to be affected, in young adults with DD, individuals with comorbid DD and DCD (DYSCO), and typical readers (TYP). We hypothesized that DD and DYSCO would show atypical recruitment of classical motor-imagery networks, reflecting intrinsic but differential sensorimotor alterations rather than additive comorbidity effects. By combining 7T task fMRI with precise individual-subject analyses, we aim to characterize subject-specific activation signatures that may clarify the transdiagnostic nature of sensorimotor components in neurodevelopmental disorders.

Methods

Participants. Young adults with DD (N=17) and matched typical readers (TYP; N=12) underwent neuropsychological assessment (reading fluency: Alouette; reading history: ARHQ; ADHD likelihood: ASRS-1.1; motor skills: M-ABC2).Groups were matched on age, sex, and education. No participants were excluded due to motion or data quality.

Tasks. Two block-design imagery paradigms were used. A previously validated gait imagery paradigm3, entailing 10-trial blocks (5 narrow/5 wide) presented pseudorandomly. For each trial, there was a sequence of jittered fixation (3–5 s), corridor image (3 s), with participants then initiating and terminating kinaesthetic, egocentric gait imagery with button presses (mean trial duration ~7–8 s). Visual-observation blocks (disk-sliding control, allocentrically imagined) matched timing (Figure).

For the second task, an articulation imagery paradigm entailed two blocks per modality (overt articulation, imagined articulation, inner reading), with fixation (3–5 s) followed by monosyllabic pseudoword triplets derived from a French corpus. Responses were single button presses. Inter-block intervals varied (4–12 s). Practice ensured instruction comprehension before entering the scanner. Imagery vividness (MIQ-3 and task-specific metacognitive appraisal) was collected.

MRI acquisition (7T Siemens Terra, 1Tx/32Rx head coil) & Procedure. For structural MP2RAGE acquisition, 0.6 mm isotropic; TR=5 s; TE=3.13 ms; TI=900 ms; FA=6°. ForTask/rest fMRI mbep2d acquisition, slice thickness=1.6 mm; TR=1.08 s; TE=25.2 ms; FA=38°; MB factor=5; 600–800 volumes per run depending on imagery durations. Two runs (short LR, 5 volumes and long RL, actual task) per task were collected, with different phase-encoding directions for distortion correction, plus resting-state (eyes open, black screen). GRE field maps were acquired. DWI was acquired but not analyzed for the present report.

The acquisition lasted around 1h10 on average and included anatomical, resting-state, task-based fMRI, and DWI. Before entering the scan, participants gave their informed consent and underwent a familiarisation session. Behavioural assessments, including neuropsychological evaluations and demographics, were performed on a different session

Preprocessing and Surface Reconstruction

fMRIPrep was used for motion correction, SDC/topup, susceptibility distortion correction, aCompCor, and confound modeling. MP2RAGE images were UNIDEN B1-corrected before FreeSurfer reconstruction. Functional data were mapped to individual native-space surfaces (fsnative), and subcortical structures remained in native volumetric space. First-level GLMs (SPM HRF+derivatives, motion/physio regressors, 8s high-pass) modeled imagery and observation regressors.

Surface-based Group-Level Analyses

For cortical analyses, individual contrasts were projected to fsaverage and smoothed (4mm FWHM) to preserve 7T spatial precision. Group-level inference used mri_glmfit and permutation-based cluster-wise correction (mri_glmfit-sim, 5000 permutations, cluster-forming threshold t > 3.0, FWE p < 0.05). Contrasts included imagery>baseline, gait typical>observation, and narrow>wide gait imagery. Because data collection is ongoing, emphasis is placed on individual-level activation profiles, supplemented by exploratory group maps. Surface maps were displayed on native midthickness meshes, and group maps used fsaverage. Resting-state and diffusion MRI preprocessing is complete but analyses are ongoing.

Atlas-Based ROI Analysis (Volumetric Validation)

To substantiate surface findings, a complementary ROI-based analysis was conducted using cortical (Harvard-Oxford), cerebellar (SUIT), and subcortical atlases. For each ROI, we quantified (1) percent suprathreshold voxels (t > 3.0), (2) peak t-value, and (3) overlap with surface-defined clusters. This provided an anatomically grounded validation of cortical and subcortical recruitment patterns.

Results

Results

Behaviour
Both TYP and DD participants showed significantly longer gait-imagery durations during the biomechanically constrained condition (i.e., precision gait), hence adhering to Fitts’ Law and validating paradigm engagement. Interestingly, compared to TYP, metacognitive vividness was lower, consistent with reduced efficiency of internal action representations. For the neuropsychological assessment, ADHD-likelihood scores were comparable across groups.

Task-fMRI: Individual-Level Surface Maps
At 7T resolution, TYP participants showed the expected, sharply localized engagement of the premotor–parietal motor imagery network, including SMA, dorsal premotor cortex, superior parietal lobule, and precuneus during both gait and articulation imagery. DD individuals, however, exhibited increased and spatially more widespread activation, extending into fronto-temporo-parietal association regions, alongside reduced subcortical and cerebellar engagement. This pattern replicated across both the locomotor and oromotor tasks.

Group-Level Surface and Volume Analyses

For the gait typical > observation contrast, isolating the specific egocentric perspective, permutation-corrected surface analyses (p<0.05 FWE) in TYP revealed significant clusters in somatomotor areas including the central, precentral, and postcentral gyri, medial parietal regions, and precuneus. DD produced weaker and fragmented clusters in the somatomotor areas, while more consistent activations were recorded in inferior parietal gyrus (supramarginal and angular) bilaterally and in prefrontal areas. 

Atlas-based ROI quantification reinforced these findings. In TYP, the most consistently activated ROI was in the somatomotor areas, with peak t-values > 4. DD showed reduced ROI engagement and attenuated peaks (< 4.0), especially in basal ganglia.

Exploratory group maps demonstrated higher cross-subject consistency of fronto-parietal recruitment in TYP and over-recruitment of associative regions in DD, highlighting altered neural strategies for generating internal action representations (Figure 1a).

For the articulation > imagined articulation observation, significant clusters in frontotemporal areas involved in language production were more activated in TYP. In contrast, DD produced bilateral decreased activity and again associative areas in prefrontal and frontal clusters. This was substantiated by exploratory group maps (Figure 1b).

Conclusion

Discussion

Preliminary 7T individual-subject analyses reveal atypical engagement of internal action representation networks in DD, expressed as increased reliance on fronto-temporo-parietal regions and reduced subcortical recruitment during both locomotor and oromotor imagery. These findings align with behavioral evidence for intrinsic sensorimotor contributions to DD and reinforce emerging transdiagnostic perspectives on neurodevelopmental disorders 4, 5. The latter is particularly relevant to bridge the clinic-research gap in NDD classification at the nosological and potentially at the interventional levels. 

Through high-field, surface-based analyses and precise within-subject mapping, this study demonstrates the feasibility and added value of 7T imaging for characterising mechanistic phenotypes in neurodevelopmental conditions while accounting for individual variability. Ongoing data collection will enable multimodal integration across structural, functional, and diffusion measures to achieve a comprehensive structural and functional description of young adults with DD.
