Introduction

Introduction: Brain tumors induce widespread alterations in structural and functional brain networks, extending well beyond the lesion site and leading to large‑scale reorganization of the connectome1,2 . The extent and pattern of this reorganization are closely linked to cognitive outcome and survival 3. In this study, we aim to investigate how tumor location shapes the reorganization of functional brain networks at the level of large-scale connectivity gradients and local graph topology, particularly within cognitive systems critical for language. 

Methods

Methods: We analyzed resting-state fMRI (rs-fMRI) data from two patient groups stratified by tumor location: frontal lobe tumors (n = 21) and temporal lobe tumors (n = 31). Whole-brain functional connectivity (FC) matrices were constructed among 360 cortical regions-of-interest (ROIs) from the HCP-MMP1.0 atlas. To derive FC gradients, each subject’s FC matrix was thresholded by retaining the top 15% strongest positive connections for each ROI. Diffusion map embedding was applied to characterize large-scale cortical organization, yielding three principal functional gradients (G1–G3; Fig.1 a-b) using the BrainSpace toolbox 4. Individual gradients were aligned to a common reference defined by group-level gradients computed from the mean FC matrix across all subjects. Multivariate group differences were quantified as Euclidean distances in the 3D gradient space (G1–G3), with nonparametric permutation testing using shuffled group labels. Then, group differences along each gradient were assessed using t-tests at each ROI, with effect sizes quantified by Cohen’s d. To examine language-specific reorganization, a predefined language network was extracted, and graph-theoretical measures—including node degree and weighted betweenness centrality—were computed on a thresholded network retaining the top 30% strongest absolute connections. Global language-network strength was defined as the summed absolute edge weights and compared between groups.

Results

Results: As shown in Fig. 1a and Fig. 1b, functional gradients were highly consistent across groups, exhibiting similar global organization and spatial patterns along G1–G3. Procrustes analysis further demonstrated high geometric similarity across gradient dimensions, with low disparity between G1–G2 (disparity = 0.064; similarity = 0.94) and G1–G3 (disparity = 0.075; similarity = 0.93). Multivariate permutation testing in the 3D gradient space identified several regions showing uncorrected group differences (p_perm < 0.05) with large multivariate effect sizes (d ≈ 0.9–1.6), primarily involving inferior frontal (L_IFJ, L_IFSp), temporal (R_EC, L_TGv, R_PeEc), auditory (L_A1), and inferior parietal (L_IP0) cortices, indicating spatially structured but subthreshold manifold displacement between groups (Fig. 1c). Along individual gradients, frontal and temporal tumor groups exhibited gradient-specific cortical shifts (Fig. 1d). On G1, differences were most pronounced in inferior frontal, medial and lateral temporal, early auditory, and inferior parietal regions (|d| ≈ 0.55–1.05), suggesting altered positioning along the principal sensory–transmodal axis. On G2, group differences involved superior parietal, auditory association, premotor, and orbitofrontal prefrontal regions (|d| ≈ 0.56–0.89), consistent with shifts along a fronto-parietal control–association gradient. On G3, differences were observed in anterior-medial cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex, premotor regions, lateral temporal cortex (|d| ≈ 0.56–0.90), broadly aligned with a limbic–visuomotor axis. Within the language network, focal topological alterations were observed despite preserved global coupling strength. Node degree was significantly increased in posterior frontal, auditory, superior temporal, and temporo-parieto-occipital junction regions (p < 0.05; d ≈ 0.60–0.81, Fig. 2a ), whereas weighted betweenness centrality was significantly reduced in right temporal auditory and language-related regions (d ≈ 0.56–0.65, Fig. 2b). Global language-network strength did not differ significantly between groups.

Conclusion

Conclusion: Frontal and temporal brain tumors are associated with subtle but spatially organized reconfiguration of the cortical functional manifold, characterized by gradient-specific displacement of language- related and fronto-parietal control organization, and limbic–visuomotor regions rather than global network disruption.
