Introduction

Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD) is associated with alterations in resting-state connectivity and task-based fMRI (tb-fMRI) BOLD signal^1-2. Prior tb-fMRI studies are compounded by different task paradigms, small sample sizes, and varied measures used to assess the domains of craving, drinking behaviors, and symptom severity in order to examine potential clinical biomarkers. To this aim, we examined these associations with Alcohol Cue Reactivity (ACR) both within and among several datasets.

Methods

ACR tb-fmri data from 20 ENIGMA Addiction Working Group datasets (N = 1,098; 725 AUD, 373 non-dependent controls (NDC); ages 16-81) were preprocessed using fmriprep. The Alcohol > Neutral cue contrast and its relationship with 8 clinical variables were examined, both within- and between-groups, with all results corrected for multiple comparisons (FDR p<0.05). Symptom severity measures included DSM criteria, alcohol dependence scale, alcohol use disorder identification test, and the obsessive compulsive drinking scale. Craving measures included the alcohol urge questionnaire and alcohol craving automated scale. Measures of drinking behavior included amount consumed and number of days (in the past three months) abstaining from consumption.

Results

AUD showed significantly greater BOLD signal in rostral anterior cingulate, posterior cingulate and ventral medial prefrontal cortices compared to NDC for whole-brain voxelwise analyses. Using the Yeo 7-network atlas, network-level relationships with ACR were compared between AUD and NDC, showing the DMN having the most number of significant voxels out of any network. Comparisons between ACR and clinical data revealed that the DMN showed significant group-level differences in the aforementioned associations. Of note, opposing relationships between abstinent days and ACR (AUD (negative) and NDC (positive)) was observed in all 5 networks that survived FDR-correction, despite abstinence and consumption over a 90 day period being only weakly correlated (rho = -0.38, CI = [-0.47, -0.28]). This was also the case for alcohol consumed (AUD = positive, NDC = negative).

Conclusion

Overall, these results demonstrate the importance of pooling large-scale data and selection of functionally-derived atlases for conducting tb-fmri in AUD. Results show an inverse relationship with ACR between abstinence from alcohol and alcohol consumption. While the DMN was the primary network associated with AUD, ACR, and clinical measures, targeted interventions (i.e. rTMS) tend to focus on non-DMN regions (i.e. dorsolateral prefrontal cortex). Ongoing work entails testing the DMN’s involvement in AUD treatment outcomes, across multiple types of interventions, using the ENIGMA Addiction’s pooled, ACR datasets. We will discuss the implications of targeted^2 (i.e. rTMS) versus generalized^3,4 (i.e. psychopharmacological) interventions on ACR and future directions of the ENIGMA Addiction working group.
