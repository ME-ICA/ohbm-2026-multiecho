Introduction

Mammillary bodies (MB) are small, circular, bilateral structures located at the base of the hypothalamus. Part of the limbic system, they are crucially involved in memory especially in the formation of long-term episodic memory and spatial navigation and a key node of the Papez circuit. Automatic segmentation of mammillary bodies has been very difficult due to their small size and poor contrast on standard T1 MRI. As a result, MB is not part of the outputs of standard subcortical segmentation tools such as FSL-FIRST and Freesurfer. 

White-matter nulled (WMn) contrast1, where white-matter as opposed to cerebrospinal fluid is nulled in T1 weighted MRI acquisitions, was shown to significantly improve intrathalamic contrast, enabling direct visualization of thalamic nuclei. However, it requires the acquisition of a specialized MRI pulse sequence and WMn MRI data is not part of many public databases. Recently, a simple method for synthesis of WMn contrast from T1 MRI was proposed2 in the context of thalamic nuclear segmentation, enabling accurate segmentation from standard T1 MRI data from public databases. In this work, we used this idea of WMn contrast synthesis for visualization followed by automatic segmentation of bilateral mammillary bodies from standard T1 MRI. 

Methods

Data: This study utilized 5 T1 MPRAGE datasets taken from the Human Connectome Project Young Adult database and 5 WMn MPRAGE datasets acquired in house. All data were acquired on 3T (Siemens for HCP, Philips for inhouse) and were 1mm isotropic resolution. The WMn MRI was a modified MPRAGE sequence with TI set to 450ms to null WM and a TR of 4.5s. Data were acquired from subjects following prior informed consent adhering to local institutional review board guidelines.

Multi-atlas segmentation: We modified the existing multi-atlas segmentation framework of Thalamus Optimized Multi Atlas Segmentation (THOMAS)3 to include MB labels. For thalamic nuclei, manual segmentation labels from each of the 20 priors are warped to an average WMn template. The average WMn template is nonlinearly registered to the input image, and the 20 labels are warped to input image space and then combined using label fusion. To reduce the burden of manual segmentation, we just manually segmented the average WMn template to add bilateral MB labels, eliminating the need for label fusion and prior-to-template warps. For T1 MRI, an additional Histogram-based polynomial synthesis (HIPS) step2 was applied to synthesize WMn-like contrast from T1, prior to THOMAS processing. This significantly improves accuracy due to increased image contrast. The modified pipeline is shown in Figure 1.

Analysis: The 5 T1 MPRAGE and 5 WMn MPRAGE data sets were manually segmented by an experienced neuroscientist. For T1 MRI, the HIPS-synthesized version was used to identify the boundaries. Segmentation accuracy was determined using Dice similarity coefficients.

Results

Figure 2 shows 2 examples of the proposed MB segmentation- one from T1 showing native T1 (A) and HIPS-synthesized WMn like contrast (B). Manual segmentation is shown as red outlines while the proposed automated segmentation as filled green labels. Note the dramatic change in contrast following HIPS preprocessing for the T1 image (B). For T1 MPRAGE data, mean Dice of 0.9 and 0.88 for left and right sides were achieved compared to mean Dice of 0.86 and 0.88 for the WMn MPRAGE data. The overall Dice for all 10 subjects were 0.88 +/- 0.04 for both sides indicating excellent accuracy and robustness.

Conclusion

Very few tools exist for automated MB segmentation. The sclimbic4 toolbox that is part of the Freesurfer distribution is one of the few tools segmenting MB and reports Dice of 0.78 and 0.8 for left and right MB respectively. Here, we have successfully demonstrated the segmentation of MB leveraging WMn contrast and using a multi-atlas framework, achieving bilateral Dice of 0.88. The use of HIPS synthesis enabled the use of our method in standard T1 MRI via synthesis of WMn-like image prior to segmentation. We will be releasing this feature in the next release of THOMAS which is already widely used for thalamic nuclei segmentation. This open source free software would open the door for analysis of previously unexplored data for investigating the role of MB in neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer’s disease as well as neuropsychiatric illnesses such as schizophrenia.
