Introduction

The identification of Parkinson’s disease (PD) subtypes is crucial for predicting the disease course and designing personalized therapeutic strategies. This study aimed to define PD subtypes based on the spatiotemporal progression patterns of striatal dopamine depletion and cerebral hypoperfusion using Subtype and Stage Inference (SuStaIn).

Methods

In total, 708 patients with newly diagnosed PD and 50 healthy controls who underwent dual-phase 18F-N-(3-fluoropropyl)-2β-carbomethoxy-3β-(4-iodophenyl) nortropane (18F-FP-CIT) PET scans were retrospectively enrolled. After quantifying the dual-phase 18F-FP-CIT PET images, we applied SuStaIn, a probabilistic machine learning method, to characterize the heterogeneity of the spatiotemporal evolutionary patterns of striatal dopamine depletion and regional cerebral hypoperfusion in patients with PD. In the spatial dimension, we defined 12 regions of interest (ROIs; more-affected and less-affected anterior caudate, posterior caudate, anterior putamen, posterior putamen, ventral putamen, and ventral striatum) for decreases in striatal dopamine transporter (DAT) availability, and eight ROIs (frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, posterior cingulate, limbic cortices, precuneus, and insula) for cerebral hypoperfusion. Each ROI included three event stages (z = 1, 3, and 5) representing the degree of abnormality relative to the control group. Subsequently, we compared the baseline clinical features between the PD subtypes.

Results

The SuStaIn analysis delineated three subtypes of PD with distinct spatiotemporal evolutionary patterns of striatal dopamine depletion and cerebral hypoperfusion: Subtype 1 (n = 406) initially presented with selective and asymmetric dopamine loss in the posterior putamen, followed by dopaminergic deficits in the associative and limbic striatum, and later cerebral hypoperfusion progressing from the occipital region to the temporal, frontal, and parietal regions; Subtype 2 (n = 205) exhibited diffuse and symmetric striatal dopamine loss in the early-stages, followed by cerebral hypoperfusion progressing from the occipital region to the temporal, frontal, and parietal regions in the mid-stages; and Subtype 3 (n = 89) initially showed hypoperfusion in the parieto-occipital regions, followed by cerebral hypoperfusion in other cortical areas and striatal dopamine depletion. The remaining eight patients with PD were designated as stage 0, indicating that they did not have sufficiently abnormal imaging findings to be categorized into a defined subtype. Patients in Subtype 2 were older at the time of disease onset. Subtype 3 was characterized by a younger age of onset and a lower prevalence of rapid eye movement sleep behavior disorder.

Conclusion

Our SuStaIn model revealed three PD subtypes with distinct spatiotemporal evolutionary patterns of striatal dopamine depletion and cerebral hypoperfusion. Based on this model, the spatiotemporal trajectories of dual-phase 18F-FP-CIT images may serve as potential progression markers in PD.
