Introduction

Autism is believed to originate early in development, yet its neurobiology remains poorly understood, partly because most neuroimaging evidence comes from studies conducted after diagnosis. Although some studies have examined infants before diagnosis and identified postnatal enlargement of brain and cerebrospinal fluid volumes from around 6 months of age, these did not assess prenatal brain development. It therefore remains difficult to distinguish brain features that precede the emergence of autistic traits from those influenced by postnatal factors. In our previous work, children diagnosed prenatally with isolated ventriculomegaly were more likely to show autistic traits later in childhood. We hypothesised that ventricular enlargement may represent an early specific feature of autism-related brain development seen at a population level, beyond a diagnosis of isolated ventriculomegaly. We examined whether children who later showed autistic traits had early ventricular enlargement.

Methods

Participants were enrolled in the Brain Imaging in Babies study, a prospective longitudinal MRI study of 103 children imaged at three timepoints: fetal (n=57, 21–37 gestational weeks), neonatal (n=61, birth-4 weeks postnatal) and infant (n=40, 4-7 months). At 3 years of age, children were assessed for autistic traits using the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule–2 (ADOS-2) (Figure 1). Imaging quantification focused a priori on the lateral ventricles, with secondary analyses of related brain regions. MRI data were processed using automated deep learning–based segmentation methods. Ventricular volumes were adjusted for gestational age at birth, age at scan, sex, and supratentorial brain tissue volume. An independent replication cohort (n=38, Sweden) imaged at 5–6 months and assessed at 3 years was used to test our findings.

Results

At the 3-year follow-up, 21% of fetal, 29% of neonatal, 25% of 6-month participants, and 39% from the replication cohort scored above the ADOS-2 Calibrated Severity Score (CSS) Total threshold for autism (≥4). Children scoring above the ADOS-2 threshold had significantly larger lateral ventricles compared with those below, at all imaging timepoints: fetal (22% increase, p=0.008), neonatal (22%, p=0.010), infant (34%, p=0.004) and replication infant cohort (p=0.03). Ventricular volume correlated positively with ADOS-2 CSS Total (fetal: ρ=0.37, p=0.003; neonatal: ρ=0.25, p=0.024; infant: ρ=0.40, p=0.006) and remained significant after adjustment for cognitive ability. Additionally, cortical enlargement was observed postnatally in the frontal (p=0.032), temporal (p=0.045), and cingulate (p=0.018) cortices. Conclusion

This study provides the earliest longitudinal evidence that autism-related brain features, particularly ventricular enlargement, are present before birth and persist through early infancy. These findings support a prenatal origin for autism-related brain development features established before postnatal influences.
