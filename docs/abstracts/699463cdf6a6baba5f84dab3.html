Introduction

In visual search, a robust early N2pc indexes spatially selective attention. In addition, recent work in singleton/feature search has reported a bilateral occipital positive-going activity linked to target detection, typically computed as target-present minus target-absent ERPs. However, in search for nonsalient complex shapes, training is often required and conscious report (“Hit” vs “Miss”) frequently dissociates from performance. Qu et al. showed that for complex shapes, early N2pc depends more on training than reportable awareness. It remains unclear whether a target-detection positivity exists for nonsalient complex stimuli and how training vs reportable awareness influence it. We reanalyzed the EEG dataset from Qu et al. to test whether a complexity detection positivity (CDP) is present, whether CDP tracks awareness (Hit>Miss) rather than training, and whether the previously reported training–awareness dissociation for N2pc replicates under an alternative baseline.

Methods

We reanalyzed existing EEG data from 30 healthy young adults. Participants completed 8 training sessions of a triangle-orientation visual search over ≤12 days; each training session contained 1800 trials (80% target-present). One to two days later, participants performed EEG visual search tasks with trained vs untrained targets; each task included 720 trials with 80% target-present arrays and 20% target-absent arrays, with trained and untrained singletons appearing at balanced left/right positions. EEG was recorded from 57 scalp sites (10–10 montage), referenced to average mastoids offline; EOG was monitored. A retest session occurred 3–5 months later in a subset (8 from Group 1; 14 from Group 2). We quantified N2pc in 180–230 ms and tested CDP as target-present minus target-absent activity over posterior/occipital sites, focusing on 400–600 ms and 600–800 ms windows, and compared Train vs Untrain and Hit vs Miss conditions.

Results

Posttest: Early N2pc (180–230 ms) was significant for trained targets (−0.439±0.087 µV, t(29)=−5.038, p<0.001) but not for untrained targets (−0.099±0.076 µV, t(29)=−1.312, p=0.200); Train>Untrain (t(29)=−3.340, p=0.002). A late positive activity (CDP) was evident over posterior sites. In 400–600 ms, ipsilateral CDP was significant in Train (0.671±0.154 µV, t(29)=4.370, p<0.001) but not Untrain (0.090±0.130 µV, p=0.496); Train>Untrain (t(29)=3.277, p=0.003). In 600–800 ms, ipsilateral CDP was significant in both Train (0.561±0.169 µV, p=0.002) and Untrain (0.348±0.125 µV, p=0.009), with no Train–Untrain difference (p=0.410). Awareness (subset with sufficient trials, n=12): Contralateral N2pc was significant for Train_Hit and Train_Miss, but absent for Untrain_Hit and Untrain_Miss, with no Hit–Miss difference within Train. In contrast, CDP (600–800 ms, ipsilateral) was significant for Hit trials in both Train_Hit and Untrain_Hit, but not for Miss trials in either condition, and Train_Hit≈Untrain_Hit.

     Retest: The key pattern replicated: trained targets still elicited a significant early N2pc while untrained targets did not.

Conclusion

In visual search for nonsalient complex shapes, we identify a posterior positive-going activity (CDP) associated with successful conscious detection (Hit>Miss) regardless of whether the target is trained, whereas early N2pc reflects training-dependent attentional selection and is largely insensitive to reportable awareness. Together, these results provide evidence for a double dissociation between N2pc and CDP, suggesting that attention and awareness can be distinct yet interacting processes during complex target detection.
