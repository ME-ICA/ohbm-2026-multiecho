Introduction

Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) is a non-invasive neuromodulation technique with therapeutic potential across various neuropsychiatric disorders, particularly major depressive disorder (MDD). Its mechanism of action is thought to involve the modulation of resting-state functional connectivity (FC). Clinical protocols for MDD often target the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC), but other frontal regions like the medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC or dorsomedial prefrontal cortex, DMPFC) are also implicated in mood regulation and are emerging targets. Therefore, a comparative understanding of the specific brain networks recruited by these different frontal cortical targets (Lt.DLPFC vs. MPFC) is essential for optimizing treatment protocols in depression. Furthermore, the underlying neuro-metabolic mechanisms that predict tDCS-induced changes remain poorly understood. A comprehensive understanding of the metabolic correlates, such as the balance of excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmitters (E/I balance), neuronal integrity, and glial activity, is critical for identifying effective neurometabolic biomarkers and optimizing tDCS protocols. This study aimed to (1) investigate the effect of Lt.DLPFC and MPFC tDCS on FC, (2) compare the networks targeted by these two frontal regions for discovering new therapeutic targets, and (3) discover the specific magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) metabolite measures associated with FC changes.

Methods

Thirty-four healthy, psychotropic-free participants (23 female, mean age 37.3 years, standard deviation 16.0) completed three visits with resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging (rs-fMRI) and MRS scans. Participants had no psychiatric history and a low depressive symptom burden (BDI-II median 4.5, range 0-18). The visits included: baseline, post-single-session tDCS on the Lt.DLPFC, and post-single-session tDCS on the MPFC, with a 3-7 day washout period. MRS was used to measure Glx/GABA (E/I balance), NAA/tCr and Glx/NAA (neuronal integrity), and mI/tCr (glial activity). Rs-fMRI data was analyzed using seed-to-voxel analysis in the CONN toolbox. Seeds were placed in the Lt.DLPFC (Fronto-Parietal Network), MPFC (Default Mode Network), and dorsal anterior cingulate cortex (ACC, Salience Network VOI). Statistical analysis utilized repeated measures ANOVA with MRS metabolite measures as covariates. Cluster-level significance was set at p<0.05 (FDR corrected) with a voxel threshold of p<0.001 (uncorrected), based on Random Field Theory parametric statistics.

Results

Compared to the MPFC-tDCS condition, Lt.DLPFC-tDCS resulted in weaker functional connectivity (FC) between the Lt.DLPFC seed and the left postcentral gyrus-precentral gyrus. MPFC-tDCS, relative to baseline, resulted in decreased FC between the MPFC seed and the cerebellum and right anterior middle temporal gyrus-temporal pole. Similarly, Lt.DLPFC-tDCS showed weaker FC from the MPFC seed to the right precentral gyrus-superior frontal gyrus compared to MPFC-tDCS. 

Critically, correlation analysis revealed neuro-metabolic modulation of FC. Baseline neuronal integrity (NAA/tCr) was positively correlated with baseline Lt.DLPFC FC to regions including the right superior marginal gyrus-angular gyrus and left postcentral gyrus. Furthermore, baseline NAA/tCr was associated with increased Lt.DLPFC connectivity to the right superior frontal gyrus-supplementary motor area following MPFC-tDCS from baseline. For the ACC seed, baseline NAA/tCr was positively correlated with baseline FC to the right occipital fusiform gyrus. Additionally, baseline glial activity (mI/tCr) was negatively correlated with baseline FC from the ACC to the bilateral frontal pole. Baseline mI/tCr was also associated with increased ACC connectivity to the right superior lateral occipital cortex from baseline, and with weaker ACC connectivity to the left superior lateral occipital cortex by Lt.DLPFC-tDCS relative to MPFC-tDCS. No significant correlations were found for MPFC FC changes.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that single-session tDCS targeting the Lt.DLPFC and MPFC produces specific, differential modulations of resting-state functional connectivity (FC) across distinct brain networks. The primary finding is the region-dependent effect: Lt.DLPFC-tDCS primarily impacted FC within the sensorimotor network, showing weaker connectivity relative to MPFC-tDCS, while MPFC-tDCS induced decreases in connectivity within temporal-parietal regions. These differential network effects suggest that tailoring tDCS target regions is critical for engaging specific therapeutic networks. Crucially, these observed FC changes are significantly moderated by baseline neuro-metabolic status. Baseline measures of neuronal integrity (NAA/tCr) and glial activity (mI/tCr) act as specific biomarkers that predict both baseline FC and the direction of tDCS-induced connectivity change. For instance, NAA/tCr was associated with enhanced Lt.DLPFC FC after MPFC-tDCS. In conclusion, the differential network effects of Lt.DLPFC and MPFC tDCS are strongly influenced by underlying neuronal and glial markers, highlighting the need to utilize neuro-metabolic profiling for personalized neuromodulation.
