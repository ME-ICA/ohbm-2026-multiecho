Introduction

Diffusion MRI (dMRI) enables in-vivo characterization of white matter (WM) micro- and macro-structure through diffusion modeling and tractography. After bundle extraction, tractometry is commonly used to derive along-tract profiles of microstructural measures such as those from diffusion tensor imaging (DTI). However, existing approaches typically collapse the bundle cross-section into a single value. For bundles with sheet-like, fanning geometries (cite) such as the projection pathways, this 1D representation aggregates measures over a large area and may fail to capture local signals. We propose a 2D parametric mapping framework that preserves spatial heterogeneity across the full extent of the WM bundles^1. Building on the along-tract parameterization from BUndle ANalytics (BUAN)^2, our method defines a 2D coordinate system directly on atlas bundle streamlines with user-specified resolutions along- and radially across the bundle. This produces a common grid that enables cross subject correspondence, local measures of microstructure and spatially resolved statistical testing. We demonstrate our approach on a large multi-site sample of Alzheimer’s disease and compare the resulting spatial patterns of WM abnormalities of dementia with those obtained from conventional 1D tractometry. Code for 2D parameterization is available at https://github.com/wendyfyx/TPA.

Methods

We analyzed dMRI data from 4264 subjects (2592 F/1672 M, age 67.57 ± 9.12 years) from the Alzheimer’s disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI)^3 Phase 3/4 and the Health & Aging Brain Study (HABS-HD)^4, including 2966 cognitively normal (CN) participants, 981 with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and 317 with dementia from 12 scanner protocols. Preprocessing steps include denoising, susceptibility distortion, eddy and motion correction. We extracted 65 WM bundles using bundle tractography^5 with a modified version of the HCP-1065 atlas. DTI was fitted on the dMRI data to obtain fractional anisotropy (FA) and mean diffusivity (MD) maps.

In our 2D tractometry framework, a 2D grid was first defined on an atlas bundle in the MNI space with an along-tract and radial dimension. Along-tract segments were created using BUAN by discretizing the atlas centroid into equidistant points based on the desired segment length. At each segment, streamline points were projected onto the local tangent plane and the radial vector was defined as the direction of maximum variance using Principal Component Analysis. Radial vectors were oriented and smoothed along consecutive segments (Fig 1a), and signed radial distances were binned to produce a fixed-size grid (Fig 1b). Subject bundles registered to MNI space were parameterized using this grid: Streamline points were assigned to along-tract segments based on the closest atlas centroid point, then to radial bins using the predefined radial vectors and bin edges (Fig 1c). Microstructural maps were projected onto all points (Fig 1d) and averaged per grid cell to produce 2D profiles (Fig 1e). Grid cells containing fewer than 30% of subjects were excluded (Fig 1f), and local statistics were obtained for the remaining grid cells (Fig 1g). We derived 1D and 2D profiles for FA and MD across 65 WM bundles using 3mm segments and bin lengths. The 1D profiles were generated with the weighted mean version of BUAN where DTI measures were weighted by distance to the centroid. Linear mixed models were fitted at every segment or grid cell with age and sex as fixed effects and scanner protocol as random effect. False discovery rate was controlled using the Benjamini-Hochberg method across all bundles, and partial effect sizes (d) were calculated as the ratio of the fixed effect coefficient to the residual standard deviation.

Results

Dementia effects on FA and MD are shown in Fig 2 for both 1D and 2D tractometry across 9 bilateral WM tracts: 3 limbic tracts (fornix, frontal-parietal, and parahippocampal cingulum), the inferior longitudinal fasciculus (ILF), 3 corticostriatal tracts (anterior, pre- and post-central), and 2 corticopontine tracts (frontal and parietal). Effect sizes passing global FDR were plotted on atlas bundles, with 1D results shown along the centroid. For FA in dementia relative to controls, both methods showed consistent decreases in the limbic, ILF, and anterior corticostriatal tract. Both methods also identified areas of increased FA in the corticopontine, pre- and post-central corticostriatal tracts with the 2D approach revealing finer details radially. FA increases are concentrated in the proximal areas of the corticostriatal and corticopontine tracts, whereas the distal regions near the cortex exhibit decreased FA. For MD, patterns revealed by two methods diverge substantially. The 1D approach showed widespread increases across nearly all tracts, whereas 2D revealed both increases and decreases. Notable MD decreases were observed in the anterior cortical projections of the cingulum and in the posterior corticostriatal and corticopontine tracts. The ILF showed large increases (d>0.8) along the core but decreased MD at the dorsal periphery. Regions with concurrent FA and MD decreases may reflect neuroinflammatory processes resulting in restricted water diffusion and disrupted tissue organization, though this interpretation remains speculative as DTI metrics cannot distinguish among contributing cellular mechanisms.

Conclusion

We introduced a novel 2D parametric mapping framework for WM bundles that extends conventional 1D tractometry by capturing variability both along and across the tract. In dementia compared to controls, we found predominant FA decreases with good cross-method agreement, whereas MD exhibited bidirectional changes with notable inconsistencies with the 2D approach resolving radial patterns across the tract. Conventional 1D tractometry typically emphasizes bundle core measures where streamline density is highest, whereas our 2D approach captures signals from peripheral regions that may reflect distinct biological processes but are more susceptible to tracking uncertainty. Due to the increased number of statistical tests, we recommend our 2D approach as a second-level analysis following ROI-based or 1D methods, particularly with strong priors or large sample sizes. Additionally, 1D parameterization may be sufficient for bundles with smaller diameters such as the fornix and provide greater statistical power. Overall, 1D tractometry methods offer robust detection of along-tract effects with high sensitivity, while 2D mapping provides richer spatial interpretation with high specificity. Together, these approaches offer a principled approach to map disease effects on neural pathways at a finer spatial scale.
