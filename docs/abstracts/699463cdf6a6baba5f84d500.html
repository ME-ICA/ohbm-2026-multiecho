Introduction

              Multiple sclerosis (MS) causes cognitive impairment in 43-70% of patients, particularly affecting information processing speed, executive function, and attention. Advanced neuroimaging, including myelin water imaging and diffusion tensor imaging, has identified associations between cognitive dysfunction and abnormalities in white matter tracts, deep gray matter, and cortical structures. However, whether cognitive deficits correlate uniformly with tissue damage across all brain compartments or preferentially with specific structures remains incompletely understood.1
               The T1-weighted/T2-weighted (T1/T2) ratio serves as a myelin-related surrogate marker; pathological studies demonstrate T1/T2 ratio correlates with white matter myelin content. T1/T2 ratio changes in cortical gray matter, white matter, and deep gray matter have been reported separately.2,3 However, comprehensive studies examining all three regions and cognitive correlations are lacking. Therefore, we investigated T1/T2 ratio changes across these three regions in patients with MS, examining associations with multiple cognitive measures, including information processing speed, attention, and memory performance.

Methods

              This study included 91 patients with MS (relapsing-remitting MS and secondary progressive MS) and 91 age- and sex-matched healthy controls. MS diagnosis followed the 2017 McDonald criteria.4 Cognitive assessments performed within 6 months of MRI scanning included the Paced Auditory Serial Addition Test (PASAT; 1-second and 2-second versions), Symbol Digit Modalities Test (SDMT), Trail Making Test (TMT; parts A and B), and Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE). (Figure 1A)
               MRI was performed using a 3T scanner (Achieva, Philips Medical Systems, Best, Netherlands). T1-weighted images (MPRAGE/GRE): TR, 1800/7.3 ms; TE, 2.3/3.4 ms; matrix size, 320–384 × 320–384 × 224/300; FOV, 250–260 × 250–260 × 179.2–180.0 mm; voxel size, 0.6–0.8 mm. T2-weighted images: TR, 4448–5083 ms; TE, 80–81 ms; matrix size, 464–640 × 512–640 × 32–35; FOV, 199.4–230.0 × 220.0–230.0 × 144.0–153.0 mm; slice thickness, 3-4 mm. 
               T1/T2 ratio maps were generated using KUL Neuro Imaging Suite with 1 mm isotropic reslicing, N4 bias correction, registration, brain extraction, and nonlinear calibration.3 FreeSurfer 8.00 segmented cortical gray matter, white matter (excluding T1-hypointense lesions), and deep gray matter. Voxel-based analysis (VBA) was performed using FSL 6.07 with common masks in the MNI space. (Figure1B) Group comparisons and correlation analyses used general linear models with permutation-based inference (5,000 permutations), threshold-free cluster enhancement, and family-wise error correction, adjusted for age and sex. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. 

Results

               VBA group comparison showed widespread significant T1/T2 ratio reductions in patients with MS across all tissue compartments. Cortical reductions were diffuse across bilateral frontal, temporal, parietal, and occipital regions. Deep gray matter showed significant involvement of bilateral thalami, caudate, putamen, and globus pallidus. White matter demonstrated extensive significant changes, particularly in periventricular regions, corpus callosum, and corona radiata. (Figure 2A)
               PASAT and SDMT showed significant positive correlations with T1/T2 ratio in white matter (periventricular regions, corpus callosum, corona radiata) and deep gray matter (caudate, medial thalami). TMT completion times showed significant negative correlations with the T1/T2 ratio in regions similar to those of the PASAT and SDMT. MMSE showed no significant correlations. None of the cognitive tests showed significant correlations in cortical regions. (Figure 2B)

Conclusion

               This study demonstrated widespread T1/T2 ratio reductions throughout the cerebral cortical gray matter, white matter, and a part of deep gray matter structures in patients with MS. Although cortical T1/T2 ratios were widely reduced, information processing and executive function (SDMT, PASAT, and TMT) correlated selectively with deep gray matter and white matter structures (thalamus, corpus callosum), whereas MMSE showed no associations. This selective association with subcortical structures supports their critical role in MS-related cognitive impairment.5 T1/T2 ratio mapping, obtainable from routine sequences, may serve as a practical marker of cognitive network damage in MS.
