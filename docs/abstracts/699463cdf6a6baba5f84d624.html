Introduction

    Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder affecting over 35 million individuals and accounting for 60–70% of dementia. Beyond regional atrophy, AD is fundamentally characterized by the disruption of large-scale brain networks, often described as a disconnection syndrome.1 Clinically, structural MRI (sMRI) serves as an important imaging modality for visualizing this neurodegeneration. Building on this, by effectively capturing these local structural changes, deep learning models utilizing sMRI, specifically 3D-VGG, ResNet18, and conventional 3D-CNNs, have demonstrated strong diagnostic performance.2 However, these architectures are inherently limited by their local receptive fields, consequently restricting their ability to capture long-range dependencies across spatially distributed brain networks characteristic of AD pathology. To address this gap, 3D attention mechanisms leverage self-attention principles to model dependencies between spatial locations across the brain features, establishing global receptive fields that extend beyond fixed convolutional kernels.3 Through dynamic weighting, this model adaptively prioritizes structurally relevant distant regions while suppressing irrelevant background noise, reflecting the widespread network disruptions characteristic of AD.

    Consequently, this study integrated 3D attention blocks into three CNN architectures and trained the models on T1-weighted MRI from the Alzheimer’s disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset. We compared classification performance between these attention-enhanced models and their baseline counterparts. Additionally, we employed Explainable AI (XAI) to visualize model decision processes for interpretability analysis. We hypothesized that attentional blocks would capture long-range spatial dependencies across distributed brain regions that better reflect network-level AD pathology compared to baseline models.

Methods

       A total of 783 participants from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) database (adni.loni.usc.edu) were included in this study, comprising 367 individuals with AD and 416 healthy controls (HC). All structural T1-weighted MRI scans underwent preprocessing, including skull-stripping, registration to the MNI152 template, min–max intensity normalization, and resizing to uniform dimensions of 108 × 128 × 108 voxels. The dataset was divided into training (n=500; HC=266, AD=234), validation (n=126; HC=67, AD=59), and test (n=157; HC=83, AD=74) sets using a fixed split to ensure consistent evaluation across all subsequent analyses.

    For the development of AD classification models, three different 3D CNN architectures were implemented as baseline models: a 5-layer CNN, ResNet-18, and VGG-16. For each backbone, an attention-enhanced variant was developed by integrating a 3D attention block, resulting in a total of six models. The 5-layer CNN architecture comprised five convolutional layers with kernel sizes of 3 × 3 × 3. Each convolutional layer was followed by batch normalization, a ReLU activation and a max-pooling layer. The network concluded with two fully connected layers, separated by a ReLU activation and a dropout layer (rate = 0.5) to mitigate overfitting. The ResNet-18 architecture was an 18-layer residual network characterized by skip connections that facilitate gradient flow and enable the training of deeper architectures. The VGG-16 architecture was characterized by its uniform architecture, employing a stack of small 3 x 3 x 3 convolutional filters to extract hierarchical features. To address the inherent local receptive field limitations of standard CNNs, a custom 3D attention block was integrated into each of the three baseline models. This attention block modeled voxel-wise relationships by generating query, key, and value feature maps from intermediate convolutional representations using 1 x 1 x 1 convolutions. Attention weights were derived from similarity scores between query and key projections, producing a volumetric attention map that dynamically highlighted salient brain regions. The weighted value features were aggregated to produce enhanced representations, integrated with the original input through a residual connection to stabilize training. Regarding integration strategies, the attention block was positioned after the final convolutional layer in the 5-layer CNN and VGG-16, whereas in ResNet-18, it was inserted between the third (conv4_x) and fourth (conv5_x) residual blocks. All attention operations were performed directly on 3D volumetric data, with channel dimensions reduced by a factor of 16 to optimize computational efficiency.

    To enhance model robustness, intensity-based data augmentation was applied with a 30% probability. This involved random intensity scaling (±5%) to simulate contrast variations and intensity shifting (±0.03) to mimic global signal offset, followed by clipping to the [0, 1] range. Network optimization was performed using the AdamW optimizer for 100 epochs, with a batch size of 2, an initial learning rate of 1 x 10-4, and a weight decay rate of 1 x 10-4. Model performance was evaluated on the held-out test set, with accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, and the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC) reported as primary metrics. Additionally, to investigate the biological plausibility of learned features and interpret decision-making patterns, we employed SmoothGrad for saliency map generation. Implemented via the Zennit library, the method was configured with a noise level of 0.1 and 50 iterations to generate voxel-wise attribution maps.4 These maps facilitated a qualitative topological comparison of feature importance between the baseline CNNs and their attention-augmented variants in AD. Results

    A comparative evaluation revealed that attention-augmented variants consistently outperformed their baseline counterparts on the independent test set. Specifically, classification accuracy improved from 86.60% to 89.20% for the 5-layer CNN, and from 83.40% to 87.90% for VGG-16. Notably, the attention-enhanced ResNet-18 achieved the highest overall efficacy, with an accuracy of 91.10% and AUC of 96.40% (sensitivity: 82.40%; specificity: 98.80%), substantially surpassing the baseline ResNet-18 (accuracy: 86.60%; AUC: 92.20%) (Figure 2A). Analysis of confusion matrices across all models confirmed a consistent reduction in false negatives upon integrating attention blocks, demonstrating enhanced diagnostic robustness regardless of the model design.

    Regarding model interpretability, SmoothGrad attribution maps revealed a critical topological divergence between the baseline and attention-enhanced ResNet-18 models (Figure 2B). The baseline ResNet-18 maps exhibited activation patterns restricted to regionally confined clusters, primarily targeting the cerebellum, hippocampus, fusiform gyrus, subcallosal cortex, and middle temporal gyrus. Conversely, the attention-enhanced ResNet-18 model showed spatially distributed activation patterns indicative of broader network integration. While retaining sensitivity to baseline regions, it additionally recruited spatially distant cortical areas, including the lingual gyrus, thalamus, anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), and precentral gyrus. These findings indicated that attention mechanisms facilitate the capture of network-level connectivity disruptions characteristic of AD pathology, enabling the model to identify spatially distributed features reflective of large-scale brain network dysfunction.

Conclusion

    In conclusion, our study demonstrated that 3D attention mechanisms substantially enhance AD classification while providing interpretable insights into disease pathology. Attention-enhanced models consistently outperformed baseline models across all metrics, with the attention-enhanced ResNet-18 achieving superior efficacy. Additionally, explainable AI analysis revealed that attention-enhanced networks highlighted spatially distributed regions indicative of long-range dependencies (thalamus, ACC, PCC, and precentral gyrus), unlike the regionally confined patterns of baseline models. This topological divergence indicated that by capturing long-range voxel dependencies, the attention mechanism reflects the distributed network-level connectivity disruptions characteristic of AD rather than relying solely on isolated regional atrophy. These findings suggest that attention blocks successfully identify structurally relevant regions disrupted in AD, providing a more biologically plausible diagnostic framework with enhanced clinical robustness.
