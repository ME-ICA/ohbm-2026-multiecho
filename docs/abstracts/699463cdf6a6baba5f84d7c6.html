Introduction

Essential tremor (ET) is the most common movement disorder, characterized by involuntary rhythmic shaking during action or posture, often leading to substantial disability and reduced quality of life. Standard treatments tipically comprise peripherally acting beta-blockers and centrally acting GABAergic agents [1]. MRI-guided Focused Ultrasound (MRgFUS) thalamotomy has become an established option for medication-refractory ET, producing substantial reductions in tremor amplitude and associated disability [2]. However, the neurophysiological mechanisms underlying its clinical efficacy remain unclear, particularly regarding neurotransmitter-enriched functional contribution. Here we investigated neurotransmitter-enriched functional connectivity changes associated with tremor improvement following MRgFUS thalamotomy.

Methods

Seventy right-handed patients with essential tremor who underwent left-sided MRgFUS thalamotomy targeting the ventral intermediate nucleus (VIM) were enrolled at Fondazione IRCCS Istituto Neurologico Carlo Besta. MRI scans were acquired at baseline and 1–3 months post-treatment. A movement-disorder neurologist assessed all patients using the Essential Tremor Rating Assessment Scale (TETRAS) and confirmed eligibility for unilateral MRgFUS thalamotomy. Exclusion criteria comprised other neurodegenerative disorders, prior stereotactic procedures, MRI contraindications, relevant cognitive or psychiatric conditions, unstable medical status, recent stroke or seizures, intracranial tumors, and skull density ratio < 0.34 on screening CT.

MRgFUS procedures were performed on a 1.5 T GE Optima 750w scanner integrated with the ExAblate Neuro 4000 system (Insightec). VIM targeting was performed contralateral to the most affected side using conventional stereotactic planning based on anatomical landmarks.

MRI data were acquired on a Philips Achieva dStream 3T scanner with a 32-channel head coil. The protocol included a 3D T1-weighted sequence (1 mm isotropic) and a resting-state fMRI sequence (TR = 3000 ms, 2.5 mm isotropic). Preprocessing was performed in CONN (v20.b) using the default MNI pipeline (slice-timing correction, realignment, normalization, 4-mm smoothing, aCompCor denoising, 0.008–0.09 Hz bandpass filter).

Functional analyses employed the Receptor-Enriched Analysis of Functional Connectivity by Targets (REACT) framework, applied to PET-derived molecular templates for acetylcholine, GABA, glutamate, and noradrenaline [3]. For each session, neurotransmitter-enriched functional connectivity maps were generated and Δ maps (Post – Pre) were computed.

Voxelwise one-sample t-tests in SPM12 were used to identify MRgFUS-related changes, including age, pharmacological treatment, and disease duration as covariates of no interest. Additional voxelwise regressions examined associations between Δ maps and changes in tremor severity on the treated side (Δ% TETRAS), controlling for baseline tremor, age, pharmacological treatment, and disease duration. Results were thresholds at p < 0.005 (uncorrected, voxel level) and p < 0.05 FWE (cluster level).

Results

MRgFUS was associated with robust changes in neurotransmitter-enriched functional connectivity, as revealed by REACT analyses. The most prominent cortical motor effects consisted of increased glutamate-weighted connectivity across both precentral gyri and in the right postcentral gyrus. A reduction in acetylcholine-weighted connectivity also emerged in the right supplementary motor area. Reduced glutamate-weighted connectivity also emerged in left cerebellar lobules IV–VI and in the left brainstem (medulla and pons) (FIG. 1A).

A further set of alterations was identified in visual and occipital cortices. These included increased GABA-weighted connectivity in the bilateral occipital fusiform gyrus, cuneal cortex, and intracalcarine cortex, as well as increased glutamate-weighted connectivity in the bilateral cuneal cortex, right occipital fusiform gyrus, and left intracalcarine cortex. Noradrenaline-weighted connectivity also increased in the left cuneal cortex. In contrast, acetylcholine-weighted connectivity was reduced in the bilateral cuneal cortex, lateral occipital cortex (superior division), and right intracalcarine cortex (FIG. 1A).

Additional distributed changes were detected in associative areas, including increased glutamate-weighted connectivity in the right anterior and posterior superior temporal gyrus, increased acetylcholine-weighted connectivity in the left superior temporal gyrus (anterior division) and the bilateral posterior superior temporal gyri, increased noradrenaline-weighted connectivity in the right angular gyrus, and increased GABA-weighted connectivity in the posterior division of the cingulate gyrus (FIG. 1A).

Connectivity changes were also related to clinical improvement. Motor-related associations included negative correlations between acetylcholine-weighted connectivity in the right precentral gyrus and tremor reduction, and negative correlations between noradrenaline-weighted connectivity in the left postcentral gyrus and tremor reduction (FIG. 1B).

Non-motor associations included positive correlations between tremor improvement and acetylcholine-weighted connectivity in the bilateral intracalcarine and cuneal cortex, as well as glutamate-weighted connectivity in the bilateral frontal gyrus pars opercularis and the left insula, and a negative correlation with noradrenaline-weighted connectivity in the right lateral occipital cortex (superior division) (FIG. 1B).

Conclusion

Our data demonstrate that unilateral MRgFUS VIM thalamotomy in right-hand tremor patients produces a widespread reorganization of neurotransmitter-enriched functional connectivity across motor and extra-motor circuits [2]. The motor effects showed a bilateral reorganization of glutamate-weighted connectivity, with increases observed in both precentral gyri and the right postcentral gyrus. A reduction in acetylcholine-related connectivity in the right supplementary motor area further suggests altered cholinergic influences on premotor planning. 

Beyond cortical regions, reduced glutamatergic connectivity in left cerebellar lobules IV–VI and in the left brainstem. This pattern is in line with the crossed organization of cerebello–thalamo–cortical pathways and with imaging studies showing abnormal cerebellar involvement in ET, and network-level changes following VIM-targeted interventions [4].

A second cluster of effects emerged in visual and occipital cortices, with increased GABA-, glutamate-, and noradrenaline-weighted connectivity and reduced acetylcholine-weighted connectivity across cuneal, intracalcarine, fusiform, and lateral occipital cortices. These findings are in line with the contribution of visuomotor and cerebello-thalamo-visual pathways to tremor expression in ET and with evidence that visually sensitive networks modulate tremor severity and visual feedback dependence [5].

MRgFUS also altered associative and multimodal hubs, including the superior temporal gyrus, angular gyrus, and posterior cingulate cortex, which are key nodes for multisensory integration, visuospatial attention and internal monitoring, respectively [1]. Increases in temporal–parietal association cortices could reflect adaptive recalibration of perceptual–motor integration in higher-order association areas, whereas posterior cingulate changes may index modulation of internally directed networks that interface with sensorimotor output [4].

Importantly, neurotransmitter-enriched connectivity changes were also related to clinical improvement. Motor-related associations showed that reductions in acetylcholine- and noradrenaline-weighted connectivity in precentral and postcentral regions were linked to greater tremor reduction measured with TETRAS scale. 

Non-motor associations suggest that tremor suppression also depends on adjustments within visual and integrative networks. Positive correlations between improvement and acetylcholine-weighted connectivity in intracalcarine and cuneal cortex are consistent with a contribution of early visual areas to visuomotor processing, in line with evidence that visually sensitive networks modulate tremor severity and the impact of visual feedback in ET [5]. Likewise, glutamate-weighted increases in the frontal operculum and insula regions involved in sensorimotor integration, salience processing, and interoceptive awareness, may index the engagement of higher-order circuits that help stabilize motor output after thalamotomy. 

Conversely, the negative association between noradrenaline-weighted connectivity in the lateral occipital cortex and tremor improvement suggests that lower noradrenergic coupling within visual association cortices may accompany more effective tremor control, although this interpretation remains speculative.

Together, these findings indicate that MRgFUS thalamotomy induces widespread modulation of excitatory, inhibitory, and neuromodulatory circuits across motor, visual, and associative networks. By integrating neurotransmitter-enriched functional connectivity with clinical outcome, this study provides a network-level and molecularly informed perspective on how focal thalamic ablation leads to tremor suppression in essential tremor [1,4,5].
