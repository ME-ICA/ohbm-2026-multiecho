Introduction

Brain age prediction from structural MRI provides a quantitative marker of deviation from normative brain aging that is associated with neurodegeneration [1]. The difference between predicted brain age and chronological age, commonly referred to as the brain age gap (BAG) has been consistently linked to cognitive decline, including in Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [1,2]. Prior studies have demonstrated a progressive increase in BAG across the clinical continuum from cognitively normal (CN) aging to mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and AD [3]. However, most brain age prediction models have been developed using large, heterogeneous lifespan datasets, which makes interpretation difficult and limiting their applicability in controlled clinical settings.

In this study, we explored a reproducible deep-learning framework that learns a normative brain aging trajectory from cognitively normal individuals using data in the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) [4]. Within a unified preprocessing and training pipeline, we evaluated several widely used 3D convolutional neural network architectures to identify a stable model for brain age estimation and to systematically assess brain age deviations across CN, MCI, and AD groups.

Methods

We used structural T1-weighted MRI scans [4]. For model development, we used a total of 839 CN scans, which were divided into training, validation, and test subsets. To evaluate clinical utility, we used additional scans from subjects diagnosed with MCI (n = 105) and AD (n = 93).

A standardized preprocessing procedure including skull stripping, bias field correction, normalization to the MNI152 template, and intensity normalization was applied to all MRI volumes [4]. Furthermore, three different deep learning models were evaluated with the same settings: 3D ResNet-18, DenseNet3D, and TinySFCN [5]. These models were purely trained using cognitively normal (CN) subjects to learn normative aging patterns with mean absolute error (MAE) as the loss function.

Brain age prediction was performed on each of the CN, MCI, and AD groups. Brain age gap (BAG) was calculated by subtracting the chronological age from the predicted brain age [1]. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare the BAG among the cohorts, whereas the difference in effect between cohorts was measured by calculating Cohen’s d value.

Results

The 3D ResNet-18 model demonstrated the best and most robust performance among the evaluated architectures [2,3]. It achieved a mean absolute error (MAE) of 2.99 years on the validation set and 3.73 years on an independent test set held out from CN participants. In contrast, DenseNet3D and TinySFCN exhibited lower accuracy and were therefore not pursued further.

Brain age gap (BAG) estimates derived from the 3D ResNet-18 model showed a clear progression across diagnostic groups. The CN group exhibited a mean BAG slightly below zero, whereas both the MCI and AD groups showed increasingly positive BAG values, indicating accelerated brain aging [1,3]. One-way ANOVA revealed a significant effect of diagnostic group on BAG (F = 11.57). Effect size analyses indicated moderate differences between CN and MCI and larger differences between CN and AD.

Conclusion

This study presents a reproducible deep-learning framework for brain age prediction from structural MRI and examines the role of brain age difference across the AD continuum [1,4]. The 3D ResNet-18 model achieved the highest predictive performance and captured clinically meaningful deviations from normative aging that scale with disease severity. Collectively, these findings highlight the utility of the brain age gap (BAG) as a sensitive marker of Alzheimer’s-related brain aging [1,3].
