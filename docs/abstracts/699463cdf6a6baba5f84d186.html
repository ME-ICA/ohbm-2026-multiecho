Introduction

Late gestation and early infancy are critical periods of human brain development, during which key physiological processes and structural transformations, such as neurogenesis, axonal growths, myelination, and synaptic plasticity, underpin the formation of intrinsic communication modes and further guide their self-organisation into more complex functional networks.^1 These processes, in turn, establish the foundations for lifelong cognitive function. Therefore, understanding structure-function coupling (SFC) during early development is important for identifying atypical developmental trajectories and informing strategies for early intervention.
To date, most human neuroimaging studies in paediatric populations have assessed SFC using MRI, linking structural or diffusion MRI (dMRI) measures to functional connectivity (FC) derived from fMRI. Here, we present, to our knowledge, the first human infant brain model that integrates electrophysiological activity measured with EEG and structural connectivity (SC) derived from dMRI to characterise developmental trajectories of SFC in brain networks between 34 and 43 weeks postmenstrual age. 

Methods

To estimate FC, we used high-density (124-channel) EEG from 135 preterm infants, measured longitudinally from the third trimester to early postnatal age (33-43 weeks; overall N = 289 recordings). Artifact-free 5-minute EEG epochs were filtered into five frequency bands of interest: low delta (0.4–1.5 Hz), high delta (1.5–4 Hz), theta (4–8 Hz), alpha (8–13 Hz), and beta (13–22 Hz). EEG data were then reconstructed into 15000 cortical sources using a surface-based infant head model incorporating scalp, outer skull, and inner skull compartments. Sources were clustered into 62 bilaterally symmetric cortical parcels and cortical signals were computed as a weighted mean of sources within corresponding parcels.^2

For SC computation, we used age-specific (gestational ages of 34 to 43 weeks) fibre orientation distributions (FODs) template data, generated using the developing Human Connectome Project (dMRI data).^3 All FODs were aligned to a common 40-week brain template space, which also served as the cortical source space for EEG reconstruction. Cortical surface was reconstructed using Infant FreeSurfer, and the EEG-based parcellation was projected into volumetric space for alignment with dMRI. Probabilistic streamline tractography was performed using iFOD2 algorithm in MRtrix3 and streamline densities were optimized using SIFT2. SC matrices were computed for 62 regions and converted to similarity matrices using cosine similarity.

We estimated FC for two distinct coupling modes: amplitude-amplitude correlations (AAC; Pearson correlation between envelopes)^4 and phase-phase correlations (PPC; phase locking value). Age-specific FC matrices were derived by averaging individual matrices within one-week bins and standardised using Fisher’s r-to-z transformation. Global SFC was quantified for each week using Spearman correlations (p < 0.001) between vectorized SC similarity matrices and the corresponding AAC- and PPC-based FC matrices. Developmental trajectories were assessed using linear and quadratic regression models, with significance of the quadratic term (p < 0.05) indicating non-linear age-related effects. P-values for age-related effects were FDR-corrected across frequency bands within each coupling mode using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure.



Results

Representative examples of modality-specific (AAC- and PPC-based) and frequency-specific (low delta and alpha) SFC trajectories, that significantly changed with age, are shown in Figure 1.

For global AAC-based SFC, significant inverted-U trends were identified in the low delta (p = 0.008, pFDR = 0.041, adjusted R2 = 0.62) and beta (p = 0.019, pFDR = 0.047, adjusted R2 = 0.75) bands. These bands showed rising SFC that peaked around 37 weeks, followed by a decline. The high delta band did not survive FDR correction (p = 0.045, pFDR = 0.075, adjusted R2 = 0.53). In contrast, theta (p = 0.008, pFDR = 0.015, adjusted R2 = 0.56) and alpha (p = 0.002, pFDR = 0.012, adjusted R2 = 0.67) bands showed significant linear decreases with age. 

Global PPC-based SFC showed significant inverted-U trajectories in the low delta (p = 0.002, pFDR = 0.009, adjusted R2 = 0.76) and high delta (p = 0.015, pFDR = 0.036, adjusted R2 = 0.75) bands, whereas theta (p < 0.001, pFDR < 0.001, adjusted R2 = 0.82), alpha (p = 0.001, pFDR = 0.002, adjusted R2 = 0.71), and beta (p < 0.001, pFDR = 0.002, adjusted R2 = 0.74) bands exhibited significant linear increases with age.

These findings indicate a frequency-specific transition. While low-frequency synchrony undergoes non-linear reorganisation near term, higher-frequency coupling shows steady integration with the structural connectome.

Conclusion

Overall, both AAC- and PPC-based SFC demonstrates selective non-linear and linear trajectories in specific oscillatory ranges during the late-preterm to term-equivalent period. While both AAC and PPC show non-linear (inverted U-shape) reorganisation in the delta range (0.4-4 Hz) peaking around 37-41 weeks, higher-frequency bands predominantly follow linear trends. AAC displays decreasing linear (theta-alpha) and non-linear (beta) coupling with SC, whereas PPC demonstrates progressively increasing coupling across all three higher bands (theta-beta). These results suggest that amplitude- and phase-based interactions reflect different aspects of early neural coordination. Our current findings may reflect a shift from more widespread coordination of neural activity orchestrated by low-frequency bursts (spontaneous activity transients) in the preterm brain, when structural connectivity is still maturing, toward more precise communication via high-frequency PPC, enabled by a more developed structural connectome.^5 This integrative EEG-dMRI framework offers a powerful tool for characterising structure-function interplay in early developmental studies and can be used to derive individualised markers to test for atypical SFC.
