Introduction

Self-injurious behaviours (SIB) are prevalent among children with profound autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and can lead to permanent disability1. Deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the nucleus accumbens (NAc) has recently been developed in a world-first, phase I clinical trial at The Hospital for Sick Children as a potential treatment for affected children (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03982888)2. However, the neural underpinnings of NAc-DBS for SIB are poorly understood, and multimodal translational studies using animal models and clinical data are necessary to explore the mechanisms underlying treatment. 

Dysregulation in dopaminergic, glutamatergic, and GABAergic systems has been implicated in the pathophysiology of SIB, with impairments in fronto-striatal inhibitory control proposed to contribute to the emergence and maintenance of SIB1. By characterizing the chemoarchitectural changes that co-occur with NAc-DBS-evoked structural plasticity, we sought to identify the neurochemical substrates that govern SIB and its modulation through NAc-DBS. 

Methods

BTBR T+ Itpr3tf/J (BTBR) mouse models of SIB and ASD received chronic DBS targeting the bilateral NAc (NAc-DBS). In parallel, 6 children with ASD and severe, refractory SIB underwent one year of active NAc-DBS (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT03982888)2. Structural MRI’s from both BTBR mice and human participants were assessed using deformation-based morphometry (DBM)3 and Multiple Automatically Generated Templates Brain Segmentation Algorithm (MAGeTbrain)4 pipelines to identify brain-wide morphological alterations and circuitry implicated in the pathophysiology and modulation of SIB. 

To identify potential biological drivers of the DBM-detected structural changes, we performed targeted neurochemical analyses within the fronto-limbic-striatal nodes altered by NAc-DBS. In BTBR mice, immunohistochemistry was used to quantify neuronal activity (c-Fos) and the distribution of key neurotransmitter-specific cell populations implicated in SIB, including dopamine 1 (Drd1) and 2 (Drd2) medium spiny neurons, glutamatergic neurons (CAMKII), and GABAergic (GAD67) interneurons. To assess whether homologous neurochemical mechanisms may underlie the structural changes observed in the clinical population, we correlated the human DBM map from children undergoing NAc-DBS to the topology of brain receptor and transporter densities across 9 different neurotransmitter systems from over 1200 healthy individuals5. Each correlation was assessed against a null distribution generated via spin permutation testing to identify the neurotransmitter systems that are selectively enriched within NAc-DBS-responsive regions, and thus, likely to contribute to the circuit-level structural plasticity.

Results

Electrical stimulation of the NAc significantly mitigated SIB (Fig. 1A-D) and induced convergent structural changes along fronto-limbic-striatal circuits (Fig. 1E) in BTBR mice and children with severe SIB undergoing NAc-DBS. In BTBR mice, NAc-DBS altered fronto-striatal chemoarchitecture, characterized by reduced Drd1 and Drd2 expression in the striatum, increased Drd1 and Drd2 in the prefrontal cortex, and a concurrent reduction in the ratio of glutamatergic to GABAergic neuron activation along fronto-striatal pathways (Fig. 1F-K). The reduction in excitatory drive extended into interconnected limbic regions, including the thalamus, hypothalamus, and amygdala, suggesting a broader dampening of signalling (Fig. 1L-N), whereas no significant differences were detected in the hippocampus or habenula (Fig. 1O-P). In the clinical cohort, longitudinal NAc-DBS-evoked volumetric changes localized to regions enriched in dopamine, glutamate, and GABA systems. The receptor profiles identified in humans align with the cell-type-specific alterations observed in mice, highlighting cross-species convergence in the neurotransmitter systems engaged by NAc-DBS.

Conclusion

We demonstrate that NAc-DBS improves SIB by modulating dopaminergic signalling and excitation-inhibition balance in both BTBR mice and children with profound ASD. These cross-species findings implicate coordinated modulation of dopaminergic, glutamatergic, and GABAergic systems as a key mechanism through which NAc-DBS reduces SIB and induces circuit-level structural plasticity. This work establishes a mechanistic framework linking circuit-level plasticity and chemoarchitecture alterations to therapeutic response, and lays the foundation for refining fronto-limbic-striatal neuromodulation to optimize outcomes in this extremely vulnerable population of children with SIB.
