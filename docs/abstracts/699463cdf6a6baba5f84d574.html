Introduction

The brain is a highly complex system, consisting of numerous “networks”, where multiple brain regions work in synchrony to facilitate information processing. Functional MRI (fMRI) provides non-invasive images of whole-brain activity, which has enabled brain characterisation through identifying common networks across the population. Spatial arrangement of networks (spatial topography) and their interactions over time (temporal connectivity) vary across individuals. Convincing evidence has linked these variations to individualistic traits, e.g., cognitive abilities and disease. However, trait prediction based on network variations remains <<50% accurate, partly due to the lack of techniques that can yield fully-individualised functional networks. 

Existing methods typically estimate common patterns across populations, and infer on individuals from the group model1. However, this is prone to biasing estimations towards population averages. The framework of Probabilistic Functional Modes (PROFUMO) 2,3 - an upcoming FSL tool - proposes a novel approach, where cross-individual variability is at the heart of network modelling, in a way that is informed by patterns of population variations. Here we introduce three variations of this framework: classic PROFUMO (cPROFUMO) for medium-sized data, stochastic PROFUMO (sPROFUMO) for big data , and predefined PROFUMO (pPROFUMO) for big-to-small data transfer of information. Our results provide a summary of the model outputs and how to interpret them. 

Methods

PROFUMO is a matrix factorisation model for inferring brain networks for population and individuals simultaneously. For this purpose, it uses hierarchical Bayesian inference. 

The goal is to decompose fMRI data (Dsr) into spatial maps (Ps), time courses (Asr) and amplitudes (Hsr), with residuals Esr :

Dsr = Ps Hsr Asr + Esr

Where Dsr is of size  Nv x Nt , Ps is of size Nv x Nm, Hsr is of size Nm x Nm and Asr is of size Nm x Nt, s denotes subject, r denotes a recording session and Hsr denotes mode amplitudes. Nv, Nm and Nt denote the number of voxels, modes and time points, respectively.

Ps represents spatial maps across the brain voxels, and it is modelled hierarchically, thus yielding Ps for each subject, as well as a consensus group model. Asr represents mode time courses per subject and scan, and PROFUMO allows for HRF-constrained or HRF-unconstrained estimation of A, as needed. Temporal correlations between the timecourses of the modes (represented by precision matrix ) are also modelled hierarchically and can be constrained by HRF-induced autocorrelations, when needed. Hsr denotes mode amplitudes and is also modelled hierarchically. Distinct variational distributions are used in the generative model for each of these model elements, details of which described in 2,3.

Three PROFUMO inference regimes (Figure 2): 

Classic PROFUMO (cPROFUMO):

cPROFUMO uses Variational Bayesian (VB) inference to find a solution for the probabilistic model described above. The group model is used for top-down regularisation of subject-specific matrix factorisations through Bayesian priors. Subsequently, posterior distributions are accumulated across individuals and fed back to the group to update the group model. The model iterates between these two levels of estimation until convergence.

Stochastic PROFUMO (sPROFUMO):

sPROFUMO implements stochastic Variational Bayesian (VB) inference. This includes dividing subjects from large data into small batches, and optimising the parameters of an approximating posterior distribution. The group model is updated continuously across batches, and within each iteration of each batch, it is used for top-down regularisation of subject-specific matrix factorisations. Subsequently, posterior distributions are accumulated across individuals within each batch, scaled and fed back to update the group model. The model iterates between these two levels, as moving forward across batches, until convergence.

Predefined PROFUMO (pPROFUMO):

pPROFUMO is built on predefined group models, which can be obtained from applying cPROFUMO/sPROFUMO to new datasets, or obtained from existing publicly available pretrained models on HCP or UK Biobank (https://www.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/ukbiobank/PROFUMO/). In pPROFUMO, the spatiotemporal group-level parameters are kept fixed, and subject-level parameters are estimated with group model as priors – this is similar to cPROFUMO, with the exception that group priors are kept fixed and only subject level posteriors are updated over model iterations. As such, pPROFUMO allows the user to estimate population-informed individualised brain networks, even with one sample size. 

Results

The functional modes that are estimated using PROFUMO are referred to as Probabilistic Functional Modes (PFMs). The key outputs of the software are as follows (Figure 2):

1- Subject level spatial maps: these summarise the spatial topographies of the PFMs across the brain voxels, and are estimated using a Double Gaussian Mixture Model per voxel. PFMs provide 5 spatial maps at subject level: Signal mean and standard deviation, background noise mean and standard deviation, and additional membership probability maps that characterise the probability of a voxel belonging to signal versus background noise. Signal means (normalised by membership probabilities) are typically used in downstream analyses.

2- Group level spatial maps: PFMs provide 4 spatial maps at group level: signal mean and standard deviation, noise standard deviation, and membership probabilities. Signal means (normalised by membership probabilities) are typically used in downstream analyses.

3- PFM time courses: signal and background noise time courses are estimated for each PFM per subject and scan, and the signal component can be HRF-constrained.

4- Connectivity: spatial and temporal connectivity between the modes (SNET and TNET) can be computed at both group and subject level. TNET estimation is integrated within the hierarchical model and results are saved by default. SNET can be estimated post-hoc using spatial correlations between the PFM spatial maps. 

cPROFUMO, sPROFUMO and pPROFUMO all produce the abovementioned outputs. 

Conclusion

PROFUMO is an open software tool for population-informed individual-specific modelling of brain networks, and it integrates the three variations of the model within a single package. cPROFUMO is suitable for medium-sized datasets, sPROFUMO is suitable for large scale datasets, and pPROFUMO is suitable for building the analysis of small, especially clinical data, on the models trained on existing, preferably large-scale, datasets. The software can be accessed from the following link, and it is scheduled to be made available in an upcoming FSL release: https://git.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/profumo/profumo/-/tree/sprofumo-cpp-clean?ref_type=heads.
