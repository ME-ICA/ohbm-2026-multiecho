Introduction

Brainstem and subcortical white matter pathways - including basal ganglia circuits, cerebellar loops, limbic connections, and ascending/descending tracts - are critical for motor control, sensory processing, and autonomic function. Dysfunction in these tracts is implicated in diverse conditions, from Parkinson’s disease to psychiatric disorders, and these bundles and locations are vital targets for deep brain stimulation (DBS) [1]. Despite their importance, these pathways remain underexplored in vivo due to complex, dense anatomy and a lack of standardized, automated segmentation protocols. Existing methods often rely on computationally expensive tractography or registration, which are prone to failure in these regions. And several of these pathways have been left completely unexplored in the literature. To address this, we present an automated, subject-specific segmentation pipeline using the BundleParc framework to delineate 98 distinct brainstem and subcortical bundles directly from fiber orientation distribution functions (fODFs).

Methods

Dataset:

We utilized high-quality diffusion MRI (dMRI) data from the Human Connectome Project (HCP) Young Adult dataset (n=1065) for training, with the goal to generate high-fidelity ground truth bundles.

Ground Truth Generation:

Using neuroanatomical texts and recent literature [1-4], we manually reconstructed and segmented 98 fiber bundles. Due to space limitations, an exhaustive list is not included, but these included Basal Ganglia circuits (e.g., Ansa Lenticularis, Nigrostriatal), Cerebellar loops (e.g., DRTT subdivisions), Limbic pathways (e.g., Medial Forebrain Bundle), and Brainstem tracts (e.g., MLF, DLF). Streamlines were generated using inclusion/exclusion and/or seed/stop regions of interest (ROIs) on high-resolution tractography, filtered using an automated outlier removal, and manually curated for quality assurance (which included both manual edits, as well as indicating success/failure for each bundle, and each subject). Figure 1 visualizes example bundles for an individual subject showing the left hemisphere bundle for all bilateral pathways.

BundleParc Training: 

We utilized BundleParc, a deep learning framework that segments and sections bundles directly from fODFs without requiring tracking, registration, or centroid computation during inference. To ensure robustness across variable acquisition qualities, the network was trained on fODFs derived from three schemes: (1) multi-shell multi-tissue CSD (270 directions), (2) single-shell high-angular resolution (90 directions), and (3) single-shell low b-value (b=1000, 12 directions). Data was split 80/10/10 (train/test/validation).

Results

Feasibility: 

98 bundles were successfully reconstructed in training subjects. Figure 1 illustrates the anatomical breadth, covering Basal Ganglia, Cerebellar, Corticopontine, Limbic, and Sensory/Motor systems, as well as the 23 brainstem pathways described by Tang et al. [6].

Robustness: 

Figure 2 demonstrates performance on a withheld test subject and external validation dataset. The algorithm produced anatomically plausible bundles - with correct endpoints, trajectories, and spatial relationships - across drastic differences in data quality. Segmentations derived from simulated low-quality data (12 directions) visually matches that from high quality data (270 directions, multi-shell). External clinical data (2.5mm isotropic) also resulted in anatomically feasible segmentations. The figure highlights the successful segmentation of complex crossing systems, such as the three Dentatorubrothalamic tract subdivisions (crossed DRTT, non-decussating ndDRTT, and cortical DRTCT) and small, disperse pathways (Ansa lenticularis (AL); Lenticular fasciculus; Ansa subthalamica (AS); Fasciculus subthalamicus (FS)).

Efficiency: 

Inference required <1 minute per subject for all pathways on an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU.

Conclusion

We present the first automated, holistic segmentation tool for 98 underexplored brainstem and subcortical pathways. By leveraging BundleParc, the method is robust, simple, and efficient, eliminating the need for tractography or registration during inference. The model’s ability to generalize to low-angular resolution data suggests high utility for clinical datasets where acquisition time is limited.

This algoritm can be used to segment these relatively unstudied bundles on new datasets, enabling quantitative description of these white matter pathways microstructure (e.g., diffusion-based measures of neurite density, dispersion, etc) and macrostructure (e.g., volumes, lengths, areas). We envision this tool used to study normative brain development and aging, disease or disorder, and study the function/dysfunction associated with these pathways.

Future work will extend this binary segmentation to continuous along-tract parcellation to enable spatially specific microstructural analysis and streamline generation. We plan to release intuitive, fast, and containerized open-source tools to facilitate study of brainstem and sub-cortical white matter pathways.
