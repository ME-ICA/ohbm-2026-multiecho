Introduction

The cerebellum is a key brain structure supporting motor coordination, higher-order cognition, and affective functions1. This functional versatility arises from its extensive reciprocal connections with the cerebrum, forming a topographically organised system of intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar connectivity2. In adults, functional gradient analysis has been used to map this macroscale organisation, revealing continuous transitions in functional connectivity that follow a hierarchical axis from unimodal regions (supporting primary somatomotor processing) to transmodal association regions (integrating information across multiple cognitive domains)3. 

Characterising cerebellar organisation in the neonatal period is essential for understanding how its early functional connectivity patterns go on to shape the brain’s lifelong functional architecture and support later cognitive and behavioural development. If gradients reflect intrinsic organisational principles of cerebellar function, arising from the cerebellum’s modular and topographically organised architecture, they should already be present within the neonatal cerebellum and show correspondence between intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar connectivity. In this study, we applied gradient analysis to neonatal resting-state fMRI data to characterise the intrinsic hierarchical organisation of the developing cerebellum and its coupling with cerebral regions in a low-dimensional space. These gradients provide a continuous representation of functional organisation, reflecting how intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar connectivity develop during the first weeks of postnatal life. This work provides evidence of a hierarchical organisation within the neonatal cerebellum and further demonstrates that intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar connectivity share similar organisation principles in early development.

Methods

Acquisition: Data analysis was performed on pre-processed neonatal resting-state functional MRI data from the developing Human Connectome Project (dHCP) dataset4. Subjects were selected based on qualitative and quantitative assessment of cerebellar coverage and image quality. Subjects with excessive in-scanner motion (>10% framewise displacement [FD] motion outliers [FD > 1mm]) and signal dropout in the cerebellum (> 8.5% low-signal outliers [20% below 99th centile of signal intensity]) were excluded. The resulting sample consisted of 155 term-born healthy neonates (67 females; gestational age at birth [GA] 37-42.5 weeks) that were imaged on a 3T Philips Achieva MRI system at term-equivalent postmenstrual age (PMA) between 37.5-44.5 weeks.  T2-weighted images were acquired with isotropic spatial resolution 0.8 x 0.8 x 0.8 mm3 isotropic, field of view 145 x 145 x 108 mm3, TR=12s, and echo time (TE) = 156ms. Resting-state functional data were acquired with a gradient-echo EPI sequence with multiband excitation (factor 9), TR: 392 ms, TE: 38ms, acquired spatial resolution 2.15 x 2.15 x 2.15 mm3 isotropic, for 15 min (2300 volumes).

Data analysis: Cerebro-cerebellar and intra-cerebellar functional connectivity matrices were calculated as voxel-wise Pearson’s correlation coefficients between cerebral-cerebellar and cerebellar-cerebellar time-series respectively. Functional gradients were derived using diffusion map embedding, a nonlinear dimensionality reduction technique that identifies low-dimensional components capturing the principal patterns of variation within high-dimensional connectivity data. These gradients indicate the similarity of each voxel’s functional connections in a continuous space. Gradients were first computed at group-level in the 20 oldest neonates (PMA > 42.5 weeks, n=20) with the best cerebellar coverage to define neonatal cerebellar gradient templates5. The number of gradients was chosen based on a balance of variance explained and visual inspection of spatial patterns. For further analysis, volume-level gradients and atlases were projected to adult cerebellar surfaces using the SUITpy toolbox. To contextualise gradients in relation to cerebellar resting-state networks, cerebellar vertices were assigned to networks using the Buckner 7-network atlas2 and colour-coded in scatterplots of gradient pairs. To characterise inter-gradient similarity, intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar gradients were correlated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (Spearman’s r). To assess developmental change, subject-level gradients were computed from the remaining neonates and aligned to template gradients using Procrustes rotation. Subject-to-group gradient similarity was calculated using Spearman’s r and compared across PMA at scan using partial correlations correcting for motion (Figure 1)

Results

Gradient decomposition yielded four principal axes of functional connectivity within the neonatal cerebellum (Figure 1). The dominant intra-cerebellar gradient I spanned a transmodal-to-unimodal axis,  reflecting a hierarchical continuum from somatomotor and ventral networks through to higher-order regions associated with the cerebral dorsal attention, frontoparietal and default-mode network. Intra-cerebellar gradient II reflected hemispheric differentiation, gradient III captured a posterior-anterior lobe gradient, while gradient IV transitioned from the vermis to the cerebellar hemispheres. Gradients based on cerebro-cerebellar connectivity (Figure 1) yielded a similar transmodal-unimodal gradient I, which was highly correlated with intra-cerebellar gradient I (r = 0.668) (Figure 2). Cerebro-cerebellar gradient II represents a posterior-anterior axis and shows significant overlap with intra-cerebellar gradient I (r = 0.549) and III (r = 0.486) (Figure 2).

Gradient similarity between subject-level gradients and the group template was calculated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Subject-to-group gradient similarity increased with PMA at scan for intra-cerebellar gradient IV (r = 0.234, p (FDR) = 0.026) but no other intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar gradients (p > 0.05 after FDR correction).

Conclusion

This study provides the first mapping of functional connectivity gradients within the neonatal cerebellum, revealing that the macroscale hierarchical cerebro-cerebellar organisation seen in adults is already emerging at birth. The presence of a transmodal-unimodal axis suggests early differentiation of motor and higher-order functional domains. Somatomotor and stimulus-driven ventral attention networks show clear functional alignment across cerebellar gradients, as do default mode, frontoparietal and goal-directed dorsal attention networks. The strong correspondence between intra-cerebellar and cerebro-cerebellar gradients suggests early integration between cerebellar and cerebral systems, and that cerebro-cerebellar coupling follows shared principles of hierarchical organisation from the earliest stages of development. One intra-cerebellar gradient showed increasing maturation with age, demonstrating ongoing development. Together, these findings highlight the cerebellum as an integral component of the neonatal functional connectome and provide a foundational framework for understanding its role in typical and atypical motor and cognitive development.
