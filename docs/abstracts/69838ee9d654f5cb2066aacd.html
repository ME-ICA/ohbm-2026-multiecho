Introduction

The glymphatic system facilitates brain-wide clearance of metabolic waste through coordinated exchange between cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and interstitial spaces. Resting-state fMRI offers a non-invasive approach to probing this process, where coupling between CSF inflow and global BOLD (gBOLD) fluctuations serves as a functional proxy for interpreting glymphatic dynamics.1 A growing body of work has examined this relationship, and while disrupted coupling has been linked to neurodegenerative pathology,2 the field currently lacks the large-scale characterizations necessary to overcome dependencies (e.g age, sex, site) that confound smaller studies. Moreover, most studies rely exclusively on linear gBOLD-CSF associations to quantify disruption. Here, we present a large-scale (N > 15000) study for mapping gBOLD–CSF that examines both linear and non-linear interactions while controlling for major biological and technical sources of variability within a normative modelling framework. This approach provides subject-level deviation scores that separate pathological alterations from expected physiological changes,3 revealing a pathophysiological dissociation between dampened coupling strength in development and temporal instability in neurodegeneration. 

Methods

Study Data: We assembled a massive multi-site rs-fMRI dataset (N=15,307; 5–100 years) aggregated from major public repositories (e.g., ABIDE, HBN, FCON1000, HCPAging etc.) spanning 98 scanners across 13 datasets. The sample included a substantial healthy cohort (N=11139) and a transdiagnostic clinical cohort (N=4168). Healthy participants were divided into a training set (N=8873; 49.6% female) for learning normative trajectories and a held-out test set (N=2266; 48.0% female) for validation. The clinical cohort (N=4168, 34.4% female) was used exclusively for downstream evaluation. 

Preprocessing: All datasets were preprocessed with fMRIPrep including slice-timing correction, motion correction, susceptibility-distortion correction, and skull stripping. Analyses were conducted in each participant’s native functional space to preserve anatomical fidelity. The functional data were registered to each subject’s T1-weighted anatomical space to enable transfer of structural masks. In line with prior work, we intentionally omitted regression of global signal, CSF signal, and motion parameters to retain the physiological components relevant to the coupling phenomenon. The extracted time series were z-scored, detrended, and bandpass filtered (0.01–0.1 Hz) 

Anatomical Segmentation Structural segmentation was performed using the FreeSurfer v8 pipeline to generate precise cortical and subcortical masks grey matter masks from which the averaged signal was treated as the global BOLD signal. To extract the CSF signal, we identified the most inferior slice with >95% brainstem coverage and applied an automated seed-based region-growing procedure targeting the bright voxels surrounding the brainstem boundary. This approach in conjunction with an adaptive thresholding paradigm and geometric constraint isolated the high-intensity CSF inflow signal in the imaging volume. 

Coupling/Interaction Metrics: We used three complementary metrics to quantify coupling between the gBOLD signal and CSF inflow. (1) time-lagged cross-correlation computed within ±12 seconds. Following prior work,2 we identified a canonical negative lag from the population-averaged curve and defined each participant’s coupling index (CI) as the correlation at this lag. (2) we assessed non-linear dependencies using Mutual Information (MI) discretizing signals into 30 bins to estimate the joint probability distribution; to mitigate time length bias, MI was averaged over 50 bootstrapped temporal windows matched to the HC cohort’s minimum scan length. (3) temporal alignment stability was characterized with Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), where the normalized alignment cost was computed as the total Euclidean distance along the optimal warp path divided by its length. 

Normative Modelling: To characterize age and sex related variation in gBOLD-CSF coupling, we employed Generalized Additive Models for Location, Scale, and Shape (GAMLSS). Each metric was modelled using 4-parameter SHASH distribution where location and scale parameters were modelled as smooth age functions using penalized B-splines, with models stratified by sex. Site-specific variability across the 98 sites was rigorously controlled by including scanner identity as a random intercept in both the μ and σ components. The normative models were first validated on the held-out healthy test set before being transferred to the clinical cohort. For each clinical subject, individual deviations were computed as Z-scores from age and sex matched normative estimates. For clinical group-level analysis we assessed the systematic mean shifts and effect sizes relative to healthy norms, while a Levene’s test was used to identify groups with increased biological heterogeneity (variance analysis). We further quantified the specific prevalence of extreme physiological deviations (∣Z∣>1.96) using Fisher’s exact tests and examined age – Z score interactions to compute age related disease progression.

Results

Analysis of the healthy training cohort (n=8873) established the normative physiological baseline for gBOLD-CSF coupling. We identified a population-level "canonical" lag of +3.80 seconds, at which the negative correlation between global BOLD and CSF signals was maximal (mean r=−0.24). GAMLSS modelling of the CI revealed a robust non-linear lifespan trajectory (p<0.001), aligning with established findings, 4 this profile was characterized by stability throughout early and mid-adulthood followed by a progressive decline in magnitude around age 50. 

We projected the clinical population (n=4168, groups: Autism Spectrum disorders, ADHD, Alzheimer's, Schizophrenia, Major Depressive Disorder) into this normative space, where disorders with prominent neurodevelopmental or affective components exhibited significant reductions in coupling strength relative to age and sex-matched expectations. Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) displayed the most pronounced mean deviation (Mean Z=−0.16, p<0.001), followed by Depression (Mean Z=−0.10, p=0.039) and ADHD (Mean Z=−0.09, p=0.005). MI analysis corroborated these linear findings, revealing a profound reduction in shared information content in ASD (Mean Z=−0.25, p<0.001). This was driven by a disproportionate burden of pathologically extreme MI values in the ASD cohort (9.4% prevalence vs 5.6% in controls; Odd’s Ratio=1.75, p<0.001), indicating a subset of individuals with markedly impaired non-linear coupling. In surprising contrast, neither Dementia/AD nor Schizophrenia demonstrated significant shifts in mean coupling magnitude (Mean Z≈0, p>0.05), suggesting preservation of average amplitude-based synchrony in these cohorts.

While mean CI and MI was preserved in neurodegenerative and psychotic disorders, DTW revealed a distinct phenotype of temporal instability. Across all diagnostic groups, mean normalized alignment cost did not deviate from the normative reference (all group means Z ≈ 0). However, Dementia/AD exhibited a striking increase in biological heterogeneity (Variance Ratio=1.48, p<0.001) and the highest prevalence of extreme dysynchrony outliers (10.4%; OR=2.23, p<0.001). Significant variance inflation was also observed in Depression (Variance Ratio=1.32, p<0.001) and ASD (Variance Ratio=1.17, p=0.003). Interaction tests between age and Z-scores across all three interaction metrics yielded no significant linear effects (all interaction p>0.05).

Conclusion

This study establishes a normative framework for quantifying the gBOLD-CSF interaction in a principled manner that captures linear and non-linear dynamics at scale. By referencing individual patients against a massive multi-site dataset (N>15,000), we identified a distinct pathophysiological dissociation: neurodevelopmental conditions (particularly ASD) are characterized by a generalized reduction in coupling strength, whereas neurodegenerative conditions (Dementia/Alzheimer’s) manifest as temporal instability. This dissociation between preserved mean and increased variability (Depression, ASD) could suggest that while the average patient maintains normative synchronization, these clinical populations harbour distinct subpopulations characterized by highly unstable temporal coupling. These findings suggest that the gBOLD-CSF axis could reflect distinct failure modes: dampened arousal-fluid dynamics in impaired development versus a dysregulated neurovascular response in aging populations. Furthermore, the absence of progressive age-related worsening in clinical deviations suggests these metrics represent stable phenotypic traits given that no age dependent worsening was observed within the distinct age ranges characteristic of each disorder.
