Introduction

Intermittent theta-burst stimulation (iTBS) targeting the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) is widely used to modulate cortical excitability in both experimental and clinical contexts. Despite its growing application, effects are heterogeneous and therapeutic gains remain limited. One proposed strategy to increase efficacy is stimulation priming, whereby a conditioning theta-burst protocol precedes iTBS. In particular, combining protocols with opposing physiological effects has been hypothesized to enhance downstream plasticity. Despite growing interest, the impact of prefrontal TBS priming on whole-brain functional connectivity remains insufficiently understood.

Methods

Forty-seven healthy adults completed a randomized, within-subject, counterbalanced study comprising four stimulation sessions separated by one week. Sessions included: (1) sham priming with sham stimulation, (2) iTBS without priming, (3) iTBS primed by iTBS, and (4) iTBS primed by continuous TBS (cTBS). All stimulation targeted the left DLPFC. Resting-state fMRI data were acquired immediately before and after each session on a 3T MRI scanner. fMRI analyses used CONN v22.a and SPM12. Preprocessing followed CONN’s modular pipeline, including voxel-displacement map creation, realignment with fieldmap-based susceptibility distortion correction, slice-timing correction, outlier detection, indirect segmentation and MNI normalization, and spatial smoothing. Whole-brain seed-based connectivity (SBC) maps were computed as Fisher-transformed bivariate correlation coefficients between a left DLPFC seed and each voxel, estimated via a weighted GLM. The seed was a 10-mm radius sphere centered at the left DLFPC (MNI coordinates:−38, 44, 26), gray-matter masked using each participant’s anatomy. To quantify stimulation-induced effects, delta SBC maps (post − pre) were entered into a within-subject one-way ANOVA in SPM12 using a flexible factorial design. Whole-brain inference tested the main effect of condition using an F-contrast with cluster-wise FWE correction (p < 0.05) and uncorrected voxel-wise threshold p < 0.005. Significant F-maps were used as masks for post hoc contrasts. Post hoc maps were thresholded at p < 0.00625 within the mask. 

Results

A one-way within-subjects ANOVA on stimulation-induced connectivity change maps revealed a significant main effect of stimulation condition, F(3,138) = 4.47, p = 0.005, η²ₚ = 0.09. Significant effects were observed in three clusters: a left frontal cluster, a right middle temporal cluster, and a left occipital cluster. Post hoc contrasts within these clusters demonstrated condition-specific modulation of functional connectivity. Compared with the control condition, iTBS alone did not produce significant connectivity increases; instead, a significant decrease was observed in the left middle occipital gyrus. In this same occipital cluster, priming iTBS with iTBS resulted in significantly greater connectivity change relative to iTBS alone. In contrast, priming iTBS with either iTBS or cTBS led to significantly reduced connectivity changes in the left frontal cluster compared with iTBS alone, encompassing dorsolateral superior frontal, middle frontal, and inferior frontal (triangular) regions. Similarly, in the right temporal cluster, cTBS-iTBS priming produced significantly lower connectivity changes than iTBS alone. No priming condition yielded significant connectivity increases relative to iTBS. Direct comparison of the two priming protocols showed that cTBS-iTBS induced significantly stronger reductions in connectivity than iTBS-iTBS in both occipital and temporal clusters.

Conclusion

Prefrontal TBS priming does not  necessarily enhance iTBS-induced resting-state connectivity changes. Instead, sequential stimulation, particularly cTBS followed by iTBS, was associated with reduced connectivity modulation in prefrontal and distributed cortical regions relative to iTBS alone. These results indicate that prefrontal TBS priming may constrain, rather than facilitate, network-level reconfiguration, highlighting the need for caution when applying priming theta-burst protocols in both experimental and clinical settings.
