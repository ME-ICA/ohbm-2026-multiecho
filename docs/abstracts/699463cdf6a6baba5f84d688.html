Introduction

Estimating effective connectivity (EC) from fMRI data using vector autoregressive (VAR) models offers a powerful framework for uncovering directed neural interactions.1,2 However, VAR-based EC estimates are highly sensitive to preprocessing choices, which can substantially affect both the reliability and interpretability of connectivity measures.2,3 The primary objective of this study was to systematically evaluate the effects of preprocessing choices on VAR-based EC analysis and to extend EC assessment beyond conventional VAR coefficients by incorporating cumulative impulse response functions (CIRF) and forecast error variance decomposition (FEVD).4,5

Methods

We evaluated the effects of preprocessing strategies on VAR-based EC estimation using two complementary datasets—(i) synthetic fMRI data with predefined causal structures under varying noise levels and spatial smoothing conditions, and (ii) task-based fMRI data acquired during visuomotor tasks.2,4 Preprocessing factors included spatial smoothing, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) levels, and representative time series extraction methods.3 Using simulated fMRI data with known ground-truth causal structures, we assessed the impact of spatial smoothing and representative signal extraction techniques—including voxel averaging (TSavg), first principal component (PC1st), and cumulative 95% variance principal component (PC95p) under varying SNR conditions.4,5 The empirical analysis utilized publicly available visuomotor task data to examine preprocessing effects under realistic experimental conditions. VAR models were applied across datasets using a unified pipeline, and EC was evaluated using multiple metrics—VAR coefficients representing direct effects, CIRF coefficients capturing cumulative and indirect dynamics, and FEVD quantifying directional contribution across prediction horizons.

Results

Across preprocessing strategies, VAR-based EC estimates showed differences in both the inferred directionality and magnitude of interregional interactions, depending on the choices of time series extraction methods and assessment metrics. These effects were consistently observed across direct effects, cumulative network dynamics, and contribution-based measures, highlighting the multi-level impact of preprocessing on EC interpretation. Fig. 1 summarizes differences in significant edges between time series extraction methods for both direct effects and total dynamics. Paired comparisons revealed that several edges identified as significant in VAR coefficients (β) showed altered sign or directionality when evaluated using CIRF-based total dynamics (c). Directed graphs indicated that excitatory or inhibitory influences inferred from direct effects were not always preserved when cumulative network dynamics were considered (Fig. 1A, 1C, 1E, and 1G). Box plots further demonstrated systematic differences in the magnitude distributions of β and c across extraction methods, suggesting that preprocessing choices influence not only statistical significance but also the interpretation of directional effects (Fig. 1B, 1D, 1F, 1H). Fig. 2 focuses on FEVD-based contributions for edges that were significant at the maximum prediction horizon (H=6).  Significant differences in ΔFEVDH6 were observed between PC-based and averaging-based time series (Fig. 2A, 2E). Across horizons, TSavg exhibited consistently larger absolute FEVD values compared to PC-based approaches (PC1st and PC95p), indicating scale-dependent differences in estimated contribution (Figs. 2B-D, 2F-H). While PC-based methods showed more gradual changes in contribution across horizons, the averaging-based method demonstrated amplified contribution patterns, particularly in smoothed data.

Conclusion

Our findings indicate that preprocessing choices substantially affect VAR-based EC estimates across multiple assessment metrics and that exclusive reliance on VAR coefficients may provide an incomplete characterization of brain network dynamics. By integrating CIRF and FEVD with traditional VAR-based measures, this approach enables a more comprehensive assessment of direct, indirect, and cumulative interactions in fMRI data. This study provides practical guidance for preprocessing decisions and underscores the value of multi-metric EC evaluation in both simulation and task-based neuroimaging studies.
