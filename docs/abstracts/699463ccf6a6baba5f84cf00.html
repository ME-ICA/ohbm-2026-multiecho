Introduction

The power spectrum of electrophysiological time series mixes information from a variety of sources in the time domain. Oscillations form the periodic component of brain dynamics and reflect rhythmic synchronisation of populations of neurons. Whilst oscillations have received widespread attention, there is growing interest in the non-oscillatory, aperiodic component of the neuronal power spectrum. This component reflects the inverse relationship between frequency and power across the spectrum of the form 1/f^alpha. The interpretation of the aperiodic component is less clear than for oscillations, they may reflect the low-pass frequency properties of dendrites1 or synaptic excitation/inhibition balance2.

Irregular‑Resampling Auto‑Spectral Analysis (IRASA)3 is a popular non‑parametric method to separate the aperiodic background from narrowband oscillations in neural power spectra. It works through the pragmatic insight that resampling timeseries will shift oscillatory components to different centre frequencies but, on average, will preserve the aperiodic components. In practice IRASA works by resampling the time series to faster and slower sampling rates and averaging spectra so that oscillatory peaks are suppressed while scale‑free components are preserved.  

Whilst intuitive, clearly motivated and highly impactful on the field of electrophysiology, IRASA has shortcomings4. Critically, running IRASA on time series with broad oscillatory components can lead to spreading and attenuation but not full removal of the oscillatory peak. This abstract presents a set of simplifications and improvements for IRASA that aim to enhance its performance on noisy electrophysiological time series.

Methods

IRASA separates aperiodic 1/f structure from narrowband oscillations in neural power spectra. The signal is resampled by a factor ‘h’ and its reciprocal ‘1/h’. The spectrum of the resampled signals are computed with the same PSD estimator, and each reciprocal pair is combined with the geometric mean. This process is repeated across values of ‘h’ and ‘1/h’ before full set of geometric mean spectra are averaged using the median. This final averaged spectrum is the estimate of the aperiodic component which can be subtracted from the original signal to isolate the oscillatory components. Linear model fits can be performed on the aperiodic component to estimate the aperiodic slope.

This abstract looks to improve on one critical aspect of IRASA, performance when there are large or broad oscillatory peaks in the spectrum. Two modifications are made to achieve this goal and are visualised in Figure 1.

Firstly, the geometric mean step is removed altogether. This step was designed to provide an initial estimate of the fractal power spectrum. However, both the upsampled and downsampled reflection of the oscillatory peak is preserved in geometric mean of the reciprocal pair geometric mean. It is not clear why the geometric mean across a single pair of spectra appropriate for an intermediate estimate, and moreover, why an intermediate estimate is needed at all. In this abstract, all resampled spectra contribute to a single computation of the median which is robust to outliers and effective in attenuating broad oscillatory peaks.

Secondly, the reciprocal pairings of resampling factors is replaced with fixed log-linear set of resampling factors. The reciprocal pairings are a simple heuristic for choosing resampling factors, however they have different behaviours for the up- and down- sampling. Using the default settings, the up-sampling factors ‘h’ increase linearly between 1.1 and 1.9 with a fixed separation between each step (eg [1.1, 1.3, 1.5, 1.7, 1.9]. In contrast, the ‘1/h’ downsampling factors non-linearly converge towards 0.5 with the separation decreasing between each step (eg [0.91, 0.77, 0.67, 0.59, 0.53]). This effect is stronger with larger numbers of resampling factors.

Practically, this leads to ‘bunching’ of oscillatory peaks on the low side of the spectrum. Whilst this is countered by some compression in the downsampled spectra, it still contributes the presence of residual oscillatory peaks in the aperiodic spectrum estimate. In this abstract, the resampling factors are chosen to be log-linearly spaced rather than relying on a reciprocal fraction. The new factors still have a convergence point at zero rather than 0.5 which reduces bunching.

The choice of resampling factors selects the smoothness of the spectrum. IRASA combines information from across the spectrum to produce an estimate of the aperiodic component, a wider range of resampling factors will include a wider span across the spectrum and a smoother estimate.

Resting-state EEG recordings from 191 individuals from the open-source EEG-LEMON dataset were analysed. The details of the original study including participant recruitment, experimental procedures, and ethical approval can be found in the original publication5. All data pre-processing was carried out using MNE-Python and OSL-ephys.

Each dataset was loaded into MNE Python and set with a channel montage. 6 minutes of data were cropped out and the data resampled from 2,500Hz to 1280Hz. The Zapline algorithm was applied to attenuate line noise and ICA used to identify components relating to horizonal and vertical electro-oculogram measures. The final dataset was reconstructed without the components relating to eye movements. The data from POz was extracted for further analysis. Welch’s periodogram with a 10 second Hamming window was estimated from the POz data prior to application of IRASA and modified IRASA using the same settings as the initial Welch’s periodogram.

Results

The results from a single dataset show the residual oscillatory peaks present in the IRASA spectrum, and that these peaks have been attenuated in the modified algorithm. This picture is clearer still in the whole group average across all 211 datasets in the LEMON database. The modified algorithm results in a smoothly descending aperiodic component that isn’t pulled towards the oscillatory peaks. Both algorithms are able to estimate an aperiodic component that retains the ‘knee’ around 30Hz. This is as the resampling methods used in both algorithms mean that the aperiodic component is adaptive within the limits of the resampling and that the exponent of the component can change with frequency.

The difference is more pronounced when comparing the aperiodic components between the older and younger participants in the LEMON database. The residual oscillatory peaks substantially modify the slope of the IRASA aperiodic spectrum around the 10Hz peak. Critically, the residual peaks differ between groups with the higher residual peak of the older adult group appearing higher above the aperiodic component than the lower peak. The aperiodic components from the modified algorithm remove this interference and clearly replicate established findings that younger adults have a steep aperiodic component than older adults.

Conclusion

Overall, this abstract demonstrates two modifications to the IRASA algorithm that solve key shortcomings to its performance in realistic electrophysiological data. The critical issue is residual oscillatory peaks in the estimated aperiodic component. These results show that dropping the intermediate geometric mean calculation and adapting the resampling factors to avoid bunching substantially improve the aperiodic component estimation and simplify interpretation of group differences. Further work needs to be done to explore the performance of the new algorithm in relation to a broader range of applications and in particular in power spectra with a substantial 'knee' in their aperiodic spectrum.
