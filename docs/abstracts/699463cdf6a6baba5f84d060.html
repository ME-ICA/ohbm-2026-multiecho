Introduction

In diffusion MRI (dMRI), vasogenic edema manifests as a major fraction of isotropic water that dilutes the anisotropic intra-axonal portion of the signal. Many tractography algorithms mistake vasogenic edema for the white matter (WM) boundary and terminate tracking to prevent producing spurious streamlines[1]. As a result, visual representations of fascicles traversing edema are often compromised, limiting the clinical utility of tractography. 

Common techniques to mitigate the impact of vasogenic edema in dMRI, such as Free Water Elimination Diffusion Tensor Imaging (FWE-DTI)[2] or Freewater EstimatoR using iNtErpolated iniTialization (FERNET)[3], decompose the acquired signal into two compartments – an isotropic component representing edema and a residual component representing neural tissue. To satisfy the time constraints of clinical MRI, FWE-DTI and FERNET implement the Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI) model due to its low requirements on a data sampling scheme. However, DTI is unable to represent crossing WM fibers which are ubiquitous in the brain.

We address this hurdle with Orientation Distribution Function Fingerprinting (ODF-FP)[4] – a dictionary-based WM fiber reconstruction algorithm that accommodates variability of neural tissue. Thanks to the forward-modeling paradigm[5], enabled by advancement in contemporary computing technology, ODF-FP can represent complex WM fiber structures using clinically-feasible single-shell dMRI. In this work, we enhance ODF-FP with a regularization term that counterbalances the drop of anisotropy in vasogenic edema to improve WM fiber tracking inside the impacted zones.

To test the proposed approach, we quantify the volume of the WM tracts traversing vasogenic edema in preoperative dMRI of glioma patients. Our results show that ODF-FP significantly outperforms standard approaches on clinical-quality single-shell dMRI offering an opportunity for application in neurosurgery.

Methods

Patients: We studied dMRI of 19 glioma patients (6 females / 13 males, aged 32–73 y/o, mean±std: 53±13 y/o) with severe vasogenic edema. The tumor types included astrocytoma (4 patients), oligodendroglioma (7), and glioblastoma (8).

Fully sampled data: The diffusion-weighted images (DWIs) were acquired at 3 b-shells with 20,60,60 diffusion-encoding directions sampled, respectively, at b=1000, 2500, 5000 s/mm2.

Single-shell data: To assess the potential for clinical translation, we simultaneously considered a subsampled single-shell data set with 20 DWIs at b=1000 s/mm2, which contemporary clinical scanners can acquire within a few minutes.

Preprocessing: Our MRtrix3 pipeline consisted of Marchenko-Pastur PCA denoising followed by removal of artifacts from Gibbs ringing, B1 field inhomogeneity, subject motion, and eddy currents. 

ODF-FP: We executed ODF-FP with a randomly sampled ODF-dictionary of 106 elements having 0≤N≤3 fibers per voxel, generated within the default ranges of microstructure parameters[4]. We used the exact same settings when processing the fully sampled and the single-shell DWIs.

The Python source code of ODF-FP is available at: https://github.com/filipp02/odffp

Anisotropy boosting: We modified the ODF-FP matching formula as follows:

x' = argmaxdj ∈ D ( xTdj - Njλ - min(dj )μ ),

where D is an ODF-dictionary, x is an input ODF, x' is the best-matching ODF selected among all ODF-dictionary items dj ∈ D, Nj is the number of fibers per voxel, and λ, μ ≥ 0 are regularization weights. The term Njλ penalizes spurious fibers, whereas the added term min(dj ) boosts diffusion anisotropy by penalizing the lowest values of ODF to prevent premature termination of tracking in edema.

We empirically chose the spurious fibers penalty factor λ=2·10-5 and the anisotropy boosting factor μ=0.1 throughout the whole brain. On top of that, we tested an exploratory set of anisotropy boosting factors μedema∈{0.1, 0.3, 0.5} used only inside the manually drawn regions of edema. For simplicity of notation, we will refer to these cases as ODF-FP0.1, ODF-FP0.3, and ODF-FP0.5. Finally, we ran ODF-FP without any boosting either inside or outside edema (ODF-FP(no boost)).

Standard methods: For reference, we processed the fully sampled DWIs using common approaches, namely:

  • Generalized Q-sampling Imaging (GQI), 
  • FSL Bedpostx, 
  • Constrained Spherical Deconvolution (CSD) with the Multi-Shell Multi-Tissue option (CSD-MSMT). 

For the single-shell DWIs, we executed:

  • GQI and FSL as before, 
  • CSD without the MSMT option (due to the missing higher b-shells), 
  • FWE-DTI and FERNET in their original implementations dedicated for single-shell data.

Tractography: We dissected Arcuate Fasciculus (AF), Corticospinal (CST) and Corticobulbar Tract (CBT) using the atlas-based tracking in DSI Studio with the tolerance of 30 mm aimed to accommodate the variability of tissue impacted by tumor (Figure 1). As the stopping criterion, we set the threshold on Normalized Quantitative Anisotropy (NQA) at 0.10. Note that NQA is the Quantitative Anisotropy (QA) measure scaled to the [0,1] range, which we used to ensure consistency among all tested subjects. Additionally, we limited the number of random seeds to 1 million, we set the maximum turning angle of 60°, and restricted the streamline lengths to the range between 30 and 200 mm.  

Evaluation: For each WM fiber reconstruction method m and for each dissected tract, we calculated normalized edema overlap enhancement (OE) defined as:

OE(m) = ( vol(m) - minvol(·) ) / ( maxvol(·) - minvol(·) ) · 100%, 

where 

vol(m) = |tract(m) ∩ edema|    

is the volume of the overlap between the tract dissected with the method m and the vasogenic edema region.

Note that minvol(·) and maxvol(·) are, respectively, the lowest and the highest volumes among all tested methods per subject. Thus, for each patient and for each tract, OE(m) varied between 0% (worst-performing method) and 100% (best-performing method).

Results

Our experiments showed that anisotropy boosting in ODF-FP had a visible impact on tractography. Particularly, the increment of µedema gradually increased the edema OE (Figure 2).

The mean overlap between tractography and edema was significantly higher in ODF-FP0.5 than in the standard methods, both in the fully sampled (p≤0.03) and the single-shell DWIs (p≤0.05). For instance, our reconstruction of AF traversing edema in 19 patients with the fully sampled dMRI (Figure 2, top) reached OE(ODF-FP0.5) = 74±28% (mean±std), whereas the best-performing reference method, FSL, scored OE(FSL)=45±22%. In other words, on average, 74% of the edematous WM volume pertaining to AF – tracked with the combined effort of all tested methods – was de facto reconstructed with ODF-FP0.5, while FSL reconstructed 45% of this volume. 

For the single-shell data, ODF-FP0.5 maintained an almost identical overlap between AF and edema (74±29%). The best-performing reference method in the single-shell DWIs was CSD (56±24%), while the tensor-based methods produced the lowest OEs: 2±3% (FWE-DTI) and 4±5% (FERNET). 

The analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) including age, sex, tumor type and tumor laterality as covariates showed that the impact of these covariates on the overlap between tractography and edema was negligible, with the lowest p-value of 0.36 attributed to the age of patients. 

Similar patterns in the overlap measures and the low impact of covariates (Figure 2, bottom) were observed in all cases with CST impacted by vasogenic edema (15 out of 19 patients). 

Figure 1 shows a representative example of pyramidal tract (i.e., the CST/CBT complex) traversing a large region of vasogenic edema. While all tested methods visualized the cortical projections of the tracts located near the midline, only CSD, FSL, and ODF-FP reconstructed the lateral branches most impacted by edema. Among these branches, our method visualized the biggest part of the CBT. Also, note that data subsampling did not cause ODF-FP to produce less streamlines. However, it resulted in a slightly more winding shape of the reconstructed tracts and an occurrence of potential false positives at µedema=0.5.

Conclusion

The accuracy of tractography varies throughout the brain parenchyma with significant errors observed in peritumoral vasogenic edema[1]. Our experiments show that ODF-FP with the proposed diffusion anisotropy boosting mechanism can improve the robustness to signal distortions in edematous WM.

Forward modeling: Unlike the standard methods, which solve multidimensional optimization problems to find several parameters of the assumed model representing an acquired data set (inverse modeling), ODF-FP implements the opposite approach. Our method first generates an exhaustive ODF-dictionary with a variety of random WM fiber configurations called fingerprints[4], then finds the nearest match between them and the ODFs obtained from the acquired DWIs. Thanks to this paradigm shift, ODF-FP solves the linear similarity problem, which can be effectively approximated using single-shell DWIs.

Clinical utility: The standard of care in glioma treatment requires maximal safe tumor resection. However, neurosurgeons lack accurate structural imaging modalities to support their decision regarding the resection volume when vasogenic peritumoral edema distorts WM tractography. In this work, we demonstrate that ODF-FP can outperform the standard fiber reconstruction methods when executed on research-quality densely sampled DWIs as well as the clinically-feasible single-shell data with 20 encoding directions at b=1000 s/mm2. Especially the latter aspect carries the potential for clinical translation, since the standard methods often fail to compensate the impact of excessive fluids in clinically-feasible dMRI.

Practical recommendations: As with many regularization parameters, choosing the anisotropy boosting factor may require a trial and error procedure. In our experiments, µedema=0.3 or 0.5 proved more efficient than µedema=0.1. Particularly, µedema=0.5 maximized the overlap between tractography and edema; however, the risk of false positives may incline toward choosing the lower value (µedema=0.3).

Limitations: Quantification of clinical tractography is inherently limited due to the lack of ground truth. Therefore, the evaluation measure presented in this study is indirect. The edema OE metric is based on the elementary assumption that improvement in tracking across edematous WM increases the overlap between tractography and edema. However, the opposite relation (i.e., that higher overlap implies improvement in tractography) may not hold due to false positives. Additional limitations arose from the relatively low number of patients (with respect to the variability of glioma) and the use of atlas-based tracking approach, which helped us improve consistency of the results, yet also might have limited the sensitivity to inter-subject variability.

Future work: The proposed approach is promising, although its clinical applicability requires further validation. Future work should employ a dedicated synthetic model of edematous tissue or a more precise reference measurement in vivo (such as intraoperative electrical stimulation of the brain) to ensure direct evaluation of the tracking accuracy.
